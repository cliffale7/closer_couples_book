## 001 The Stranger Across From Me

**Status: AWAITING_OWNER_REAL_USE**

**Why this script:** A short discovery interview. Failure looks like an unclear clock split, questions you have already worn out, or so much drafting that the conversation never starts.

**Find it:** From the contents, ID 001, Part 1. Note the clock you chose before you sit. Short is about 10 minutes. Standard is about 30. Extended is about 50.

**Materials:** Two seats, a timer you can both see, paper or a notes app. No transcript of the answers.

**Run:**

1. Name anything already off the table. "Pass" and "smaller" need no substitute story.
2. Short: skip the three-question draft. Each of you writes one question a polite stranger could ask, not answerable in one word, and easy to decline.
3. Standard: about 5 minutes in silence. Three questions each, mixed across a year they felt like themselves, a habit a new friend would misread, and a belief they rarely say in a group.
4. Coin toss, or the more restless curiosity, decides who interviews first. Ask one drafted question. Follow-ups stay on that answer: one in the short version, up to two in the standard. The interviewer does not answer inside the turn. Silence belongs to the person asked.
5. When the turn ends, swap. Equal time. A pass still counts as the turn.
6. About 2 minutes at the end. Each of you names one fact that had gone dull. Then stop.
7. Extended, only if you still want it: one unfinished detail from the first round, about 8 minutes each. If there is no loose thread, the extended version is over. Do not invent one.

**Watch:** Did the two turns match? Did a follow-up become the interviewer's own story? Did the draft eat the hour? Did any question need this household's private history? That question belongs to 005, not here.

## 017 Four Minutes of Forever

**Status: AWAITING_OWNER_REAL_USE**

**Why this script:** Mutual attention with an exit. Failure looks like pressure to stare, confusion about stopping, or a promise that four minutes will make two people fall in love.

**Find it:** ID 017, Part 3. The bout itself is 4 minutes inside a session of about 15. Short is 1 minute inside about 8. Extended keeps the same 4 minutes and adds optional talk, about 20 in all. Longer is not more correct.

**Materials:** Two seats you can both hold, a timer a hand can stop without searching, optional soft light. Phones leave the room.

**Run:**

1. Set your own distance. Reclining counts. Agree that a raised hand or the word "stop" ends the bout, and that ending early completes it.
2. If eye contact is wrong for either of you, use the no-gaze substitute for the same number of minutes: attention to your own hands, or to voice and the quiet after one ordinary sentence. Do not call that substitute a copy of the stranger-gaze studies.
3. One optional sentence: "We look, and we notice. We can stop." Then stop talking.
4. Start the timer. Blinking is allowed. A brief glance away, then a return, still fits. Nobody opens a topic, checks a phone, or jokes their way out of the whole bout. There is no prize for an unblinking stare.
5. When the timer ends, or when someone stops it, look away on purpose. Each of you names one sensation, thought, or impulse from your own side. Do not interpret the other face.
6. If only one of you wants to talk afterward, the quiet wins.

**Watch:** Did anyone feel coached during the minutes? Did the chapter's research note stay a limit rather than a forecast? Was the stop obvious without a speech?

## 034 The Yes, No, Maybe Game

**Status: AWAITING_OWNER_REAL_USE**

**Why this script:** A private sort of romantic, affectionate, sensual, and sexual ideas. Failure looks like pressure to share the whole sheet, no clear next step, or a written yes treated as consent.

**Find it:** ID 034, Part 5. Short is about 20 minutes and uses only the affectionate and romantic lanes. Standard is about 45. Extended is about 75 and adds a review date you may later cancel.

**Materials:** Two private sheets, a pen, or a recording only the writer can open. No shared screen during the sort. Do not do this while either of you is intoxicated.

**Run:**

1. Say the rules aloud before anyone writes. Blank is not a shy yes. Every sample line is optional. A whole lane may be skipped. Sexual items are skippable even in the standard game.
2. Sort alone, about 15 minutes in the standard version. Four headings, three columns: yes, no, maybe. A maybe may carry a condition. It is not consent stored for later.
3. Still private: star up to five yeses you are willing to discuss, and up to three nos you want known so they are not stumbled into. Short caps are three yeses and two nos. Unstarred nos stay unseen. Zero starred nos is allowed.
4. Compare starred items only, one at a time. The listener says "heard," or asks one clarifying question, then stops. A no gets "heard." Do not offer a softer version unless the person who wrote the no offers it.
5. Name at most one easy shared yes, or none. If you name one, say in the same breath: easy and soon, you still ask, and nothing else is scheduled. A starred yes is not the moment.
6. If the gap hurts, skip the easy yes and end. That is a finished game.

**Watch:** Did either of you feel required to show an unstarred no? Was the next action obvious after the last star? Did anyone treat a paper yes as permission to touch or to have sex?

## 043 The Newlywed Game, Without the Wedding

**Status: AWAITING_OWNER_REAL_USE**

**Materials:** Two answer sheets, pens, optional paper stars. Sit so you cannot read each other's pages. On a call, share a screen only when that question is up.

**Run:**

## 097 The Emotional Archaeologists

**Status: AWAITING_OWNER_REAL_USE**

**Why this script:** One feeling, examined in layers. Failure looks like one partner diagnosing the other, or the hour collapsing into ordinary "how do you feel?"

**Find it:** ID 097, Part 13. Short is about 15 minutes and one layer. Standard is about 40. Extended is about 50, and the explorer may write the layers first. One reaction only. A second feeling is another day.

**Materials:** Paper or a notes app, a timer, a stop word that will not come up by accident.

**Run:**

1. Choose who explores. The reaction should be recent and small enough for tonight. If the hour is panic, crisis, or abuse, leave the roles and respond as a partner.
2. If you actually need a complaint about something the other person did, say that in ordinary language. Do not bury a case inside the dig.
3. About 3 minutes. The explorer writes one sentence: the reaction, and the cause they walked in with. Read it. Leave it visible.
4. Say the stop word aloud. "Shelf" or "red pen" is enough. It ends the dig immediately.
5. The archaeologist asks one question at a time, and may skip a layer: what happened just before; what the body did; what meaning arrived first; what else might be true; what the feeling might be protecting or wanting.
6. After each answer, reflect the explorer's nouns and ask, "Is that close?" If the answer is no, repeat the correction and stop improving it.
7. Do not use a diagnostic label, "so you're really just," a cleverer summary, or a turn that gathers evidence about the partner.
8. The explorer speaks the last sentence: keep the original cause, revise it, or abandon it with no replacement. A need, if one appeared, stays a hypothesis. The archaeologist does not assign it.

**Watch:** Who named the revised cause, the explorer or the archaeologist? Did the reflection stay inside the words that were said? Could you tell this from a normal check-in by the third question?

## S04 The Branching Evening

**Status: AWAITING_OWNER_REAL_USE**

**Why this script:** A hall and three rooms, with a return after each one. Failure looks like a branch that dies by accident, or a return that does not say what state you are in.

**Find it:** Signature S04. Choose the version at the hall before you enter anything. Short is about 30 minutes and one room. Standard is about 100 minutes and two rooms. Extended is about 2.5 hours and may open all three. Ending sooner, from the hall, is allowed in all three. This script can be run on the short version in one sitting. Use the extended version only when you have the window, and use it if you want to test a return to a room you already entered.

**Materials:** The four choice cards, or the chapter open to the card you are in. A visible timer, a warm light for the kitchen, a simple snack, coats if you might go outside, one small lamp, and a shoe or a book to mark the hall.

**Run:**

1. One minute for closed subjects. "Pass" or "smaller" needs no substitute. Mark the hall. In a studio, the door is the hall, the counter is the kitchen, and the lamp seat is the chair.
2. Read only the card for the place where you are. From the hall, one open sentence: "Kitchen," "Dark walk," "Quiet chair," or "End the night." If you want different rooms, say one word each. A match goes. The first disagreement is a coin: a right call means the caller picks, a wrong call means the flipper picks. After that, the person who did not make the last accepted choice picks. "End the night" is a real pick.
3. "Turn back," said before both of you have entered, spends nothing and returns you to the Hall card. On the short version, a second turn back ends the evening, and that ending is finished.
4. A room begins only when both of you have crossed into it. Leaving early still spends it. Say "hall," return, count it, and do not re-enter it tonight.
5. Kitchen: a snack, then a 10-minute timer and three prompts. The listener repeats one sentence. A pass replaces an answer. Then read the Kitchen card and name where you go. The path runs through the hall.
6. Dark walk: say "Block" or "Hallway" before the outer door. Weather, pain, or a no closes the block, and the hallway is a complete walk. The seated variation, a moving hand for 12 minutes, also spends the walk. Speak the rounds only while moving. "Hall" mid-route completes the walk and sends you back.
