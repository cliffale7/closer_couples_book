# Evidence review: dossiers 001–036

Reviewed 14 October 2026 against `research/CITATION_WHITELIST.md` only. Premises were checked against `source/experience_catalog.json` for ids 1–36. No paper, abstract, or book page was opened for this review. Nothing here is a new verification of an article. A dossier sentence is held to the whitelist wording.

Dossiers and chapters were not edited.

## Fail standard

Fail only for an invented citation, a claim beyond the whitelist, a copied Aron question list, a scavenger-hunt mechanic, or a missing harm limit.

Also applied: Gottman material may name only love maps, bids for connection, repair attempts, and the emotional bank account, and must not state a divorce statistic. K1989 stays two minutes of mutual gaze with strangers. A1997 stays a disclosure procedure. J2017 stays a review of wanted touch. Love languages stay outside science unless I2024 is the citation and the limits stay intact. Listening is not immunity for harm.

Notes marked “not a fail” are limits for the chapter writer. They are not failures.

No dossier in this batch invents an author, DOI, sample size, or paper. No dossier prints the A1997 question list. This review did not open that paper, so the check is of the dossier text. No dossier designs a scavenger hunt. Every source premise matches the catalog. Every dossier has a Consent and harm section that states a real limit.

## 001 — The Stranger Across From Me

**Pass.** Premise matches: a stranger interview that finds what familiarity had filed as settled. A1997 Study 1 is kept to a roughly 45-minute set of escalating self-disclosure and relationship-building tasks, greater post-interaction closeness than small talk, and participants who were not long-term couples falling in love. Studies 2 and 3 are correctly reported as finding no closeness differences for attitude matching, expected liking, or an explicit closeness goal. The dossier says this interview does not inherit Study 1’s result. L1998 matches the diary wording, including the partial mediator and the stronger emotional-disclosure predictor in Study 2. Gottman is limited to love maps, with no percentage and no divorce statistic. The harm limit lets either person decline a question and refuses use of the role to reopen a closed subject. The dossier forbids copying the A1997 question list and does not print one. The three example shapes are not a reproduced list.

## 013 — Confession Without Consequence

## 015 — When I Feel Small

## 017 — Four Minutes of Forever

**Pass.** Premise matches: four minutes of comfortable, uninterrupted eye contact, noticing what arises, without a need to talk. The dossier does not say that four minutes of eye contact makes couples fall in love. It says the opposite, in the evidence section and again in the red flags, and it tells the chapter to say that plainly. K1989 is kept to two studies of unbroken mutual gaze with an opposite-sex stranger, associated with higher self-reported affection and liking, and in Study 2 with passionate love. Study 1 is eye gaze, hand gaze, and blink-counting. Mutual gaze is distinguished from one person staring while the other looks away. Study 2 is limited to people more inclined to read their own behavior as information about themselves. The dossier says this was not a study of established couples and not a four-minute prescription. A1997 is identified as a roughly 45-minute escalating disclosure procedure, not an eye-contact recipe, and the question list is forbidden. No question list is printed. No sample size or quotation is invented. The harm limit stops the bout for dizziness, panic, or a trauma response, and it forbids combining the gaze with alcohol or a dare. Not a fail: the whitelist says two minutes, so drop “about.”
