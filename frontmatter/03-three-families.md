# Three kinds of ideas

Every chapter wears one of four labels. Three of them describe origin. The fourth, mixed, means the hour borrows from more than one.

**Established.** A practice or game with a recognizable public life: a folk game, a well-known exercise, a research procedure described honestly. We say where it comes from and what it does not prove.

**Reimagined.** A familiar impulse rebuilt so two specific people can play it with rules, examples, and a way to stop. Most of the book lives here. A new frame is not a discovery of science.

**Original formulation.** The twelve chapters 097–108 were written for this project. Their twists are deliberate. We do not claim that no one else has ever invented a neighboring game.

**Mixed.** Used when a chapter openly combines a research finding or a practitioner tradition with a design of our own. The notes at the end tell you which sentence is which.

Research in this book is a lamp, not a leash. A study of strangers in a lab does not become a promise about your Tuesday. A good ritual does not need a citation to be worth doing. What it must not do is borrow the authority of a paper it does not have.

You will not find the famous list of thirty-six questions reprinted here. The research behind that popular script is described, and then the chapters go on to ask questions built for this book. You will also not be told that 4 minutes of eye contact produces love. Looking at someone you care about can be intimate, strange, and funny. That is enough of a reason to try it, and a more accurate one.
