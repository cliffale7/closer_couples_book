# How to use this book

Start with the time you actually have. The indexes are there so you can choose in a minute, not after a summit about which chapter would be optimal.

There are six time shelves:

- **5–10 minutes — Quick Connections.** A question, a forecast, a specific thank-you.
- **10–20 minutes — Little Discoveries.** One story, one game round, one small ritual.
- **20–45 minutes — Meaningful Sessions.** Enough time to go past the first answer.
- **45–90 minutes — Deep Dives.** Museums, maps, letters, a full game.
- **90 minutes–3 hours — Full Experiences.** An evening with a shape.
- **Multi-day — The Long Game.** Notes collected in secret, missions, a week of small devotion.

Time is not the same thing as effort. A 40-minute talk on the couch can be low energy. A 15-minute dance in the kitchen is not. Each chapter separates the two.

You can also choose by appetite: laugh, discover, be a little more seen, feel chosen, talk about desire, make something, go somewhere, think about a life. Emotional depth is descriptive, not a ranking of your courage. Light is a legitimate choice on a hard day. Intense is a choice, not a destination you are supposed to reach.

Skip freely. The parts are neighborhoods, not stages. Revisit anything. Answers change, and the second time is often the more interesting one.

If you want to be surprised, use Surprise Us only after you both set the limits: time, energy, depth, and whether touch is welcome tonight. The paper method is in Surprise Us. A choice that falls outside your limits is not a choice. Draw again.

Keep one copy that you write in, if you like writing in books. Keep a clean copy if you want to read without your own past answers leaning on you. The companion journal, Our Story, is for the overflow: repeats, years, tickets, and the long record.
