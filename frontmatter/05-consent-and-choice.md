# How to start, stop, and stay willing

Read this once. The chapters will remind you briefly. They will not reprint a lecture every time.

Either of you may pass on a prompt, trade it, shorten the session, or stop. Passing is a complete answer. You do not owe a reason, though you may want to say whether you are done for the night or done with one question only.

Agree on the ceiling before you begin: time, energy, and how personal you want the hour to be. If one of you wants light and the other wants deep, play the lighter version. Depth is not a prize.

For anything involving touch, privacy, sex, or a story that could affect someone who is not in the room, consent is specific and current. Choosing a card is not permission to be touched. Yesterday’s yes is not tonight’s yes. Either person can change a touch to words, or a sexual idea to a conversation about the idea, without the evening becoming a failure.

“Confession Without Consequence” and any cousin of it set a style of listening. They do not erase real consequences. If something disclosed involves harm, safety, or a decision that needs a sober conversation, leave the game and have that conversation. Curiosity is not a gag order.

Disagreement is an allowed ending. So is laughter. So is discovering that an exercise is not for you. Mark it, skip it, and pick another. The indexes exist so you never have to force the next page.
