# Why this book exists

Familiarity is a gift and a trick. The person across from you can be the one you know best and still be slightly out of date in your mind, edited down to the stories you both tell without thinking. This book is a way to interrupt that editing. It is a library of experiences for two adults who already like each other and want the liking to stay awake.

Closeness, here, is not a grade. It is the ordinary miracle of being more accurately known, and of knowing someone else without rushing to improve them. Some of that happens in a 10-minute question. Some of it happens when you cook a strange meal, walk with your eyes closed, or say the appreciative thing you usually swallow. None of these is homework. None of them is evidence that a relationship was failing until a book arrived.

You can be silly in these pages. You can be precise, romantic, philosophical, or candid about desire. The tone assumes you are competent. You do not need to be fixed, diagnosed, or walked through your own life as if you have never had a feeling before. You also do not need to perform vulnerability on command. An invitation is not a debt.

The activities come from three places, and the book says which is which. Some are established practices or folk games. Some are those practices rebuilt for a couple who will actually do them on a weeknight. Twelve, numbered 097 through 108, were formulated for this project. That is a description of origin, not a claim that no one on earth has ever had a neighboring idea. Where research is useful, it is cited narrowly. Where a chapter is simply a good game, it is allowed to be a good game.

Use the book out of order. Use it again in five years, when the answers have moved. The point of writing in it is not to prove progress. The point is that a shared life is mostly unrecorded, and a few pages, kept, become a memoir you could not have planned in advance.
