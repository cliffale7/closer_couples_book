# Using this as a living memoir

The book is a guide and a place to keep what happened. Each chapter ends with a first-session record and two private pages of equal size, one for each of you, plus a shared page. The private pages are yours. You may read them aloud, copy a sentence onto the shared page, or never show them. The book will not assume.

Write the date. Write where you were if the place will matter later. Note which version you played: the short one, the ordinary one, the long one. Mood is optional. Completion can mean “we did it,” “we stopped halfway and that was right,” or “we postponed.”

On the shared page, prefer a few sentences of what actually happened over a stack of scores. Keep a line worth remembering: a phrase, a correction, a joke that will be obscure in a year and precious because of that. Leave room for a ticket, a sketch, or a caption. If nothing needs to happen next, “nothing” is a valid plan.

Repeats are part of the design. The same chapter in a different year is a new document. Use the revisit sheet when a chapter matters enough to compare. There is no maximum number of returns. Our Story, the companion journal, holds the extras so you are not forced to buy another copy of the whole book just to write the fourth attempt.

Once a year, if you want, read backward. Not to judge the relationship. To see what you were curious about, what you laughed at, and what you could not yet say. Call that evening The Chapters of Us. It is an invitation, not a holiday you can fail.

Here is a fictional example, from no one you know. Rowan and Vale, after a short appreciation game: “Vale wrote that Rowan always warms the mugs. Rowan had filed this under ordinary and was surprised to see it treated as a story. We kept the sentence. We did not keep a score.”

Your pages stay blank until you write them. Nothing in the printed book is pretending to be your life.
