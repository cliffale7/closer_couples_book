# Expanded design briefs for Part 13 — The Inventor's Workshop (097–108)

These are the richer originally proposed versions of the 12 candidate experiences, preserving their special mechanisms and twists. Keep stable titles/IDs and elaborate every concept into a complete, engaging chapter; do not reduce them to generic discussion prompts. Provenance: concepts formulated during development of this project; this is NOT a claim of worldwide novelty or research validation.

## 097. The Emotional Archaeologists
25–50 minutes, deep discovery. One partner chooses a strong reaction—joy, jealousy, embarrassment, pride, sadness—and the other acts as an emotional archaeologist, gently investigating it layer by layer without imposing explanations. Explore what lies beneath an initial feeling and what might have been overlooked. **Signature twist:** As the explorer reflects, the original assumed cause of the emotion may change. Explain respectful questions, consent to stop, non-diagnostic language, and how to close appreciatively.

## 098. The One-Hundred-Year Interview
30–60 minutes, shared imagination. Pretend both partners are 100-year-old versions of themselves. Interview one another about the adventures, mistakes, regrets, delights and wild stories of the decades you supposedly lived together, accepting contradictory versions as part of the fun. **Signature twist:** Each person identifies three invented memories they'd genuinely like to make real someday. Provide interviewer questions, theatrical setup and a practical optional future-memory page.

## 099. The Invisible Postcards
Multi-day ritual, thoughtful affection. Each person secretly writes small postcards about ordinary moments in which the other unknowingly delighted, helped, impressed, or attracted them. Exchange them at the end of the observation period. **Signature twist:** The most poignant notes can concern moments so tiny the recipient can't remember them. Provide print-ready postcard templates and a keepsake archive method.

## 100. The Emotional Weather Report
5–15 minutes, repeatable ritual. Replace "How was your day?" with an imaginative inner weather forecast: storms, cloud breaks, unexpected sun, emotional fog, a front of existential bullshit, etc. The listener can ask about the forecast but doesn't have to solve it. **Signature twist:** The weatherperson optionally forecasts what could help tomorrow, without making the partner responsible for changing the weather. Offer playful and serious examples and log sheets.

## 101. The Borrowed Brain Experiment
30–45 minutes, perspective-taking game. Present a hypothetical situation. Answer AS you believe your partner would, then let the partner explain their actual choice and reasoning. **Signature twist:** Reward understanding the partner's logic more than matching a yes/no answer. Supply substantial realistic, funny and morally gray scenario banks, turn structure and optional scoring without turning differences into failures.

## 102. The Museum of Unsaid Things
45–90 minutes, deep vulnerability. Create imaginary museum exhibits about the appreciation, fears, needs, funny regrets, or emotionally important words never voiced. Suggested exhibits include "The Compliment I Never Gave," "The Fear I Disguised as a Joke," or "The Moment I Needed a Hug but Didn't Ask." **Signature twist:** Each exhibit has two labels—"What happened" and "What I wish you'd understood." Provide actual museum label templates and sensitive alternatives that avoid ambushes.

## 103. The Relationship Escape Room
60–120 minutes, cooperative puzzle game without an actual escape room. Build challenges involving personal memories, knowledge about one another, storytelling, logic and complementary skills. **Signature twist:** Neither person has the complete information; the clues and puzzles require joining each other's knowledge. Provide a fully playable starter mystery, exact setup rules, solutions, resetting instructions, and optional customization. This is a cooperative puzzling experience and is entirely separate from the unrelated scavenger hunt project.

## 104. The Two-Person Civilization
45–90 minutes, values and creativity. Imagine founding an entire society together. Choose laws, traditions, holidays, moral principles, freedoms, consequences, and values; try to settle on ten founding principles while protecting both perspectives. **Signature twist:** Draw random event cards that force the couple to revise or defend the civilization's assumptions. Provide original event deck, scoring/consensus mechanics if helpful, and an illustrated constitution worksheet.

## 105. The Compliment Decoder
15–25 minutes, specific appreciation. Each person writes five observations/compliments about the other beyond looks and stock adjectives. The recipient guesses the actual lived experience that prompted each. **Signature twist:** Reveal the real memory or behavior behind the compliment, which may be entirely different from what the recipient imagined. Provide samples and a printable decoder sheet.

## 106. The Intimacy Negotiators
30–60 minutes, candid adult self-knowledge. Privately allocate a hypothetical total of 100 points among forms of intimacy: affection, romance, playfulness, meaningful conversation, sensuality, adventure, closeness, quiet, spontaneity, privacy, etc. Compare charts without treating the allocations as entitlement or demands. **Signature twist:** Design an evening that respects both people's genuine wishes and limits. Provide actual 100-point worksheets, optional category changes and examples.

## 107. The Memory Smuggler
20–40 minutes, memory storytelling. One participant tells a true story while slipping in a single fictional detail; the other investigates what doesn't belong. The twist must be meaningful to the storyteller's inner world. **Signature twist:** The fabricated element symbolizes a wished-for turn, a feared outcome, or an unrealized possibility. After revealing it, tell the true story and why the fiction appealed. Supply examples and fair-play rules.

## 108. The Secret Door
30–75 minutes, progressive voluntary disclosure. Each person imagines rooms/doors behind which sit a cherished memory, fear, unrealized ambition, private desire, paradox, or something hard to explain. The partner can ask questions, but the explorer chooses which keys to offer and how deeply to go. **Signature twist:** A door may be offered that the other person didn't know existed, but no one can demand access. Provide optional physical door cards, rules, keys, pass controls and debrief.
