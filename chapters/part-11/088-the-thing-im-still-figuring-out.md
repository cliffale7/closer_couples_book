## The hook

Some questions are still in the middle of you. Whether the work you are good at is the work you want. How much solitude you need before closeness feels like a choice. What you think about faith, or children, or the kind of body you are trying to live in. You can love your life and still have a sentence you cannot finish.

The Thing I’m Still Figuring Out is that sentence, said out loud, and then left unfinished on purpose. The listener's success is company. Advice that ties the bow ends the chapter early. Uncertainty is allowed to be adult.

## Why it might matter

Couples get practiced at reporting decisions. They are less practiced at sitting next to a decision that has not arrived. The unfinished question then shows up as irritability, as a fake certainty, or as a quiz the other person did not know they were taking.

Company is a specific offer. Silence. A curious question. A story from the listener's own unknown. The simple act of being believed when you say you do not know. Perceived partner responsiveness, in the diary research nearby, was a partial mediator between disclosure and intimacy. That finding is a neighbor to this hour. It is not a test of leaving a question open, and this chapter claims no such test.

The mechanism is restraint. You tell the truth about the blank. The other person does not research you into a conclusion before you have finished the sentence. Two people can be fond of each other and still refuse to turn a personal unknown into a referendum on the relationship.

## Before you begin

Sit, or trade letters. If you dislike open loops, you may set a date to look at the question again. The date is a revisit. It is not a deadline for an answer.

The speaker chooses a question they can hold in this company tonight. Sexuality, gender, faith, leaving, staying, or having children can be offered. They can also be too charged for a casual night. Pick a question you can stand beside without recruiting your partner into a plan.

If the unknown includes safety or a crisis, step out of the exercise and respond as a partner. Outside help is the right move when you need it. This page is for questions that can wait for company, not for emergencies that need a professional.

Passing and pausing are ordinary. So is choosing a smaller unknown because the large one is real and not for tonight. The two questions do not have to be equally dramatic. A person who is mostly content can still be figuring out how they want their Tuesdays.

## The experience

Each person completes one sentence, in writing if that helps: "I am still figuring out ___." The blank is a question you cannot honestly answer tonight, about yourself, your life, intimacy, identity, or the relationship. If you can answer it, pick another blank. The chapter loses its nerve the moment the question is already a thesis.

The speaker may add three short pieces, any of which can be skipped.

What makes this alive right now.
What you have already tried, including thinking, avoiding, and asking other people.
What kind of company helps: silence, curiosity, a story from the listener's own unknown, or simply being believed.

Then stop. The listener's first response is not advice. One fair question is, "What makes this alive right now?" Ask it only if the speaker did not already answer it, or if they invite you to ask. Reassurance that cancels the question is a miss. "You'll definitely be fine" shuts the door you just watched them open. "I believe you that this is unfinished" leaves the door as it is.

If the speaker asked for a story from your own unknown, tell a short one and hand the room back. If they asked for silence, practice silence long enough that it counts, at least 1 minute, without filling it with a framework they did not choose. Clinical labels and spiritual systems stay off the table unless the speaker brought those words themselves.

Switch. The second question is not a reply to the first, and it is not a contest about whose unknown weighs more. If you notice the contest starting, name it and drop it.

After both turns, write the two questions in the journal as open items. Solving them is not the assignment. Review weeks later is optional. At the review, you ask what is clearer and what is still honestly unfinished. A question that closed on its own can be celebrated without pretending you closed it on schedule.

One boundary. The listener does not recruit the question into a plan for the household, the body, or the future. If a plan is wanted, the speaker will say so, and you can choose another day to make one. Tonight the question stays the speaker's.

## Three ways to play

**15 minutes.** Both sentences, spoken. The listener offers the kind of company that was requested, or silence if none was named. No advice. Write the two open items in a line each. A short sitting can be complete.

**35 minutes.** Write the sentence and the three optional pieces. Each person has about 8 minutes. The listener asks at most the one fair question, then gives the company that was requested. End by reading the two open items aloud so they stay questions.

**60 minutes.** Letters. Each person writes the sentence and whatever of the three pieces they want, a page at most, and you read them in the same room or before a call. The reply is company, still not a solution. The extra time is for the part where nobody rushes the blank into a moral.

## Going deeper

If the first question was about work, a later sitting can be about intimacy, or the reverse. Same sentence stem. Leave the second category out of a tired night. People reach for the most dramatic unknown when they are spent, and drama is a poor substitute for the question that is actually alive.

You can ask, on a later day, "Do you want me to remember this question, or do you want the freedom to change it without reporting to me?" Both answers are respectful. Remembering can become surveillance if the speaker wanted privacy after the telling.

People who need a next step can choose a private experiment that does not require the question to resolve: one conversation with a friend, one hour of trying the thing, one week of not trying it. The partner witnesses the experiment if invited. They do not grade it.

## Variations and replay

Long distance suits this chapter because the pace is slow. Send the sentence. Ask for the kind of company you want in the same message, so the other person does not improvise a rescue.

If metaphor feels slippery, use the plain stem and stop. If speech is tiring, trade the pages and sit together without a discussion afterward. No ritual object is required. The motif is an open ring, a line that does not close: `assets/chapters/088/motif.svg`.

051, The Unfinished Version of Me, is about qualities still being developed, a self in progress that you can describe. 056, The Questions Without Answers, is for questions that may have no human answer at all. 087, The Question Behind the Question, climbs down from an ordinary fact. This chapter is a personal unknown that still belongs to the speaker, and it is allowed to stay unknown.

Replay when the question changes shape, or when you promised yourselves a revisit and the date has arrived. Bring the old wording. Notice what moved, and refuse to fake a conclusion for the sake of the page.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale are an illustration, not a portrait of you.

Rowan writes, "I am still figuring out whether I want work that other people can point to." What makes it alive is a colleague's promotion, which Rowan is glad about and also unsettled by. What they have tried: a class, a sketchbook, a month of telling themselves that usefulness should be enough. The company they want is curiosity, and a ban on career plans.

The promotion is already on the page, so Vale does not ask for it again. Vale says, "I believe you that the question is open. I am not going to turn it into a five-year sketch." They sit with it. Vale does not mention salaries.

Vale's sentence is quieter. "I am still figuring out how many evenings I want to spend being useful to other people, including you, before I disappear inside the usefulness." The company they want is being believed, plus one story from Rowan's own unknown if Rowan has one that is actually unknown.

Rowan says, "I believe you. Here is mine, still open: I don't know how much of my drawing is a hobby I love and how much is a dare I have not taken. I am not offering that as a lesson." Vale says that is the right size. They write both sentences in the journal as open items. Nobody assigns a task.

## Afterward

Did the question stay a question?
What kind of company actually helped, and what kind arrived uninvited?
Did either of you compete, reassure the unknown away, or make a plan nobody requested?
What is still unfinished, on purpose?

You can love someone through a blank. The blank does not have to be your project.

## Our Story

Date the session. Note the version, the place, and whether you finished or paused. Initials and mood are optional. An open question can be written as a title only. Detail that stayed spoken can stay spoken.

The two personal pages are yours. Share them, keep them, or leave them blank. Unread is a complete way to use them. The shared notes are the part you write together.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- An unresolved question about yourself, your life, intimacy, or identity that you are in the middle of.
- What kind of company helped, if any: silence, curiosity, a story, or being believed.
partner_b:
- Yours. The page does not require a solution.
- What you offered instead of advice, and whether that matched the request.
shared:
- How you keep each other company while a question stays unfinished.
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- What is clearer, and what is still honestly unfinished?
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```

## Notes

Reimagined from the ordinary human habit of unfinished questions, the kind that show up in friendship and in late conversation. No tradition was used as a manual. No empirical claim is made for the practice of leaving a question open. See L1998 only as a neighbor: in diary studies, intimacy tracked self-disclosure and partner disclosure, with perceived partner responsiveness as a partial mediator, and in Study 2 emotional disclosure was a stronger predictor than factual disclosure. That is not a test of this chapter. Uncertainty here is not immaturity, and the listener's job is company. If the unknown is a crisis or a safety matter, leave the exercise and get the right help.
