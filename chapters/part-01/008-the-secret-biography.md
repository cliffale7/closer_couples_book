## The hook

Some qualities show up more clearly on a page, before a beloved face can talk you into a smoother version. You will write a short biography of yourself. It has to contain contradictions, motivations, vulnerabilities, and less visible qualities. Then you will decide which sentences another person gets to hear.

"Secret," in this chapter, means less visible. It does not mean the most dangerous fact you know, and it does not mean a vault. A spoken self-summary, a highlight reel, and a biography of your partner all miss the premise. The page is yours. The listening comes after, and only for the lines you choose to release.

## Why it might matter

Third-person self-portraits are a writing-class exercise. Therapeutic journals sometimes ask for the same material in the first person. Neither tradition tested this hour, and this chapter does not borrow their authority. What the design does is practical. You write before you speak, at a length someone can actually hear, and you include four ingredients that a public résumé leaves out.

This book asks you to put motive and feeling on the page, along with a contradiction and a quality friends underuse. That is writing advice for this biography. It is not a research result, and it is not a ranking of one kind of page against another. A list of favorite things will not meet the four ingredients. A clinical self-diagnosis will not meet them either. The page is a portrait by the person who lives in it.

Write in the third person if you can. "They are both the person who arrives early and the person who dreads the room" is often easier than "I am both," because the pronoun lets two truths sit in one sentence without a punch line rushing in to save you. First person is allowed if third person turns into a joke you cannot exit. Comedy is welcome. A sketch that never risks a real line has not started the biography.

The listening rule you will use, two questions and no edits, is this book's design, so the portrait can be received without being rewritten. It is not a result borrowed from a diary study. The notes state what that research actually reported. They also state that the study did not test writing a biography.

## Before you begin

You need paper or a private note, something to write with, and a timer. About one page, roughly 250 to 400 words, so it will actually be read. If writing hurts, take extra time. There is no required posture. Dictation counts as writing when you can review it before anyone else hears it. If reading a page is hard, dictate and then listen back alone, and cut what you do not want shared. If hearing a page read aloud is hard, exchange the pages in silence.

Agree on the order before you draft, then be quiet. On a call or across cities, exchange the pages only after both drafts are finished, so nobody writes toward a face that is already reacting. If the room is overloaded, use the four-sentence version and stop.

The author decides which sentences are read. Writing a line does not obligate sharing it. Vulnerabilities that involve ongoing harm, or another person's safety, are outside this game. Confession Without Consequence, later in the book, names a receptive style of listening. It is not immunity for harm, and a biography marked secret is not immunity either. The partner does not psychoanalyze the page, does not edit it, and does not argue with the self-portrait.

## The experience

Both of you draft a short biography at the same time, in silence, contradictions included. About 20 minutes is the limit on the draft. Include four ingredients, in whatever order serves the prose.

Two traits that pull against each other. A motive that does not match the public story. One vulnerability, stated without a rescue clause. One quality friends underuse. A rescue clause is the sentence that arrives to make the vulnerability charming, impressive, or already solved. You may write that sentence on a scrap if it insists. It does not go in the portrait.

When the timer ends, you may cut. A page so long that it has to be summarized has outgrown the hour. Each author either reads aloud or hands the page over. Skip any sentence you want to keep unread, and do not apologize for the skip. Then the partner asks two questions of clarification. A clarification asks what a word points to. "When you say early, do you mean the appointment or the feeling?" is a clarification. "Here is what I think this means about your childhood" is an edit wearing a question mark. The author may answer, correct the question, or pass.

Swap. The second author gets the same silence, the same right to withhold a sentence, and the same two questions. You are done when each of you has written a portrait that contains the four ingredients, has shared only what you chose, and has heard two clarifications or a pass. If the draft turned into a biography of your partner, set it aside and write four sentences about yourself before the time is gone. Their character is not the assignment.

## Three ways to play

The short version takes about 15 minutes. Four sentences, one per ingredient, written in the room and read at the altitude you choose. The partner asks one clarification, not two. Withholding one of the four sentences is allowed. Name the ingredient you are keeping private, or do not name it. Both choices are complete.

The standard version takes about 40 minutes. 20 minutes of simultaneous drafting, then each page read or handed over, with two clarifications and no edits. The 20 minutes overlap. You are not waiting through 40 minutes of writing before anyone speaks.

The extended version takes about 55 minutes. After both portraits have been heard, an author may add a second paragraph on where the contradiction shows up in the relationship. Only with that author's consent, asked in a sentence and answered with a yes that can be no. The partner still does not edit. If the second paragraph would require a disclosure about harm or about someone else's safety, it does not belong in this game. Stop at the first page.

## Going deeper

A week later, reread your own page alone and underline only the sentence that still feels true. Bring that sentence back, if you want, and let your partner ask one new clarification. The rest of the page can stay in a private file. Underlining is a way to see which line had stamina.

You may invite a margin note. The partner writes one question, not a correction, in the margin or in a separate note. The author may decline to answer it. An unanswered margin is a finished exchange.

If third person keeps turning into a comedy sketch, switch to first person for the vulnerability sentence only, then return to third person for the rest. The shift in pronoun can mark the line you mean. It can also be a place you decide not to read aloud. Either use is legitimate.

## Variations and replay

Write by hand if the hand slows you into honesty. Type if the hand cramps or races. Review a dictation with the playback private. Long-distance, finish both drafts before any file is sent, and decide in advance whether the file is destroyed after the call. A biography that remains in someone else's inbox has a longer life than the hour. Say what you want done with the file.

Replay every season, or after a change in work, health, or the household. A new contradiction is allowed to replace an old one. Read the archived sentence you were willing to have remembered, and notice whether you still consent to it. Consent can expire. Cross out a stored line in the shared book when it no longer has your permission, and leave the cross-out visible if you want the record of a change.

If you want a lighter door into the same four ingredients, borrow the manners of a book jacket. Two sentences of flap copy, then the vulnerability without the jacket's usual boast. The ingredients remain. The costume is optional.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. This portrait is an illustration of the four ingredients, not a prompt you must match.

Vale writes in the third person, about 300 words, and then reads all but one sentence. The heard portion includes this passage. "Vale is the person who arrives early and the person who dreads the room once they are inside it. The public story is that Vale likes hosting. The motive underneath is a wish to have a hand on the moment people leave, because an ending Vale did not choose still lands hard. Vale is afraid of being ordinary company. Friends underuse Vale's accuracy about timing. They praise the food."

Vale says, "There is a sentence about a family member I am not reading." Rowan does not ask what it was. Rowan asks two clarifications. "When you say the moment people leave, do you mean the clock or the feeling in the doorway?" and "Which part of the hosting story do you want left as the public story?" Vale answers the first: the feeling in the doorway. Vale says the public story can stay public at parties. Rowan does not offer a better motive, does not suggest a diagnosis, and does not add a paragraph about Rowan's own arrivals. Those can be Rowan's biography, which comes next, with the same rules.

Rowan's page names a contradiction between generosity and a private ledger, a motive about being unmistakable, a vulnerability Rowan reads in one bare sentence, and a quality friends treat as fussiness that Rowan experiences as care. Vale asks what "unmistakable" points to, and whether the ledger is money or attention. Rowan answers. They put the full pages in separate private files. The shared note, later, holds one contradiction each.

## Afterward

Say, if you want to, which line you are willing to have remembered. Say which clarification helped, and which one you would rather not repeat. If a portrait left either of you raw, the next step can be a glass of water and a change of subject. Insight is optional. Accuracy to the page you actually released is enough.

The full biography can stay private. The shared book can hold the contradiction you consent to archive, and nothing else. If you wrote a line and then kept it back, you do not owe it to the journal, even as a hint. "Kept private" is a complete marker for anyone who wants a marker at all.

A week from now you may still like the sentence. You may not. The portrait was true enough for the hour in which you wrote it. It is allowed to age.

## Our Story

Record the date, time, place, version, mood if you want one, intention, and initials if you want them. Mark done or paused. Private pages are optional to share. The full biography belongs in a private file unless the author moves a sentence into the shared page. Repeat the portrait when you want a new draft. Old lines can be crossed out when consent to store them ends.

```journal
partner_b:
- The two clarifications I asked, and what they opened
- A less visible quality I understood, in language you would accept
shared:
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```
