## The hook

The next instruction has to arrive before your shin meets the chair. That is the trust this chapter practices: clear direction in a room you have already made safe. Someone guides. Someone cannot see the route, by closed eyes or by a mask they put on themselves. The space is ordinary and checked. A blindfold is an offer. Closed eyes are enough. Skipping both, and using the table version, is a full session. Risk, pranks, and obstacle courses contradict the premise. Trust here means the next instruction is reliable enough to use. This page refuses any version that asks someone to accept being endangered.

## Why it might matter

In daily life, guidance is often a vague hand-wave. "Over there" works when both people can see the there. It fails the moment one of them cannot. This walk makes the quality of a sentence obvious. Early enough, specific enough, and spoken at a human pace, an instruction can be used. Late, foggy, or playful at the wrong moment, it cannot.

Blindfold guides are a camp staple and a team-course staple. This couple version keeps that folk shape and adds a safety sequence, a no-prank rule, and a debrief about instruction. It is not a measure of loyalty, and it is not a measured builder of relationship trust. No ropes-course study was opened for this chapter. Refusing the mask, stopping mid-route, or choosing the table is information about fit and about history. It is not a defect in the relationship.

If you want a map of what helps you feel emotionally safe in words and situations, that is 062, The Safety Map. If you want to notice small everyday deposits of trust over time, that is 064, The Trust Bank. Those are different jobs. This one is a hallway, a voice, and a person who can uncover their eyes whenever they like.

## Before you begin

Do this sober. Alcohol is out. Do the safety sequence before any eyes close and before any mask appears. People with a history of being restrained or startled get the table version, or a pass, offered at this point. A pass ends the plan with nothing owed.

Walk the route together with eyes open. Stay on one level. No stairs on the first pass. Pick up bags, cords, shoes, pet bowls, and anything else that can catch a foot. The route is a short loop inside rooms you both know: hallway, rug, the space beside a chair. Kitchens with knives and heat are out. Pools are out. Public streets and anywhere near traffic are out. Darkness the guide also cannot see is out. If the guide's vision is not good enough to keep the walker safe, do not run the walking version.

Agree three words, and agree that the walker owns them:

- Stop means plant your feet and wait.
- Slow means half the pace.
- Freeze means nobody moves, including the guide, until the walker speaks again.

The guide repeats the word back so you both know it landed. Funny belongs in this planning talk. It does not belong as a sudden stimulus once eyes are closed. No spinning, no fake step, no surprise noise, no "trust fall," no changed route.

Touch is optional. Voice-only guiding has to be available, because the premise does not require touch. If you use touch, the guide warns before any contact and before letting go. A light contact at the elbow is plenty. The guide does not hold the walker's hand down if the walker reaches for the mask or opens their eyes.

A partner who is blind is not a prop for this game. Skip the blindfold. Practice describing a route you both need in ordinary life, or do the table version with the sighted partner as walker.

## The experience

1. Both of you walk the open-eyed route and name the hazards you removed. If you cannot make the route dull and obvious, pick a shorter one or move to the table.
2. Agree who guides, but only if the other person wants to walk. Switching later is a second choice, not a duty.
3. The walker closes their eyes, or puts on a mask they control, only when they say they are ready. They may uncover at any time. The guide does not tie, tighten, or surprise.
4. The guide gives concrete directions in small pieces. "Two steps forward. Rug under your feet. Stop. A chair is on your left, about a forearm away. I am still on your right. I am not touching you." "Over there" is a failed instruction. Say it again in objects and counts.
5. The guide stays where the walker can hear, warns before touch, and warns before any letting go: "I'm going to take my hand off your elbow now." Then the voice continues.
6. End at the agreed spot, or the moment the walker uncovers, or the moment either of you says stop and means the session. Uncovering completes the walk. Thank-yous can be one sentence. Skip speeches about bravery.
7. Debrief in three beats. Which instruction arrived early enough to use? Which arrived late? What did the walker need and not ask for? Keep the talk on instruction. Loyalty is not the subject.

If you both want a second role, the walker becomes the guide only by a fresh yes, and you run the safety sequence again for the new route, even if it is the same hallway. If you swap, use another partner page in the journal so each seat keeps a full set of lines.

## Three ways to play

**Short.** One room, about 1 minute of walking, after the open-eyed check. The whole sitting is about 15 minutes because the check is the chapter, not a preamble you skip. For claustrophobia, sensory overload, or a first trial, eyes closed for 10 seconds counts as a full first session. Say so out loud, so nobody stretches it to prove a point.

**Standard.** A short loop, under 5 minutes of walking, plus the debrief. Plan about 30 minutes. Most of that time is clearing, agreeing words, and talking about which sentence worked. The walking is the small middle.

**Extended.** A second loop, only if the walker wants it, done voice-only or touch-only so you can compare clarity. Plan about 45 minutes. Change one variable, not the safety rules. If the first loop was enough, the extended offer can stay unopened.

## Going deeper

Depth is specificity, not danger. Replay a loop in which the guide may use only distances, objects, and the three words, with no encouragement and no jokes. Then, if you want a comparison, replay with the same route and allow brief encouragement, still with no surprises. Ask which version the walker could actually use.

You can also debrief a moment the walker edited themselves: a doubt they swallowed, a "slow" they did not say. The repair is to practice saying it on a third pass of a few steps, eyes open if they prefer. The chapter gets more useful when the need is spoken sooner. It does not get more useful when the route gets riskier.

Chapter 067, The Comfort Zone Exchange, introduces a mutually acceptable unfamiliar activity. A trust walk that adds traffic, stairs, or an audience has left "mutually acceptable" behind. Stay indoors, on one level, in a room the guide can see.

## Variations and replay

**Table version.** For vestibular disorders, pain, mobility limits, or any preference, sit at a table. The guide sets out three ordinary objects and directs the walker's hands to them, eyes closed, with the same stop word. "Forward to the mug. Stop. Left to the spoon." The walker may open their eyes at any time. This is a complete chapter, not a lesser one.

**Hearing.** Agree tactile signals in advance, while eyes are open: one tap means stop, two taps mean slow, a hand lifted away means freeze. Practice the signals once with eyes open before any covering. If those signals are not reliable, do not walk.

**Remote.** A phone guide through the walker's own room is a remote adaptation, and it changes who holds the risk. The walker still needs a clear floor they have checked themselves. The guide gives directions the walker can refuse. If the guide cannot see the room, they should not invent obstacles or "surprises." Prefer the table version on camera, with three objects the walker has already named.

**Replay.** Another day, same hallway, better sentences. Record one instruction that worked and one that arrived too late. Decide then whether anyone wants the other role on a future date. Wanting it later is a complete answer. Being done after one walk is also complete.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not the two of you.

They walk the hall with eyes open, move a laundry basket, and agree that the loop is hallway, rug, stop beside the bookshelf, then back. Stop, slow, and freeze are the words. Touch is off. Vale will walk. Rowan will guide. Vale says, "Ready," and closes their eyes. They do not use a mask.

Rowan says, "Two steps forward. You should feel the rug. Stop." Vale stops with a little rug still ahead, so Rowan adds, "One shorter step. Rug now. Good. Turn a quarter-turn toward my voice. The bookshelf is in front of you, an arm's length away. Freeze there." Vale freezes. Rowan had almost said "you're fine" and skipped the distance. In the debrief Vale says, "The two steps were early enough. The quarter-turn was late. I had already started to drift left, and I needed 'slightly right' before 'turn.'" Rowan says, "I saw the drift and waited, which was the late part. I also wanted to grab your elbow and didn't, because we had said voice only." They skip a role switch. Vale does not want to guide tonight. That decision stays a decision about tonight.

## Afterward

Sit down where both of you can see. If the walker feels wobbly, stay seated until balance is ordinary. A near-miss, a bumped shin, a late warning, gets a plain account and, if you want, one cleaner sentence practiced in place with eyes open. Do not turn a bump into a character study.

You may feel fond, irritated, or clear that this game is not yours. Irritation at a vague instruction is the debrief working. A refusal next time is an allowed ending. Nothing in the walk is consent to be guided anywhere else, in bed or in a conflict or on a street.

## Our Story

Date the sitting, who guided, and whether you walked, used the table, or passed. If you swap roles, use a second partner page so each seat keeps a full set of lines. Partner A and Partner B are seats for this once. Private pages are optional to share. Keep the note on instruction. Skip the verdict on trust.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- From the guiding seat: the clearest direction you gave, and a moment you protected a pace, a corner, or a shoulder.
- If you did not guide, or you stopped the route, what did you need instead?
partner_b:
- From the walking seat, eyes closed or covered: a direction that landed, a moment of doubt, and what the guide did that helped.
- If you kept your eyes open, used the table, or passed, what did that choice protect?
shared:
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```

When you come back, you can ask what you trust more quickly now, and what still needs a spoken warning. "Trust" in that question means the practical kind: warnings that arrive on time.
