## The hook

Most hugs in a shared life are already employed. They carry hello, goodbye, coats, and the next sentence about groceries. This chapter asks for one embrace with that job set down. It should be unhurried, comfortable for both of you, and noticed against whatever your routine hug is, if you have one. Sixty seconds is the length this book proposes for the standard version. That length is a design choice. It is not a researched dose. If you spend the minute talking about errands, you miss the noticing. If you stay inside a squeeze you want to leave, you miss the comfort. Either of you can end it early, and ending early still counts.

## Why it might matter

A routine hug is often a hinge. The bodies meet, the message is "I'm going," and the hug is over before either person has felt its weight. A deliberate embrace removes the errand and leaves the contact long enough to notice. What you notice might be warmth, breath, awkwardness, boredom, or a clear wish to step back. Awkwardness is a finding. Ordinary is a finding. Neither one means the minute failed.

Wanted affectionate touch has a review behind the general idea. Jakubiak and Feeney argue that wanted affectionate touch can relate to relational, psychological, and physical well-being, including stress-related pathways. See J2017. The paper is a theoretical model and a review. It is not a test of a timed embrace. It does not establish a 20-second hug, a hormone change, or an oxytocin effect. This chapter does not claim those, and it does not claim a cortisol result. Unwanted touch sits outside the review. The difference between a deliberate embrace and a routine one is this chapter's question. That difference has not been measured here. You can still notice it in your own kitchen.

Doorway hugs and workshop long hugs are common. The comparison this chapter asks for is still a design.

## Before you begin

Choose a private spot where you can stand or sit without an audience. The alarm should be perceptible to both of you. A soft sound works. A silent visual timer works if you can both see it. Check comfort before you start the clock, and remember that comfort can be revoked while the clock is running.

Agree the form by asking, not by assumption. Full, side-by-side, and a seated lean all count, if both of you call the form an embrace. Chest-to-chest is not required. Height, pregnancy, pain, sensory overload, and limited arm mobility can make side-by-side or seated contact the honest version. Agree who begins the contact. Agree the release phrase out loud: "I'm going to step back." The phrase is required so nobody has to yank away to be believed.

Either person may end at 10 seconds. Say the release phrase and separate. That ending completes the session.

This chapter stays nonsexual. It is a poor tool for closing an unresolved argument. Closeness used to smother a fight is a different move, and this page does not offer it. Heat and illness are reasons to skip. If one of you cannot hug, use a sustained hand-hold and call it a substitute. The substitute is not an embrace the review tested. J2017 did not test a hand-hold either. It tested nothing in this chapter.

You cannot share one embrace over a video call. Postpone until you are in the same place. Do not replace the embrace with a self-hug and cite the review.

Every second of contact can be declined.

## The experience

1. If you have a habitual hug, do that one first, about as long as you usually would, and step apart. Let it be itself, groceries and all. If you have no habitual hug, skip this baseline and say so. You will compare the deliberate embrace with "no routine hug" rather than with a fiction.
2. Ask whether the agreed form is welcome for up to 60 seconds, with the right to leave sooner. Both answers have to be yes. If either answer is no, choose another form or use the hand-hold substitute or stop. Stopping here completes the chapter.
3. Start the timer. Come into the embrace and stay. No errand talk. No rocking or squeezing harder as the seconds pass. No pinning arms. No contest about who can last.
4. Use the release phrase whenever you want out, including at 10 seconds. The other person lets go at once. Early is a full result.
5. When the alarm sounds, use the release phrase anyway, so the ending has words as well as a chime. Step apart.
6. Each of you names one difference from the routine hug, or from the absence of one. The allowed notes are ordinary: weight, breath, time, warmth, awkwardness, or "it felt like the usual one." One sentence each. You do not owe a theory.

If the minute felt like nothing much, say that. The design does not require a revelation.

## Three ways to play

**Short.** About 15 seconds under the same rules, for pain, overload, or a day when a minute would be too much. Still do the baseline if you have one, and still name one difference. The whole sitting is about 10 minutes. Fifteen seconds that ends at five is still a session.

**Standard.** The optional baseline, then 60 seconds, then one difference each. Plan about 15 minutes. Sixty seconds is the book's proposed duration for this version. It is not a dose you must survive.

**Extended.** A second minute, only if both of you ask, after you have stepped apart and taken a breath. Plan about 20 minutes. If only one person wants the second minute, you do not take it. The first ending stands.

## Going deeper

You can add one practical question after the difference: when, in an ordinary week, would you want the deliberate version, and when is the doorway hinge the one you actually want? Answer for yourself. A wish for more is not an order, and a wish for less is not a rejection of the person.

If the embrace brings up a conversation, let go first. Talk after you are separate, and only if you both still want the talk. Chapter 019, Breathe With Me, is shared presence without an embrace, and it should not be stacked under this one unless you both ask. ## Variations and replay

Side-by-side on a couch, a seated lean, and a hand-hold substitute can be the whole practice. Name which one you used so the journal does not pretend you did a form you skipped.

Replay on a morning and on a late evening if you want to know whether the same 60 seconds are the same animal. Keep the release phrase both times. Keep the right to stop at 10 seconds. A second attempt that feels plainer than the first is worth recording. Plain is allowed to be the news.

If the alarm startled you, switch to a visual timer next time. If standing hurt, sit before you begin, not after you have endured it. Skip the chapter when someone is ill, overheated, or already at the edge of too much contact. Another week is a valid schedule.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not the two of you.

Their routine hug is a one-armed squeeze by the door, about two seconds, already inside a sentence about groceries. They do that one and step apart. Vale's shoulder is sore, so Vale asks for a side-by-side lean on the couch. Rowan agrees. They say the release phrase once, as practice, before they touch. The timer is visual.

At about 40 seconds Rowan says, "I'm going to step back." They separate. Rowan says, "The doorway one is a hinge. This one had weight, and then I wanted my arm back." Vale says, "I noticed warmth, and I would have stayed the minute. The comfortable part was that your phrase ended it." They do not lengthen it to prove affection. They do not add a hormone story. They mark the form, the early ending, and the two differences. Awkward for one of them, warm for the other. Both reports stay.

## Afterward

Get a drink of water, or go back to the groceries, so the embrace has a clear ending. If you feel fond, one sentence is enough. If you feel irritated or crowded, that report belongs in the room while it is still true. Do not smooth it into gratitude you do not feel.

Desire can be named and left for a different consent. This chapter does not continue into sex. It also does not repair a fight. If you were mid-argument, you picked the wrong hour, and the remedy is to skip, not to hug your way to a truce.

## Our Story

Date the sitting, the form, and whether you finished the minute. Partner A and Partner B are seats for this once. Private pages are optional to share. Record a difference, not a grade of anyone's body. An early ending belongs in the notes.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- How did an unhurried embrace differ, for you, from a routine one?
- Did you end before the minute, and what did ending then tell you?
partner_b:
- What did you notice while you were in the hold, and did you want it shorter, longer, or exactly that long?
- If you used a hand-hold, a side-by-side lean, or another form you both called an embrace, what did that form make noticeable?
shared:
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```

When you come back, you can ask what a deliberate embrace feels like beside the last one you recorded.
