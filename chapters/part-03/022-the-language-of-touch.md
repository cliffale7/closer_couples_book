## The hook

You already have a dialect of touch, including the sentences "not that" and "not tonight." This chapter gives that dialect a short menu and a private vote. You mark each form, keep only the overlap you both called welcome tonight, and try those forms one at a time. The discovery is which of them feel like comfort or affection for the two of you. A preset sequence of strokes would swap that discovery for a script. Sex would swap it for a different evening. Every contact can be declined, including halfway through.

## Why it might matter

People who like each other still differ about shoulders, hair, hands, and the difference between a welcome palm and a palm that stays too long. Praise does not locate that difference. A try, with a one-word report and the right to stop, does.

Wanted affectionate touch has a research review behind it. Jakubiak and Feeney argue that wanted affectionate touch can relate to relational, psychological, and physical well-being, including stress-related pathways. See J2017. The paper is a theoretical model and a review. It is not a trial of this menu. Touch that is unwanted sits outside the claim. J2017 does not establish a timed dose, a hormone change, or an oxytocin effect, and this chapter does not claim any of those. There is no 20-second prescription here. A few seconds is enough when a few seconds is what the receiver wants. There is no researched duration to hit.

Massage traditions and sex-therapy sequences use touch under other rules. This chapter does not borrow those steps. The menu, the overlap, and the one-word check are a design.

## Before you begin

Use a private room and ordinary clothes. Oils are unnecessary. Each of you needs a card the other cannot read while you mark it. Copy this menu, and leave the last line blank:

- hand held
- hand on shoulder
- hand on upper back
- arms linked
- knees touching while seated
- a palm resting on the forearm
- hair touched
- face touched
- one form you add, still nonsexual

Mark every line in private: welcome tonight, not tonight, or leave out. Leave-out is part of the activity. Pain, injury, sensory aversion, old harm, and ordinary preference all belong in that column. Do not ask why. Do not treat "not tonight" as a project for later in the same sitting.

Then compare. Keep only the lines you both marked welcome tonight. If one of you used the blank line, read that addition aloud and let the other mark it with the same three choices. It enters the session only if it, too, is welcome tonight for both.

If the overlap is empty, skip contact. Describe, without demonstrating, which forms might be welcome on another day. That conversation is a complete session. Lobbying a "not tonight" into a trial breaks the chapter.

Ask who wants to offer the first item. Then swap, so each of you receives. Welcome is specific to the item and to this night. It expires when you stop. Nonsexual is the boundary of the chapter even if the two of you are sexual together. Sex can be chosen another time, outside this data. A marked card is not consent to anything off the card, and it is not consent that survives the evening.

Gloves, a sleeve left down, and very light pressure are valid forms if both of you mark them welcome. The seated items are a full menu for pain or limited mobility.

## The experience

1. Mark the cards out of each other's sight. Compare, and list the overlap where you can both see it. Put the other lines away.
2. Offer one overlap item. The receiver sets the pressure and the duration before contact, in plain words: "light, on the shoulder, a few seconds." The giver follows that, and does not deepen, wander, or add a second place.
3. The receiver may stop during the contact with the word "stop," a hand lifted, or by moving away. Stopping retires that item for the session. The giver's hands come off. No postmortem is required.
4. If the contact finishes, the receiver says one word: comfort, affection, neutral, or stop. Stop still retires the item. The giver accepts the word. A paragraph can wait for the journal, and it is optional there too.
5. Go to the next overlap item, or stop if either of you has learned enough. Four is the standard ceiling, not a quota.
6. Swap who offers and who receives. The same item can feel different in the other direction. The new receiver may pass an item that was welcome a moment ago. A pass is a decline, and the item waits.
7. End by repeating the words you heard: comfort, affection, neutral, retired. Do not turn a body into a score.

Hearing can use signs you agree before any contact: thumb up for comfort, a flat hand for neutral, thumb down for stop, and a nod you have both defined as affection. Vision can use the spoken check. Either way, the receiver's report outranks the giver's hope.

## Three ways to play

**Short.** One overlap item, both directions if you both want the swap, inside about 15 minutes. If there is no overlap, use the same 15 minutes to describe a future welcome without demonstrating it. One item is a full chapter on a sore day, an overloaded day, or a first try.

**Standard.** Up to four overlap items, with the swap. Plan about 35 minutes so the marking is unhurried. Stop before four the moment either of you wants to. The unused overlaps can stay on the list for another night. They do not become an assignment.

**Extended.** After the trials, return to the item that drew the warmest word you both recognize, and talk about when it would be welcome in an ordinary week: morning, the door, a hard day, not in a crowd. Plan about 50 minutes. The talk does not reopen retired items and does not convert "not tonight" into homework.

## Going deeper

Depth here is context. A hand that is comfort on the couch may be unwelcome in a kitchen argument or under a fluorescent light. You can each name one setting where a welcome item would still be welcome, and one where it would not. That is a map of conditions. It is not a promise to perform the item on schedule.

You can also tell the truth that a neutral word is useful. Neutral means the form is allowed and unremarkable. It does not have to be promoted until it becomes affection.

If you want one sustained embrace, compared with your routine hug, that is 023, The Sixty-Second Embrace. If you want touch as one station among music, scent, texture, taste, and light, that is 036, The Sensory Date, where welcome touch can be skipped and the other senses still need their own limits. Chapter 025, What Loving Me Actually Looks Like, may include a gesture of touch or may leave touch off the list entirely. Save those for other days unless you both ask to continue.

## Variations and replay

The no-touch path is available even when the overlap is not empty. Either of you can choose description over demonstration for the whole session. Say so before the cards are compared, or the moment you want to switch. Switching is a successful use of the chapter.

On a call, compare the marks and wait to try contact in person. Video can hold the description. It cannot hold the touch. Do not invent a screen substitute and call it the menu.

Replay in a month. Marks move. Hair that was leave-out can stay leave-out forever, and a shoulder that was welcome can become not tonight without an explanation. Record the words and the retirements. Skip any note that grades a body.

A quiet room, no added scent, and a single item are the right scale when sensation runs hot. If contact tips into alarm, pain, or a wish to get out, stop and retire the item. The evening can become tea. It does not have to become a discussion of why.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not the two of you.

Rowan's card: hand held, welcome tonight; palm on the forearm, welcome tonight; hair, not tonight; face, leave out. Vale's card: hand held, welcome tonight; palm on the forearm, welcome tonight; shoulder, welcome tonight; hair, welcome tonight; face, not tonight. Vale added nothing on the blank line. The overlap is two items. Hair and shoulder stay off the body. Nobody asks about the face.

Vale receives the hand-hold first and says, "Light, and only a few seconds." Afterward Vale says, "Comfort." Rowan receives the same item, asks for a slower hold, and says, "Affection." The forearm is next. Vale says, "Neutral." On Rowan's turn, the palm arrives higher than Rowan had agreed, and Rowan says, "Stop." The forearm item is retired. Vale takes the hand away and does not ask for the story. They read the words back: comfort, affection, neutral, retired. They do not try the hair. They do not turn the retired palm into a problem to solve before bed.

## Afterward

The welcomes expire when you stand up. Tomorrow's hand-hold needs its own yes. If desire showed up, you can name it in one sentence and leave it outside this data. This chapter stays nonsexual. If a decline felt clumsy, the repair is the hands coming off, not a speech that talks the other person back into the contact.

Nothing you tried is permission for a different kind of touch, for sex, or for a longer session than the one you marked. If you want any of those, they start with a new question on a different night.

## Our Story

Date the sitting and list only the overlap you actually tried, plus anything retired. Partner A and Partner B are seats for this once. Private pages are optional to share. A no needs no explanation, and a leave-out can stay off the shared page. Do not record a body as a score. Nothing on these pages is consent to further touch.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- Which welcome, nonsexual touch felt like comfort or affection, and which did you decline or redirect?
- What did you mark leave-out or not tonight, and do you want that mark to stay off the shared page?
partner_b:
- Which touch did you offer or receive that felt right, and what pace or place made it welcome?
- After your one-word check, which item would you want on an ordinary week, and which stays inside this session only?
shared:
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```

When you come back, you can ask what changed among welcome, not tonight, and leave-out.
