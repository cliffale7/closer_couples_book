## The hook

Desire has weather. Time of day, a closed door, a joke in the first 5 minutes, a hip that hurts, a sink of disputed dishes, a light that is too bright, a body that has not slept. You usually discuss it once the weather has already turned, which is a bad time for cartography.

Map the conditions that support romance and desire, what impedes them, and how to design for both partners. Both partners means two authors. A map with one handwriting is a preference list. When you are done, you will have designed one occasion that respects both maps. Call it a hypothesis. Designing it does not summon desire, and it does not book a sexual result.

## Why it might matter

People already talk about mood, timing, privacy, and what kills the mood. The blueprint gives that talk a shape before anyone is disappointed: two columns each, a joint map with three kinds of marks, and one next occasion. An impediment is a condition. Exhaustion, alcohol, and pain are design facts, not character reports.

If you were about to sort the columns into five love languages and crown a primary one, set the quiz down. A review found little support for one primary love language, for the idea that there are exactly five, and for matching as what predicts satisfaction. People do seem to benefit from a range of affectionate behavior. See I2024 in the notes. That review does not validate this map. It only keeps the map from shrinking into a type.

What Loving Me Actually Looks Like compares gestures that feel like love. The Yes, No, Maybe Game sorts ideas into yes, no, and maybe. The Desire Nobody Asked About lets one unasked wish stay a wish. The Sensory Date builds an evening from sensory materials. This chapter is the prior map of conditions and impediments. No study is claimed for the blueprint. You are designing a room, a time, and a way of starting. You are not diagnosing desire, and you are not treating it.

## Before you begin

Work separately first. Paper, index cards, large type, or a spoken list the other person writes only with permission. Two columns: conditions that support romance or desire for you, and conditions that shut them down.

Use what applies: time of day, privacy, humor, who initiates, rest, pain, alcohol, unfinished logistics, audience, lighting, noise, medication, caregiving, shift work, disability, delay on a call, whether a camera is on. Those are map entries, including the unglamorous ones.

You may leave any condition unnamed. Say "unnamed" aloud if you want the blank to stay blank. The other person does not pry.

A list that is only the other person's faults will wreck the hour. Rewrite an indictment as a condition before you bring it back. "I shut down when the dishes are still in dispute" can stay. "You never help" goes back to the pencil. "Two drinks make me far away" is a design fact, and so is fatigue.

Writing a condition is not a yes to sex on the night you design. Either of you may cancel. Canceling creates no debt and requires no replacement performance. Initiation has no gender. "I want to be asked," "I want to start," and "I want a window where either of us might move first, and no is a complete answer" are all usable. If welcome touch is one condition among others, keep it specific. Wanted affectionate touch has a research review behind it, and unwanted touch is outside that claim. See J2017. Your map may leave touch off entirely.

## The experience

The standard sitting is about 50 minutes. Phones start in another room, unless a shared note is the paper.

1. Alone, fill both columns: what supports romance or desire, and what shuts them down. The limit on the draft is 12 minutes. Be concrete enough that a stranger could set the room. "Late evening, after I have eaten, door closed, a joke before any speech." "Overhead light, pain I will not perform through, being asked to decide every step." Five lines a column are plenty. Two is enough. Skip the polish.
2. Read your own list. Skip any unnamed line. The listener copies the line onto the joint map and does not cross-examine it. "Did you say afternoon?" is a fair question. "Why can't you relax?" is a different genre. Drop it.
3. Mark the joint map three ways.
   - Shared: you both named something compatible, such as privacy or food first.
   - Mine only: it matters on one map, and the other person can honor it without pretending it is their appetite.
   - Yours only: the same dignity in the other direction.
   Leave clashes visible. Bright humor and quiet can both stay. Morning and late night can both stay. This chapter designs one occasion, not a constitution. The other window can be another day. Averaging two bodies into one sad compromise erases the map.
4. Write the occasion. Include at least one supporting condition for each person, and remove one impediment. Read it as a hypothesis. "Thursday after food, logistics list closed, lights low, either of us free to initiate or decline. We will know afterward whether that was true." Add the cancel line: "Either of us can call this off without owing a different night."
5. The occasion can be romantic, sensual, sexual, or a quiet hour. Sex is not the proof the map worked. Record what the conditions did, including nothing. "Nothing stirred" is a finding, not a verdict and not a cue for a corrective performance.

## Three ways to play

### Short, 25 minutes

Use 3 minutes on the rules, especially the cancel line and the ban on indictments. Take 8 minutes apart for two supports and two impediments each. Use 8 minutes to mark those lines shared, mine only, or yours only. Use 6 minutes for one small hypothesis. "Saturday morning, no agenda, the group chat in a drawer" can be the whole design. A short map is complete.

### Standard, 50 minutes

Use 5 minutes on rules and materials, 12 minutes on the private columns, 8 minutes to read them, 12 minutes on the three-mark map, and 13 minutes on the hypothesis, the cancel line, and one sentence about what counts as a finding. "If we are irritable, we stop and eat, and we do not call that a failed romance."

### Why there is no extended version

A longer sitting starts to look like a contract, then like a test of whether desire appears on cue. The map is done when one occasion respects both authors. Sensory materials belong to The Sensory Date, and still need a fresh yes. One unasked wish belongs to The Desire Nobody Asked About. Gestures that feel like love belong to What Loving Me Actually Looks Like. Extra time here makes the hypothesis heavier, not truer.

## Going deeper

"Who starts" is a design variable. One of you may want pursuit and an easy no. The other may want a standing invitation they can decline without a speech. Write both. Inside the window you named, whoever is moved can move first, and "not this time" needs no story. If neither of you moves, the hypothesis still ran.

Pain, medication, and disability belong in the ink: posture, duration, a seat instead of a bed, a nap first, a position that stays optional. Lighting and sound are variables too. Dim, steady, no music, the fan left on because silence rings. That is the version that fits.

Long-distance maps name latency, cameras, and what privacy means in two rooms. "Camera off, both doors closed" is a real plan. So is "camera on the face, and either of us can end the call without a replacement scene." A shared mark is not more virtuous than mine only. A condition only one person needs still belongs on the map.

## Variations and replay

Use three piles of cards: shared, mine only, yours only. On a call, name the pile. If combining clothed romance and sexual desire makes one of you go vague, draw those maps on different days. Say which map you are drawing. Both use the same marks and the same cancel line.

Replay when the season, the shift, the medication, or the last hypothesis changes. Old hypotheses expire. They do not become law. If you try the occasion, add one journal line for what the conditions did. Revise the card later. Leave each other's bodies as they are.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not the two of you.

Rowan's supports: late evening, a closed door, food already eaten, a joke rather than a speech. Impediments: hip pain, a bright overhead light, an unfinished money argument, alcohol past one glass because it makes Rowan sleepy and then guilty. Vale's supports: afternoon, little or no music, the dog walked, the morning not already waiting, permission both to start and to stop. Impediments: being rushed, a camera, the group chat as an audience, having to set the mood every time.

They mark privacy and a closed logistics loop as shared. Low light, food first, and hip pain as a reason to stay seated or lying down are Rowan's alone, and Vale does not pretend to share the hip in order to be a good partner. Afternoon, and initiation that can come from either of them, are Vale's alone. Rowan would still prefer late evening. They leave that clash on the map.

The hypothesis: Sunday after lunch, phones in a drawer, lights low, a joke first, the option to stay clothed and seated, either person may start or cancel. They do not write "and then we have sex." The cancel line is on the card. When they try a version of it, Rowan's pain is louder than expected. They stop. Vale does not ask for a replacement performance. Rowan does not apologize for the weather. In the journal they write that low light and the joke worked, the hour of day was Vale's condition and it was honored, and pain removed the rest. The map did what a hypothesis is for. It was testable, and it was allowed to be wrong without turning either of them into the problem.

## Afterward

Read the hypothesis once more. Hang it where you will both see it, or fold it into the journal. A private condition stays on that person's page. The shared card carries only what you both agreed to know.

Before the occasion, one sentence is enough. "Still good," or "Canceling, no replacement owed." During it, the ceilings you wrote still apply, and a new no still works. Afterward, record the finding in ordinary language. Desire showed up, or it did not. The joke helped. The hour was wrong. The dog was not walked and you pretended otherwise, which was informative. Then leave the blueprint alone until you mean to redraw it.

## Our Story

The columns can live on these pages, or on the cards you keep. Private pages are optional to share. The person who wrote one decides. Pass a prompt, change it, or leave it blank. A condition you left unnamed stays unnamed here too.

- What we discovered

- Then versus now

## Notes

The catalog premise is the mechanism: map conditions that support romance and desire, what impedes them, and how to design for both partners. Ordinary talk about mood and timing is the folk neighbor. This blueprint is not a sex-therapy protocol, a medical assessment of desire, or a five-language system. No empirical citation is claimed for the mapping itself.

See I2024. Impett, Park, and Muise's review finds little support for one primary love language, for exactly five languages, and for matching as what predicts satisfaction. People do seem to benefit from a range of affectionate behavior. The citation keeps this chapter from becoming that quiz. It does not validate the blueprint.

See J2017. The review of wanted affectionate touch applies only if touch is one condition among others. Unwanted touch is outside that claim. The review is not a trial of this map.
