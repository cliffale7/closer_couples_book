## The hook

Some wants get good at waiting. They are not the tea, the ordinary kiss, or the show you both like. They are the ones nobody asked about, so they never had to survive a pause. This chapter takes one of them out and leaves it on the table.

You discuss previously unexpressed wishes, fantasies, or forms of affection without obligations to act. The wish is allowed to remain a wish. One of you names something previously unasked. The other receives it. Curiosity is the activity, plus one sentence said out loud: hearing it is not a yes. If the evening ends by scheduling the wish, the chapter has been replaced by a negotiation.

## Why it might matter

One wish, previously unasked, and a sentence said out loud: hearing it is not a yes. The wants that already fit the life you share are easy to schedule. This hour is for one that has been waiting. A clean hearing is a different event from a plan.

Diary studies of ordinary interactions found that intimacy tracked self-disclosure and partner disclosure, with perceived partner responsiveness as a partial mediator, and that in Study 2 emotional disclosure predicted intimacy more strongly than factual disclosure. See L1998 in the notes. Those diaries did not study fantasy, did not assign this conversation, and do not show that naming a desire changes a relationship. This chapter will not line your appetites up or repair a dry spell. It lets an unspoken want be heard without becoming a work order.

The Yes, No, Maybe Game sorts ideas and compares lists. This chapter handles what had not been asked, including wishes that will never be enacted. The Intimacy Blueprint maps conditions. Here you disclose one unasked wish. Confession Without Consequence is receptive listening. It is not immunity for harm, and it does not license you to ignore the mark on a desire.

## Before you begin

Sit where you can write, or set a voice note, or agree to speak. Any of those completes a turn. Eye contact is unnecessary. Phones can stay in the other room unless one of them is the paper.

You each get three lanes, and you do not have to use them all.

1. An affectionate gesture nobody has requested. A greeting, a hand at the neck in public, an endearment, feet touching while you read.
2. A fantasy that may stay imaginary. Sexual, romantic, or mild.
3. A form of closeness you have edited out of ordinary talk. Pace, warmth, being desired while you are in pain or tired, a posture that does not hurt, silence or talk during sex, being left alone afterward.

Passing a lane is a complete turn. Say or write "pass." Sexual vocabulary is optional. Pain, fatigue, and limited mobility are full desires. If a wish needs someone who has not agreed, or if it depends on humiliation, mark it tell only and stop before any plan. This chapter will not supply a procedure for those acts. Long-distance works by voice or letter. Sensory overload means one wish and a planned pause. Either of you may pass the whole chapter.

Agree the listener's job before anyone shares. The listener restates what they heard, names something they appreciate about being told, and says, "Hearing this is not a yes." They do not solve, bargain, grade, or look to the speaker to take the wish back. The speaker then marks the item with one of three phrases: "tell only," "maybe another day," or "please do not ask me to do this."

## The experience

The move is one unasked wish, written before it is spoken. Give the standard version 40 minutes. The short and extended cuts are below.

1. Apart, even if you are at the same table, write the wish before you speak. Two or three sentences are enough. Name the lane. A few minutes is the limit on the writing, if you want one. A voice note counts. If you are passing, the card says pass, and you can stop there.
2. One lane at a time. The speaker reads the note or plays it. They leave the wish at the size they wrote. If they hear themselves shrinking it ("it's stupid, never mind"), they may pause and either finish the original sentence or mark the turn passed. Both are clean. Apologizing a wish down to nothing is a habit this chapter interrupts.
3. The listener does three things, in order, and then stops.
   - Restate what you heard in plain language. Pause for a correction. "I heard that you want to be kissed slowly and told something specific about your mouth. Did I miss a piece?"
   - Name one thing you appreciate about being told. Appreciate the telling. A review of the wish is a different activity. "I appreciate that you let me hear the shy part without a joke glued to it" is the job. "We should do that on Thursday" is not.
   - Say the sentence. "Hearing this is not a yes." Say it even if you would have said yes in some other conversation. Especially then. Your enthusiasm is not a signature.
4. The speaker marks the item out loud. "Tell only." "Maybe another day." "Please do not ask me to do this." One mark. The listener does not haggle the mark up or down.
5. Switch. The second wish does not have to use the same lane, the same heat, or the same amount of courage. A small affectionate want is a full turn beside a sexual fantasy.
6. Silence is not assent. If the listener goes quiet, the speaker asks, "Do you want a moment, or are you done responding?" The listener picks one. A moment gets a minute. Done means they return to whichever of the three jobs they skipped, or they say "pause" and the turn waits. Quiet does not mean yes, no, or wounded. Those require words.

Three predictable misses have a repair.

If the listener starts solving, they stop and return to the restate. A pillow, a date, a toy, or a technique waits for another conversation, and only if the mark is "maybe another day" and the speaker brings it back. This chapter does not book the wish.

If the listener bargains ("I'll do yours if you do mine"), the turn ends. A wish is not a debt. Sit quietly, then continue with another lane or stop for the night.

If the listener looks hurt and the speaker rushes to retract, either person says "pause." The wish stays as told. The listener may say, "This landed in a sore place, and I can still do my three jobs," and then do them. Comfort after the mark is welcome. It is not a retraction.

## Three ways to play

### Short, 20 minutes

One lane each. Use 3 minutes to agree the listener's three jobs, 5 minutes to write in parallel, and 5 minutes per person to tell, restate, appreciate, say the sentence, and mark. The last 2 minutes repeat the marks. Stop. A single lane is the whole activity.

### Standard, 40 minutes

Use 6 minutes on the rules and 8 minutes to write up to three lanes. Share one lane at a time, alternating, about 4 minutes a turn. Six turns plus a 2-minute close fill the 40 minutes. Fewer lanes end earlier, and that ending is correct. You may pass a lane. You still do not schedule anything.

### Extended, 70 minutes

Do the standard sitting. Then leave the table for 10 minutes. Tea, a window, laundry, anything that is not this conversation. After the pause, a "maybe another day" returns only if the person who owns it brings it back, and only inside the remaining 20 minutes. The listener uses the same three jobs. The second pass still does not set a date. If nobody brings a maybe back, say, "That was the telling," and end. Ending because a wish stayed a wish is a correct use of the longer sitting.

## Going deeper

A tell-only mark protects imagination. Some fantasies arouse because they stay stories: being watched while you undress, being wanted in a particular room, a kind of power you do not want imported into Tuesday. Your partner can find the story beautiful and still have no standing to request it later.

A maybe means the speaker may open a different conversation on another day. The listener does not circle back as a surprise. "Please do not ask me to do this" is a closed door. Curiosity about why is something the speaker may offer. It is not owed.

If a wish barely includes you, or stings, do the three jobs before you discuss your own feeling. After the mark you can ask for water or a walk. Plenty of unasked desires are small, and the relief is that the small one was allowed to stay small. "I want warmth and no required position" is a complete desire. So is "I want you to talk dirtier than we ever have, and I want that sentence to be the end of it tonight."

## Variations and replay

A voice note works. The reply contains the restate, the appreciation, and the sentence. A letter works the same way: those three pieces on the back, and no logistics paragraph.

On a later month, bring a new lane. A past maybe expires unless the speaker reopens it. A passed wish is not a score. If you want sex the same night for your own reasons, that is a new conversation with its own yes. It does not borrow consent from what you just heard.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not the two of you.

They use the kitchen table and kill the overhead light. Rowan passes the affectionate-gesture lane and reads a fantasy. "I want to be kissed against the counter while the pan is still hot, and I want you to say what you like about my mouth. I have wanted that for a year. I am not asking you to do it." Vale puts both hands on a mug instead of becoming helpful. "I heard a kitchen kiss, the pan still going, and words about your mouth. About a year. You are not asking. I appreciate that you left it at its actual size. Hearing this is not a yes." Rowan marks it tell only. "If we turn it into a plan, I will start directing it. Directing is the part I keep editing out."

Vale's lane is a form of closeness. "When I am in pain I still want to be treated as someone you desire, not as a patient you are being good to. I don't have a position to request. I want the tone." Rowan almost offers a pillow arrangement for tonight, hears the solution forming, and stops. "I heard that pain doesn't cancel you as someone I might want, and that the tone matters more than a pose. This landed in a sore place, because I worry I have been careful in the wrong direction, and I can still do the three jobs. Thank you for trusting me with the part you usually translate into a joke. Hearing this is not a yes." Vale marks it maybe another day, and does not name the day.

Vale's last wish arrives tighter: being watched while undressing, slowly, with no commentary and no task. Vale marks it "please do not ask me to do this" before Rowan can fall in love with the image. Rowan restates it, thanks Vale for the boundary inside the telling, says the sentence, and does not ask why the door is closed. The closed door is the information. They put the notes in a drawer Rowan can empty alone.

## Afterward

Repeat the marks if you want them said once more in a quieter voice. Thank each other for the three jobs, briefly. Then do something ordinary. Dishes, a show, sleep.

The paper is private unless the writer shares it. Shred it, or keep it somewhere the other person does not browse. A tell-only wish stays out of later flirtation. A maybe waits for the speaker. If it never returns, the hearing still counted.

## Notes
