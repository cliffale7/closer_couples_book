## The hook

You can tell a stranger how you met and still hesitate over a plainer question: Do you want this, on purpose, with me? The hesitation is decent. Desire is specific, and a maybe said in a bright kitchen can get promoted, by optimism, into Thursday.

This game slows that promotion down. You classify alone first. Four lanes: romantic, affectionate, sensual, and sexual. Three columns: yes, no, maybe. Then you show a small starred portion. The rest of the paper can stay private. Maybe remains curiosity. No stands complete. A yes on a sheet still waits for a yes when the moment arrives.

## Why it might matter

A list lets you see your own edges before anyone else's hope is in the room. Yes, no, and maybe lists are a common consent practice in sexuality education and in kink negotiation. Many printed lists are copyrighted. This chapter does not copy one. The lanes here put romance and affection beside sensual and sexual ideas, because a life together is larger than a sex menu, and because a gentler lane is a real place to begin.

The sort predicts nothing about satisfaction or sexual harmony. It is a snapshot of appetite and limit, taken by the only person qualified to take it. Two sheets can reveal a painful gap. The evening is allowed to end with no plan attached. Neither of you owes comfort sex, a brighter mood, or a persuasive speech to close that gap.

[The Attraction Interview](033) is about how the pull has changed. This chapter sorts specific ideas and protects the nos. A wish you have never put on a list, with no obligation to act, belongs to [The Desire Nobody Asked About](037).

## Before you begin

You need two private sheets, four lane headings, three columns, and a pen. Large print counts. A voice memo on a device only you can open counts. No shared screen until the compare rules are done. No demonstration during the sort. You are classifying ideas. You are not having them tonight unless some later, separate yes says so.

If either of you has been drinking, or using anything that softens a limit, choose another night. Intoxication is not a lane.

Agree the rules out loud before anyone writes, so they exist outside a hopeful head.

- Blank is allowed. A blank is not a shy yes.
- You may add your own items. Every sample below is optional. Cross out the ones that are none of your business.
- Pain, fatigue, disability, and sensory limits can be nos. A clear no of that kind means the sheet did its job.
- The sheet is for these two adults. Leave every other person off it.
- A no about safer-sex practices stays a no. It does not become a maybe so the two of you can workshop it.
- You may pass a whole lane. Sexual items are skippable even in the standard game.

Compare rules, also said aloud: each person stars up to five yeses they are willing to discuss, and up to three nos they want known so they are not stumbled into. Unstarred nos stay unseen. You compare starred items only. If you want a gentler opening, affectionate and romantic stars come before sensual and sexual ones. A maybe can carry a condition. It cannot be spent later as consent. A smile does not edit a column.

## The experience

**Private sort, about 15 minutes.** Separate rooms, or backs turned, or different hours in the same apartment. Write the four headings even if a lane stays nearly empty. If a lane is too much, write "skipping this lane" and move on.

These samples are original to this chapter, for a page that would otherwise stay blank. Ignore any line. Add lines the samples missed.

Romantic

- A weeknight walk with both phones left in a drawer
- Cooking one meal side by side, playlist already chosen
- Taking the long way home so a conversation can finish
- A morning note that says where you will be, and that you thought of them

Affectionate

- Sitting in comfortable silence, shoulders touching or not
- A kiss in the kitchen that is allowed to remain a kiss
- Hand-holding while one of you talks through an ordinary day
- Falling asleep with a hand on a wrist, pressure chosen by the person wearing the wrist

Sensual

- A massage that stays on ground you have already agreed, and stops at a word
- Slow kissing with a time limit and clothes on
- One of you describing a specific attraction out loud, while the other only listens
- A bath or shower together, lights at a level you both can stand, sex not included

Sexual

- A planned sexual evening with a start, a check-in partway, and a clear end
- A suggestive message during the day, answered only if the reader wants to answer
- A fantasy told as a story, with no plan to act it out
- Sex slower than your habit, either of you free to change course

Put conditions in the maybe column when the idea has an edge. "Only when I am not exhausted." "Not in public." "Only after we have talked the same day." "Only with a pause word we actually use." A maybe with no edge yet is still a maybe. Write "no edge yet" if you want that visible to yourself.

**Starring, about 5 minutes, still private.** Star up to five yeses for discussion. Star up to three nos you want known. Starring a no means "please do not stumble into this." It does not mean "please persuade me." If you star zero nos, that choice stands. You will protect the unstated limits in the moment, because your partner will not have been told.

**Compare, about 20 minutes.** Sit together, or place a call. Read starred items one at a time, yours and theirs. The listener says "heard," or asks one clarifying question. Then the listener stops.

A maybe allows two questions, if the writer wants them: "What is the curiosity?" and "What is the edge?" You do not follow with "So could we do it Thursday?" A no gets "heard." You do not offer a softer version unless the person who wrote the no offers it. A yes may be greeted. The greeting is not a calendar entry. If you notice yourself campaigning, return that item to the private sheet and leave it there.

Unstarred nos stay unseen. "Just so I know you" is a request to break the game. Decline it.

**Close.** Name at most one shared yes that is easy and soon, and in the same breath say that you still ask and that nothing else is scheduled. Easy means both of you starred it, both of you still want it in the room, and it fits energy, privacy, and the bodies you have this month. A starred yes on paper is not the moment. One of you says, out loud, "Easy and soon, we still ask, and nothing else is scheduled." The other agrees, or corrects the record. When you reach that one easy thing, you still ask. Paper is not the moment.

If the stars show a gap that hurts, skip the easy yes. End without scheduling. Eat something, or say goodnight. The game is complete either way.

## Three ways to play

**Short, 20 minutes.** Affectionate and romantic lanes only. Sort in private, star up to three yeses and up to two nos, compare those, and if you name one easy yes, keep "easy and soon" in the same breath as "you still ask" and "nothing else is scheduled." Sensual and sexual lanes can wait, or they can stay unplayed. The short game is a whole game.

**Standard, 45 minutes.** All four lanes, sexual still skippable, including the private time. Use the star caps above. Compare. One easy yes, or none. If you name it, "easy and soon" stays in the same breath as "you still ask" and "nothing else is scheduled." The closing sentence is part of the game. If you skip it and wander into bargaining, you have started a different evening.

**Extended, 75 minutes.** Play the standard sort. Then give every maybe you chose to show one written edge, or the words "no edge yet." Set a review date weeks or months out, and write it where you will see it. On that date you may sort again. You may also cancel. A review date is a calendar mark. It is a summons in no court.

## Going deeper

After the close, each of you may add three items the samples missed, still in private. If your star caps are full, the new items wait for another night. They do not bump a no off the protected list.

You can mark time of day on a yes. "Yes on a weekend afternoon, and no on a weeknight after work" is cleaner than a vague yes. The no half can stay unstarred if you only want the yes discussed.

On a later day, you may take a single maybe and ask what would help you understand it better. Understanding can change. The column changes when the person who wrote it changes it, on a fresh private sort. There is no quota for moving maybe to yes.

If affectionate touch needs a wider vocabulary than four sample lines, [The Language of Touch](022) is a separate, nonsexual exploration. Bring nothing from that chapter back as an assignment.

## Variations and replay

One lane, named before you write, is the right size when overload is high. A verbal sort works: record alone, then copy only your stars onto a fresh card. The recording stays sealed.

At a distance, sort apart and compare on a call with the same caps. Keep the sheets out of a shared photo roll. Low energy can be the short version, in bed, pens only.

Replay on the review date, or after a change in health, housing, privacy, or desire. Old private sheets can be destroyed. The shared journal keeps the one yes you meant to remember, and the review date if you set one. It does not archive your partner's nos.

House rule worth repeating on every replay: a result on paper is not consent to touch or sex. You ask again when you are in the room where it would happen. Easy and soon does not take the place of that ask, and nothing else is scheduled.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not the two of you.

They take 15 minutes in different rooms. Vale's romantic yeses include the weeknight walk and the long way home. In the sexual column, Vale marks yes beside a planned sexual evening, and maybe beside a suggestive message, with the edge "only on days I have said I want flirting." Several nos stay unstarred. Vale stars two nos to make known: sex when exhausted, and anything public.

Rowan stars a kitchen kiss, comfortable silence, slow kissing with clothes on, and a fantasy told as a story with no plan to enact it. Rowan stars one no: massage that wanders past the agreed map.

They compare in the kitchen with the sheets still angled away. Vale says the walk is a real yes. Rowan agrees. Vale's yes for a planned sexual evening stays on the private sheet and off the calendar, because they already have one easy yes and the cap is one. Vale says, "Easy and soon, we still ask, and nothing else is scheduled. A starred yes on paper is not the moment." Rowan says, "Agreed. If we kiss in the kitchen this week, that is a separate ask."

Vale's maybe about messages stays a maybe. Rowan feels the tug to treat a smile as a yes, says so, and puts the tug down. They do not book sex to smooth the moment. The walk can happen Sunday, phones in a drawer, and either of them may still cancel it that afternoon.

## Afterward

A few questions, if you want them. You can also stop at the sentence about nothing else being scheduled.

- Did you leave a blank you are glad you left blank?
- Did the one easy yes still feel easy after you said it aloud?
- Where did you feel the tug to persuade, and what did you do with it?
- Which nos were offered as warnings, and which ones did you correctly never see?

If you saw a no you were not shown, hand it back to privacy. You do not get a second look as a prize for noticing.

## Our Story

Use this spread for the sort you just played. Private pages are optional to share. Private sheets stay private. The shared page can hold the one yes and a review date. It does not hold your partner's nos.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- A yes I was willing to show, what would make it easy and soon, and that I still ask, because nothing else is scheduled
- A maybe I am keeping as curiosity, including its edge, or a blank I am glad I left blank
partner_b:
- A no I chose to make known, with no case to argue, or my choice to keep my nos off this page
- What I want us to remember about asking again when the moment actually arrives
shared:
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```

## Notes

No empirical citation is claimed. Yes, no, and maybe lists are a common consent practice. This version is reimagined: four lanes, a private sort, a starred compare, and maybe left unspent. It is a compatibility test in no study. No published questionnaire is reproduced here. Adult partners only. Pressure, third parties, and intoxication have no role in moving a column.
