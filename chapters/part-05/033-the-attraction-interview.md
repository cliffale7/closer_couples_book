## The hook

The meeting story gets smoother every year, and a little less true. You met at a party, a shift, a friend's crowded table. Someone was funny. Someone had a voice you still hear in other rooms. The retelling sands the hour down until it could belong to anyone pleasant.

Meanwhile the attraction that is actually in the house this month gets less practice, because it is harder to toast. It might be competence. It might be kindness with a spine. It might be want that is quieter than it was, and more exact. It might be complicated on a weekday morning, with one part still unmistakably alive.

This is an interview in three movements, in this order: first attraction, current attraction, and the way you understand chemistry now. Both of you sit in the guest chair. Leave the scorecard in the other room.

## Why it might matter

Three movements, in order: first attraction, current attraction, and the way you understand chemistry now. Praise can land on the portrait from the first month. Being accurately imagined is a different event. The current picture includes what draws you now: humor, mind, voice, kindness, competence, the way they handle a stranger, and straightforward want. A face can be part of it. A face is a thin account if it is the whole account.

"What first attracted you?" is folk conversation. People have asked it over dinner for longer than this book has been a book. Models of love and desire exist in relationship science. This interview does not use them as proof, and it does not claim that talking about chemistry increases it. A flat answer is information. A changed answer is information. Quieter, narrower, wider, or more specific can all be honest. None of them is a verdict on the relationship you are sitting inside.

What you can actually do is give each other a current account, with a beginning kept for contrast, and let the interviewer stay curious for 8 minutes.

## Before you begin

Sit where you can hear each other, set a call, or agree to trade letters. A timer helps the swap happen. Optional cards labeled First, Now, and How I understand it now keep the third movement from being eaten by a good story. Paper, large type, a screen reader, or a voice memo all work. You do not need to talk about appearance. If hearing is the hard channel, write the interview and pass the pages. If you live apart, the call is the whole room.

Phones face down if you are in the same place. "Pass," "pause," and "different question" are complete moves. The interviewer gets two follow-ups in a turn and does not grade what they hear. One follow-up is worth keeping in a pocket: "What do you notice now that you did not have language for then?"

Sexual detail and bodily detail are optional. Either of you may stay with humor, mind, voice, kindness, competence, and the feel of ordinary company. A comment about the other person's body is an offering. The person being described may redirect it, and the redirect ends that thread. You do not owe a history of other lovers. If current attraction is complicated, "complicated, and here is one true part" finishes the sentence. The interviewer does not repair that answer before the timer ends.

Include at least one question that can be answered with the lights off. Sound, humor, and action count as attraction.

## The experience

Choose who is interviewed first by a method more boring than flirting. A coin, or whoever has the colder drink. Open on the three movements, in order: first attraction, current attraction, and how you understand chemistry now. The limit on the turn is 8 minutes. The interviewer covers all three. Time need not be equal. A movement left out means the interview is unfinished, so ask a short version of it before those minutes end.

The guest answers in ordinary speech. Silence counts. A short answer counts. Performing desire you do not feel tonight misses the point. Say the true temperature.

**First.** Stay near the beginning you actually remember. The first hour, if you have it. The first week, if the hour has gone blurry.

- What caught you before you had a speech about it?
- What did they do, say, or decide that pulled you, apart from how they looked?
- If you tell that stretch with the lights off, which detail remains?
- What did you call the feeling then, even if the word now seems clumsy?

**Now.** An ordinary Tuesday is the point. A holiday can be a footnote.

- What draws you on a day that nobody arranged as a date?
- Where does ease show up, and where does want show up, if it does?
- What sound in the week would you miss if they were away? A laugh, a muttered explanation, the particular way a door closes.
- If pain, illness, or energy has changed how you want closeness, you may say what fits the body you have. One sentence can be the whole of that subject.

**How I understand it now.** This movement keeps the hour from becoming a souvenir.

- Which early idea of chemistry have you retired?
- What do you notice now that you did not have language for then?
- Has the pull gotten quieter, narrower, wider, or more specific? Name one, and give it an example.
- What still feels like attraction that a stranger at a party would miss?

You can be as explicit as the room welcomes. "I wanted you to kiss me before we ordered" is a clean sentence. So is "I wanted the way you think." Match the detail to the privacy you actually have. Explicit is allowed. Required is a different interview.

Two follow-ups, then the timer wins, even if a movement was thin. Thank them in one sentence. Swap chairs and repeat, including the guest who just interviewed. A night when one person only asks has not played this chapter.

When both turns are done, take 2 minutes. Each of you names one sentence you heard and want the other person to know you kept. No rebuttal. No number. If you later want to sort particular ideas into yes, no, and maybe, that work lives in [The Yes, No, Maybe Game](034), on a different evening unless you both clearly want it now. This interview books nothing.

## Three ways to play

**Short, 15 minutes.** Each of you chooses one movement and answers it properly. Then each of you adds one sentence on each of the other two. You still cover first, now, and the change in understanding. Both people answer. Skip the formal 8-minute clock. Keep the one-sentence echo if you want it.

**Standard, 35 minutes.** 8 minutes each, two follow-ups inside the turn, then the echo. Use a card or a finger on the page so the third movement survives the first-hour story.

**Extended, 60 minutes.** Play the standard interview. Then take a second pass of about 20 minutes on the third movement only. Trade the interviewer halfway through that pass. Quieter, narrower, and more specific are all available answers. The second pass is for better language. It is a poor courtroom in which to decide whether the relationship is "enough."

## Going deeper

Write three lines before you speak, one per movement, and bring the paper. Some people tell the truth faster in handwriting. Read the lines and stop, or use them as a start and then talk past them.

If voice is the harder channel, trade letters over a day. The interviewer still gets two written follow-ups, sent after they have read. The guest may still pass a line.

One question can be about desire and still only be a description. "Lately I want more kissing in the kitchen. I am telling you that as a fact about me." If you both want facts to become a sort, leave the sort for 034. If you want the story of when the other person started to matter, which can rhyme with first attraction and is a different hour, use [The Moment I Knew](027).

When attraction and a body's limits are tangled, one honest sentence is enough. "I want closeness that does not ask me to be athletic." The interviewer receives it. They do not design a treatment in the chair.

## Variations and replay

On a call, cameras are optional. The three movements still go in order. A written interview is a full version, not a lesser one, including when sensory overload makes speech expensive. On those nights, play the short version, and skip any flirtation game you were tempted to stack on top.

Walk if talking is easier in motion. Name the movements out loud so scenery does not retire the third one. Low energy belongs in the short version, on the couch, with the timer visible to one of you.

Replay it in a year. If you kept the third-movement lines, read only those before you begin, and notice what your understanding has done. [The Romantic Time Machine](039) asks a cousin question, what would still attract you in another era or circumstance, when you want imagination after this more local account.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not the two of you.

Vale is in the guest chair. Rowan asks what caught Vale in the first hour.

Vale says, "I barely filed your face. You were in a fight with a menu, and then you asked the server a real question instead of performing patience. I wanted the person who does that. I also wanted your voice. It dropped when you were interested, and I felt it in my ribs. I called the whole thing spark, which is a word I have retired for being useless."

Rowan spends one follow-up. "What do you notice now that you did not have language for then?"

Vale says, "Attention. On Tuesdays it is you in the other room, explaining a problem you are good at, muttering, then laughing at your own sentence. Want is in that. So is the relief of not performing. Some weeks the want is loud. I am not grading the week."

They swap. Rowan says, "My early idea was that chemistry had to feel like trouble, a little dangerous, a little like I might blow up my calendar. I have retired that. What I have with you is more specific. I want your hands. I want the quiet you get when you are thinking. I want the competence, and competence is not a polite substitute for the want. It is its own pull. Short version of now: complicated in the mornings, and here is one true part, I still turn toward your voice when you come in."

Vale asks, "Which early idea did I never actually fit?" Rowan says, "The one where attraction means we never feel ordinary. Ordinary is where I find you attractive now. The menu story can stay. It is no longer the whole portrait."

They each keep one sentence. Vale keeps "ordinary is where I find you attractive now." Rowan keeps "I am not grading the week." They stop. Nobody schedules sex to prove the interview succeeded.

## Afterward

Stay for a minute. The echo can be the whole close. If you want questions, use a few of these.

- Which movement was easiest, and which one did you almost turn into a compliment?
- Did anything you heard update a picture you had been using without noticing?
- What one true part of current attraction do you want remembered, including a complicated part?
- Did either of you redirect a body comment, and did the redirect get respected?

A good interview can end without a moral. The sentence you repeated back is enough to carry into the kitchen.

## Notes

No whitelist source is claimed. "What first attracted you?" is a folk question. The three-part interview, with both people in both roles, is a design for adults who already share a relationship. It is an assessment of chemistry in nobody's data. Talking about attraction is the activity. A rise in desire is not a result you are owed. No published question list is reprinted here.
