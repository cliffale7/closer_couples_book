## The hook

Wishes live in the future tense, which is a polite tense. "Someday we might." The verb stays hypothetical, and the scene stays foggy enough that nobody has to want it on a real Thursday. A memory is ruder, and kinder. It has a place, a line someone said, a smell, a feeling you can almost date.

The Future Memory asks for one happy scene that has not happened, written as if it already has. You read it the way you would read a diary page. Then you write a shorter page that tells the truth about the bridge: what is already true, what would have to be built, what you could do this month, and what is weather. Weather cannot be forced. The other person's desire is often weather.

## Why it might matter

A foggy wish is hard to join and hard to decline. A scene with a coat, a sentence, and a feeling can be loved, revised, or set beside a different scene without anyone pretending it was only a mood. The past tense is the mechanism. It makes you choose details. Details are where a fantasy stops spending the other person as a prop and starts describing a day.

Imagining is not causing. A scene can be vivid and still depend on money, health, timing, and a yes you do not control. The practical page exists so the memory does not get promoted into a plan by tone of voice alone. Happiness in the scene can be quiet. A rented kitchen, a train, a nap, a joke that landed. Cinematic romance is one option among quieter ones.

098, The One-Hundred-Year Interview, puts you both in a long-life frame, welcomes contradictory decades, and later asks each person for three invented memories they would like to make real. 031, A Love Letter From the Future, is a letter. This chapter is one scene and one bridge. 095 is where a sentence becomes a promise. A future memory is earlier than a promise, and it can stay a story.

Schacter, Addis, and Buckner argue that imagining the future depends on much of the same neural machinery as remembering. See S2007. Their constructive episodic simulation hypothesis says future simulation recombines details from the past, and the review says that hypothesis has support. The same review says further work still has to test whether future simulations recombine elements from different episodes or retrieve fragments. That argument is about how a future scene is built. It does not show that the scene will happen, and it does not validate this chapter. The past tense here is a writing device. Label the page fiction-as-memory so a later reader knows it is not a diary of record.

## Before you begin

Sit, or exchange pages before a call so the call is for listening. Dictation is welcome if writing is hard.

Choose a scene that does not require an unstated physical ability. If the scene includes dancing, travel, sex, children, relocation, or a proposal, mark it as one possible future. It is a desire on paper until the other person wants the same scene. You may keep a scene private and offer only the practical step you control.

Skip any scene whose hidden sentence is a complaint. "In the memory, you finally..." spends the partner as a corrected version of themselves. Write a day you would be glad to have lived, including the people as they actually are, with room for them to want something else.

If the two scenes conflict, they stay side by side. Nobody edits their hope into the other's paragraph.

## The experience

Each person writes one page, about 10 minutes. Four ingredients, all in the past tense, about a happy time that has not happened.

A place.
A line of dialogue, something one of you said.
A sensory detail: wool, rain, sugar, the sound of a station.
A feeling, named plainly.

Read it aloud as a memory. The listener does not interrupt to fact-check the future. When the reading ends, the listener may say one sentence of what they heard, the way you would if a friend told you about a day. "I hear a wet Thursday and a cake you bought anyway." They do not grade the dream. They do not annex it.

Then a second, shorter page, a few minutes. Four lines.

One condition already true.
One condition that would have to be built.
One step available this month.
One element that is weather and cannot be forced.

Read the practical page in a different voice if you can, a plainer one. The memory was allowed to glow. The bridge is allowed to be ordinary. If the step this month is "look at a timetable," it counts. If the weather is "you would have to want this too," say that without a sulk.

If you want a shared project, it comes after both scenes exist, and only where they overlap by a real yes. One of you may love a scene and still decline to build it. The decliner can name a piece they would join, or they can leave the scene as the other person's private hope. Private hopes are allowed to stay single-author.

Label both pages fiction-as-memory. A later rereading should be able to tell a wish from a diary.

## Three ways to play

**15 minutes.** One scene each, spoken in the past tense, with the four practical lines said aloud and unwritten if you are short on paper. The listener offers one hearing sentence. A spoken pair of scenes is a finished session.

**35 minutes.** Write the memory page and the practical page. Read both. Leave the scenes side by side if they differ. Choose a shared step only if you both want one. This is the version that keeps the tense and the bridge in the same hour.

**55 minutes.** Exchange the pages before you speak, and read them in silence first. Then each listener says what felt like the speaker, and what detail they are not ready to treat as a plan. Still one scene each. The extra time is for the difference between a day you can almost remember and a day you are willing to build.

## Going deeper

A second scene, on another day, can be a different scale. If the first was a trip, the second can be a Tuesday at home. Quiet futures are easy to skip, and they are often the ones with a real step attached.

You can ask the speaker, only with a yes, which detail is load-bearing. The cake. The empty afternoon. The particular city. Load-bearing details tell you what a smaller version could keep. A smaller version is still the memory's cousin. It is not a consolation prize you announce without them.

If a scene keeps returning in conversation as a complaint about the present, set the page down and have the direct conversation. The past tense is a poor place to hide a grievance.

## Variations and replay

Long distance: send the memory ahead, and use the call for listening and for the practical page, which you can write while you talk. Dictate into a phone if a pen is the obstacle.

Scenes that need a body to do something difficult are marked possible, and a seated or home version is welcome beside them. The motif is a photo frame with the date left blank: `assets/chapters/090/motif.svg`.

Replay when a season changes and the old scene no longer fits, or when a piece of it has become a memory of something that happened. Bring both pages. Notice which line moved from fiction into the diary, and which line is still weather.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale are an illustration, not a portrait of you.

Rowan reads, past tense. "We took a Thursday train to the city we can reach without a plane. It rained. In the museum cafe, Vale said the cake was overpriced and then bought it anyway. I remember the wool of their coat against my wrist on the platform, and the feeling was uncomplicated pleasure, the kind that does not need a lesson afterward."

Vale says, "I hear rain, a train, and a cake you were glad to share. I also hear my coat doing some of the work."

Rowan's practical page: already true, they both like trains and neither needs the trip to be a production. To build, a free Thursday and the fare. This month, look at one timetable and a free museum hour. Weather: Vale has to want the day, and the rain is none of their business.

Vale's scene is different, and Rowan does not edit it. "We spent a whole Sunday in the apartment. The project bench stayed covered. Rowan read on the floor. I fixed the rattle in the cabinet with one screw, badly, and we laughed at the extra screw I still had in my hand. I was proud and unhurried."

Vale's bridge: already true, the rattle is real and the floor is available. To build, a Sunday with no guests. This month, pick a Sunday and put a note on the bench that says covered. Weather: Rowan may want to go out, and a covered bench cannot be a law.

They do not merge the train and the Sunday into a compromise city picnic. Rowan says the train can wait. Vale says the Sunday is the one they are willing to try. The first step is the note on the bench. Both pages are labeled fiction-as-memory, including the Sunday, until it happens.

## Afterward

Which detail made the scene feel like a memory, and which detail was costume?
What is already true?
What is weather, including the other person's want?
Did you use the scene to scold the present? If you did, say so in the present tense, outside the story.

You can keep a future memory as a story you like. Liking it is a complete outcome. Building it is a second decision, made with a yes.

## Our Story

Date the session. Note the version, the place, and whether you finished or paused. Initials and mood are optional. Mark the scene as fiction-as-memory so it is not later mistaken for a record of an event.

The two personal pages are yours. Share them, keep them, or leave them blank. Unread is a complete way to use them. A private scene can stay on the personal page. The shared notes are the part you write together.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- A happy future scene, written as if it had already happened.
- One condition already true, one step this month, and one element that is weather.
partner_b:
- Yours, a scene of your own hoping. It is not a correction of the first scene.
- The practical step you control, if you want one on the page.
shared:
- One scene you are willing to try to make, and the first practical step if you want one.
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- Has any piece of that future memory become a memory of something that happened?
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```
