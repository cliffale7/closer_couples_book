## The hook

Promises get big when they are performing. Forever. Always. The feeling you will arrange to have on demand. Then a Thursday arrives, the phone stays on the table, and the vow has no handle. You can want a promise that is smaller than a vow and still mean it. The test is private: would you want to keep this if nobody clapped, and if your partner never used it as a receipt?

A Promise I Actually Want to Keep is a few of those, one to three, each made by the person who will do it. Realistic. Specific. Possible inside a month. Attached to a follow-through they control. The partner does not enlarge it. At the review, a promise can be kept, rewritten, or retired. A miss gets named. Shame is not the method.

## Why it might matter

A relationship collects floating intentions. "We should talk more." "I'll be better about the mornings." Floating intentions are easy to love and hard to do, because they never say when, or what the hands will actually do. A cue and a small action give the week something to recognize.

Gollwitzer distinguishes a goal intention, "I intend to reach x," from an implementation intention, "When situation x arises, I will perform response y." See G1999. The review describes a research program in which those if-then plans supported goal attainment in the domains studied. In that review, goal intentions had modest effects. They were not useless. That program is not a study of promises between partners. It does not show that a sentence you make in this chapter will be kept. The if-then line below is optional scaffolding. It is not a tested couple treatment, and it is not a reason to trust a vow you do not actually want.

The mechanism in this room is consent and scale. Few promises. Each one wanted by the speaker. Each one small enough to meet on a low-energy week. Meaning lives in ordinary care as easily as in grand language.

## Before you begin

You will each offer promises you control. You cannot promise the other person's feelings, friends, body, or time. You can promise what you will do with your own.

No one may demand a promise in exchange for affection or safety. Promises about sex are welcome only as commitments the speaker wants, and they never bind the other person's body. Either of you may refuse to witness a promise that pressures you. If witnessing it would corner you, say so, and the sentence goes back to the speaker to revise or drop.

Gates, applied before a promise is spoken as final:

It matters to the relationship, or to how you show up in it.
You control it.
It can be done within a month.
It is specific.

A promise of a feeling fails the gates. "I will always desire you" is a wish, and it belongs in a different conversation if you want to talk about desire. This page is for actions.

Write where both of you can see the wording, in the speaker's own verbs. If you want a signature, use a letter page and sign only what you just said. The signature is a private emphasis. It is not a legal document and not a wedding liturgy.

## The experience

Take 10 minutes alone. Each person drafts one to three promises they would still want to keep if the other person never thanked them. Read each draft against the gates. Cut anything grand, anything that polices a partner's other relationships, and anything you are offering in order to be seen as good.

Optional second sentence, if you want the cue: "When [situation], I will [response]." An example of the shape, not a script you must adopt: "When we sit down on Thursday, I will put my phone in the other room until the tea is finished." The first sentence can be the promise in plain speech. The second sentence is the if-then plan. You may keep only the first. In the G1999 review, goal intentions had modest effects. They were not useless. Choose the shape you will actually recognize on the day.

Read your promises aloud. The partner's job is to witness, ask one clarifying question if the action is fuzzy, and stop. They do not add "and also the dishes," and they do not trade their affection for a larger version. If they want a promise of their own, it belongs in their turn, chosen by them. Symmetry is optional. One person may make one promise and the other may make three, or one, or none tonight.

Set a review date inside the month, or just past it. Put the date on the same page as the words. At the review, the outcomes are kept, rewritten, or retired. A broken promise is named in one or two sentences: what the cue was, what happened, what you will change in the wording or release. Then you rewrite or retire. You do not hold court on character.

Across distance, promises about calls, letters, and logistics are the natural set. "When your morning is my evening, I will send the one-line note before I open the other app." The act still has to be possible on a tired week. If the only version requires a heroic day, shrink it until a hard week can carry it.

## Three ways to play

**15 minutes.** One promise each, already specific, with or without the if-then sentence, plus a review date. Witness, and do not enlarge. A pair of small promises is a finished session. More can wait.

**30 minutes.** Draft with the gates. Up to three each. Read them. One clarifying question allowed, aimed at the action, not at the speaker's worth. Write the cue, the act, and the review date in the speaker's verbs. This is the version that keeps the list short on purpose.

**50 minutes.** Include a first look at an old promise you are still carrying from somewhere else, and decide whether it belongs in this set, rewritten, or released. The extra time is for retiring sentences you never wanted. Still no more than three live promises each when you stand up.

## Going deeper

At the review, describe the keeping in ordinary detail. "The phone was in the other room for three Thursdays, and on the fourth I answered a work text and then put it back" is a useful record. It gives you something to rewrite. A perfect record is not the entrance fee for the next promise.

You can also notice promises you are keeping without having written them down. Naming one of those can be enough for a season. The chapter does not require you to manufacture new virtue. It asks you to be picky about the sentences you attach your name to.

If a promise keeps breaking at the same cue, the cue may be fantasy. Change the situation you are waiting for. "When we have a free Sunday" may never arrive. "When the kettle boils on Thursday" arrives. Shrink until the world you live in can host the act.

## Variations and replay

Non-physical promises belong beside any that involve errands or outings. A promise to let a sentence finish. A promise to say you are leaving the room before you disappear into a task. A promise to ask before you add a guest. These travel into sick weeks and small apartments.

The motif is a small knot tied on purpose, easy to retie: `assets/chapters/095/motif.svg`.

A scene is earlier than a promise. Come back when the review date arrives. What you kept, what you amended, and what you released all belong on the page.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale are an illustration, not a portrait of you.

Rowan drafts two and cuts one. The cut line was "I will be more affectionate," which fails the gates by being a feeling and a fog. The line that stays: "When we sit down on Thursday, I will put my phone in the other room until the tea is finished." Rowan wants this even if Vale never mentions it. It can be done in a month. The phone is Rowan's.

Vale asks one question: "If the tea is the 5-minute kind, does the phone stay away for 5 minutes?" Rowan says yes. Vale does not add the dishes, the inbox, or a speech about presence.

Vale's promise is different, and unpaired. "When I notice I am about to joke over your sentence, I will let the sentence finish." Vale does not promise to stop being funny. The cue is the moment of noticing. The act is the pause. A second promise: "When I say I will look at the shared bill, I will open it the same day, even if I only look for 2 minutes." Both are Vale's to keep. Neither requires Rowan to be impressive.

They write the sentences where both can see them, in the speakers' verbs. Review in three weeks, on a Sunday they already have. They agree that a miss will be named and rewritten, and that nobody will be prosecuted for character. Rowan signs the Thursday line on a letter page because the ritual of the signature pleases them. Vale does not sign, and the unsigned promises count the same.

## Afterward

Which promise would you keep if it stayed unwitnessed?
Where did a draft fail the gates, and did you cut it?
Did either of you try to enlarge the other's sentence?
When will you look again, and what will you do with a miss?

A small promise kept, or honestly rewritten, is the whole achievement. Forever can remain a private hope. This page is for Thursdays.
