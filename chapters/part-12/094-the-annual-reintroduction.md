## The hook

People keep changing in ordinary ways, and couples often meet the change late, in a mood, because nobody scheduled a day to be reintroduced. A new sleep. A value that shifted half an inch. A wish to retire a ritual that used to be the point. A need that is the same as last year, which is also news, because you checked.

The Annual Reintroduction is an invitation to do that checking on purpose, about once a year, in a week you can actually protect. Needs, interests, values, and wishes are all in the room. If you miss the week, the next calm week counts. There is no penalty, no performance review, and no prize for displaying growth. "I want the same things, and I checked" is a complete answer.

## Why it might matter

Anniversaries, birthdays, and the turn of a year already give people an excuse to talk. Those occasions often fill up with guests, gifts, and the story of how you began. The reintroduction uses the calendar for a different job: to notice who is here now. A yearly page gives last year's portrait a chance to be wrong.

The ritual is repeatable because the point is change, including the change that did not happen. A one-time recap of a book, or a date that assumes nobody moved, is a different hour. 001, The Stranger Across From Me, pretends you have just met, for an evening. 096, The Next Chapter, is a landing after a season of experiences, where you design a few practices. This chapter is the invitation you can keep on a calendar of your own, including in a year when you are tired of designing anything.

No study is claimed for annual talks. They do not come with a promise about staying together. They are a craft: five questions, one shared revision, a page you can set beside next year's.

## Before you begin

Tie it to a week you can protect. The week does not have to be a New Year, a wedding anniversary, or anyone's holiday. A borrowed room, a kitchen table, a video call, or a hospital dayroom will do. A walk is welcome only if walking is welcome. The seated form is the default.

Send the prompts ahead if either of you thinks better with warning. No gift is required. A lavish setting is how this ritual becomes easy to postpone. Illness, grief, or a tired mouth may mean you answer three of the five. That is a full reintroduction.

Changes in desire, faith, family role, or the wish to stay are welcome as topics, and they are declinable if the speaker is not ready. The listener does not cross-examine a change. New agreements about sex, money, or where you live can be noted, then given a separate sober conversation. The annual date does not have to close them. Either of you may pause, or bring a support person if that is what makes the hour possible.

Passing a prompt is ordinary. Saving every grievance for this day will swamp it. If a grievance is live, give it a nearer conversation, and let this one stay a reintroduction.

## The experience

Each person answers all five, or the three they can manage. Speak, or write and then read your own words. Leave room between you so the second set of answers is not a revision of the first.

What is new in your body, your work, or your daily life?
What do you want more of?
What do you want retired?
What do you still want known?
Which value has shifted, even slightly?

"Nothing new, and I looked" belongs in every blank it truthfully fits. Identical answers copied from last year out of politeness are the failure mode. If the answer is the same because it is still true, say that you checked. The checking is the ritual.

Then one shared revision, chosen together. A ritual to keep. A ritual to drop. Or a wish to carry forward. One. A stack of revisions turns the hour into a summit. If you cannot agree, write both wishes down and pick a later day. Disagreement is allowed to remain unfinished when the tea is gone.

Write a single journal page and date it for next year's comparison. Include the year, the place, and the answers you are willing to reread. Next year, set the pages side by side and ask what would have surprised the people who wrote the old one.

If you keep a chapters-of-us recap in the companion to this book, it can be written beside this sitting, or in a year you skip the reintroduction. The recap is a story of the year. This chapter is a meeting between two changing people. They can share a weekend. They do not have to.

Close with one sentence each: who you understand the other person to be this year, said lightly enough that they can correct you. The correction is part of the reintroduction. You are not drafting their biography.

## Three ways to play

**25 minutes.** Three prompts each, chosen by the speaker, plus one shared revision if you have it. Say which prompts you left, so the skip is visible and unpunished. A shorter return still counts as the annual invitation. Put the date on the page anyway.

**50 minutes.** All five, both people, then one shared revision and the dated page. This is the version to protect if you can. Send the prompts ahead so the hour is for saying, not for inventing under a clock.

**70 minutes.** Read last year's page first, slowly, and mark one surprise before you answer this year's five. If this is the first year, skip the reading and use the extra time for quieter pauses between answers. Still one shared revision. Still no review of each other's character.

## Going deeper

The prompts can be answered in writing the week before, and the meeting can be the reading. Some people are more accurate on paper. The listener's job in the room is to receive, ask one curious question if invited, and survive the correction.

You might notice a value that shifted in opposite directions. One of you wants more public life. The other wants a smaller room. Write both. The annual hour does not have to reconcile them. It has to make them visible before the year spends itself on the louder one.

A further kindness: each person names one interest the other has taken up that they want to keep understanding. A show, a friend, a tool, a god, a sport, a joke. Curiosity is welcome here. Interrogation is how an invitation becomes an audit, and this chapter declines that job.

## Variations and replay

Long distance is a natural fit. Same prompts, cameras on or off, pages exchanged the day before so the call can be unhurried. If one of you is linguistically tired, answer in the language that is easiest and let the other ask for a gloss only where it matters.

The motif is a doorway with two chalk marks, last year's and this year's: `assets/chapters/094/motif.svg`. The marks are allowed to match.

Replay this one yearly, or in the next calm week if the year got away. The people who wrote last year's page are allowed to be surprised.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale are an illustration, not a portrait of you.

Rowan, at the kitchen table, no gift in sight: "New in my body: I am sleeping earlier, and I am less willing to pretend that midnight is a personality. More of: mornings, and friends in small numbers. Retire: the Sunday planning meeting. It became a trial. Still want known: I get quiet when work is good, not only when it is bad. Value: I care more about being a reliable friend than about being impressive. I checked. That last one moved."

Vale answers, given the same room. "Body: the knee is a fact this year, not a plot twist. More of: soup, and being asked before plans land. Retire: nothing structural. I want the Wednesday call, and I checked. Still want known: my quiet is often contentment. Shifted value: I want our home to be easy to leave for other people, and easy to come back to. Last year I was more interested in being hard to find."

They do not cross-examine the knee or the midnight. Rowan asks one question, invited: "Easy to leave, meaning guests, or meaning us going out?" Vale says both, in small doses.

Shared revision: keep the Wednesday call. Drop the Sunday meeting. They write one page, dated, and leave a blank line for next year. Vale says, "If we miss the week, we take the next calm one. I will not build a case about it." Rowan agrees. The invitation stands.

## Afterward

What changed, and what did you check and find steady?
Which prompt did you skip, and was the skip a kindness?
Did the listener try to close a change before the speaker was finished?
What one ritual stays, drops, or gets carried, and what waits for a separate conversation?

You can be proud of a year that looks similar on paper. The work was the reintroduction, not the plot twist.

## Our Story

Date the session. Note the version, the place, and whether you finished or paused. Initials and mood are optional. This page is meant to sit beside next year's. A chapters-of-us recap may live next to it, or in a year you skip this sitting.

The two personal pages are yours. Share them, keep them, or leave them blank. Unread is a complete way to use them. The shared notes are the part you write together.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- What has changed in your needs, interests, values, or wishes since last year, or since you began if this is the first time.
- A ritual to keep, a ritual to drop, or a wish to carry, in one line.
partner_b:
- Your changes, in your words, given the same room.
- Which of the five prompts you answered, and which you left for another week.
shared:
- Who you are this year, as two people and as a pair.
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- Set last year's page beside this one. What would have surprised the people who wrote it?
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```

## Notes

Reimagined from the calendar excuses couples already have, turned into an explicit reintroduction. No empirical claim is made. An annual conversation is not presented as protection against parting. The date need not be a New Year, an anniversary, or a holiday. "I want the same things, and I checked" is a valid return. Missed weeks move to the next calm week. Topics that touch sex, money, or living arrangements can be noted and then given their own conversation. This is an invitation you can decline in a given year.
