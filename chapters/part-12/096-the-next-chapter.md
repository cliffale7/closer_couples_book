## The hook

After a season of talking, playing, and telling the truth in pieces, it is tempting to design a second life: every good idea, every skipped chapter, a calendar that would impress a stranger. The season you lived was smaller than that, and more specific. You learned a few things. You liked a few practices. You are done pretending to want some of the rest.

The Next Chapter is a landing. You reflect on discoveries you actually made, then you choose at most two recurring practices and one dated future experience. Each practice needs a smallest version for a hard week. Further ideas go in a margin labeled "not now." You may also choose no new practice, and keep one old kindness. That is a complete next chapter. It may be quiet.

## Why it might matter

Endings in workbooks often become goal avalanches. The avalanche feels like seriousness, and then it becomes guilt, which is a poor material for a life. A landing works when it is short enough to remember without the book in your hand.

The look-back is limited to experiences you truly did. A discovery about a chapter you meant to get to is a discovery about intention, and it can sit in the margin. What you carry forward should have already survived contact with your actual week: your energy, your distance, your humor, your limits.

This hour can install 094, The Annual Reintroduction, if you want a repeating invitation on the calendar. It does not replace that yearly meeting, and it does not assign the original games that follow this part as homework. Closeness does not require finishing every experience in a book. The next chapter is yours. No finding here claims the book's arc is an evidence-based program.

## Before you begin

You do not have to reread anything first. If your memory of earlier hours is fuzzy, use whatever you still carry. A clear sentence you can say without notes is enough. A foggy sense that "the listening one mattered" can be enough if you can say what you do differently when you remember it.

Discoveries that were vulnerable belong to the person who made them. Reuse one only with their yes. The dated experience is a plan you both want. It is not a surprise that spends someone's body, money, or stamina.

If what you actually need to renegotiate is safety, monogamy, or where you live, flag it for a dedicated conversation. This landing is the wrong pocket for a stealth rewrite of the terms of your life.

Passing is allowed. So is the sentence, "No new practice. We are keeping one old kindness." Name the kindness so it is real.

## The experience

Set a margin on the page and write "not now" at the top of it. Anything that arrives as a good idea and is not one of the few choices goes there without debate. The margin is a gift to your future restless selves. It is not a debt.

Each person, in turn, names four things, and any of them can be "nothing new under this heading."

One discovery about yourself, from something you actually did.
One discovery about the other, offered gently, and corrected if they say you missed them.
One practice worth keeping.
One practice worth dropping, including a practice of saving every hard topic for a summit, if that is what you are done with.

Keep each item to a few sentences. A discovery is not a verdict on the relationship's health. "I learned I like ordinary questions more than grand ones" is a discovery. "We are finally doing this right" is a poster. Leave the poster in the margin or off the page.

Then choose together, and stop when you have chosen:

At most two recurring practices.
One dated future experience, or none.
The smallest version of each practice on a hard week: in bed, on a call, or inside 10 minutes.
When you will notice how it is going. One date, not a surveillance schedule.

Write them on a single page: what, how often, the smallest version, and the noticing date. Read the page aloud. If it sounds like a second job, cut it in the room. Two practices that survive a hard week will outlast twelve that require a personality transplant.

Examples of scale, not assignments: a monthly page in the shape of a future memory, whose smallest version is a voice note. A bench from a shared list, on a Saturday you already have off. The two-question cap from a disagreement, used once the next time opinions heat up. A Wednesday call you already know how to keep. If 094 appeals, the practice can be "the reintroduction in a week we can protect, and the next calm week if we miss it." That is an invitation you are choosing, not a compulsory audit.

Close by naming what you are done pretending to want. One thing each is plenty. A trip, a ritual, a kind of talk, a fantasy of being people who journal every night. Saying it aloud keeps it from sneaking back in as guilt.

## Three ways to play

**20 minutes.** One discovery each, one practice you are keeping, and its hard-week version. The dated experience can wait. "No new practice" is available in this version and counts as done. Put the not-now margin on the page even if it holds a single line.

**40 minutes.** The four headings each, then at most two practices, one optional dated experience, smallest versions, and a noticing date. Include the thing you are done pretending to want. This is the landing to use if you have had a real season and want it distilled.

**60 minutes.** Begin by listing, briefly, the experiences you actually did, titles or phrases only, so the discoveries have to come from that list. Then the same small design. The extra minutes are for accuracy about the past, not for a larger future. Still two practices at most. Still a margin.

## Going deeper

On the noticing date, ask three questions and then leave it. Are we doing the smallest version? Has the practice become a resentment? Does the dated experience still belong to both of us? Revise in a few lines. Do not open the margin and promote everything.

You can keep a practice that looks like almost nothing. Tea without phones. A question behind an ordinary answer, once a week, with permission to stop at lunch. Company for an unfinished question, with advice staying outside until invited. Small is not a rehearsal for large. Small is often the whole intimacy.

If the season was thin, because of illness, work, grief, or a book that stayed mostly closed, the next chapter can be one kindness you already perform and want to keep seeing. You do not owe the unread chapters a penance.

## Variations and replay

Across distance, name practices that survive screens and travel. A letter. A call with a start time and an end. A photo of the ordinary day, sent without a caption requirement. The smallest version should be possible in a bad time zone.

The motif is a page with two written lines and a wide margin: `assets/chapters/096/motif.svg`. The margin is part of the design.

073, The First Chapter, is for the beginning of the story you already share. 091, What We Want More Of, starts from appetite and chooses increases for a short period. 094 is the yearly invitation to be reintroduced, if you want it on the calendar. Come back to this landing when a season ends, or when the practices have quietly become either beloved or fake. Life may hand you a different chapter than the one you designed. The page can say so.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale are an illustration, not a portrait of you.

They list what they actually did: an unpopular opinion apiece, a ladder that started at a cheese sandwich, two unfinished questions left open, a draft of how they greet each other, one future memory about rain and cake, a short "more of" list, a bike-repair dream, a bucket list starring a bench. They do not inventory the hours they skipped.

Rowan's discovery about themself: "I like being asked ordinary questions, and I hate being handed a plan in the same minute." Discovery about Vale, offered and accepted: "You want company beside an unfinished question, and advice later, if ever." Practice to keep: the two-question cap when an opinion shows up. Practice to drop: the Sunday meeting that turned into a trial.

Vale's discovery about themself: "I disappear into usefulness, and I want that noticed without a rescue." Discovery about Rowan: "Your quiet is sometimes a good work week." Practice to keep: reading in the same room, which can be 15 minutes. Practice to drop: pretending they want a passport trip this year.

Together they choose two practices. Once a week, the shared reading, smallest version 15 minutes or a call where they both read a page and say one line. Once a month, a future-memory page, smallest version a voice note in the past tense plus one practical step. Dated experience: the local bench, two Saturdays from now, tea from home, 15 minutes, coats allowed. Noticing date, the Sunday after the bench.

In the margin, labeled not now: the motel by the lake, a night class critique, a resolution spreadsheet, the rest of the unread book. Rowan says, "I am done pretending I want a journal practice that happens nightly." Vale says, "I am done pretending the December dinner is a moral duty." They write that on the page. The next chapter fits on one sheet.

## Afterward

Which discoveries came from hours you actually lived?
What did you drop, and did you drop it cleanly?
Do the practices have a hard-week version you believe?
What went to "not now," and can it stay there without a guilt tax?

You can leave a season with two lines and a margin. The margin is how the next chapter stays yours.

## Our Story

Date the session. Note the version, the place, and whether you finished or paused. Initials and mood are optional. One page: the practices, the smallest versions, the dated experience if you chose one, and the noticing date. The margin is allowed to be long.

The two personal pages are yours. Share them, keep them, or leave them blank. Unread is a complete way to use them. Vulnerable discoveries stay off the shared page unless the speaker wants them written. The shared notes are the part you write together.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- A discovery from this season that you want carried forward.
- The smallest version of a practice on a hard week.
partner_b:
- A practice, or a future experience, you want designed in from your side of the planning.
- What belongs in the margin labeled not now.
shared:
- The next chapter as you mean it, including what you are done pretending to want.
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- Are you in that chapter, or did life hand you a different one?
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```

## Notes

Retreats and workbooks often end with goal setting. That custom is not a validated protocol, and this landing claims no clinical standing for the book. The look-back stays with experiences actually done. At most two recurring practices and one dated experience, each with a smallest version. "No new practice; we are keeping one old kindness" is a complete result. 094 may be chosen here as a yearly invitation. It is not installed by default, and the chapters after this one are not assigned. A next chapter may be quiet.
