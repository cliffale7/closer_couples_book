## The hook

Every relationship arrives with a suitcase it did not pack. Who apologizes first. How a holiday is supposed to look. Whether an evening alone needs a defense. What counts as a date. The suitcase comes from families, religions, movies, apps, and old relationships, and a lot of it is useful. Some of it is only heavy.

The Relationship We Would Invent is a draft you make on purpose. First you set the borrowed assumptions where you can see them, and where you may decline them. Then you write a few living agreements in plain behavior. Then you try one tiny ritual for two weeks. Starting from scratch includes the freedom to keep a familiar form because you want it, including a form one of you needs for culture, disability, or simple love of the old way.

## Why it might matter

Unnamed defaults feel like nature. When they clash, people argue as if the other person invented selfishness, when both of you may be reciting a script with the label worn off. Putting the script on a card does not make it a villain. It makes it a choice. Chosen is a different feeling from inherited, even when the behavior stays the same.

This is a craft conversation about the relationship you are already in. You design for the channel you actually have: the kitchen, the call, the week with low money and low energy. A beautiful agreement that only works on vacation is a poster.

104, The Two-Person Civilization, builds a fictional society, with founding principles and random events that press on them. That game can rhyme with a real life, and it stays inside the fiction. Here the agreements have to be something both of you can picture this month, in your real week. 095, A Promise I Actually Want to Keep, is a later kind of sentence, small and followable, said by the person who will do it. Tonight's draft is revisable on purpose. It is a design, and it can be retired without a moral.

No study is claimed for inventing a relationship this way. Two people talking carefully is the method.

## Before you begin

Give yourselves a protected stretch. Phones elsewhere. If you are designing across distance, use the channel you actually share, and write where both of you can see the words.

Either person may mark a topic "not tonight": sex, money, children, family contact, or public identity. Those can be named as parked. They are not smuggled into a greeting ritual.

Agreements that police a body, a phone, or a friendship are out. So is a utopia document. You are drafting four behaviors and one small ritual, not a constitution for a better species.

A partner who wants to keep a family ritual, a religious practice, or an access need says so early. Retaining it, with both of you clear, counts as designing. Discarding is one freedom. Keeping is another.

## The experience

Three rounds. Write as you go, on one page, so the borrowed cards stay visible beside the new lines.

Round 1, about 15 minutes. Each person names two assumptions they imported. Sources you might recognize: family, religion, culture, an app, an old relationship, a friend group that seemed to have it figured out. Examples of scale: who apologizes first, how holidays run, whether solitude needs an excuse, what a date is required to include. Put each one on a borrowed card, in a sentence a stranger could follow. Read them aloud. The listener's job is to understand the import, not to prosecute the family that handed it over. You may decline a card, keep it, or mark it "examine." Decline means it is not your default anymore. It does not mean the person who grew up with it was doing relationship wrong.

Round 2, about 25 minutes. Draft four living agreements in plain language.

How you greet each other after time apart.
How you repair a small miss.
How you celebrate.
How you protect solitude.

Each agreement is a behavior both of you can picture this month. "We honor each other's autonomy" is a value, and it is not yet an agreement. Translate it. "When one of us closes the office door, the other waits for the door to open, unless the building is actually on fire" is an agreement. If you cannot picture it during illness, low money, and low energy, write a smaller version underneath. At least one of the four should survive a hard week. Long-distance couples write for the call, the voice note, or the letter they really use.

Read the four. Either of you may refuse a line. A refusal is recorded in a few words, so the draft does not quietly reinstall the refused idea. Abstract warmth does not overrule a no.

Round 3, about 10 minutes. Choose one ritual to try for two weeks. Keep it tiny. Tea poured on Sunday. A three-line note after a night apart. A 10-minute walk that becomes standing at a window if walking is too much. Set a review date. The review asks what you lived by, and what looked better on the page than in the week. The ritual does not receive a forever stamp tonight.

Stop. Further ideas go in a margin labeled "later." A draft that becomes a second job has left the chapter.

## Three ways to play

**25 minutes.** One borrowed assumption each, one agreement you can picture this week, and one tiny ritual with a review date. Name the assumption out loud so "from scratch" still has something to push against. A thin draft is a finished session if the borrowed card is real.

**55 minutes.** The three rounds as written: two borrowed cards each, four agreements, one ritual, a review date. This is the version that keeps the defaults visible beside the choices.

**75 minutes.** Read the cards and the agreements back on another pass, slowly, and test each agreement against a hard week: sick, broke, traveling, or just finished with people. Revise the line that fails the test. Still four agreements. The extra time is for honesty, not for a fifth category.

## Going deeper

On a later sitting, take one card you marked "examine" and live with the question for a week before you draft again. Some imports only show their shape on a Tuesday. A holiday rule, for example, can look sweet in the abstract and expensive in December.

You can also design a ritual that belongs to one person and is witnessed by the other. Solitude, prayer, a game, a nap with the door shut. Shared life includes unshared practices. The agreement is how the witness behaves, which is often "do not turn it into a group project."

If a cultural or religious form matters to one of you, describe what it gives, in concrete terms, before anyone proposes a replacement. Replacements are optional. Understanding is the first draft.

## Variations and replay

Speech or writing both work. If one of you thinks better on paper, write the cards first and talk second. No purchases. The motif is a simple floor plan with one old postcard pinned in the margin, still visible: `assets/chapters/089/motif.svg`.

032, The Everyday Devotion Experiment, tries a small act of care in the life you already have. 095 is where a sentence becomes a promise the speaker wants to keep, with a cue and a follow-through. 104 is the civilization game, event cards included, played as fiction. Come back to this chapter when a default starts running the week again, or when the two-week ritual is ready for its review. Date the page. A line you drop is part of the record, not a failure of imagination.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale are an illustration, not a portrait of you.

Rowan's borrowed cards: "In my family, the person who is less upset apologizes first." And: "A proper date involves leaving the house and spending money."

Vale's: "Solitude needs a reason, or it looks like withdrawal." And: "Holidays mean the largest available table, even if the cook is miserable."

They decline the apology rule. Rowan says it trained them to perform calm in order to be the one who repairs. They keep a rewritten date card, on purpose: a date can be a walk or a desk picnic, and a paid outing is a treat, not a test. Vale's solitude card is marked "examine." The holiday card is declined for this household. Vale still wants one loud meal a year with their people, hosted by someone who likes hosting, which might be a restaurant or a sibling.

The four agreements, in language they can picture:

After time apart, they say one true thing about the day before they open mail or a feed.
A small miss gets a specific sentence within a day: what happened, and one repair that is actually available. Grand apologies can wait.
A celebration can be a cake from the shop and a song sung badly. Achievement is named out loud.
Solitude: a closed door means the person inside is not available for logistics. A text that says "door" counts when they are apart. Emergencies are named as emergencies.

The two-week ritual: on Sunday, one of them boils water and the other brings two mugs. If someone is ill, the ritual shrinks to a message that says the kettle is imaginary and the greeting is real. Review in fourteen days. They do not add a clause about phones, sex, or families. Those are marked "not tonight," on the same page, so the silence is visible.

## Afterward

Which borrowed card surprised you by still running?
Which agreement can you picture on a tired Wednesday?
What did you keep on purpose, and what did you decline?
Did the ritual stay small enough to survive a bad week?

The draft is allowed to be modest. A relationship you would invent can look a lot like care you already know how to do, chosen with your eyes open.

## Our Story

Date the session. Note the version, the place, and whether you finished or paused. Initials and mood are optional. Keep the borrowed cards on the page with the agreements, so the defaults remain visible.

The two personal pages are yours. Share them, keep them, or leave them blank. Unread is a complete way to use them. The shared notes are the part you write together.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused

- A ritual, expectation, or rule you would design from scratch, and a borrowed assumption you would drop.
- The tiny ritual you are willing to try for two weeks, including its low-energy version.

- An agreement you refused or parked, and why it can wait.

- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything

- What you lived by, and what looked better on the page than in the week.
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```

## Notes

Reimagined from the ordinary fact that couples inherit rituals and expectations, then sometimes design agreements on purpose. That design is a common therapeutic and secular habit. It is not a validated protocol, and no empirical claim is made. Traditional and cultural forms are available to keep on purpose. The draft assumes nothing about marriage, monogamy, shared finances, or children. Agreements are revisable. They are a craft conversation between these two people.
