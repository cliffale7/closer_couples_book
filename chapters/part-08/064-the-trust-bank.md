## The hook

Someone texted when the train ran late. Someone forgot to pass on a message from the dentist. Neither moment was a summit or a betrayal. Over a few ordinary days, trust shifts on pieces that small: a kept word, a check-in, a joke that cut, an interruption that happened twice. This chapter asks you to notice a few of those pieces, in both directions, and then to stop.

The bank is a metaphor for attention. Clinical teaching, in Gottman's work, popularized an "emotional bank account" beside love maps, bids, and repair. This chapter borrows the image of deposits and withdrawals and refuses the arithmetic. There is no balance, no interest, and no letter that declares anyone overdrawn. You will each note up to three deposits and three withdrawals. Then you close the notebook. Rest is not a withdrawal. A nap is not a debt. Low energy is not a spend.

You are watching everyday trust between the two of you. You are not building a case, and you are not watching anyone in secret.

## Why it might matter

Memory keeps the loud moments and misfiles the quiet ones. A cutting "obviously" can occupy a whole evening, while the text that said "train is late, home at 6:40" disappears into the category of basic competence. Basic competence is one of the ways trust actually accumulates. So is remembering the oat milk. So is sitting through a story without upgrading it into advice. If you only discuss trust when it breaks, you will know your injuries better than your ordinary care.

The cap of three is the method. A list that long can be held in one conversation. A longer list becomes a dossier, and the mood shifts from noticing to prosecution. Both directions matter. The useful talk is the pattern: what each of you treats as trust, and where you disagree about the size of a gesture.

No empirical citation is claimed for a trust ledger. The few-day practice is this book's design. The metaphor arrives from practitioner teaching with no study, quotation, or divorce statistic attached, and it has not been tested as a treatment.

The Repair Shop (060) is the chapter for one cooled misstep that needs acknowledgment, one's own part, and a fitting amend. You can notice a withdrawal here without opening that shop. The Appreciation Avalanche (026) is a round of specific appreciations that does not ask you to log misses. The Appreciation Behind the Complaint (063) turns one irritation into a wish and a request. Stay here with a short run of small moments, both kinds, and no repair required.

## Before you begin

Agree on the window before you start noting. The standard window is three days. The extended window is five. The short version skips the live window and recalls the last day or two, with the same caps. Pick one and write the end time, including when you will sit down together. An open-ended log will eat the month.

Each person keeps their notes privately until the review. You do not track a phone, a location, a browser, or a set of clues. Hidden monitoring is outside this chapter. If a note required secrecy to obtain, it does not qualify as an everyday observation. Write what either of you could have seen: a text sent, a promise kept or dropped, a tone, a practical help, an interruption.

Up to three deposits and three withdrawals. When you hit the cap, you stop, even if a fourth example is charming or infuriating. The cap is the ethics of the exercise.

Fair deposits include a kept word, a check-in, a small repair, a reliable text, practical help during pain, and a sensory accommodation you asked for and received. Fair withdrawals include a forgotten promise, a cutting joke, and a repeated interruption. Unfair withdrawals include rest, pain, illness, and a day of low energy. Say that to each other before you begin, in a sentence you can repeat if the notes drift: "Resting is not a withdrawal."

Leave sex, money, and chore tallies out of the notes. A kept word can be a deposit. A smaller share of the dishes on a tired day is not a debt.

Either person may decline, at the review, to discuss a note that feels like an ambush. "I do not want that one in the room" ends that item. The rest of the notes can still be talked about.

## The experience

**During the days.** Once a day is enough. At a calm moment, jot what you noticed since the last jot. A deposit is a small action that made ordinary trust easier to feel. A withdrawal is a small action that spent it. Write the observable piece, not the essay.

"Texted when the train was late."
"Remembered oat milk without being asked."
"Listened to the work story without fixing it."
"Said 'obviously' in a tone that cut."
"Forgot to pass on the dentist message."
"Talked over the ending of my story, twice."

If you are unsure, leave it off. The chapter rewards a clean small set, not a complete history. If the day was mostly rest, you may write "rest, not a withdrawal" and add nothing else. An empty day is an allowed day.

**At the review.** Sit down as scheduled. Each person shares up to three deposits and up to three withdrawals. Read them as observations. The listener may ask one clarifying question per item and may say whether it landed the same way from their side. "I did not know the oat milk mattered. I am glad it did." "I remember the 'obviously.' I was tired, and the cut still landed." Context can be one sentence. A defense speech means the item has swollen past this chapter. Shrink it or set it aside.

Look for patterns, lightly. Maybe the deposits you feel are mostly practical, and the deposits your partner feels are mostly "you stayed for the story." Maybe the withdrawals cluster around hurry at the door. Name the pattern in one sentence. Do not convert it into a balance. There is nothing to sum.

**One deposit to repeat on purpose.** Each person chooses one. "I will send the late-train text, including a time, when it happens." "I will let the work story end before I offer an idea." The choice is yours to repeat. You do not assign your partner's deposit. If you want to request something, that is chapter 063, on another day, with a wish and a no available. Here you only volunteer a repeat.

Then close the notes. You may keep them in the private journal. You may throw them out. They are a poor exhibit. They are not evidence in any later argument, and this chapter asks you not to retrieve them as one.

## Three ways to play

**Short, 20 minutes.** Skip the live multi-day window. Each of you recalls the last day or two, deposits first, because memory favors the loud withdrawal. Up to three and three. Share, name one deposit to repeat, and stop. Say aloud that resting is not a withdrawal, so the short version does not forget the rule it is most tempted to skip. This version is thinner because it relies on memory. It still notices both directions.

**Standard, three days of notes, then 30 minutes.** Private jots under the cap, then the review as written. One clarifying question per item at most. One deposit each to repeat. Close the notebook in the room, so closing is a shared act.

**Extended, five days of notes, then 45 minutes.** The same caps, including when a fourth example starts campaigning. Use the longer review for one pattern, and strike any note that charged someone for pain, rest, or low energy. Still no totals.

## Going deeper

If the review stays kind, you may each add one deposit the other person might have thought was too small to count. "You moved the lamp because the glare was on my page. I felt that for an hour." Small is the point. Trust often lives at that size, and people apologize for mentioning it. Mention it.

You may also name a withdrawal you own, before your partner has to. "I am putting my own interruption on the list." This chapter still requires no repair. If that item wants the full shop, schedule chapter 060 for another day. Most evenings, noticing is enough.

Stop before anyone ranks the items. The first time you say "that one counts double," you have invented interest, and the metaphor has started lying.

## Variations and replay

Speech and a short written log are equal. At a distance, trust is often follow-through on calls and plans. A deposit may be "started the call at the time we named." A withdrawal may be "moved the call with no new time." Reliable texts and a word when you will be late belong in the deposit column when they are what makes the distance feel held. Practical help during pain counts, including help given by staying off the call so the other person can sleep. That last one is easy to misfile as absence. Ask before you file it.

Replay a month later with a fresh window and a fresh cap. The question worth keeping is what you want to repeat on purpose.

If a review feels like an ambush, end it. You may have collected notes in good faith and still have aimed them like a brief. Name that, scrap the prosecution items, and try again another month with the cap and the privacy rule said out loud at the start.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. Their three days are a sample of scale, and they are not your books.

Rowan's deposits: Vale texted "train late, home 6:40" on Monday. Vale bought oat milk without a reminder. Vale listened to a boring story about a meeting and did not offer a five-point plan.

Rowan's withdrawals: Vale said "obviously" when Rowan asked where the stamps were, and the tone cut. Vale forgot to pass on a dentist message. Rowan also writes, and then strikes, "Vale napped on Sunday." The strike is the point. The nap stays off the list. Rowan says, in the review, "I almost charged you for resting. I am glad the rule was already in the room."

Vale's deposits: Rowan sat on the floor and helped with a pain flare by bringing the heating pad and then being quiet. Rowan kept a promise to call the pharmacy. Rowan waited out the end of Vale's sentence on Wednesday.

Vale's withdrawals: Rowan talked over the ending of a story twice on Tuesday. Rowan joked about Vale's pace on the walk, once, and it stung. Vale does not list the days Rowan did fewer chores. Vale names that omission so it is visible as a choice.

They do not add the columns. They notice a pattern: Rowan's felt deposits are often "you told me what was happening," and Vale's are often "you helped and then you were quiet." Each picks one deposit to repeat. Rowan will send a late-arrival text with a time. Vale will let one story end before any idea. They close the notes without a total.

## Afterward

Do something ordinary and unserious. The review should not become the mood of the night.

If you want questions:

- Which deposit was so small you almost failed to count it?
- Which withdrawal is an observation, and which one almost became a case?
- Did rest, pain, or low energy try to sneak onto the list?
- What one deposit will you repeat because you choose to?

Use this chapter when you are willing to see both directions and to stop at three.

## Our Story

The private jots can stay private. Private memoir pages are optional to share. You may bring to the shared page only the pattern and the deposit you intend to repeat, and leave the raw notes in your own keeping. Say that once, and mean it. These notes are a record for the two of you. They are not evidence.

Date the window, the version, and whether you finished or paused.
