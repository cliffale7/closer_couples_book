## The hook

You can live with someone a long time and still be handed a title you have never heard. A shop they visit alone. A job they have not described. A Sunday feeling with two weathers in it. The title is a door. It may be a door you did not know existed. That is an offer, not a tease that has to be satisfied.

You may ask for a key. You may not demand one. The person who offered the door decides which key, if any, gets handed over, and how far the sentence goes. A key already given can be put back down mid-sentence. The other person does not finish it.

## Why it might matter

Shared life builds a map, and maps go out of date quietly. New rooms appear. Old rooms get renamed. The surprise of a title you have never heard is already a kind of news. News is not a debt. If every unknown door had to be opened because love was in the room, privacy would be a failure. It is a successful outcome of this hour.

The mechanism is revocable depth. The partner may ask. The sharer chooses the key, refuses the key, or ends the tour. Asking by name, Brass or Glass or nothing, gives the request a shape, so "tell me everything" has somewhere specific to land and somewhere specific to be declined. A listener who can tolerate a closed door stays interested. The rest is not owed.

This hour was formulated for this project. That is not a claim of worldwide novelty. Leave therapy, shadow work, and trauma-method language off it. A door offered once may be declined the next time. Love does not mean every door eventually opens.

## Before you begin

Each person prepares up to four doors. The front of a card shows a title only. The back stays blank, or holds notes only the sharer will see. Themes you might use: a cherished memory, a fear, an unrealized ambition, a private desire, a paradox, or something hard to explain. Include at least one lighter door if the others are heavy. A stack of only the hardest rooms wears the evening out.

Titles can be new to your partner. That is the twist, and it is allowed to remain a title.

Optional physical cards. No dark room, and no locked physical space. The door is verbal. If symbolic play feels silly or wrong, use the word "topic" instead of "door" and "level" instead of "key." The control rule does not change.

Keys the sharer may offer or refuse:

Brass. The facts of what happened.
Glass. How it felt.
Iron. What it costs to say more.
Ribbon. What I hope you do with this, which can be "nothing."
Blank. The door exists, and the tour stops.

Three agreements:

The partner may request a key by name. Silence, and "not that key," are complete answers.
A demanded key ends the round. Wheedling after a refusal ends it too.
No one earns access by being a good partner. You do not unlock a room as payment for listening well.

Sexual doors require a prior willingness to have that kind of conversation at all. A key is not consent to touch or sex. Desires and fears can be named at Brass and stopped there. A Ribbon key is a hope, not a contract.

Ongoing danger, abuse, or a crisis is not game content. Those need a direct conversation, and outside help when someone is unsafe.

You may pass, pause, or put a card away before it is read. Stop on fatigue.

## The experience

Trade stacks, or keep your own cards and read titles one at a time. The sharer reads a title. They may add, "This is a door you have not heard of," or they may let the newness be obvious. They do not apologize for the newness, and they do not promise a tour.

The partner may request one key by name. "Brass, if you're offering." "Glass?" "Ribbon, and I can hear 'nothing.'" The sharer answers with the key, with "not that key," or with silence. If they offer it, they speak only as far as that key goes.

Brass sounds like facts. "A shop on 4th. I go alone. About 20 minutes. I buy nothing."
Glass sounds like weather inside the facts. "I like the paper smell. I don't want to explain my week."
Iron sounds like the price of continuing. "Saying the next part would wreck the afternoon, so I am stopping."
Ribbon sounds like a hope with the contract taken out. "Enjoy knowing the shop exists. Don't suggest we go. That is a hope, not a rule."
Blank sounds like a title and a stop. "The door is real. The tour is over."

A key already offered can be set down mid-sentence. The partner does not supply the ending, the diagnosis, or the clever interpretation. Clinical language stays outside. If you hear yourself explaining what the room "really means," you have left the game. Return the sentence to its owner.

Then the next door, alternating, until you have each shown the titles you meant to show. You do not have to offer a key for every title. Two doors and one key, total, is a complete short path.

Debrief, short, and not a second attempt on a refused key:

Which door surprised you by existing?
Which closed door can you respect with no story attached?
Is there anything that needs a conversation outside the game?

The partner does not keep notes on a closed door. Journal titles only, unless the sharer wants a sentence kept. A blank key is not a puzzle to solve later.

## Three ways to play

**30 minutes.** Two doors each. One key offered somewhere in the round, or none, if that is the true offer. Read the debrief questions and stop. Use this when four doors would become a confession marathon.

**50 minutes.** Up to four doors each, titles first, keys by request. Time for at least one refusal to stand without a follow-up question. This is the version that lets a new door be news and lets an old door stay locked in the same evening.

**75 minutes.** The standard round, with a few extra minutes of quiet before the debrief so each person can decide what, if anything, belongs on paper. The extra time is for choice. It is not a second pass at a key that was refused. If you are tired at 50 minutes, the 50-minute version was the right one. Stop.

## Going deeper

On a later day, you may return to a door that was offered at Brass and ask whether Glass is available now. The answer can be no. A no this week does not cancel a yes from last month, and a yes last month does not create a standing ticket.

You can label a door more clearly without opening it. "The paradox about Sundays" can become "Sundays, still a paradox," and the tour can remain Blank. Better labeled is a real change.

If the debrief surfaced something that needs a conversation outside the game, schedule that conversation in ordinary language. Do not smuggle it back in as a bonus key.

## Variations and replay

By video, each person holds their own cards, backs unseen. By voice, titles are spoken. Cards can be large print. Seated the whole time.

People who want no metaphor still use the list: topic, then the level they are willing to give, facts, feeling, cost, hope, or stop. Write the five level names on a card if the metal and ribbon are in the way.

The motif is a door and several keys, one of them blank, none of them in the other person's hand.

Replay when a new room becomes nameable, or when an old title needs a more careful lock. Date the titles you are willing to keep. A door that was open in one sitting may be declined in the next, and the journal should be allowed to show that without apology.

013, Confession Without Consequence, offers one chosen embarrassment, regret, insecurity, or harmless private truth for curious listening. The frame is receptive listening. It is not immunity for harmful behavior. This chapter is a tour of several possible rooms, with a pass on any door, and it can include a door the other person did not know was there. 011, What I Don’t Show the World, describes parts you hide and what would make them safer to reveal. 081, The Forbidden Question Box, lets either of you pass on a drawn question. Here the sharer controls the keys for the whole round, including doors they themselves opened.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale are an illustration, not a portrait of you.

Rowan lays down four titles: "The map store on 4th," "The version of my job I have not said out loud," "A fear with a boring name," "Something I want that I have not scheduled." Vale has never heard of the map store. That is the news.

Vale asks for Brass. Rowan gives facts. A shop, 20 minutes, alone, nothing purchased, the smell of paper. Vale asks for Glass. Rowan says, "Not that key." Vale asks for Ribbon. Rowan says, "Nothing. Enjoy knowing the shop exists. Don't suggest we go." The job door gets Blank. Vale does not guess the job. The fear gets Brass only: "I get sharp about being late, and the sharpness is embarrassment, not a verdict on you." Iron is refused. The unscheduled want stays a title.

Vale's door: "The paradox about Sundays." Vale offers Glass without a full Brass tour. "Sundays feel like a gift and like a clock. I don't want to explain the week that made them that way tonight." Rowan asks for Iron. "Not that key." Ribbon: "If you make a plan on Sunday morning, ask first. That is a hope, not a rule I am installing." A second Vale title is read and then given Blank. Rowan does not fish.

In the debrief, the map store is the door that surprised Vale by existing. The blank job title is the closed door Vale can respect with no story. Nothing needs a meeting outside the game. Rowan does not write Vale's closed title in a notebook. Vale writes "map store" only because Rowan said the title could be kept.

## Afterward

The debrief above is the whole ending. If you want one more pass, use these, and then leave the keys alone:

Which title was news?
Which refusal are you willing to leave intact?
Did anyone start to earn a key by being good, patient, or hurt? If yes, put that key back.
Is a Ribbon hope being mistaken for a contract?
Do you need a conversation that is not a game?

You do not owe each other the house. A title, a refusal, and a thank-you are a complete visit.

## Our Story

Date the session. Note the version, the place, and whether you finished or paused. Initials and mood are optional.

The two personal pages are yours. Share them, keep them, or leave them blank. Unread is a complete way to use them. The key-holder decides the depth, including on the page. A lock that stays locked is a finished choice. Do not keep notes on a door the other person closed.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- The door you offered, the key you were willing to give, and how far you wanted the exploration to go. All three stay in your control.
- A title you are willing to keep on paper. Leave the room behind the title blank if you want.
partner_b:
- The door you offered from your inner life, with the same control. A lock that stays locked is a finished choice.
- Which key you gave, which you refused, and the fact that a refusal needs no sequel.
shared:
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- Which doors you opened together, and which keys were declined with the decline left intact
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
- Is a given door more open, more carefully locked, or simply better labeled?
```
