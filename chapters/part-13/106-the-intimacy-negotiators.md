## The hook

You each receive exactly 100 points and a blank evening. The points make appetite visible, including the zeros and the categories you would rather rename. They expire when you stand up. They are a toy for seeing what you feel like spending. They are not a bill, a debt, an entitlement, or consent.

The evening is the real object. You design one that both of you can picture, from wishes that survived the cross-outs, with every piece still free to be declined when the night actually arrives. A chart with no evening abandons the chapter. An evening that overrides a limit because a number was higher abandons it too.

## Why it might matter

A fixed sum forces a preference into the open. You cannot give every form of closeness a perfect score. That would teach you nothing. What you spend, what you leave at zero, and what you leave unspent are three different sentences. Being shown someone's spending is different from being praised, and different from being assigned their evening.

People sometimes want a chart like this to prove a single main way of loving, sorted into exactly five types, with matching as the thing that makes a relationship work. A review of that popular claim found little support for one primary language, for exactly five, or for matching as what predicts satisfaction. A range of affectionate behavior seems to matter instead. See I2024 in the notes. This sheet will not sort you into a type. A balanced chart is not healthier than a lopsided one. Two charts that fail to match are a conversation, not a diagnosis.

Wanted affectionate touch has its own literature, a review and a model, not a trial of point allocation. See J2017. Unwanted touch sits outside that claim. A high number beside any form of touch does not create a duty or a permission. A game result is not consent to touch or to sex. A change of mind after the chart is shared outranks the chart. A headache outranks the chart. A zero outranks a speech about the zero.

## Before you begin

You need two copies of `journal/templates/hundred-points.md`, or a careful handwritten twin with the same number of rows and the same spacing. Baselines are at least 0.32 inch apart. Both columns have eight rows. Leave a row blank if you want. Put a form on a private copy if you do not want it on the shared page. Either person may refuse the whole exercise. Refusal finishes the chapter.

Sit apart for the first pass, even if apart is two ends of the same table with the papers angled away. No commentary while the numbers are being placed. Phones down. The evening you design has to have a version that is low-pain, low-cost, and at home. Sex and adventure are ordinary things to cross out. Long-distance, the evening may be a call plus a solitary pleasure each person actually wants. No one has to disclose why a category is zero.

Say these once:

You have exactly 100 points. Every point goes on a form or sits in the remainder. Forms plus remainder equal 100. You do not exceed 100.
Integers. Zeros allowed. A zero describes this season or this evening. It is not a permanent identity.
Points never outvote a limit.
Crossing out is unilateral and sufficient. A reason is optional.
You will not average the charts. The larger number is not the winner.
Renaming a category in order to dissolve the other person's no is a foul. Restore the no.

## The experience

**The default menu, which you may change.** Affection, romance, playfulness, meaningful conversation, sensuality, adventure, closeness, quiet, spontaneity, privacy. That is ten names for a sheet with eight rows. You choose what to write down. You may rename up to three. If you both want the distinction, split sensuality so sexual closeness has its own name, and drop a row you are leaving at zero or add a line at the same spacing. Privacy may be renamed solitude when you mean kindly separate time. Words you may ignore, from the sheet itself: a long talk, a walk, cooking, sitting quietly, playful nonsense, affectionate touch. Use them, replace them, or leave them off.

If you want a wholly different menu, agree the new names before you score, or let each person name their own eight and spend the comparison on what the words were doing. You are not required to share a vocabulary. You are required to hear a limit.

**Filling the sheet.** Alone, place the points. The sum line reads 100, or less if you left some unspent. Unspent is a choice. Write the remainder so the arithmetic is honest: the points you were given are still exactly 100.

Read your own chart aloud. The listener asks one curious question per category they do not understand, and then stops. The listener does not say what the other person should have scored.

**Circles and crosses.** On your own chart, circle up to three forms you would enjoy tonight. Cross out any that are off the table. A zero may be crossed out or simply left. Crossing out needs no explanation. A form you do not want named on the shared page can live on the private copy, and the cross-out can be spoken as "that one is off the table" without the name, if that is the privacy you need. If the designer of the evening cannot see the limit, say the limit in whatever words you can stand. A hidden no that gets planned over has not been honored.

**The menu.** Lay the charts side by side. Any form either person crossed out leaves the menu, even if the other person scored it high and circled it. What remains is available. Offers are volunteered. A circle is an invitation to consider. It is not a claim. From that menu, design one evening both of you can picture. Write the shape on the sheet, including rest, food, talk, or an early night. Name one thing you are willing to drop without argument.

If every circled form was crossed out by the other person, or every offer is declined, the result is parallel quiet or a rain check. That completes the chapter. Parallel quiet means you share a place, or a call, without turning the hour into a project. A rain check means you do not schedule the crossed-out forms as a debt.

**The evening rule, in one breath.** Use only forms nobody crossed out. Build from volunteered offers. Include a version that fits the rooms, the money, and the bodies you have tonight. Every piece can be declined in the moment. The points expire at the end of the conversation. Tomorrow's want can be different, and it does not owe this sheet an explanation.

**Worksheet logic, field by field.** The sheet is `journal/templates/hundred-points.md`, activity 106.

Date and attempt at the top.

Partner A, "my 100," eight rows: a form, then points. Then a sum, 100 or less if some were left unspent.

Partner B, the same eight rows, the same spacing, the same size of sum. Equal lines. Neither column is a correction of the other.

"The evening we actually want to try" is eight writing lines. Every piece optional. Write the shape, including rest, food, talk, or an early night.

The last line is what you are willing to drop without argument.

A private copy follows the same logic if a form should stay off the shared page. The shared evening still has to respect a cross-out that was spoken.

Close by reading the evening aloud once. Each person says "yes, with these pieces" or "rain check" or "parallel quiet." A yes to the shape is still not a yes to every gesture inside it when the hour comes.

## Three ways to play

**30 minutes.** Fill the sheets, read them, cross out, and write a one-sentence evening or an honest rain check. Skip the curious questions except where a word is genuinely opaque. A short page is a finished negotiation.

**45 minutes.** The full pass: private scoring, one question per unclear form, circles, crosses, and an evening written in enough detail that you could start it tonight. Include the thing you will drop without argument.

**60 minutes.** Add a second look after the evening is drafted. Each person may move up to 10 points on their own sheet, once, in light of what they heard, without erasing a zero they meant. Then check the evening again. A moved point does not reopen a cross-out. If the second look turns into lobbying, return to the first sheets and keep the first evening.

## Going deeper

On another night, spend the 100 points on a different horizon: a month, or a season, rather than tonight. Keep the same rule. The number is information. The plan, if you make one, still cannot spend a no.

You may each add one form the default menu missed, in your own words, and give it points by taking them from somewhere else. The new form can be crossed out by the other person. Invention does not smuggle a demand.

If the overlap is rich and you are both glad, you may sketch a second evening and then choose one. Two sketches are play. Neither sketch is owed.

## Variations and replay

Low energy: the evening is soup, a shared silence, and an early night, if those forms survived. If they did not, parallel quiet is the variation, and it counts.

Low pain: stay horizontal, shorten the talk, drop anything that asks for stamina. Adventure and sexual closeness are easy to cross out, and a high score beside them stays on the paper.

Long-distance: design the call, its length, and one solitary pleasure each of you actually wants during the same evening. The solitary pleasure can be privacy or quiet. It can also be crossed out if separate time is the wrong gift tonight.

Budget: the home version is the default. If a form seems to require tickets, write the version that happens in the kitchen.

Replay when the season changes, or when a zero moves. Date the sheet. Old points expire. They are not a constitution. [025](025), What Loving Me Actually Looks Like, names behaviors that land as love. [040](040), The Intimacy Blueprint, maps conditions. This chapter spends a toy sum and then builds one evening inside the limits.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale are an illustration, not a portrait of you.

Rowan spends exactly 100: affection 15, romance 10, playfulness 20, meaningful conversation 15, sensuality 0, adventure 0, closeness 10, quiet 30. Rowan renames closeness, on the sheet, to "being in the same room without a job."

Vale spends exactly 100: affection 10, romance 20, playfulness 15, meaningful conversation 5, sensuality 20, adventure 15, quiet 5, privacy 10. Vale renames privacy to "an hour with the door closed."

Rowan asks one question: "Door closed means the apartment, or a room?" Vale says, "A room. The apartment can stay alive." Vale does not tell Rowan to move the 30 on quiet. Rowan does not tell Vale that 15 on adventure is too many.

Rowan circles quiet, playfulness, and meaningful conversation, and crosses out adventure and sensuality. Vale circles romance, playfulness, and sensuality, and crosses out nothing. Sensuality and adventure leave the menu because Rowan crossed them out. Vale's 20 and Vale's 15 do not buy them back.

The evening they write: cook side by side, which they are both willing to call playfulness; 20 minutes of one real conversation; then Rowan reads in the room while Vale does a crossword, which Rowan calls quiet and Vale can live with. Vale offers to switch on the lamp Rowan likes. Rowan accepts the lamp and declines anything further. Early night. They are willing to drop the crossword without argument if the conversation runs long.

On another fictional night the circles fail to overlap. Vale has circled only sensuality and adventure, and Rowan has crossed both out. Neither person volunteers a remaining form. They call a rain check, sit in the same room with separate books, and the chapter is complete. The points are not collected later as a debt.

## Afterward

What did your own zeros say that a speech would have padded?
Where did you want the larger number to win, and did you let the cross-out stand?
What did you design that both of you can picture, including the piece you can drop?
Did the evening ask for a body, a budget, or a bravery you do not have tonight? Rewrite that piece before you start.
What expires with the conversation, on purpose?

You do not owe each other the evening you designed. You can start it, change it, or let it become quiet.

## Our Story

Date the attempt. Note whether you designed an evening, called a rain check, or sat in parallel quiet. The two personal pages are yours. Share them, keep them, or leave them blank. Unread is a complete way to use them. The hundred-points sheet is the working copy. These prompts are the record. Printed lines are at least eight per prompt, baselines at least 0.32 inch apart. A category, a zero, or a change of mind is a complete entry. Nothing on the page is a promise to act.

session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- How you spent your 100 points across forms of closeness you named, and what the totals were trying to say.
- A form you crossed out. The reason can stay off the page.
partner_b:
- How you spent yours, and which of the other forms you do not share.
- The piece of the evening you were willing to drop without argument.

- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- The evening you designed together. Every piece can be declined. The points are not a bill.

- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
- What you actually did with the evening, and whether you would spend the points differently.

## Notes

That is a provenance note. It is not a claim of worldwide novelty, and it is not a test of desire.
