## The hook

A list of only summits is a press release. The experiences worth naming before you die include the repeated Tuesday pleasure and the trip you may never afford, the conversation, the skill, the nearby street, the mountain if you want a mountain. This list is personal. Your partner witnesses it. They may offer help with one item. They leave at least one item entirely yours. "Someday, and maybe never" still counts as worth saying.

Hearing a wish is not a budget, a safety waiver, or a joint plan.

## Why it might matter

The popular custom of a bucket list drifted toward the spectacular, which is a shame, because spectacle is a small slice of what people actually hope to meet. A last-meal conversation, a song learned badly, a tomato sandwich in a specific month, a train window. Those items tell the truth about appetite. Grand items tell a truth too, including the truth that some hopes are allowed to stay unlikely.

The mechanism protects the list from becoming a couple's contract. You draft your own. At least half the items are ordinary or cheap. Grand items are labeled grand, so they do not quietly set the standard for what counts. You share. Your partner marks one item they would truly like to help with, and one they will not take over. Then you sort with three marks: ordinary and soon, grand and maybe never, help welcome. The partner initials only the help-welcome items they mean. Warmth is not a pledge of money.

Items need not assume walking, travel, or a long future. From this chair, in this city, and this year are legitimate horizons. Illness and pain can shape a list around company, taste, and texture without anyone treating that shape as a smaller life. If mortality talk floods the speaker, the list gets shorter or the speaker passes. The partner does not push for more items in order to be brave on someone else's behalf.

No pressure attaches to sexual, parental, or legacy items. You may include them. You may keep them off the page. Private items can remain private and still be part of your real list, unperformed for the exercise.

## Before you begin

Paper, or large type, or speech if hands and eyes would rather not do the writing. You are making a personal list, not a shared itinerary. Say that once so the warmth of the conversation does not start signing checks.

You may pass the whole chapter, shorten the list, or keep any item unspoken. A short list is a finished list. A local list is a finished list. Nothing on it has to be impressive, photogenic, or survivable as a story at dinner.

If a recent death or a scary diagnosis has made this title feel like a trap, switch to ordinary pleasures only, or move to 050, which asks what already makes a life feel worthwhile, without the horizon of death. You can also simply close the book. The partner's job in that moment is to stop, not to coax one more dream into the open.

Agree on the help rule before the lists are read. An offer of help is specific and declineable. An item without an initial stays the speaker's. Dangerous wishes do not become plans because they were said beside someone you love.

## The experience

Draft at least eight items. At least four are ordinary or cheap: a meal, a skill, a conversation, a nearby place, a repeated pleasure. If eight is too many today, the short version below is the honest count. Do not pad with summits in order to look alive.

When you stall, use the prompts as a tray, not as extra obligations.

From this chair. A taste, a texture, a voice, a record, a person's company in the room you are already in.
In this city. A street, a kitchen, a body of water you can reach without turning the wish into a production.
This year. Something with a season attached, small enough that "soon" is not a fantasy of a different life.

Grand items are welcome. Mark them grand in the margin as you write, so the eye can see the proportion. A list that tips into expeditions gets one more ordinary item before it is shared, until the ordinary half is real.

Mark any item that is "someday, and maybe never." Those words are a respectful category, not a failure of nerve. Say them without a nervous laugh that asks your partner to talk you into hope.

Share the lists by reading them at human speed. The witness listens. The witness does not upgrade a sandwich into a metaphor, and does not downgrade a mountain into a joke about your knees, unless you have invited the joke. Questions are for understanding the item. "Is the train the point, or the being gone?" is fair. "We should book it" is a takeover.

After both lists are heard, each partner makes two marks on the other's page, and only two.

One item you would like to help with. Be concrete about the help. Company, a reminder, a ride, a search for the cheap version, sitting beside the attempt. If the help would cost money or risk, say the cost out loud and let it remain an offer, not a decision.

One item you will not take over. Choose it kindly. Often it should be the item that is most clearly theirs, the one your enthusiasm would dent. Say, "I am leaving this one entirely with you." That sentence is part of the gift.

Then the speaker sorts the finished list with three handwritten marks, or spoken ones if writing is a bad tool.

Ordinary and soon.
Grand and maybe never.
Help welcome.

An item can wear one mark or two, if that is true. Tuesday lunch can be ordinary and soon, and also help welcome if what you want is someone to make it with you once. A desert night can be grand and maybe never, and not help welcome, because help would turn it into a project with a deadline and a witness you did not order.

The partner may initial only the help-welcome items they truly mean. No initial, no obligation. Do not initial in the warmth and renegotiate in the morning. If you need a night to know whether you mean it, say that, and leave the blank.

Close by reading aloud one ordinary item and one maybe-never item, so the proportion stays in the air. Then stop. The list is not a planning meeting.

## Three ways to play

**20 minutes.** Four items each, at least two ordinary. Read them. Each partner names one help and one hands-off item, in a sentence. Skip the full three-mark sort if talk of dying has gotten loud. A shortened list is complete. The partner does not ask for the missing four.

**50 minutes.** Eight items, the ordinary half guarded, the grand items labeled, the two partner marks, and the three-way sort. This is the standard list.

**85 minutes.** Draft in silence a little longer. Include the prompts from this chair, in this city, and this year if any of those horizons were empty. After the sort, each person may tell the story of one item, why it has lasted, without turning the telling into a commitment. Still no funding votes. If you want a shared collection of adventures, that is 093, on a day when co-ownership is the actual point.

## Going deeper

Separate "I want to taste this once" from "I want this to become a rhythm." Both can sit on a list before death. They ask different things of a partner. A once-only meal is easy to crush with too much ceremony. A rhythm is easy to crush by helping so hard it becomes their project.

You may star one item that would still matter if your body or your budget stayed exactly as it is. That star keeps the list from depending on a miracle. The grand items remain grand and maybe never.

090, The Future Memory, is a different tool. There you describe a happy future scene as if it had already happened. Here you do not have to pretend the item is likely. Likelihood is not the entrance fee.

## Variations and replay

Long-distance sharing is native to lists. Read it, or send it, and wait for the two marks before you discuss. The marks are the witness.

Speech, large type, and a single column on a phone are equal. If a hand cannot write the three phrases, the speaker says them and the partner writes only what they are asked to initial.

Chronic pain and limited travel are not problems for the list to overcome. Company, taste, texture, a window, a song, a conversation you want while you are both awake. Put those on the page without an apology clause.

Replay annually, or after a big change, and keep the old list. Items fall off because they happened, because they lost their charge, or because they were someone else's idea of a daring life.

050 looks at worth already present in scenes. 093, The Relationship Bucket List, builds a shared collection of adventures, traditions, and goals. Leave shared plans there. This list remains personally owned, even the items that receive an initial.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale's lists show the proportion. They are not a challenge and not your homework.

Rowan writes eight. Ordinary: a tomato sandwich in August, eaten over the sink, at least once more with the good salt. Learning the names of 10 trees on the block. A phone call with an old friend that is not about logistics. Sitting through one full album without a second task. Grand, labeled grand: a long train ride through a country Rowan has not seen, window seat, no agenda. Someday and maybe never: sleeping outside in a desert. Also ordinary, and soon if someone will do it too: Tuesday lunch that is just lunch, not a meeting. From the chair: tea hot enough to matter, with a record on.

Vale marks help on the album, company on the couch, one time, phones in the other room. Vale will not take over the desert. "That one is yours, including the possibility that it never happens." Vale does not offer to price the train. Rowan did not mark the train help welcome.

Vale's list leans local. Three songs learned well enough to sing without wincing. The bakery on Pine on a weekday morning. Hemming one pair of pants. A Tuesday photo of the sky, kept and sent to no one. A conversation with a sibling that includes an apology Vale has been editing for too long, marked private enough that the content stays off the shared page. Grand and maybe never: seeing the northern lights, cold counted as a real barrier. From this chair: someone's hand in Vale's hair while the news is off. This year: a single swim, if the summer allows, and no program of swims.

Rowan offers help with the three songs, as a listener who will not coach, and leaves the sibling conversation entirely alone. The northern lights get no initial. Vale sorts the songs as ordinary and soon, and as help welcome. The lights are grand and maybe never. The apology conversation is not help welcome, even though it matters. Rowan initials the songs only.

Nobody books a desert. Nobody treats the sandwich as trivial. The maybe-never items were said, which was the whole requirement.

## Afterward

Which ordinary item felt strangely exposing?
Which grand item were you glad you did not have to justify?
Did any offer of help get bigger than you meant? Shrink it before you sleep, in words.
What did you leave entirely with your partner, and did they look relieved?

A list can be tender without becoming a plan. You witnessed an appetite. Appetite is not a contract.

## Our Story

Date the list. If you initialed help, write what the help actually is, in one concrete phrase, so memory cannot inflate it. Personal pages hold items you did not read aloud. You do not have to share those pages, and an unspoken item is not a test you failed. The shared page is only for what both of you are willing to keep in the common record.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- The ordinary items I was willing to say, and the grand one I labeled as grand
- Which item I marked help welcome, and which I want left entirely mine
partner_b:
- An item I marked someday and maybe never, and why it was still worth saying
- The one item I offered to help with, including the limit of that help, and the one I will not take over
shared:
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```

## Notes

Reimagined from the popular custom of a bucket list, with ordinary pleasures required beside grand adventures. The list is personal. The partner witnesses, may help with one item, and leaves at least one item alone. No empirical claim is made. No mortality, bucket-list, or goal study on the whitelist applies, and none is invented here. Listing experiences is not presented as a way to extend life, reduce fear, or organize grief. Hearing a wish is not agreeing to fund it, to do something dangerous, or to adopt it as a couple's plan.
