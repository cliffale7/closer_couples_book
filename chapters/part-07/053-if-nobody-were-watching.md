## The hook

Most days arrive with an audience already seated. Family, with its long memory. Coworkers, with their sense of what is professional. Friends, who know the version of you that gets invited back. The person across from you, who is not an audience except when you treat them as one. And a former self, still unimpressed, still taking notes.

This hour asks those spectators to wait outside. Then three verbs, no more than one answer each. Pursue. Express. Abandon. At least one of the three should be ordinary, so the evening is not required to produce a revelation. Afterward you sort the items. Some you might want help with. Some must stay yours alone.

## Why it might matter

A life without spectators is a daydream almost everyone has already had, usually in a car or a shower, and usually without telling the person it would affect. The daydream mixes harmless wishes with relief fantasies and, sometimes, with real requests wearing a costume. Left unsorted, the mix is how a partner hears "I would quit being reliable" and cannot tell whether to laugh, to worry, or to start a budget.

The mechanism separates the mix. First the audiences are named, so the freedom is specific. A blank "if nobody judged me" tends to produce a poster about authenticity. A named audience produces a particular flinch. Then three verbs. Pursue, something you would go toward. Express, something you would say, make, wear, or stop translating into acceptable language. Abandon, something you would set down.

Abandon is the verb that needs a fork, because it is the one a partner can reasonably hear as a threat. If the item is a private habit or a costume of politeness, it can stay a wish. If it would drop a duty that affects the partner, whether care, money, fidelity, or safety, you label it before the sentence cools. Two labels exist. "Fantasy of relief." "An actual request." You do not leave the ambiguity standing.

The partner's needs are not external judgment. Sending the partner outside applies to their role as spectator, not to their role as a person with a stake. Imagined privacy is not permission to harm anyone, and it is not permission to drop care for a dependent. Nothing in the hour has to be acted out.

This is not an authenticity test. You can discover a wish and still decline to live it. You can also discover that a so-called obligation was mostly a costume, and decide, later, with your eyes open, to wear something else.

## Before you begin

Write first if shame makes speech clumsy. You may pass any verb. One verb is a complete short session. Long-distance works. No mobility is required, and no performance is required. The empty theater in this chapter's motif is a picture, not a stage you have to stand on.

Name the audiences you are setting outside, using whatever fits.

Family. Coworkers. Friends. A former self. The partner, as judge only.

You may add one audience of your own, a teacher, a group chat, a town. You may also say, "My partner stays in the room as themselves. I am only asking the score-keeping version to step out."

Agree on the fork before anyone reads an abandonment. If a wish would change care, money, fidelity, or safety, it gets one of the two labels, said in ordinary words. The partner may ask which fork an abandonment sits on. The partner may say what they need if the label is "an actual request." Either of you may stop.

One more kindness. Do not argue a harmless wish out of the speaker. The loud shirt, the bad singing, the night class, and the unsent style of sentence can survive your taste. And do not perform a bravery you do not mean. A daring item offered for applause is still an audience, just a nearer one.

## The experience

List the audiences in a column. Read the column aloud. Then imagine them in the hall, not annihilated, not defeated. Waiting. You are allowed to notice which one is hardest to send out. You do not have to expel that one if the exercise would become a lie without them. Say, "This one stays," and adjust.

Write three lines.

Pursue. One thing you would go toward with those spectators in the hall.
Express. One thing you would let be visible.
Abandon. One thing you would set down.

At least one line should be ordinary enough to say without a drum roll. A hobby, a garment, a sentence, a social reflex. If all three lines are life-altering, revise one downward until it sounds like you on a Wednesday.

Read them. After each person, sort every item into one of two piles, spoken or marked on the page.

Help wanted. You would like your partner's practical company, a ride, a reminder, a witness, a bit of money you will discuss as money and not as a vow made inside a fantasy.
Mine alone. The wish stays yours. Company would turn it into a performance or a joint project you did not ask for.

Then the abandon fork, item by item, even when the item seems harmless. Say which side it is on.

A private habit or a costume of politeness can remain a wish. "I would stop narrating my plans until someone approves them." That can sit in mine alone, or in help wanted if what you want is a raised eyebrow when the old narration starts.

A duty that touches the partner cannot remain atmospheric. "I would stop managing our calendar" is either a fantasy of relief, said so the other person can hear the tiredness without hearing a resignation, or an actual request, said so they can answer. If you do not know which, say "I don't know yet, so I am labeling it fantasy of relief for tonight, and I will not act on it under this spell." That sentence is how you keep the ambiguity from standing up and walking into the week.

The partner responds to an actual request as themselves, with a stake, not as a judge you failed to expel. They can say what they need. They can ask for time. They do not have to solve it before the evening ends.

Close by returning the audiences to ordinary life on purpose. "They can come back. I still want one of these." Or, "They can come back. I was visiting." The visit is not a contract.

## Three ways to play

**15 minutes.** Name the audiences in a sentence. One verb each, speaker's choice. If the verb is abandon, use the fork before you move on. Sort help wanted or mine alone in a single phrase. One verb is enough when you are tired.

**40 minutes.** The column of audiences, three lines each, the sort, and the fork wherever abandon touches a shared stake. Leave time for the partner to answer an actual request with something other than instant agreement. This is the standard hour.

**70 minutes.** Write the three lines as short notes, a few sentences each, and exchange them to read in silence. Then talk. The extra time is for the fork and for harmless wishes that deserve to be enjoyed rather than analyzed. You may add a second ordinary item if the first pass came out grander than you meant. You may not add a second abandonment of a shared duty in order to seem thorough.

## Going deeper

If a harmless pursuit interests you both, 092, The Dream Exchange, is where support can be designed without the partner taking ownership. Save the design for then. Tonight, "help wanted" can stay a sticky note.

You may each name the audience whose absence changed your answers the most. That is often more revealing than the wish. A person who becomes suddenly specific when coworkers leave the room is telling you about a costume. A person who cannot set their partner-as-judge outside, even imaginatively, is telling you the exercise has found a limit. Respect the limit.

A later replay can change the audiences. Family out, former self in. The wishes move. You are not trying to find the one true unwatched self. You are trying to see which spectator has been holding the pen.

## Variations and replay

Shame-heavy topics can stay on paper, slid across a table or sent as a letter, with the sort written at the bottom so nobody has to perform the label. Speech is equal when speech is easier.

If you are depleted, pursue is often the gentlest verb, and abandon the heaviest. Choose accordingly. Nothing must be acted out, including the expressive one. Wanting to sing badly in the kitchen is not a dare.

Do not use this chapter to coach anyone out of safety, caregiving, or consent. A fantasy of dropping those things can be spoken only with the label on it, and even then you may pass. The partner is allowed to say, "I can hear the relief, and I still need the care to continue."

049 starts from a past fork and the dream still stuck to it. 083, The Secret Nobody Would Guess, is a surprising fact you volunteer. This chapter is a present-tense counterfactual about judgment. Let the three stay unmixed. Replay when the audiences change, after a new job, a move, a death in the family of expectations. Date the column. The old spectators are part of the record.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale are not confessing for you, and their wishes are not instructions.

Rowan's audiences: a competent older sibling, a team at work, Vale-as-judge, and a 24-year-old self who thought taste was a moral achievement. Rowan sends them into the hall, with a note that Vale-as-person stays.

Pursue: a beginner ceramics class on Wednesday nights, ordinary and a little muddy.
Express: the loud shirt already owned and never worn outside the bedroom.
Abandon: "the reflex to narrate every plan until someone nods."

Sort. Ceramics is help wanted, in the form of company on the first night only. The shirt is mine alone. The narration reflex is a wish, a costume of politeness, and Rowan would like a friendly signal when it starts, so help wanted.

Vale does not talk Rowan out of the shirt.

Vale's lines. Pursue: nothing grand, a weekly hour with a pencil and bad drawings of street trees. Express: anger in a complete sentence, instead of a joke that arrives first. Abandon: "being the easy one about our calendar, the person who always absorbs the change."

The abandon touches a shared duty. Vale labels it before Rowan has to guess. "Tonight this is a fantasy of relief. I am tired, and I am not requesting that you take the calendar over. If I want that, I will ask on a different day, in request language, not in a spell." Rowan says what they need anyway, briefly. "If it becomes a request, I need a specific piece, not the whole year." They leave it labeled and unenacted.

The ordinary item did its job. Nobody had to unveil a secret life in order to have a true hour. The street trees and the loud shirt were enough to keep the conversation from becoming a confession contest.

## Afterward

Which audience was hardest to send outside, and did you let them stay?
Which item was ordinary, and did you trust it to count?
Where did abandon need a label, and did the label actually get said?
What is help wanted, what is mine alone, and did either of you agree to a help you do not mean?

A wish can be real and still be a visit. An actual request can be real and still wait for a clearer day. The audiences can have their seats back without pretending they were never in the hall.

## Our Story

Date the session. If an abandonment was labeled, note the label on the shared page so memory does not upgrade a fantasy into a decision. The personal pages can hold wishes you do not want acted on or even reread together. Sharing those pages is optional. Silence there is not a hidden request.

## Notes

Reimagined from the private habit of imagining a life without spectators. Three verbs structure it: pursue, express, and abandon. Audiences are named first. Shared stakes get an explicit label, fantasy of relief or an actual request. No empirical claim is made. Imagining the absence of judgment has no outcome study on the whitelist, and none is invented here. A judgment-free life is not promised, and it is not assumed to be kinder.
