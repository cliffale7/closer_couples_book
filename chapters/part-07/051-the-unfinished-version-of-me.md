## The hook

Some portraits get finished and framed. This one stays in pencil. You are not delivering a self-improvement plan, and you are not handing your partner a riddle to solve. You are showing two different kinds of unfinished.

One is a quality you are still practicing, with a recent day attached so it cannot float off into a slogan. The other is a mystery about yourself you have not solved. The listener may be curious. The listener may not close the file.

## Why it might matter

Partners get used to a finished sketch. Reliable, funny, bad at mornings, good in a crisis. The sketch is often affectionate and slightly out of date. It misses the skill that is only half learned, and it misses the question the person is living with on purpose.

The mechanism uses two lists because they do different work. "Still practicing" needs a behavior and a recent example, or it turns into a compliment you pay yourself. "Still mysterious" needs a question you cannot yet answer, or the partner's theory will rush in to be helpful. Help, in this hour, is a question. Advice waits for an invitation that may not come.

The portrait stays unfinished on purpose. A second pass names one mystery you are content not to solve this year. That sentence is the guardrail. Without it, every unknown starts to look like homework.

Talking about who you are still becoming is an ordinary custom. Personality inventories and type labels are a different custom, and they are not invited. There is no score. There is no finished state you are behind on. A mystery may stay partly closed because you prefer it that way, or because you do not know it well enough to share the whole of it. You choose what is sayable.

## Before you begin

Write first, even if the writing is two lines. Speech from a cold start tends to produce the flattering version: growth, insight, a lesson already learned. The page is allowed to be plainer than that.

You may pass, change a prompt, or keep a mystery at the level of a hint. Fatigue means one quality and one mystery, then stop. Long-distance is a natural fit. If trait language irritates you, stay with scenes. "I am still learning how to leave a party before I am miserable" is enough psychology for anyone.

Agree, out loud, that the listener will not diagnose, type, or finish the sentence. Two curious questions are the allowance. A third question is fine only if the speaker asks for it. Advice is allowed when the speaker says, "I want a suggestion." Until that sentence, suggestions stay in the listener's mouth.

This is not a demand to improve, and it is not a tour of flaws. Less flattering tendencies have their own chapter, 084, for a different day.

## The experience

Two headings. Write one item under each. If you think of more, keep the extras in the margin. You will share one of each.

Still practicing. A quality, plus a recent concrete example. Recent means a week, a month, a season you can still picture. "I am getting better at patience" is a poster. "On Wednesday I put the argument down long enough to get a glass of water before I answered" is a practice. The example can be clumsy. Clumsy is evidence that the practice is alive.

Still mysterious. A question you cannot yet answer about yourself. Not a problem you are assigning your partner. "I don't know whether the ambition I had at 12 still fits, or whether I am keeping it as a souvenir." "I don't know why praise makes me want to leave the room." If you can already answer it, it belongs in a different conversation.

Read them. Speaker first. A few minutes is enough. Then the listener asks up to two questions. Useful shapes:

"When did you last notice that, besides the example you gave?"
"What does the mystery feel like on an ordinary afternoon?"
"Is this something you want company with, or company near?"

Less useful, and out of bounds unless invited: "I think what you are really doing is..." and any sentence that begins by naming a type.

Switch.

Then the second pass, called still unfinished on purpose. Each speaker names one mystery they are content not to solve this year. It may be the mystery they just told, or a different one they do not want pried at. The listener repeats it back as a closed door they will respect. "You're leaving the question about the old ambition unanswered this year. I'll leave it there too." No improvement plan gets attached. No deadline. If the only mystery you feel is one you do want to work on, you may say that, and you still do not have to schedule it tonight.

The session ends when both doors have been named. You do not compare whose unfinishedness is more interesting. That comparison is a contest, and this portrait is not a contest.

## Three ways to play

**15 minutes.** One quality and one mystery each, spoken, with the recent example included. The listener asks one question or none. Skip the second pass if you are spent, and say so. One of each is a complete short session.

**40 minutes.** Write first. Share one of each. Two curious questions. Then the unfinished-on-purpose sentence, repeated back. This is the version that keeps the portrait from collapsing into a plan.

**70 minutes.** Write a little more slowly. After the questions, the speaker may add a second quality or a second mystery from the margin, still without a deadline. End with the same closed door. The extra time is for accuracy and for silence. It is not for a workshop.

## Going deeper

If both of you want a later session, trade roles of emphasis. One evening leans on practices. Another leans on mysteries. Keep the rule that the listener does not solve what the speaker has left open.

You may ask, once, "What would count as practicing this badly, in a way I might misread as not caring?" That question is practical. It gives your partner a way to see the attempt. It still does not set a quota.

A mystery that touches the relationship can be named here if it is genuinely about the speaker. "I don't know why I go formal when I am scared" belongs. A mutual knot you are trying to untangle together belongs in a different chapter, on a day you both choose it. 088 can range across self, life, intimacy, identity, or the relationship. This one stays on the speaker's becoming.

## Variations and replay

Letters work. The listener's reply is two questions at most, and a repetition of the door the speaker wants left shut. No paragraph of insight.

If writing is hard, talk the two headings into a voice note and play it back before you share, so you can delete the slogan and keep the scene. If a recent example would expose someone else's private life, use a smaller example. Asking for help with the jar you could not open is a legitimate practice. Heroic examples are not more advanced.

Replay every so often. A quality that was "still practicing" may have become ordinary, which is worth noticing, or it may have stalled, which is also worth noticing without a lecture. A mystery may have answered itself in the dark. You may retire it. You may also find it unchanged and be glad you did not force it.

008, The Secret Biography, is a written portrait of contradictions and less visible motives. 084, The Dark Side of Me, is for tendencies you do not admire. 088, The Thing I’m Still Figuring Out, can land on the relationship as easily as on the self. Use those when you want them. Tonight the pencil stays on one person's unfinished edges.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale are sample portraits, not a diagnosis and not you.

Rowan, still practicing: "I am still learning to ask for help with ordinary things. Last Sunday the pickle jar beat me, and I stood in the kitchen being proud and slightly furious until I said, out loud, 'Will you open this?' It was a small sentence and it took a while."

Still mysterious: "I don't know whether I like being the competent one, or whether I learned it so early that I never checked."

Vale's two questions: "Was there a moment after the jar when you wanted to take the sentence back?" and "Does the mystery show up more at home or at work?" Rowan answers the first. "Yes. I made a joke immediately." Rowan leaves the second half-open. "Both, and I don't want a theory about my childhood tonight." Vale stops.

Unfinished on purpose, Rowan: "The competent-one question stays unanswered this year." Vale repeats that, and does not add a hunch.

Vale, still practicing: "I am still learning a calmer exit from an argument. On Tuesday I said, 'I am going to the other room for 10 minutes,' and I actually came back. I used to disappear into a task and call it perspective."

Still mysterious: "I don't know if the mapmaking ambition from when I was 12 still fits, or if I like the idea of being a person who once wanted that."

Rowan asks: "What did the 12-year-old actually do, maps or daydreams?" and "Do you want that question touched, or named and left?" Vale says the 12-year-old drew bus routes on scrap paper, and that the question is for the door. They leave it. Nobody builds a five-year plan about cartography. The portrait remains unfinished, which is the point of the hour.

## Afterward

What did the recent example show that the quality-word would have hidden?
Which question from your partner was curious, and which one, if any, tried to finish you?
What are you content to leave unknown this year?
Did advice arrive uninvited? If it did, you can say so without staging a trial.

You are allowed to be in the middle of a skill and in the middle of a question at the same time. Neither condition is a problem your partner has to solve before bed.

## Our Story

Date the session and the version. The personal pages can hold the mystery you did not say aloud. They are optional to share, including with each other. The shared notes keep the door you both agreed to leave shut, so a later reread does not turn into a surprise investigation.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- The quality I am still practicing, with the recent example I actually told
- A mystery I am content not to solve this year
partner_b:
- The question about myself I cannot yet answer
- What it was like to be asked about, and to have the question left open
shared:
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```

## Notes

Reimagined from the ordinary custom of talking about who one is still becoming. Two lists do the work: a quality in progress, with a recent example, and a mystery the speaker has not solved. No empirical claim is made. The whitelist is not cited, because no study there tested this portrait. Personality types are not treated as science. The listener's insight does not outrank the speaker's unknown.
