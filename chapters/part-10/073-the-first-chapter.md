## The hook

Write the beginning twice, apart, before either of you can steer it. The story of how you two began has a runtime by now. Friends have heard the short version. One of you tells it more cleanly in public, so that version starts to win. Rain gets heavier. A nervous week becomes a charming beat. What drops out is the part you were unsure of, and the detail the other person never stored.

This sitting asks for two first chapters, written apart, then read in the order they were written. A later change of picture is 076. The long line, disputes included, is 077. One day, rebuilt, is 080. The mismatches stay on the table. They are information about memory and about two interiors. A smooth meet-cute, composed together from the opening minute, is a fine story for a dinner. It is a different hour from this one. This one wants the draft that has not yet been negotiated.

## Why it might matter

Once a beginning can be told in under a minute, the uncertainties stop getting air. You can know someone for years and still be wrong about which afternoon they count as the start.

The mechanism is separate recall, then comparison. Each person reconstructs the early days alone, including what was uncertain, before either voice can steer the other. Then you listen all the way through. Same memory, news, and a different memory are three different findings. None of them is a grade.

Couples already tell this story at tables. The reimagining is the private draft. Confidence is a feeling a memory produces. Accuracy is a separate question, and some details are worth keeping when neither of you can prove them. A fond telling is a piece of writing you are making together. It is a forecast in nobody's files. The notes say what a coding study of oral histories did and did not show.

The more cinematic draft is allowed to be the polished one. You get to notice the polish.

## Before you begin

You need two private pages and, later, one shared page. Paper, a notes app, or a voice memo recorded alone will do. Sit where you can hear each other, or agree that the telling happens on a call after the drafts exist. Large print is welcome. If hearing makes a live telling hard, trade the pages and read them.

About 40 minutes fits the full shape. If you are tired, say so and use the short version.

A few permissions, once, before anyone performs a past:

Either of you may leave out sexual firsts, a family conflict, a former partner, or any stretch you do not want replayed. No reason is required. If the beginning holds grief or pressure, start the draft at a later scene and mark the earlier stretch private.

A live argument about faithfulness will not fit on these pages. Stop if that is the conversation waiting. Have it in plain time, when you both mean to. This hour is for memory.

The faster writer does not own the record. A forgotten detail is a fact about memory. You may pass a prompt, change it, or stop after one scene.

## The experience

**First round, about 10 minutes, no talking.** Each person writes five lines. Fragments are enough.

Where the relationship begins for you. A month, a season, or "after the second Thursday" is a real date. Your partner's beginning is allowed to fall on another day.

What you were unsure of then. A feeling, a fact, a worry you did not say.

One detail later retellings have polished. The rain that may have been drizzle. The joke that has gotten funnier because other people laugh. The hour you have moved so the story lands.

What you thought the other person felt. Mark it as a guess.

One fact you still do not know. Leave it as a question. You may keep that question on your private page if you would rather not ask it aloud.

If writing tires you, record the five lines alone and play them later. Take the quiet either way. The silence is what keeps the second draft from becoming a revision of the first.

**Second round.** One person reads or tells the five lines. The listener does not correct, supply the restaurant's real name, or offer a better punch line. They may jot three marks on their own page:

Same memory.
News to me.
I remember it differently.

The marks stay private until the third round. A nod is enough while the story is moving. When the first person finishes, leave a breath. Then switch. Both drafts get a full hearing. A shorter draft is still a complete draft.

**Third round.** Now you may talk. Start with the starting points. If the dates differ, say both, and say what each one is counting. A first conversation, a first plan, and a first night you knew you would continue can all be true and still refuse to share a Tuesday.

Then the surprises. "News to me" is the interesting pile. One curious question is plenty. "I had no idea you were unsure about the call" belongs here. A cross-examination can wait outside.

Then the disagreements. Set them side by side. A Wednesday and a Friday do not need to be averaged into a Thursday so the story will be tidy. Say the keeper out loud if you have one: "We will keep your red coat and my memory of a jacket, and we will leave the forecast alone."

A joint paragraph is optional. If you write one, give it one agreed fact and one uncertainty you are willing to leave in the story. Three sentences is a full paragraph for this purpose. If you would rather keep the drafts unfused, the two pages are the chapter.

Close with one sentence each: the uncertainty you are leaving in. Then stop. The long arc has its own map. One later change in how you saw each other has its own hour. A single day you would enter again is a different reconstruction.

## Three ways to play

**15 minutes.** Each person writes three things in silence: a scene you trust, something you were unsure of, and one detail the other person might not hold. Read them. The listener makes the three marks and names one surprise. A single scene each finishes the session. The short version leaves out the guess about the other's feeling, and the fact you still do not know. Pick those up if the evening grows.

**40 minutes.** The three rounds as written. About 10 minutes of silence, two uninterrupted tellings, then comparison. This is the version that lets a mismatch survive contact with the other person's charm.

**70 minutes.** Write a page, including a sensory detail and the bit you have been polishing for other people. Trade pages and read in silence, then do the second and third rounds aloud. Still no correction in the middle of a reading. The extra time is for accuracy, and for a joint paragraph if you want one. A page is enough. The rest of the early relationship can stay complex on another day.

## Going deeper

On a later day, take one item marked "I remember it differently" and give it two short paragraphs, one in each voice, dated. Leave them side by side. Two interiors are already a kind of record.

You can ask, once, "What did you think I felt, and where did that guess come from?" Answer only if you want to. A wrong guess from years ago can be tender. It can also be none of tonight's business. Passing finishes the question.

If a photograph, a message, or a calendar later corrects a date, add a new line and date it. The old draft stays. It records what you believed when you wrote it.

## Variations and replay

Voice memo, letter, or a shared document plus one call: record or write the first round alone, then use the live time for the third round.

No darkness, no walk, and no required eye contact. A table is enough. If fatigue shows up, stop after the two drafts and schedule the comparison. Drafts you have not yet compared are still drafts.

A beginning you do not want to retell can be replaced by the first ordinary week you trust. Say that you moved the starting line. Your partner leaves it where you moved it.

The motif is two handwritten openings laid side by side. A private sketch of your own draft is optional.

076, The Moment I Saw You Differently, is one later change in perception. 077, The Relationship Timeline, is the long arc, challenges included, drawn where you can both see it. 080, The Day Worth Reliving, stays inside a single date. This chapter is the beginning, drafted twice before it is shared.

Replay when a piece of evidence shows up, or when you notice the party version has gotten smoother again. Date the attempt. The first chapter is allowed to stay slightly unfinished.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale are an illustration, not a portrait of you.

Rowan writes, alone: "I start us at the bus stop in March, the week of the late rain. I was unsure you would text after I made that stiff joke about the timetable. Later tellings have turned the rain into weather worth a story. I thought you were mildly entertained and already headed somewhere better. I still do not know whether you missed the first bus on purpose."

Vale writes, alone: "I start us three weeks later, in the hardware store, because that was the first time I decided to see you again on purpose. The bus stop was pleasant and, in my memory, unfinished. I remember your chipped blue mug from the next morning more clearly than the rain. I thought you were being polite. I still do not know what you told your sister that night."

Rowan reads. Vale marks "news to me" beside the stiff joke and "I remember it differently" beside the start date. Vale does not interrupt to relocate the beginning. When Vale reads, Rowan marks "news to me" beside the mug, which Rowan had forgotten. "Same memory" gets no mark at all, and both of them laugh.

In the third round they keep both start dates. Rowan says, "Your hardware store is when it became mutual. My bus stop is when I started hoping." Vale says, "I will keep the joke. I had filed you as smooth." They leave the sister's phone call unasked. The uncertainty they agree to leave in the story is the rain. Neither of them will check a forecast from that March. A forecast would still fail to say who was hoping.

## Afterward

Sit with the two pages before you sand them back into a single anecdote.

Where did your beginnings land, and what is each one counting?
What arrived as news, and where did you want to correct a sentence before it had finished?
Which polished detail are you willing to let stay a little duller?
What uncertainty will you leave in the story on purpose?

Two drafts that disagree in good faith are a finished chapter. You can love the party version and still keep the smudge.

## Our Story

Date the session, the place, and the version. Mood and initials are optional. Note whether you finished or paused.

The private pages can stay private. Sharing them is a choice you can make once and then leave alone. The shared page is for the overlap, the news, and the uncertainty you are willing to keep together.

## Notes

Reimagined from the ordinary habit of telling how a relationship began. The separate draft is the part that matters. This private comparison has not been tested as an intervention.

See B1992. Buehlman, Gottman, and Katz (1992) coded an Oral History Interview about dating and marital history, philosophy of marriage, and change over time, in a selected sample of married parents. The prompts fair to name are good times and bad times, philosophy, and change. Methods recruited 56 families; the summary and follow-up concern 52. The interview was conducted for the study. Over about three years, the manner of telling was associated with later marital stability. That association is a correlate in that sample, not a reminiscence treatment, and not this chapter.
