## The hook

The job is a manual in three headings: operating notes, warning signs, and maintenance. 025, What Loving Me Actually Looks Like, is which acts land as love. 057, The Instruction Manual for My Bad Days, is the first 10 minutes of a hard day. This page is how you run when you are running.

A toaster comes with a page about what not to put in it. A person you love usually comes with guesswork, a few scars from early guesses, and a running joke that may be doing too much work. This hour is a personal owner's manual, written by each of you, about yourselves. Playful means specific and lightly worded. It does not mean the needs are a joke.

Three headings, and only three. Operating notes. Warning signs. Maintenance. You are describing conditions, early signals, and the small care that keeps an ordinary week from grinding. You are not filing a diagnosis, and you are not writing the manual for the other person's behavior.

## Why it might matter

People in long familiarity start to treat each other as appliances they have already mastered. They know the sound of the keys, the order of the morning, the face that means the day went sideways. Mastery goes stale. The settings change, and the old instructions keep getting followed because nobody printed a revision.

The use of the page is portrait. Two emotional needs, stated as situations. Two signs that you are overloaded, withdrawn, or prickly, described as things a person could notice without a clinical vocabulary. Two pieces of maintenance for an ordinary week, at least one of which you can do without your partner's hands. The partner is in the story. The partner is not the only tool in the kit.

No psychological instrument sits under this chapter. Following the manual does not guarantee love, and nobody signs a compliance line at the bottom. The neighboring chapters do neighboring jobs. 025, What Loving Me Actually Looks Like, compares exact loving behaviors. 029, The Reassurance Menu, sorts help by the state you are in. 057, The Instruction Manual for My Bad Days, is for the days that have already gone wrong. This one is closer to a plate on the side of a machine: how it runs when it is running, and what the lights mean before it stalls.

## Before you begin

One sheet each, or a note in large type. The three headings go at the top before you feel clever. Dictation counts if the author approves the wording after hearing it back. If hearing makes a read-aloud difficult, exchange pages and read in silence. If print is the barrier, read aloud and let the listener ask before they interpret. Extra time, a lap desk, or a phone note is a legitimate sheet. If you live apart, both drafts finish before either is sent, so the second manual does not become a courteous copy of the first. If the day is overloaded already, use the short version and stop.

About 25 minutes to draft, then about 20 to read, in the standard session. You may keep a line unread. Say "this one stays on my page" and move on. Curiosity does not outrank that.

A need that lands on the other person's body, money, or time becomes a request when you read it. They may decline. Warning signs are information. They are not permission to police a mood, and they are not a license to monitor a phone. If a so-called sign is actually a harmful behavior, name the behavior and the limit in ordinary language. Charm is the wrong wrapping for that sentence.

## The experience

Draft in parallel. Operating notes get two emotional needs, written as situations with a time and a texture. "I need a few quiet minutes after work before I can talk about plans." "I need food that is hot and unfancy when I am finished being useful to other people." "I need you to ask before a plan lands on the calendar." "I need to eat the meal slowly, from a shared bowl, without a debrief of the sauce." Those are different households. Write the one you actually live in. A quota is a failed note. "Text within 5 minutes or I am unsafe" is not operating instructions. It is an alarm you should talk about directly, outside the joke of a manual.

Warning signs get two observable tells that you are overloaded, withdrawn, or prickly. One-word replies. A drawer reorganized for no reason. A sudden excess of agreeableness followed by vanishing into a task. A joke that gets a little mean and then gets defended. Describe what a camera would catch. Leave diagnostic labels off the page, including the ones you find charming. You are not a flavor of disorder. You are a person with a pattern.

Maintenance gets two small things that help on ordinary weeks. At least one is yours to do for yourself. Sitting in the car for 4 minutes before you come inside. Putting your own phone in another room. A shower that is actually hot. A standing call with a friend that your partner does not have to host. The second item may involve your partner, and when it does, it is a request. "It helps when the first question of the evening waits until I have had water" is a request. "You are responsible for my regulation" is a job posting they did not apply for.

Read, one manual at a time. The reader asks one curious question. One. "When you say quiet minutes, do you want me in the next room or out of the apartment?" is a good question. A cross-examination about whether the need is reasonable is a bad one. The reader may also say which maintenance item they can support and which they cannot. Then you swap. Nobody edits the other person's page. If a wording is unclear, the author rewrites it. The reader does not "fix" the need into something more convenient.

If you want the longer session, add one "please do not" line. It names a common misfire, still lightly, and it is not a house rule carved over the door. "Please do not ask what is wrong three times in the doorway." "Please do not treat the quiet as a problem you have to solve before you take your shoes off." The line should be something the author has actually experienced, not a preemptive law for every future guest.

## Three ways to play

**20 minutes.** One item under each heading. Read them. One question each. A thin manual is a finished manual.

**45 minutes.** Two items under each heading, drafted before anyone reads. One question, plus a clear yes or no on the maintenance item that involves the partner. This is the version with enough detail to be usable next Tuesday.

**60 minutes.** The standard page, plus the "please do not" line. Spend the extra minutes on examples, not on negotiations you are too tired to finish. If a request conflicts, name the conflict and set a later time. The manual does not have to be reconciled into a single policy tonight.

## Going deeper

Manuals expire. Pick the maintenance item you hope is still true in six months and date it. When the date arrives, you are allowed to want something else. A revised manual is a sign that someone was paying attention to a life, not a sign that the first draft was naive.

Playfulness dies in two directions. Too cute, and the need disappears into a bit. Too solemn, and you have written a clinical file with headings borrowed from a product insert. Read your page once in a voice you would use with a friend. If you wince at the joke, plain the sentence down. If you sound like a form, put one specific object back in. The chipped mug. The minutes in the car. The shared bowl. Objects keep the page from floating.

There is a cultural width to this that a single household script will miss. Some people ask for space by going quiet and trusting the quiet to be read kindly. Some ask by naming the hour they will be back. Some experience care as food that appears without a speech, and some experience unasked food as a crossing of a line. Some need time requested in advance because surprise reads as pressure. Some need a little surplus time, unstructured, because a fully booked kindness still feels like a shift. Your manual can borrow any of those shapes. It has to be yours, in the grain of your week, not a costume of sensitivity.

Warning signs can be misused. If you catch yourself writing tells about your partner so you can manage them, stop. That page is surveillance with charming headings. Your signs are about you. Their signs are theirs, on their sheet, in their words.

## Variations and replay

A voice note can be the manual if the author listens back and keeps only the sentences they still endorse. Across languages, write in the one that feels intimate. Translate only if you both want the translation more than you want the original music of the sentence.

Replay every six months, or after a change in work, health, housing, or grief. Bring the old page. Circle what is still true. Cross out what has become a museum label. Add one new maintenance item you can do for yourself, so the revision does not become a longer work order for the other person.

If writing with your partner in the room makes you perform a more easygoing self, draft alone and exchange later the same day. The delay is part of the design, not a lack of intimacy.

## Examples

Fictional. Rowan and Vale are an illustration, not a portrait of you.

Rowan's operating notes: "I need a few quiet minutes after I get home before anyone aims a plan at me." "When I have cooked, I need one specific true sentence about the food, not a review and not silence." Warning signs: "I answer in one-word texts, and I reorganize a drawer that was fine." Maintenance: "I sit in the car for 4 minutes before I come in. I can do that alone." "It helps if the first question waits until I have had water." Please do not: "Please do not ask what is wrong three times in the doorway."

Vale's operating notes: "Ask me before something lands on the calendar, even a small something." "On a sore day I need the walk around the block to be allowed to become a lap of the apartment. The outing can shrink without being canceled in spirit." Warning signs: "I get very agreeable, and then I disappear into a task so I do not have to say no." Maintenance: "I put my phone in the other room during dinner. That one is mine." "It helps when a plan includes a seat. My knee gets a vote." Please do not: "Please do not treat my quiet as a malfunction."

They read. Rowan asks one question: "When you disappear into a task, do you want a knock at 20 minutes or no knock at all?" Vale says, "A knock, and then you leave if I say later." Vale asks, "Are the 4 minutes in the car a request that I stay out, or a thing you do in your own company?" Rowan says, "My own company. You can be in the kitchen. Just do not open with logistics." Vale can support the water-before-questions item, and cannot promise a food speech on raw days. Rowan rewrites that maintenance line to "one ingredient you noticed, when you have it." Nobody signs. They date the pages and pick a Saturday six months out, already expecting edits.

## Afterward

Read the maintenance item you hope survives six months, out loud or on the page, with the review date beside it.

Which request did you hear as a request, and which one did you almost hear as a verdict on your character?
Which of your own warning signs felt accurate, and which one was really a criticism of your partner in disguise?
What can you do for yourself this week without waiting to be loved correctly first?

The manual is a portrait of conditions. It is not a vow, and it is not a grade.

## Our Story

Date the session and the review date you chose. Note the version and whether any line stayed unread on purpose. Initials and mood are optional. Write needs as situations.

The two personal pages are yours. Share them, keep them, or leave them blank. Unread is a complete way to use them. The shared notes are the part you write together.

```journal
session: date, time, place, version played, mood if wanted, intention, initials if wanted, done or paused
partner_a:
- The maintenance item I hope is still true in six months, and the date I will check it
- One warning sign that is mine, described as something a person could see or hear
partner_b:
- The operating note I most want understood as a situation rather than a flaw
- Which of my partner's maintenance items I can support, and which I cannot
shared:
- What actually happened
- The line or moment worth keeping
- What we discovered
- What we might do next, if anything
revisit:
- Attempt, date, and what changed
- Then versus now
```

## Notes

Reimagined from the popular "owner's manual" writing exercise. The three sections are a design, not a validated self-assessment. No empirical claim is made, and no psychological instrument is implied. A manual does not guarantee love. Needs that cost the partner time, money, or bodily effort remain requests they may decline. Trademarked personality systems are not the headings, on purpose.
