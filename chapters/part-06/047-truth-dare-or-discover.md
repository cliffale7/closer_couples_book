## The hook

Truth or dare usually pretends there are two brave doors and a coward's exit. This game has three doors with the same dignity. On your turn you choose Truth, Dare, or Discover. Truth is a short question you may answer or pass. Dare is a funny, reversible, seated bit from a printed deck that never humiliates you. Discover is a joint micro-exploration, so you can stay in the game without performing bravery or handing over a disclosure.

The choice of mode is the mechanism. Pressure to pick the "spicy" option ends the game you came to play. A pass returns the turn with no penalty. A declined dare is complete. Discover is not a consolation prize.

## Why it might matter

Some evenings want a secret. Some want a performance. Some want a shared glance at the room you are already in: a meal you both remember, a 10-second skill, the texture of a sleeve. A game that offers only confession and stunts teaches people to equate intimacy with exposure. Adding Discover keeps curiosity available when exposure is the wrong tool.

The dare deck in this chapter is optional in a stronger sense than most party games allow. You can play an entire night on Truth and Discover and still be playing Truth, Dare, or Discover. You chose. The third pile can sit untouched and be proud of itself.

Playful affection, if you want it at all, comes from a menu you both accept before the first turn. A hand squeeze, a short compliment, a 10-second hug, sitting closer for one turn. Any item can be declined in the moment, with no replacement penalty. Sexual dares are outside the default rules. If you want a sexual version someday, that requires a separate, explicit agreement in your own words, and it can be withdrawn. This chapter does not print that deck. A yes to a truth is not a yes to a dare, and a dare is not consent to sex or to a harder dare.

Entry 013, Confession Without Consequence, is one chosen disclosure, received with curiosity, with no dare attached. Entry 081, The Forbidden Question Box, is a pile of rarely asked questions and a free pass. This chapter is a three-mode party structure. All three can exist in a life. They do different jobs.

## Before you begin

Sit. Put three piles where you can both reach them, or simply agree to read from the lists below and cross off what you use. On a call, number the items and have the chooser say a number, or send the card in text. Speech and text are equal. If the night is bright and loud in the wrong way, keep Discover at "name a texture you like," and keep dares at whisper volume.

Agree, or explicitly decline, the affection menu. Write the accepted items on one card. An empty card is a valid menu. If the card is empty, dare 12 becomes an automatic complete turn should it appear, and you move on. Read the menu aloud so both of you hear the same limits.

Who starts: flip a coin, or let the person who most recently laughed go first. Then alternate. The person whose turn it is announces the mode before drawing. They choose Truth, Dare, or Discover. Their partner does not assign the mode. After hearing the card, the chooser may pass. A passed card is set aside. It is not inherited by the partner. Passing does not cost a turn, a point, or a kiss, because this game has no points and no forfeits.

Leave alcohol out of the rules. Leave audiences out. Nothing in the deck asks you to post, perform in public, involve an ex-partner, endure pain, or mock a body. If a card feels humiliating in your particular life, treat it as declined. Declined is complete.

Long-distance favors truths and discovers. Dares stay inside the call: a silly face, a sung line, a written toast. No errands, no "go to the store," no dark-room searching, no balance feats. Every printed dare includes a seated, low-pain alternative that does not require you to get up.

## The experience

**A turn.**

1. Announce the mode.
2. Draw one card from that pile only, or pick a number your partner reads aloud if you would rather be surprised within the mode you chose. You may change mode before the card is read. After it is read, your options are do it, use the seated alternative, or pass.
3. If you pass, say "pass." The turn ends. Your partner does not answer the card for you.
4. If it is a Discover, both of you take part. It still counts as the chooser's turn. The partner's next turn remains theirs.
5. Stop when you reach the turn limit for the version you chose, or earlier if someone is done. Ending early is a clean finish.

There is no escalation. The decks do not get harder, hotter, or more revealing as the night goes on. Shuffle. There is no spicy tier hiding at the bottom.

**Affection, only from the menu.** Dare 12 points at the menu. Any other dare stays nonsexual and non-affectionate unless you both, in the moment, want to add a menu item, and either of you can still decline it. A declined hug is a completed moment. You do not owe a substitute hug, a joke, or an explanation.

**What you might print.** Three piles of 12 cards, about three by five inches, 14-point type. Truth cards are questions. Dare cards print the dare and the seated alternative on the same face. Discover cards print a joint action of 1 minute or less. One extra card titled Affection menu, mostly blank. No score column. No "level two." A pass token, if you want something physical, can be a paper clip you move back to the center after each pass, so it never accumulates into shame.

**Ending.** The game ends at the turn limit or at the first "let's stop." The last move, if you want a shared close, is one Discover you both choose from the pile, or a simple goodnight if you are tired. Do not finish on a dare. Do not add a final truth that "proves" anything. Trust is not measured by which door you used.

### Truth

Answer in a few sentences, or pass. These questions are original to this book.

1. What is a compliment you remember because you almost waved it off?
2. What small competence are you proud of that rarely gets mentioned?
3. What did you believe as a child about adulthood that turned out charmingly wrong?
4. What preference of yours has changed in the last year?
5. When, in an ordinary week, do you feel most like yourself?
6. What kindness do you hope you repeat without announcing it?
7. What sound, taste, or smell steadies you?
8. What rule do you keep for yourself that nobody else assigned?
9. What are you looking forward to that would sound minor if you said it at a party?
10. What story about you do people tell slightly wrong?
11. What do you want more of in the next month that is not a purchase?
12. What do you like about our life that you have not said aloud this week?

### Dare

Funny, reversible, and optional. The seated alternative is part of the card, not a lesser version you must apologize for using.

1. Speak in a whisper for 2 minutes. Seated alternative: the same whisper, or write the whispers and pass the note.
2. Invent a ridiculous toast to the nearest piece of furniture and deliver it from your chair. Seated alternative: write the toast and let either of you read it.
3. Swap seats. Seated alternative: swap cushions, or swap which side of the table your cup is on. On a call, lean to the other side of the frame, or rename your chair aloud. If changing seats would hurt, the alternative is the dare.
4. Narrate the next minute as a nature documentary, hushed and admiring. Seated alternative: write three documentary sentences about the room.
5. Give a sports-announcer call of pouring a glass of water. Seated alternative: type the call in three lines and send it.
6. Invent a kind, ridiculous title for your partner based on a real kindness, and use it once. "Patron of the Second Cup" is the altitude. Seated alternative: write the title on a card. A title that mocks a body, a mood, or a flaw is a declined dare, and declined is complete.
7. Hum an original tune of six notes. Seated alternative: tap the rhythm on the table or on your own knee.
8. Describe a mug, a book, or a pair of shoes in the room as if a museum had acquired it, kindly and in under 30 seconds. Seated alternative: describe a sound you can hear right now in the same museum voice.
9. Give a sincere weather report about the mood of the room. Seated alternative: write the forecast in two sentences.
10. Applaud an ordinary object for 10 seconds. Seated alternative: a quiet nod and one sentence of praise aimed at the object.
11. Tell the plot of today in exactly four words, then translate them into one ordinary sentence. Seated alternative: write the four words and the translation.
12. Offer one item from the affection menu you both already accepted. If the menu is empty or you decline, the turn is complete. Seated alternative: a compliment already on the menu, spoken from where you sit. No substitute is required if you pass.

### Discover

A Discover is something you do together, in the seats you already occupy. It is not a trail, a list of objects to collect, or a set of errands.

1. Recall a shared meal. Each of you names one detail.
2. Teach a 10-second skill: a word in another language, the way you fold a sleeve, a knot demonstrated in the air with no need to stand.
3. Describe a texture you can touch without getting up, without naming the object. The other person guesses. If guessing is tiresome, skip the guess and both name a texture you like.
4. Name a texture you like. That can be the whole discover.
5. Each name a song you both already know. Hum one line if you want. Silence after the titles still counts.
6. From where you sit, name the warmest color you can both see, and each say why it appeals, in one sentence.
7. Teach your current tea or coffee order as if your partner might order it for you tomorrow. They repeat it back. Nobody has to leave and buy it.
8. Build a two-sentence story about a polite dragon who is bad at queues. One sentence each.
9. Notice how you each hold a cup or a pen. Name one difference without ranking it.
10. Each say, in one sentence, the last time you laughed at the same thing. If neither of you can recall it, invent a laugh you would like to share later. You do not go looking through photos unless you both feel like it, and even then it is optional reminiscing, not a search with a list.
11. Name a tool you can already see without getting up, and describe its least dignified use. If nothing is in view, name a tool you used today and let it stay offstage.
12. Each offer a word you like the sound of. Put both words in one shared sentence.

## Three ways to play

**Short, 20 minutes.** Four turns each. Modes are free, including four Discovers in a row. Use the whisper dare only if you want it. Close with a goodnight or with Discover 4, a texture you like.

**Standard, 40 minutes.** Six turns each. The affection menu is settled before turn one. Passes count as turns taken, so a person who passes twice has still played. End on a Discover you choose together, or end in the middle of the six if the room is full.

**Extended, 70 minutes.** Eight turns each, with a pause around the middle for water and a check: "Do we still want the dare pile?" If the answer is no, the remaining turns are Truth and Discover only. That check is allowed to change the evening. After the last turn, do one extra Discover and stop. Do not open a second, secret deck.

## Going deeper

A deeper round stays inside Truth, and it changes the listening, not the stakes. After an answer, the listener may ask one curious question. They may not attach a dare to the answer. They may not say "prove it." If the truth was small, let it be small. "I feel most like myself on the walk between the bus and the door" does not need a childhood excavation.

You may also play a Discover-only round in which each card is done slowly, at 2 minutes instead of 30 seconds. Slowness is a form of attention. It is a poor form of courage contest, so do not keep score of who chose the "harder" mode. There is no harder mode.

If what you want is a single disclosure with a careful listener, use Confession Without Consequence. If what you want is a box of questions you both filled in advance, use The Forbidden Question Box. Come back to these three piles when the choosing itself is the part you like.

## Variations and replay

**Two-pile night.** Remove Dare from the table at the start and say so. The chapter still works. Put the pile back on another evening if you miss the toast to the furniture.

**Text-only.** Send mode, card, and response in a thread. Discover 8, the dragon, is surprisingly good in writing. Dare 3's call alternative is the lean or the renamed chair.

**Receiver's choice of card, chooser's choice of mode.** If you want more surprise, the partner can pick the number inside the mode you named. They still cannot move you from Discover to Dare. That border is the game.

**Replay.** Retire any card that landed wrong. Write a replacement in the same mode, with a seated alternative if it is a dare. Replacements that humiliate, that require an audience, or that turn Discover into a list of stops do not enter the pile. A good replacement dare can be done in a chair, undone by sitting back, and laughed about without a flinch.

The Secret Mission Exchange, entry 048, is the multi-day cousin if you want assigned kindness rather than a parlor round. It is not a dare that escaped into the week.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not you.

Their affection menu contains one item: a compliment of 10 words or fewer. The hug line is blank. They say the blank aloud so it is a decision.

Vale's first turn: "Discover." They draw the shared meal. Vale remembers orange slices on a wet park bench. Rowan remembers that the sandwiches were cut badly and eaten anyway. The turn ends. Nobody upgrades it into a confession.

Rowan's first turn: "Dare." The card is the furniture toast. Rowan stays seated and addresses a lamp. "To this lamp, which has seen us lose our keys and has never filed a report." Vale uses the quiet nod from dare 10 on a later turn, not as a punishment, as a choice. When dare 12 appears, Rowan offers a nine-word compliment about Vale's patience with a tangled charger. Vale accepts it and does not repay it. Repayment was never in the rules.

Near the end, Vale draws a truth about a childhood theory of adulthood, laughs, and says "pass." Rowan does not take the question. They play one more Discover, the polite dragon, and stop. In the fictional room the dragon is still bad at queues, and both of them are still in the game they chose, which includes the pass.

## Afterward

Name the mode you were glad existed. If it was Discover, that is a fine report. If a truth is still glowing and you want it held carefully, say whether it can be referred to later. Some truths are for the minute they were spoken.

If a dare is still funny, quote it once, kindly. If it stung, retire the card before you leave the chairs. Sting is not the point of this deck, and a card that stings has failed its job.

Nothing you did tonight is a standing permission. The menu expires when the evening ends, or when you close the call. Next time, you write it again.

## Our Story

Note the modes you chose and any card you retired. You do not have to transcribe a truth in full. A phrase is enough, and a blank is enough. Private pages are optional to share.
