## The hook

A mission, in this chapter, is a small action you hand to someone you like. You each write three: one thoughtful, one funny, one affectionate. You exchange them sealed. Over several days the receiver carries a mission out, shrinks it, or declines it. Then you sit down and compare notes, not scores.

Privacy means the assignment lives between the two of you. It does not mean one of you is watched. A declined mission is a successful use of the rules. The funny mission is playful. It is not a prank. Nothing in the default set is sexual, expensive, or owed to an audience.

## Why it might matter

Being given a specific kindness can feel different from being generally appreciated. A note on the tea tin, a radio-bulletin version of an ordinary errand, a sentence about a quality someone noticed: these are concrete. They also land on a real week, with a real body, a real budget, and a real desire to be left alone sometimes. The shrink and the pass are how the receiver stays the author of their days. The giver learns what it was like to offer care, including care that was returned unopened.

This is an action you assign. Entry 099, The Invisible Postcards, is a different instrument. Over several days you secretly collect short notes about moments your partner already helped, impressed, or delighted you, then you exchange the notes. You are gathering observations, not handing over tasks. Entry 032, The Everyday Devotion Experiment, is a stretch of days in which each of you chooses your own affectionate acts. Nobody assigns them. The Secret Mission Exchange is the one where a mission crosses the pair and the receiver may complete it, shrink it, or pass. A couple can use all three in a season. They do not replace one another.

The mischief is light. You are not building a trail of clues, a race, or a checklist of places and objects. There is no hidden monitoring, no location check, no reading of message logs to see whether the mission "really" happened. At the reunion you talk about what it was like to be given care. Performance quality is not the subject.

## Before you begin

Pick the length. Short is two days and one mission each, any tone. Standard is four days and three missions each. Extended is seven days, the three missions, and one optional encore that the receiver may request. All three lengths are multi-day. The reunion is the seated part: about 20, 30, or 45 minutes.

You need paper, a pen, and a way to seal the exchange: an envelope, a folded note, a delayed message you do not read until the hour you agreed. Agree the hour you will open, or agree that each person opens on their own schedule. Agree the reunion time now, while you are still in the same conversation. A mission without a reunion turns into homework.

Each mission has to fit the receiver's week, budget, body, and privacy. Use the fit test before you seal anything. Every answer should be yes.

- Can this happen inside the week they are already having?
- Can they do it without spending money you have not handed them, in cash or as the object itself?
- Can they do it without driving, staying up late, or lifting, unless they have already told you that particular effort is easy?
- Can they do it without an audience, a post, or an unbriefed third person?
- Is there a smaller version written on the card?
- Is the funny one free of embarrassment, mess, and "got you"?
- Would you be willing to receive this exact mission yourself?

If any answer is no, rewrite. Sensory limits belong on the card when you know them: "no strong scent," "daytime only," "text rather than a call." When you do not know, write the mission so it can be done in daylight, in quiet, without a surprise of smell or noise.

No sexual mission in the default set. No mission that outs your partner. No prank. No list of stops to visit in order. A pass, later, will be reported as a pass, not as a failed grade. Tell each other that out loud before you write, so the pass exists before anyone is tempted to be heroic.

## The experience

**Day 0, about 25 minutes, together and then private.** Sit long enough to agree the calendar. Then write apart so the missions stay sealed.

Each person produces three cards, or one card with three short blocks.

Tone: thoughtful, funny, or affectionate.
The mission, in one or two sentences.
The limits you already know.
A smaller version, already authorized.
What you hope it feels like, in one line. Hope is not a grade.

At least one of the three may be written as "receive this" rather than "perform this," for pain, fatigue, or a week that is already full. "Receive a note and do nothing else." "Be brought tea, and you do not get up." "Hear one compliment, and you are not required to deflect it or repay it." A receive-this mission is still a mission. It counts as thoughtful or affectionate, whichever it truly is.

If you go blank, adapt from the sample bank below. The bank is a set of examples, not an assignment you must use. Check the fit test even when the line looks charming. A charming mission that ignores a bad knee is a badly written card.

Seal and exchange. Do not explain your intentions yet. Do not add a verbal footnote that turns the funny card into a warning. If it needs a warning, it needs a rewrite.

**The days in between.** The receiver opens on the agreed schedule, all at once or one a day. For each mission they choose:

- Did: they carried it out, at its written size or close to it.
- Shrank: they used the smaller version, or a smaller version they invented. A note on the tea tin becomes a text. A 30-second voice bulletin becomes two sentences. Shrinking is success.
- Passed: they returned it, in a sentence, whenever they like before the reunion. "I am passing the proclamation." A pass does not require a reason. A reason is a gift, not a fee.

They may tell the giver in the moment, or save the report for the reunion. The giver does not prompt, ping, or investigate. No asking a friend whether the note appeared. No treating silence as a clue to be followed. Silence until the reunion is an allowed pace.

The giver, during these days, does their own receiving. You are both on missions. There is no handler and no agent in the field.

**Sample bank.** Original examples. Rewrite the details so they fit a real week.

Thoughtful:

- Leave a note on the tea tin, or on the place they first stand in the morning, naming a quality you noticed this week. Smaller: send the sentence by text in daylight.
- Set out the thing they reach for first, then leave. Smaller: name the thing in a message so they can set it out themselves.
- Send one sentence about a difficulty you know they are carrying, with no advice attached. Smaller: "I remember this is a heavy week," and nothing more.
- Receive-this: a glass of water and the show they rewatch, with you out of the room if they want solitude.

Funny:

- Retell an ordinary errand as a radio bulletin in a voice message under 30 seconds. Smaller: two sentences in text, still in bulletin voice.
- Rename three leftovers, or three ordinary objects, as if they were on a grand hotel menu. Smaller: rename one.
- Issue a one-sentence proclamation that the dishes, or the inbox, have been pardoned for the evening. Smaller: pardon one mug.
- Narrate making toast, or pouring water, as breaking news. Smaller: a headline only.

Affectionate:

- A handwritten sentence about a quality you noticed, left where they will find it in daylight. Smaller: the same sentence aloud, once, easy to nod at and keep moving.
- Offer a 10-second hug once, easy to decline. Smaller, and fully sufficient: a hand squeeze, or no touch at all, replaced by the sentence "I am glad you are here." Declining the hug completes the affectionate mission.
- Sit nearby for 10 minutes while they do something ordinary, without improving it. Smaller: 3 minutes, or a message that says you are thinking of them at an agreed hour without requiring a reply.
- A voice note that says only "I like how you ___," with a true detail. Smaller: the same words in writing.

Long-distance, still not a trail:

- A mailed note that will arrive when it arrives. The mission is the sending, and the receiver may pass before it arrives by saying so.
- A parallel action: both make a warm drink at an agreed hour and, if you want, describe the mug. Skip photos if location feels exposed. A description is enough.
- A voice message the receiver plays when they choose.

**A tiny log, not a chart of virtue.** For each mission, the receiver may jot:

Date opened | tone | did, shrank, or passed | one line about being given it

The line is about the experience of being offered care. "The pardon made me laugh." "The hug was too much on Tuesday, and the sentence was right." "I passed the bulletin because my voice was gone." None of these lines is a grade. You do not assign points, streaks, or a winner. Incomplete is not a category you need. Passed and shrank already cover it.

**Reunion.** Receiver speaks first, one mission at a time.

1. Did, shrank, or passed.
2. What it was like to be given that card.
3. Anything they want retired next time.

Then the giver speaks.

1. What they hoped the mission would feel like.
2. What the response taught them.
3. If it was a pass, a thank-you for the pass. No makeup mission unless the receiver asks for one.

Go through all the cards this way. If a mission involved touch, a pass or a flinch still ends in thanks, not in a second attempt that evening. Touch in this chapter is never the price of the game. The default affectionate mission can always be words.

**Ending.** Each of you names one mission you would like again, or says "none this round." The game ends when every mission has been reported as did, shrank, or passed. You do not leave one card in a mysterious unfinished state so the mischief can linger. Lingering, here, would feel like surveillance. Close the envelopes. Keep a card only if both of you like it as a memento.

## Three ways to play

**Short, two days, 20-minute reunion.** On day 0, each of you writes one mission. Agree the tone in advance if you want both to be funny, or let each giver choose. Open on day 1. Reunion on day 2, even if the mission was passed in the first hour. Report it anyway. A two-day pass is a complete short game.

**Standard, four days, 30-minute reunion.** Three missions each, sealed on day 0. The receiver opens at their pace across the next few days. Reunion on day 4 or on the morning of day 5 if the week slipped, which it may. Slipping the reunion by a day is still the standard game. Skipping the reunion is the thing to avoid. If you must delay, set the new time before you hang up.

**Extended, seven days, 45-minute reunion.** The same three missions, with more air around them. Midweek, either of you may send a one-line check that is not a status demand: "Still a good week for these?" The other person may say "yes," "shrink everything," or "pause until reunion." On the last day, the receiver may request one encore, built from a mission they actually liked, at the same size or smaller. The giver may also pass on creating an encore. No encore is required to complete the week. The reunion includes the encore only if it happened.

## Going deeper

Depth is specificity, not difficulty. In a deeper round, the thoughtful mission must refer to something true of this month, not a generic romance. "You have been carrying the Tuesday appointment" beats "you are amazing." The funny mission must be shareable in the reunion without a wince. The affectionate mission must include its smaller version on the same card, so a tired day is already provided for.

You may add, on each card, "A version that is receive-only." That gives the receiver two legal sizes plus a pass. More options make refusal easier to say, which makes acceptance mean more.

Do not deepen the week by making the missions grander, more public, or more like tests of devotion. Grandeur is how a kind note becomes a production. If what you want is to notice what your partner already does, The Invisible Postcards is the fitting chapter. If what you want is to choose your own acts without receiving an assignment, The Everyday Devotion Experiment is the fitting one. Play this chapter when the gift is the assignment, and the freedom inside it is the right to shrink or return it.

## Variations and replay

**One tone.** A funny-only week, or an affectionate-only week, if three tones feel like too many costumes. Say so on day 0. The fit test remains.

**Parallel distance.** You never see the execution. You hear about it at the reunion. This variation suits pairs who like a reveal and pairs who do not want to be observed.

**Receiver drafts the limit line.** Before day 0 ends, each of you writes limits the other must respect: bedtime, scent, budget, no surprise guests, no voice notes after 8. The giver writes inside those limits. This is often kinder than guessing.

**Replay.** Once in a season is plenty for many pairs. When you play again, read the retired lines first. A mission that delighted in April may be noise in November. Let November win. Keep one card that aged well, if you have one, and rewrite the other two from scratch.

Entry 047, Truth, Dare, or Discover, is a same-evening game with a pass inside the hour. This one spreads the pass across days. Use both if you like both clocks.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not you.

On a Sunday they seal three missions each and agree to a Thursday reunion. Vale's thoughtful card for Rowan says: "Receive a note on the tea tin that names a quality from this week. You do not have to perform anything. Smaller: I will text the sentence if the tin is a bad place. Daytime. No scent." The hope line says, "I hope it feels like being seen on an ordinary morning."

Rowan opens it Tuesday, reads a sentence about the way Rowan talks to a worried neighbor, and leaves the note on the counter because the tin is empty. Shrank, slightly, by moving the paper. The log line: "It was good to be caught being decent when I thought nobody was scoring decency." They will say at reunion that they do not want a score. Vale will agree, and will be glad the sentence landed.

Vale's funny card: "Send a radio bulletin, under 30 seconds, about your walk to the mailbox." Rowan passes on Wednesday. "Voice is tired. Passing the bulletin." Vale does not send a follow-up. At reunion Vale says, "Thank you for the pass," and means it. The fictional pass is not a crack in the week.

Rowan's affectionate card for Vale: "A 10-second hug on a day you choose, easy to decline. Smaller: the sentence 'I like how you read instructions when I am already guessing,' spoken once." Vale takes the sentence on Thursday morning and declines the hug because the day is hot and touch is too much. The decline completes the mission. Rowan does not offer a second hug at the reunion to "finish." It is already finished.

They do not check each other's phones to confirm the bulletin was never recorded. There is nothing to confirm. The report was the game.

## Afterward

Thank each other for one thing: a mission that fit, a shrink that showed good sense, or a pass that kept the week honest. If you want a repeat, name the tone, not a permanent program. "Another funny one next month" is enough. A weekly quota would turn care into a chart, and this chapter is not a chart.

If a mission uncovered a preference you did not know, write it where you will see it before the next exchange. "No voice notes after 8." "Tea-tin notes are welcome." Preferences are the real souvenir.

A mission is not consent to touch, to spend money, or to be watched. The reunion closes the file.
