## The hook

Courage, in this chapter, is a practical size. A phone call you have delayed. A question asked of a clerk. Four sentences read aloud. A form started and stopped after the first box. The person who feels the difficulty sets the ceiling. The other person takes a role that was agreed in advance: stay close, read the plan back once, or celebrate at the end. Pushing is not a role. A smaller completed version counts. Stopping counts.

## Why it might matter

Doing a hard ordinary thing with a companion is an old custom. The companion can make the thing more possible, and the companion can also add pressure without meaning to. Naming the role beforehand keeps encouragement from turning into a shove. You find out, in the debrief, which moves helped and which moves raised the temperature.

The Trust Walk (021) is guided movement with covered eyes. The First-Time Challenge (065) is something new to both of you, followed by a look at the hour. The Comfort Zone Exchange (067) is a hosted beginner slice. Stay here when the task is a chosen challenge, manageable on purpose, with teamwork written down.

This chapter claims no study. It does not measure bravery, and it does not repair trust by itself. Clinical care for phobias, panic, or trauma is a different practice. This chapter does not describe that practice. If a fear is bound up with trauma or panic, choose a milder everyday task from the bank below, or pass.

## Before you begin

Each of you proposes two challenges that are actually manageable. Then you choose one for this sitting. The person who feels the difficulty has the final cut, sets the ceiling, and sets the smaller success. If you both feel it, the lower ceiling wins.

Either of you may veto a proposal. "Enough," or whatever stop word you chose, ends the attempt. Encouragement stops when the person says stop. Completing less than planned is a valid result. Say this sentence when you stop, for any reason: "This is the version we completed."

Passing the whole chapter is a complete choice. Changing a prompt is allowed. A safety no does not need an explanation. No filming. No audience. No surprise. The challenge is on the card before it starts. Heights, water, crowds, and driving are not required, and they stay off the card unless you both want them after a plain description, which this chapter does not ask you to want. Sexual tasks are off the card. Medical or legal trouble that needs a professional stays off the card. Humiliation is off the card.

Seated and home-based challenges belong in the main list. Social courage, sensory courage, and the courage to ask for a break all count. For chronic pain, a manageable challenge might be one sentence that asks for a pause. For overload, it might be leaving a room 10 minutes earlier than usual, with the exit already planned.

If you live apart, the partner can stay on the phone during a local challenge. Agree what happens if the call drops. The default is that the attempt pauses until you reconnect. You do not continue the hard part alone unless the card already says you will. A text that says "paused" is enough.

Touch is not part of the role. A hand on a shoulder waits for a separate yes, and the yes can be withdrawn. Finishing the challenge is not an agreement to be touched.

## The experience

**The courage card.** One card for the challenge you actually attempt.

- The challenge, in one sentence.
- Who feels the difficulty. They set the ceiling. If both do, write "lower ceiling wins."
- The ceiling: the most you will do.
- The smaller success, which counts as complete.
- The stop word.
- The partner's role, circled: quiet presence, read the plan back once, or celebrate at the end.
- The time box.
- How you will stop, including the sentence "This is the version we completed."

**A bank you may borrow.** Use these only if the person who feels the difficulty accepts the scale. Shrink them further if they are still too big.

- Dial a delayed call, say your name, ask one question, and hang up. Smaller success: dial and reach a person or a recording.
- Ask a clerk one question, then leave. Smaller success: enter, ask where an item is, and leave.
- Walk one flat block and turn around. Smaller success: the end of the hall, or the doorway and back.
- Read four sentences aloud to your partner. Smaller success: two sentences.
- Cook a simple plate for a guest who is only your partner, using foods you both eat. Smaller success: the plate assembled, heat optional.
- Ask for a break in a sentence you rehearse. Smaller success: the sentence said once, at home, to your partner.
- Start a form and stop after the first box. Smaller success: the form opened and the first field read aloud.
- Play a few minutes of a beginner class video, with the pause button and the door as exits. Smaller success: the video started and stopped on purpose.

**Choose.** Write two proposals each, on paper or in notes. Scale them until they feel almost too ordinary. The person who will feel the difficulty picks the challenge and fills the ceiling and the smaller success. The partner agrees to one role and may decline a role they cannot play kindly. If they decline, pick another role or another challenge. Pushing, cheering past a stop, and "just one more minute" are not available roles.

Say the stop word once, in an ordinary voice, before you begin, so it is not new in the moment.

**Attempt.** Stay inside the time box. The partner does the circled role and then stops doing things. Quiet presence means present and unbusy: same room, or same call, without extra advice. Reading the plan back once means reading the ceiling and the smaller success aloud, once, then silence. Celebrating at the end means a few warm sentences after the attempt, not a commentary during it.

If the stop word appears, the attempt ends. No negotiation in the same minute. Use the completed-version sentence. If you reach the smaller success and want to stop there, that is a finish. If you reach the ceiling, stop at the ceiling even if the mood is good. The ceiling was the consent.

**Debrief.** What did the partner do that helped? What added pressure? Do you want to repeat, shrink, or leave it? The person who felt the difficulty answers first.

## Three ways to play

**Short, 20 minutes.** Fill the card and do only the smaller success. The time box is 10 minutes plus the debrief. The full ceiling can wait for another day, or it can retire.

**Standard, 40 minutes.** Two proposals each, one chosen challenge, the card, the attempt inside a time box of about 15 minutes, and the debrief. One challenge is the whole session.

**Extended, 70 minutes.** Keep one challenge. Use the extra time for a slower approach, a rehearsal of the sentence or the call, and a longer debrief. A second challenge can be booked, not stacked, if you still have appetite. Book it with a day and a ceiling. If the first attempt was enough, the extended hour can be tea and the debrief. Adding a harder stunt in the extra minutes breaks the chapter.

## Going deeper

The debrief can hold one more question. What did I almost say that would have been pressure?

Many companions reach for "you can do it" at the exact moment the person needs the exit described again. If that happened, say it without a trial. The companion can say "I wanted to push, and I did not," which is its own kind of teamwork. The person who felt the difficulty can say which sentence helped, in case you repeat the challenge later at the same size or smaller.

You can also decide the challenge was a poor choice: too big, too public, too close to something that needs a professional. Thank the card for making that visible, and retire it. Retiring a challenge is a result.

## Variations and replay

Replay only if the person who feels the difficulty wants a repeat. Shrink before you enlarge. A repeat uses a fresh card, even if the words are similar.

Home-based replays are complete versions of the chapter. So are seated ones. A social challenge can move from "ask the clerk" to "ask your partner," and it still counts. The long-distance version is the phone beside a local task, with the drop plan written on the card.

The Trust Walk remains the chapter for guided movement and covered eyes. This card can live beside it on another day. It does not borrow that chapter's method.

Leave filming, grading, and surprise off every replay. A smaller finish written at the top of the card is the success you promised to count.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not you.

Rowan has been delaying a call to a clinic about a bill. The ceiling Rowan sets is: dial, say Rowan's name, ask one question, hang up if the menu is endless. The smaller success is: dial and reach a person or a recording. The stop word is "yellow." Vale's role is quiet presence, in the room, not on speaker unless Rowan asks. They say "yellow" once before the call, in ordinary voices, and then Rowan dials.

The hold music goes on. Rowan says "yellow" and hangs up. Vale feels the sentence "just one more minute" arrive and does not say it. They use the finish line. "This is the version we completed." The smaller success happened. The ceiling did not, and the ceiling does not get to sulk.

In the debrief, Rowan says the help was Vale's silence and the fact that the phone was not on speaker. The pressure was the sentence Vale swallowed, which Rowan could see on Vale's face. Vale says, "I wanted to push, and I did not, and my face still pushed." They do not repeat the call that night. They book nothing. The card goes in a drawer. It was enough courage for a Tuesday.

## Afterward

Put the card where you can find it if you want a repeat, or recycle it if the challenge is retired. Thank each other for the size you actually did.

If you want a last pass, use these.

- Was the ceiling honest?
- Did the role stay in its lane?
- Repeat, shrink, or leave it?

Then do something easy on purpose. Tea, a window, a show you already like. The experiment is over when you say it is over.

## Our Story

Private pages are optional to share. The content of a hard call or a fear can stay off the shared page. Say so if you need that, then treat it as ordinary.

Date the entry. Note the version, the role you used, and whether you finished, shrank, or stopped.
