## The hook

A finished thing is a pleasure. A finished thing with no memory of how you treated each other while making it is just an object. This chapter asks for both: something you can point at when you are done, and a midpoint where you notice who is leading, who is helping, and what you do when the thing collapses, burns, tears, or looks like a hat.

The object may be humble. A bridge made of junk mail that holds a spoon for three seconds is a completed build. A plate you assembled without heat is a completed build. An imperfect result with the conversation attached finishes the chapter. A perfect result with silence where the conversation should be does not.

## Why it might matter

Making something side by side shows the working style you use when there is a task in the room. Some pairs talk the whole time. Some pairs go quiet and efficient. Some pairs have one person take the tape while the other is still in the middle of a sentence. None of that is a personality test. Who chopped, who directed, who held the paper: those are facts about this sitting. They are not a biography.

The Relationship Escape Room (103) is a cooperative puzzle built from your history and your skills. This chapter ends with an object, or a plate, and with a look at the collaboration. The First-Time Challenge (065) reflects on novelty. The Night of Random Decisions (068) lets a toy pick among accepted options. Stay here when you want to make a thing and watch the making.

No study is claimed for cooking together, for paper engineering, or for what your kitchen behavior means.

## Before you begin

Choose one project and finish it in this sitting. Allergies, aversions, and safety come before shopping or before you open the fridge. Name them out loud. Knives, heat, and anything tall are optional. The printed projects below do not need them. Strong smells and a loud kitchen are optional. If either is wrong tonight, use the paper bridge and leave the food for another day.

Pick roles, and keep them swappable. Reader: read one step, then wait. Maker: do that step. Switch every two steps, or sooner if someone asks. Nobody is the apprentice unless they agree to that word, and you do not need the word. Either of you may change roles in the middle. A takeover without asking is allowed to be noticed. It is not allowed to be punished with a speech about someone's character.

"Pause" stops the build or the talk. Passing a question is a complete answer. You may change a prompt. Pain means a shorter project and a chair. Low vision can use the paper bridge by touch, with the other person narrating each step before it happens. If hearing is hard, the steps stay written on the counter in large type. Fine-motor trouble uses large sheets, bags, or torn food, not tiny folds. Seated prep is the default.

If you live apart, build the same paper bridge on a call and compare at the end. Cameras are optional. One person can narrate while the other works from the written steps. You still pause at the midpoint. You still answer what happened when it went sideways.

Agree the smaller success before you start. For the bridge, the smaller success is a span the width of one hand and a conversation at the midpoint, whether or not the spoon stays. For the plate, the smaller success is food on a plate that you both can eat, plus the same conversation.

## The experience

Do one project. The bridge is the default because it fails in useful ways and asks for no stove. The plate is the alternative when you want to cook or assemble a meal.

**Roles, said out loud.** Name the first Reader and the first Maker. Set a visible note: switch after step 2, and again after step 4. If a step is dangerous for one person, that person is the Reader for that step. Heat, if you use it at all, belongs to whoever wants the pan.

**The spoon bridge.**

Materials: about 12 sheets of paper, newspaper, or junk mail. Large pieces if small folds are hard. A grocery bag cut open is a legal sheet. Tape, clips, or a tucked corner. Two shores: stacks of books, boxes, or bowls. One light load: a spoon, a pen, or a folded sock.

1. Set the shores one hand-span apart. A hand-span is the width of one person's hand, fingers together, measured between the inside edges of the shores. This distance is the smaller success. You may widen it later by a finger-width, and only if you both want to.
2. Make two beams. Roll two sheets into tubes and fasten them, or fold each sheet into a thick strip if rolling is hard. Switch roles after this step if you are due.
3. Lay the beams across the gap. Lay one flat sheet on top as the deck.
4. Place the load gently in the middle. Count three seconds. However it went, stop. Do not rebuild yet.

**Midpoint, both of you.** Answer these before any repair.

- Who is leading right now?
- Who is assisting?
- What did each of us do when it went wrong? If it has not gone wrong yet, what did we do when we were unsure?
- Did we consult, or did someone take over?

Then name one change together. One. Rebuild once with that change. Test again. Look at the bridge, including a heap that did not survive. Say, "This is what we made." Then answer: when it went sideways, what did you each do?

You are done when you have looked and answered. The spoon does not have to stay for the chapter to be complete.

**The shared plate.** Use this instead of the bridge, not as a second project in the same sitting, unless you are playing the extended version and you still have attention left for the questions.

Before you touch food, list what you both can eat. Skip strong smells if either of you needs a quiet nose. Knives stay in the drawer unless you both want them.

The plate has three layers, all tearable or spoonable: a base (bread, rice you already have, yogurt, or leaves), a soft topping, and a crunch only if you both want it. Salt only if you both want it.

The first Maker builds the base. Switch. The next Maker adds the topping. Before any extra ingredient, ask. The midpoint is after the base is down and before the topping. Ask the same four questions. Then finish the plate, look at it, and answer what you each did when something went sideways. If nothing went sideways, say what you did when you were unsure about a taste.

**Heat, optional.** Only if you both want a pan. Warm the base for about 2 minutes in a little oil you both eat. The person at the pan may ask the other to watch the clock. If it burns, that is the sideways moment. Eat the unburned part, or switch to the no-heat plate. You are still done if you answer the questions.

## Three ways to play

**Short, 30 minutes.** Build the spoon bridge at one hand-span. Keep the midpoint and the final sideways question. Skip any widening of the gap. If food is the short version, assemble the plate with two ingredients and the same pause.

**Standard, 60 minutes.** One project, role switches, midpoint, one rebuild or one topping, the look, and the sideways answers. Leave the second project unstarted.

**Extended, 90 minutes.** You may do the bridge and then the plate, in that order, if energy remains. Each project gets its own midpoint. If the second project would turn the evening into a performance, stop after the first. A longer single project also fits: widen the bridge once, or add a third layer to the plate, and keep the debrief longer than the decorating.

## Going deeper

After the sideways question, you can ask what you want the other person to do the next time you make something. Specific is better than global. "Wait until I finish the sentence before you take the tape" is a request. "Be less controlling" is a fog.

If the conversation gets tender or sharp, shrink it to the last 20 minutes. You are allowed to say "that stung" about a takeover. You are also allowed to laugh at the hat-shaped bridge and keep the hat. Insults about clumsiness are out. The object is allowed to be bad. The collaboration notes are the part you came for.

A silent build can be restarted by reading the four questions aloud. If you still do not want to answer, the chapter is paused, and the object can sit there without a moral.

## Variations and replay

Replay with the other project, or with a third one-sitting make you both can finish: a one-page comic with four panels, a repaired hem with large stitches, a cushion fort that stays low, a playlist written on a handmade cover card. Match the tools to the skills in the room. If a project needs a specialist, it is the wrong project.

Low energy uses the plate or a four-panel comic. Pain uses a chair and the short version. No-heat and no-knife are already in the main instructions. The long-distance version is the same bridge on a call, compared at the end, cameras optional.

The Night of Random Decisions can choose which project you build only if both projects were acceptable before the roll. Otherwise, choose while you are still allowed to talk.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not you.

They build the spoon bridge out of old envelopes. Rowan is Reader first. Vale rolls a sloppy tube. At step 4 the spoon falls through. Rowan, who was supposed to wait, takes the tape and starts a second tube while Vale is still saying "what if we fold instead." The bridge becomes a heap.

Vale says, "You took the tape while I was still talking." Rowan says, "I did." They sit in it for a second. Then they laugh once, a small laugh, at the heap, not at each other. The midpoint answers are plain. Rowan was leading and then took over. Vale was assisting and wanted to be consulted. The one change they name is: no hands on the tape unless the Maker asks.

The second bridge holds the spoon for three seconds and looks like a bad hat. They say, "This is what we made." They keep the hat on the counter until morning. Nobody decides who is the controlling one in the relationship. They decide that, in this sitting, the tape changed owners too fast.

## Afterward

Leave the object where you can see it for the rest of the evening, if that is pleasant. Throw it out if it is in the way. The chapter survives either choice.

If you want a last pass, use these.

- Where did consulting work?
- Where did speed beat courtesy, and do we want that trade again?
- What is the next thing small enough to finish in one sitting?

Then let the roles expire.
