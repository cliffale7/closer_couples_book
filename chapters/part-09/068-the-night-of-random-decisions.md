## The hook

Choosing dinner by coin toss is a folk art. The coin is funny because both dinners were already acceptable, and because someone still groans when their secret favorite loses. This chapter stretches that custom across one evening. Chance gets three decisions. You two get the menus, the veto, and the right to stop. The die is a toy. It does not know you, and it does not get the last word.

## Why it might matter

A little randomness can knock a rut without handing the night to impulse. The pleasure, when it comes, is the click of a choice you both can live with and neither of you had to negotiate for 10 minutes. The pinch, when it comes, is the click of a choice you can live with and did not want. Telling those two feelings apart is the debrief.

Would You Rather: Unhinged Edition (042) poses hypothetical pairs. Truth, Dare, or Discover (047) has a dare among its doors. Tonight the randomizer picks among real options you have already accepted. It assigns no dare, no destiny, and no verdict. The Twenty-Dollar Adventure (066) is the chapter where a money ceiling is the design problem. If a menu line costs money, the price is written before the roll, inside a cap you set. The default cap is $0 beyond what is already in the house.

No study is claimed for spontaneity, coin tosses, or evenings like this.

## Before you begin

Build the menus before anyone rolls, flips, or draws. Every option has to be acceptable to both of you. Read each line aloud, or put it in text large enough to read, before it is locked. Hearing and sight both get a channel. If an option is livable for only one of you, it comes off the menu. Blaming the die later is out of bounds, because the die only met options you signed.

Each of you gets one veto for the whole night. A veto cancels that result, puts the choice back on the menu, and makes you roll again. The option stays available. If the reroll repeats it, you do not get a second token. A safety no handles that repeat, and any other result you refuse. A safety no spends nothing. The decision becomes "stay in," unless you say "stop the game," which ends the night. You do not have to justify a safety no.

"Stay in" and "stop the game" are faces that can come up. They are real options, written on the activity menu.

Alcohol, sex, unplanned spending, and other people stay off the default menus. Adding any of them requires a separate explicit agreement, written on the menu before the roll. A lucky number is not that agreement. A rolled result is not consent to touch.

Keep every menu inside the bodies you have tonight. Include at least one seated option, one quiet option, one low-cost option, and one low-sensory option. Mobility and transit are filters before the roll. If a line needs a vehicle, a crowd, or a flight of stairs, it is off unless you both still want it after you picture the return trip. Fatigue is a design constraint: the night is three decisions, not an all-nighter.

If you live apart, build one shared set of menus on a call. Randomize a film you both can start, a topic, and a snack each of you already has. Use the same veto rules. If the call drops, the night pauses until you reconnect, or you end it.

## The experience

Pick one randomizer and keep it all night, so nobody switches tools after seeing an outcome.

**Die.** Number the six options on a menu from 1 to 6. Roll once. A cocked die, or a die on the floor, is rolled again. That reroll is still the same decision, not a veto.

**Cards.** Write the six options on six cards. One of you shuffles. The other cuts. Draw one card and show it to both. Put the card back afterward, including after a veto. The option remains in the deck.

**Coin.** Flip three times. Call them in order: first, second, third. Heads is H. Tails is T.

- HHH is 1
- HHT is 2
- HTH is 3
- HTT is 4
- THH is 5
- THT is 6
- TTH is blank; flip the set again
- TTT is blank; flip the set again

**Phone.** Use this only if you have no die, no coin, and no cards. One person generates a random integer from 1 to 6 and shows the screen to both before anyone comments. One generation is the decision. A disliked number is not a reason to generate again. Dislike spends a veto or uses a safety no.

**Veto tokens.** Two small objects, one each. A cap, a ring, a scrap that says veto. To spend it, put it in the center and name the result you refuse. That roll is cancelled. The option goes back on the menu. Roll again with the full menu. You cannot spend a second token. If the same option returns, use a safety no.

**Three menus, six slots each.** You may adapt the words. You may not roll until both of you have marked every remaining line as acceptable.

Activity:

1. A card game you both already like, for 20 minutes, or a game whose rules are already in the house.
2. One episode of a show you have both already agreed is fine.
3. A walk to the mailbox, the end of the hall, or another flat turnaround you name in the slot.
4. 10 photos from an album or a camera roll you both are willing to open.
5. Stay in and do nothing extra.
6. Stop the game.

Food or drink:

1. Tea, or water.
2. Toast or bread with something you both eat.
3. Fruit you both eat.
4. Leftovers you both eat.
5. A snack already in the house that you both eat.
6. The simplest thing already in the house.

Conversation or music:

1. A playlist you both already like, at a volume you name now.
2. One song each, chosen from music you both tolerate.
3. Silence for 10 minutes.
4. A page you already own, read aloud or passed in text.
5. One question: what was a good 10 minutes this week?
6. Quiet, no prompt.

Strike any line that fails tonight. Replace it with another line you both accept. If you cannot fill a slot, copy "stay in," "the simplest thing already in the house," or "quiet" into the empty slot so the numbers still run from 1 to 6. Write a price on any line that costs money. Unknown prices are off the menu. The default spending cap is $0 beyond what is already home.

**The night, in order.** Roll the activity menu. Do that result for up to 40 minutes, or until it ends. If you roll "stop the game," stop. You may spend a veto to refuse that stop and roll again, and you may also stop without a roll, at any time, for free. Roll the food menu and have that food or drink. Roll the conversation or music menu during the food or just after. Then end. Three rolls is a complete night.

**Debrief, a few minutes.** Where did the constraint feel like play? Where did it pinch? A pinch is "I would have preferred toast, and leftovers were still fine." A no is "this is not happening," and it does not require the token.

## Three ways to play

**Short, 40 minutes.** Lock the three menus. Play all three rolls at a small size: an activity of about 10 minutes, a small bite, and one song or one question. Use the same veto and the same safety no. Ask where it played and where it pinched.

**Standard, 2 hours.** Menus, three rolls, an activity up to 40 minutes, the food, the sound or the talk, and the debrief. Let the evening have some unstructured minutes between rolls. You do not fill them with extra decisions.

**Extended, 3 hours.** After the third roll, you may build one extra menu of six, including "no extra" and "stop the game." One more roll, then stop. If either of you is tired, skip the fourth roll. Three rolls still count as a finished night. The veto you already spent stays spent. If you have not spent it, it still works on the fourth roll. The safety no remains free.

## Going deeper

The useful conversation is the difference between a groan and a limit. Chance is fun when the menu was honest. It turns sour when a line was accepted out of politeness and then came up. If that happened, say so, take the line off any future menu, and skip the lecture about being easygoing.

You can also notice who reaches for the token first, and whether the other person felt glad. A veto that saves the night is a successful rule. A veto that gets teased is a broken rule. Teasing a no, a token, or a safety stop ends the game.

## Variations and replay

Replay with new menus. Keep "stay in" and "stop the game" on the activity list every time.

A quiet replay makes slots 3, 5, and 6 on the sound menu into different kinds of quiet: dim lights, a closed laptop, a window. A low-pain replay keeps every activity seated. A low-sensory replay strikes music, strong food smells, and photos if the camera roll is a flood. Write those strikes before the roll.

The long-distance replay is the shared film, the topic, and the snack you each already have. High-contrast dice, large type, or a spoken number cover low vision. Reading every option in text covers a night when someone cannot hear the list. If the randomizer itself is hard to see, lock the phone method, in large type, at the start.

Would You Rather remains the chapter for imaginary forks. This night follows a list you wrote while you were still calm.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not you.

They lock the starter menus after striking the camera roll, because Vale does not want the last year of photos on the table. Slot 4 becomes "two chapters of a comic you both already like, on the couch." They choose a die. The first roll is 3, the mailbox walk. It is cold. They go to the mailbox and back, which takes 6 minutes, and they count that as the activity because the slot never promised a pilgrimage.

The food roll is 4, leftovers. Rowan wanted toast, feels the pinch, says so, and does not call it a no. The leftovers are fine. The third roll is 2, one song each, played low. While building the menu, Vale replaced silence with "sing one chorus in the kitchen," and both of them accepted it. It does not come up. In the debrief, the pinch was the leftovers. The play was the mailbox, boring in a way they both liked. Vale still has the veto, and says the chorus leaves the next menu: "I accepted that to be game, and I do not want it." They cross it off. Three rolls, and the night is over.

## Afterward

Put the tokens back in a drawer. Leave the menus until you have said where the night played and where it pinched.

If you want a last pass, use these.

- Which option should never have been eligible?
- Which result would we repeat on purpose, with no die at all?
- Did three decisions feel like enough?

Then make one ordinary choice the die does not get: when to sleep, or when to say goodnight.
