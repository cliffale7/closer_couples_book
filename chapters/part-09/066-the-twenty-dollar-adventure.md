## The hook

Twenty dollars is a ridiculous budget for a memory and a generous budget for an hour that has a shape. The ceiling is the game. It forces the two of you to invent the outing instead of buying a larger one. You might have a memorable time. You might have a perfectly nice time that you would not retell. Both results are allowed, and you only find out which one you had by going and then asking.

## Why it might matter

A plan you both design is a different object from a plan one person presents. Under a small ceiling, the interesting moves are usually free: a room, a question, a story, a page, a bench, a song you already own. Cooperation shows up when a cost gets cut and the hour still needs a middle.

The Twelve-Hour Vacation (071) shapes a day with an opening and a closing. The Night of Random Decisions (068) lets chance pick among options you already accepted. Stay here when the design problem is the money, and when both of you hold the pencil.

This chapter claims no study. Memorable is a question you answer afterward. It is not a guarantee, and it is not a score.

## Before you begin

Write the ceiling at the top of the page before you fall in love with a plan. The named ceiling is $20, in cash or in a card payment you both can see. A local equivalent is fine: the amount you both agree is the same kind of small, written as a number. The $0 version sits beside that title as a full adventure. Same four slots. Every price written as zero. If money is tight, the zero plan is the same game with a stricter rule. It is quiet about income, virtue, and generosity.

Each of you may veto one cost and one distance before you leave the planning table. A veto is one sentence. "I veto the bus." "I veto the second pastry." You replace the line together. You do not audit anyone's character.

Either of you may end the outing by saying "done." You do not owe the unused minutes. Passing a debrief question is a complete answer. Pause if you need to pause.

Name dietary needs, pain, weather, and sensory load while you are still sitting down. Transit is a filter, not a surprise. If a fare is unknown, that ride is unavailable. If a shop is too far, too loud, or too bright, the plan stays home. Seated counts. Horizontal counts. A window counts as a view. Strong smells and crowds stay off the plan unless you both wrote them in.

No surprise may spend the other person's money or ask for a body they did not agree to use. One place is the shape. You may add a single errand and the table you return to. You may not add a third stop. The free surprise is a story, a view, a playlist, or a question. It costs nothing.

## The experience

The plan has four lines, and both of you build it.

1. **Place.** A room, a window, a bench, a library table, or one ride you have already priced.
2. **Shared activity.** One thing you do together, long enough to be the middle of the hour.
3. **Food or drink.** Something, or nothing. Write the price. Water you already have is a legal feast.
4. **Free surprise.** One story, one view, one short playlist, or one question. The price is zero, and it stays zero.

**How you design it.** Take 15 minutes. Each of you drafts one plan with those four lines and a price on every line that could cost money. Read both drafts. Steal freely. The merged plan has to add up to the ceiling or less. Use the price on the item in your hand, or the fare printed by the transit agency. If a number would break the ceiling, that item stays in the shop and the plan continues without it.

Then take the two vetoes if you want them. Lock the plan by reading it once. Do that plan. When you come back, ask two questions. What did the ceiling force us to invent? Did memorable happen: yes, no, or partly?

If you would rather leave the house, or the sofa, with a plan already in your pocket, use one of these. Both of you still have to say yes to every line. Change the prices to the real ones. If the real sum breaks the ceiling, drop the paid line.

**The room visit, $0.** Place: one room, lights the way you like them. Activity: 20 minutes as visitors. Each of you points out three things as if the other person has never seen the room. Food: water, or something you both eat that is already there, priced at $0. Free surprise: one true story each, told in under 2 minutes, starting with a cheap good thing. Shoes on or off. Sitting, lying down, or standing at the window all qualify.

**The corner fruit.** Place: the nearest shop you can both reach without blowing the distance veto, then home. If the shop fails the distance, the weather, or the body, use fruit you already have and reprice the plan at $0. Activity: choose one piece of fruit you both eat, bring it home, and split it. If knives are unwelcome, tear it or take bites. Price: the shelf price. If that single price is over the ceiling, put it back and finish the hour at the table with water and the free surprise. Free surprise: each of you names a meal you ate together that cost little and still shows up in memory. If no meal comes to mind, name a cheap smell you like and leave it there.

**The priced ride.** Look up two fares before you stand up. If two fares fit under the ceiling, and the distance is acceptable to both, ride one stop or one loop and come back. Pack water from home. Activity: on the way, each of you names one thing the place is doing that has nothing to do with you. A bus, a train, a ferry, or a free shuttle all fit if the number fits. Free surprise: write one question on scrap paper before you board, and ask it once, after you sit down. If either fare is unknown, this plan is closed. You do not guess a fare and hope.

**The page.** Place: a library branch you can both use, or the books already in the house if the branch is far, closed, loud, or too much. Activity: each of you picks one page. Read it aloud, or sit with it in silence if reading aloud is the wrong channel. Cost: $0. If a library card, a printout, or a fare would cost money, it has to fit the ceiling or it is out. Free surprise: each of you speaks a title for a book that does not exist. You keep the titles. You do not buy anything to house them.

**Apart.** Postage counts toward the ceiling. One flat envelope, one stamp, a drawn map of your room or a recipe card, and then you stop. If the stamp breaks the ceiling, skip the mail. On a call, eat a snack you each already have. Open one free page from a museum, a park, or a library. If the page asks for money, close it. Look at one object or one view for 10 minutes. The free surprise is a question. Cameras are optional. This is still one outing, shared on a clock.

## Three ways to play

**Short, 30 minutes.** Play the room visit at $0, or a 20-minute version of the page using books you have. Keep all four lines. Ask the two debrief questions in a few sentences.

**Standard, 90 minutes.** Design with two drafts, merge, veto, and do one plan. The corner fruit, the page, or a ride whose fares you checked all fit if the clock and the bodies agree. Leave time for the two questions.

**Extended, 3 hours.** The priced ride can be the long version if the fare still fits and you both want the time. Add nothing but the ride, the packed water, the question, and the trip home. A long room visit also qualifies: the same four lines, with the visitor game, a slow snack you already have, and a playlist of four songs you already own. The ceiling does not rise because the clock did. If fatigue shows up, say "done" and keep the debrief.

## Going deeper

After the two questions, you can ask one more. Which free line did the most work?

Sometimes the story carries the hour. Sometimes the constraint is the whole joke, and the fruit is only a prop. Say which it was. You can also say the hour was thin. "Partly" is an honest answer, and so is "no." A thin hour still counts as a played game. You invented under a rule. Invention and fireworks are different deliveries.

If one of you did the planning while the other nodded, name that. The replay is simple. Next time the quiet person drafts first, and the draft has to be used for at least one of the four lines.

## Variations and replay

Replay by changing the ceiling's costume, not by raising it in secret. A $0 replay in a different room is a new plan. A local-currency replay uses the number you write down, still small, still shared.

Bad weather uses the room or the page. Pain uses the horizontal room visit: same three pointed-out things, said from a pillow. Sensory overload uses dim light, no shop, no transit, and a silent page. The long-distance version is the stamp or the free page on a call, inside the same ceiling.

The First-Time Challenge (065) is the chapter for a mild thing neither of you has done. You can spend part of this budget on that, and you still keep this chapter's four lines and its two questions. Do not grade who was more inventive. The plan is the score, and the plan is shared.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not you.

They start with two drafts. Rowan wants the bus to the end of the line and a pastry. Vale looks up the fares, adds them to the pastry Rowan remembers from last month, and the sum clears $20 before they have left the chair. Vale vetoes the distance. Rowan vetoes the second pastry they had not even bought yet, which Vale allows, because a preemptive veto is still a veto.

They lock a smaller plan. One shared orange from the corner shop, actual price $1.25. The table by the window. 20 minutes as visitors in their own kitchen, which is funnier than they expected, because Rowan describes the toaster as if it were a monument. The free surprise is a story about a bowl of noodles they split on a platform six years ago. Memorable, they decide, happened partly. The orange was fine. The toaster eulogy is the line they keep.

They do not go back for a second orange to make the day feel bigger. The ceiling already did its job.

## Afterward

Leave the plan on the table until the two questions have answers, even short ones.

If you want a last pass, use these.

- What did we almost buy, and what did we do instead?
- Where was the plan actually shared?
- Would we run this ceiling again, or switch to the $0 costume?

Then the ordinary evening can have the room back.
