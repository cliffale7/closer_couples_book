## The hook

A permission slip is a school object with one serious trick. It lets someone leave the room they are supposed to stay in. In this chapter you are the person who signs it. Your partner may witness the sentence. They do not become the office that approves your personality.

The menu is a pile of examples, not six assignments: imperfect, playful, uncertain, ambitious, needy, emotionally open. One true permission is the whole exercise. Six tidy adjectives, cranked out so the list is covered, miss the point.

## Why it might matter

A lot of self-management is a private hall pass you never write down. You may speak once the answer is polished. You may be silly once the kitchen is finished. You may want something once you have proved you do not need it. The slip makes one of those rules visible, and it puts the signature on your side of the table.

No empirical study is claimed for this activity. Permission slips are a familiar prop from school and, later, from self-help writing prompts. Used here, the prop does one job: one adult authorizes a specific way of being, and another adult hears the sentence without taking it over. That is a design. It is not a treatment, and it is not a complete model of a healthy personality. The six words in the premise are examples of doors you might open. They are not a chart of the self.

Witnessing is optional. You may write the slip and put it in a book, unread. If you do read it, the witness says what they heard and stops. Improvements to your wording wait until you ask for them. A joke that dissolves the sentence before it has been heard sends you back to the hallway.

## Before you begin

Each of you needs a small card and a pen, and a surface that can be a table or a lap. Large print or a phone note works. If writing hurts, dictate, and still choose the final words yourself. If one of you cannot see the card, the witness reads it back and then stops. If hearing is hard, exchange the cards in silence and read them with your eyes. On a call, draft first, then read, or send the sentence after both drafts exist. If the room is overloaded, one sentence and a break. No candles, no audience, no ceremony.

The sentence has three parts when you are playing the standard version: the permission, the situation where it is hard, and one thing you will stop requiring of yourself this week. Keep it specific enough that you could notice it happening on a Tuesday.

A slip that is really a rule for the other person has to be rewritten. "I give myself permission to need a reply within 5 minutes" puts their phone on the card. Permission to be needy, or emotionally open, does not license you to ignore their limits. If the want would land on their body, their time, or their money, the slip stays a self-permission until you make a separate request, and that request can be declined.

Either of you can pass, write and pocket the card, or change the adjective. One honest permission outranks a complete set.

## The experience

1. Read the menu once, aloud or silently: imperfect, playful, uncertain, ambitious, needy, emotionally open. Circle nothing. Let one word tug, or ignore the menu and write the permission that is actually yours.

2. Draft in silence. Shape, if you want a shape: "I give myself permission to be [one way] when [a real situation], and I will stop requiring [a specific self-demand] of myself this week." The weekly clause is for the standard and extended versions. It is a practice, not a vow to become a new person by Sunday.

3. Read the sentence to yourself. Ask whether you signed it, or whether you wrote your partner a job. If their body, time, or money is the object of the sentence, split the card. The permission stays. The request, if you still want to make it, waits for a separate, decline-able sentence after the exercise, or on another day.

4. Read aloud only if you want to. The witness says what they heard, in a sentence or two, then stops. They do not upgrade the wording. They do not add a virtue you left out. They do not grant the permission with "You have my blessing" or "I allow it." You already signed.

5. If you want a wording change, ask. Otherwise the card stands as written, including a clumsy phrase you meant.

6. Swap. Equal time, including the right to keep a card unread.

7. Optional: put the card in a wallet, a book, or the private journal. Date it. The witness's reaction stays off the card unless you invite it on.

You are done when each person who wanted a slip has either signed one or chosen not to show it. There is no second signature required.

## Three ways to play

**Short, about 10 minutes.** One sentence each. Permission and situation. No weekly clause. Read it or pocket it. If you read it, the witness reflects and stops.

**Standard, about 25 minutes.** Silent drafting, the full sentence with the weekly clause, optional reading, a witness reflection, then the swap. This is the version that fits a kitchen table after dinner.

**Extended, about 40 minutes.** After the first slips, write a second card for a different example from the menu, only if you want a second door. Add one note about how a witness can avoid trampling the permission. "Do not finish the sentence for me." "Do not clap." "Do not turn it into a plan." The witness hears that note and does not negotiate it into something easier for them.

## Going deeper

Take the weekly clause into the actual week, then add one dated line later: used, unused, or used and awkward. Awkward counts. The slip is not a streak.

If the permission was "needy" or "emotionally open," write the separate request on a different card, in a form the other person can refuse. "I may ask you to sit with me for 10 minutes tonight. No is a complete answer." Keep the permission and the request from collapsing into one instruction.

016, No Armor Tonight, is useful when the permission is about plain speech and the joke usually gets there first. 011, What I Don’t Show the World, is useful when the permission depends on a condition about who hears it. 028, Instructions for Loving Me, is a wider manual. This slip is one line, signed by the author.

## Variations and replay

Write a slip for a role that is usually polished: the host, the competent one, the person who does not make a fuss. Or write one for a private hour, with no witness at all. Both are the chapter.

Replay on a different adjective, or on the same one in a new situation. Money, a party, a creative project, a sick day. Retire a slip that has become a slogan you no longer mean. The interesting revisit is whether you stopped requiring the old thing of yourself, even once.

Low energy: the short sentence, in bed, dictated into a phone. Long-distance: draft apart, read on a call, hang up before either of you edits the other. If you want a keepsake, the card is the keepsake. Date it and keep it with the private pages or in the book.

## Examples

A fictional pair, Rowan and Vale. They are not you.

Rowan writes, "I give myself permission to be ambitious about the workshop I want to teach, even when the plan is half finished, and I will stop requiring a full syllabus before I let the idea be heard."

Vale says, "I heard permission to be ambitious while the plan is unfinished, and a stop to waiting for a full syllabus before you speak." Vale does not add, "You should also be confident," and does not offer a schedule.

Vale's card: "I give myself permission to be playful on a weeknight while the dishes are still in the sink, and I will stop requiring a finished kitchen before I am allowed to be silly."

Rowan reflects the sentence and stops. Nobody improves "silly."

A slip they do not use, shown here so the failure has a shape: "I give myself permission to need you to answer within 5 minutes." That sentence is a rule for the other person. The repair, if they want one, is a self-permission ("I give myself permission to be needy and say so") and, separately, a request the partner can decline.

They keep the real cards. Vale does not sign Rowan's ambition. Rowan does not sign Vale's silliness. The ink is already dry on the author's side.

## Afterward

You might ask:

- Which self-demand did you actually name, and which one did you almost hide inside a nobler sentence?
- Did the witness stop, or did a better version try to climb onto the card?
- Is there a request sitting next to this permission that still needs its own yes or no?
- Do you want the card in a pocket, or was saying it enough?

The week will not grade you. One use of the permission is a use. Forgetting it is information, not a broken streak. You can write the slip again when the old requirement sneaks back.
