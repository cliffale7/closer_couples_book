## The hook

Most people keep a second coat on the hook. The one in public is competent, funny, a little above needing anything. Under it there is a tenderness, an ambition, a shame, a taste you have decided the room cannot hold. What I Don’t Show the World asks for both halves of that fact: one specific hidden part, and the conditions that would make showing it less costly.

Hiding can be skill. Privacy is not a flaw this chapter is here to cure. A reveal with no conditions is a dare. A list of conditions with nothing hidden is a memo. You need the pair.

## Why it might matter

The world in the title is flexible. It may include your partner, and it may mean everyone except your partner. You get to say which. Sometimes the useful news is not the secret. It is the rule that has been protecting it: who may be in the room, what happens to the information, which joke is retired, how fast advice is allowed to arrive.

In diary studies of daily interactions, intimacy tracked self-disclosure and partner disclosure, with perceived partner responsiveness as a partial mediator. In Study 2, emotional disclosure was a stronger predictor than factual disclosure. See L1998. Those diaries did not map hiding, and they did not test a conversation about safety conditions. Perceived safety in this chapter is the speaker's report. It is not a measured state, and writing a condition down does not create it.

R1988 is only a framework: disclosure, response, and the feeling of being understood. The choice of what stays covered remains yours. The design here is the pair of prompts, a hidden part plus conditions a person could actually offer.

If the part is already known between you, the live half is often somewhere else: where it is still hidden from other people, and what your partner does that helps that privacy or complicates it. 002, The Life You’ve Never Told Me, is for an episode they have never heard in detail. 008, The Secret Biography, is a written portrait of contradictions. This chapter is narrower. One part, and the terms.

## Before you begin

You need a page with two columns, Hide and What would help, plus a pen and a timer. Typing or dictation into a private note is the same draft. Trade paper or talk if vision or hearing makes one of them easier. Stay seated. Take extra draft time if pain asks for it. On a call, draft offline, then read. If the day is already loud, one item is the whole session. No audience, no group, and no recording unless you both ask for it.

Agree that either of you may leave an item in the unread column. A pass is a finished contribution. Changing a prompt to something smaller is ordinary. Agree a pause word before you write.

Each item in Hide should be specific. A tenderness, an ambition, a shame, a taste. "The real me" is too large to use, and it turns the page into a performance of mystery. Beside each item, write two conditions another person could actually offer. At least one of them should be a behavior you could see: who is in the room, what they do with the information afterward, what they do not joke about, how soon they give advice.

A part that involves harm, illegal risk, or another person's safety is not a reveal-for-intimacy item. Leave it off the page. This exercise does not ask for the thing you are most afraid to say.

## The experience

1. Write in private. The page is a hidden part you might actually say, and the conditions that would make saying it less costly. About 8 minutes is the limit on the draft. Two items in Hide, so one can stay unread. Two conditions beside each. Keep the conditions observable. "Be trustworthy" is a wish. "Ask me once, then wait a week before you mention it again" is a condition.

2. Mark, for the item you might read, whether it is hidden from everyone or only outside this relationship. That sentence can be spoken even if you leave the content vague.

3. Each person chooses one pair to read. The other item can stay unread. Say so if you are leaving it: "One stays in the unread column." You do not owe its name.

4. Read the pair. The partner's only reply is a restatement of the conditions, plus whether they can offer each one, cannot, or are unsure. They do not negotiate the conditions down. They do not answer "You are safe with me" as a blanket. They do not turn the condition into a plan to fix the feeling.

5. The speaker corrects the restatement if a condition got softened. The corrected version stands.

6. Swap, with equal time. The second person is not required to match the size or the category of the first reveal.

7. Stop. You have finished when each person who wanted a turn has been heard under that reply rule, or has passed.

If your partner already knows the part, use the turn for the outer world. Where is it still hidden, and what does your partner do, in actual behavior, that protects that or makes it harder? "You announce it at dinner" is a complication with a shape. "You love me" is not yet a condition.

## Three ways to play

**Short, about 15 minutes.** One pair each. Drafting can be a few quiet minutes, or a single spoken pair if writing is the hard part. The reply rule stays the same: restate, then can, cannot, or unsure.

**Standard, about 30 minutes.** About 8 minutes of private drafting, two items each, then one pair read aloud by each person. The second item remains on the page, unread, unless you both move to the extended path.

**Extended, about 45 minutes.** After the standard round, each person may read the second written item. It is still optional. Unread is a complete ending. If you read it, the same reply rule applies, and you still do not promise a blanket of safety.

## Going deeper

You can take one condition and make it so concrete that a stranger could see it on a video. "Don't make it weird" becomes "If I tell you I still write songs, you do not sing them back at the table." The partner says again whether that behavior is one they can offer.

On another night, look only at a part that is hidden from other people and already known between you. The question is what the partner's habits do to that privacy. Helps, complicates, or both. This is information, not a trial of their character.

012, The Permission Slip, is a neighbor when the hidden part is less a secret than a way of being you have not allowed yourself. You can do that chapter with the condition still in mind. You do not have to open the hidden content in order to sign a permission.

## Variations and replay

Low energy: one item, spoken, conditions included, then stop. Outdoors works if you can write on a phone note and no one is close enough to overhear. Budget is a pen. Mobility stays in the chair you already have.

Replay when the conditions change, or when you want to test one condition for a week and report only whether you used it. Do not collect hidden parts as a series you must complete. A month later, the interesting line is often the condition you still want remembered, not a new confession.

If you both like written portraits, 008 can come later, and you may still leave this chapter's unread column unread. Different pages, different jobs.

## Examples

A fictional pair, Rowan and Vale. They are not you.

Rowan's page has two items. They read one.

"I want to apply for the night program, and I have not told friends. Hidden from everyone outside this room. Conditions: you do not mention it to anyone this month, and if you ask, you ask one question and then leave it. No jokes about the odds."

Vale says, "You want silence outside this room for a month, one question from me, and no odds joke. I can keep it quiet. I can skip the joke. I am unsure about asking only once, because I get curious. I will not promise a feeling of safety. I can promise the quiet and the skipped joke."

Rowan says, "Keep those two. Leave the question unless I open it."

Vale does not improve the ambition, does not offer a study plan, and does not say that Rowan is safe in general.

Vale's turn is a taste, not a matching ambition. "I still get teary at a particular cartoon, and I hide it from colleagues. At home you already know. Conditions: if your sister is here, you do not bring it up as a bit. If we are alone, you can sit down and not narrate it."

Rowan restates both conditions and says they can do both. Vale leaves the second written item, a shame, in the unread column, and says only that. Rowan does not guess.

The instructions fail if Vale says, "What if the quiet only lasts a week, that is more realistic," or if Rowan treats the cartoon as too small to need conditions. They also fail if either person presses for a larger secret than the one offered.

## Afterward

You might ask:

- Which condition was the one you actually needed, and which was decoration?
- Did "can," "cannot," or "unsure" feel more usable than a promise?
- Is this part hidden from everyone, or only past the door of this relationship?
- What, if anything, do you want tried this week, as a behavior?

You may feel more known, or you may feel that the coat stays on, and that is the right call. Both are finishes. Naming a condition is not the same as being safe, and it is not a repair for the feeling underneath the hidden part.
