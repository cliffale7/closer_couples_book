## The hook

The hall is the least celebrated room you own, which is why it can hold a decision. Kitchens tug toward hunger and hosting. Chairs tug toward staying put. The outer door tugs toward weather. Left alone, the nearest soft surface wins, along with a conversation you have already had three times this week.

This evening starts between the rooms. From the hall you may enter the kitchen, take the dark walk, sit in the quiet chair, or end the night. After a room, you come back to the same spot. Turning back is a complete choice. Saying "End the night" in the hall is a finished evening. Any room you never entered stays unentered, and it is not a debt.

## Why it might matter

The first move is a choice you say in the hall: kitchen, dark walk, quiet chair, or end. You do know who wants the light off, who tells the day in the doorway, who is already halfway to the couch. The hall asks the smaller questions anyway: what you are hungry for once you drop the nice version, what a caring hand should stop doing, and what you will say while moving and then leave there.

A hall gives those questions a container with an exit. You name a room out loud, you do it, and you return to a place where stopping counts. A preference repeated in one sentence lands differently from a compliment. A sentence that stays on the walk stays off the agenda.

In laboratory experiments, about seven minutes of a novel and arousing joint task increased experienced relationship quality more than a mundane task (see A2000 in the notes). That is short-term experienced quality. This evening is a different procedure, and it does not inherit that result.

## Before you begin

Choose the version at the hall mark. Short is one room, about 30 minutes. Standard is the hall plus two rooms, about 100 minutes. Extended leaves all three rooms open, about two and a half hours. Ending from the hall sooner is allowed in all three.

Take one minute for closed subjects, one sentence each. A pass needs no substitute confession. Stories about other people, bodies, money, and family stay optional. If a prompt lands on a closed subject, say "pass" or "smaller." Smaller means one tinier version, food only or the mug only, and a pass is still available after it. If the night is already a fight, take the short version or wait.

Phones may keep time if notifications are quiet. Mark the hall with a shoe or a book. Both of you there means you have returned. In a studio, the door is the hall, the counter is the kitchen, and the lamp seat is the chair.

When you want different rooms, each of you says one word. If the words match, go. On the first disagreement, one of you flips a coin and the other calls it in the air. A right call means the caller picks. A wrong call means the flipper picks. After that, the person who did not make the last accepted choice picks. "End the night" is a real pick. Either person may say "turn back" before both of you have entered the next room. Turn back spends nothing. You read the Hall card again, and you do not debate it.

A room begins only when both of you have crossed into it. Once you are inside, leaving early still spends the room. Say "hall," return, count it, and do not re-enter it tonight.

The walk's safety rule sits in that room: weather, a dark street, pain, or either person's no closes the block, and the hallway is a complete dark walk. Touch happens in one place, the quiet chair, and only as the hand named there. Nothing tonight is an agreement to sex. A yes to sitting is not a yes to the hand.

If speaking is hard, write and read aloud only what you want heard. If hearing is hard, face each other and use a visible timer. When a step needs a starter and the room has not named one, the person who did not choose that room goes first. If you chose it together, the later name in the alphabet goes first.

Print the four cards if you want them in hand: one choice point each, large type, a half sheet at most. Otherwise read only the card for the place where you are, and leave the next room's prompts unread until you enter. If a memory and a card disagree, the card wins.

## The experience

Have the timer, a warm light, the lamp, and a snack within reach. Toast, a peeled orange, crackers, leftovers, tea, or water all count. No recipe, and no knife if a knife is a bad idea. You are finished when "End the night" is accepted at the mark, then go to Afterward. If you must stop sooner, say it where you are. The evening is still finished.

### The hall

1. Read the Hall card and speak one open sentence: "Kitchen." "Dark walk." "Quiet chair." or "End the night."
2. "End the night" stops the evening. Unvisited rooms stay unvisited.
3. For a room, both of you cross and start at step 1 of that room. "Turn back," said before both are in, returns you to this card.
4. Every time you come back from a room, say the count before anything else. "One room." "Two rooms." "Three rooms." Short ends at one. Standard ends at two. Extended may end at three, or earlier from this mark.
5. If the next room is already named and you have not entered it, pass through in about a minute and do not vote again. A room you have already entered is closed. Naming it does not spend another opening and does not pass you through. Read the Hall card and choose a room you have not entered, or end. Also read the Hall card if the sentence was "Hall," or if someone says "hall instead" before the next threshold. "Hall instead" replaces the named room. That replacement is a complete choice.

### The kitchen

Copper means a warm light you already own: a yellow shade, an under-cabinet bulb, a dimmed overhead. Colder lights can go off if the room stays safe.

1. Get a drink or a simple snack into both pairs of hands. Short version: whatever is already in reach, under 3 minutes. Standard and extended: up to 10 minutes, and still no project. Hands stay busy. Hosting speeches can wait.
2. Set 10 minutes. This timer runs in every version. Whoever did not pour, slice, or fetch reads. If you shared the work, the later name reads. The reader answers second on the first prompt. You swap for the second, and the person who has said less starts the third. If you cannot tell, the first starter takes the third too.
3. Appetite: "What are you hungry for tonight that you would admit if we dropped the nice version? Food counts. A kind of hour counts."
4. Care: "When someone looks after you in a small way, what should their hands do, and what should they stop doing?"
5. Ordinary preference: "Name one ordinary preference you have been folding away because it seemed too small to announce."
6. After each answer, the listener repeats it in one sentence. The speaker says "yes" or gives one correction. The listener does not upgrade the preference into a better one.
7. A pass replaces an answer. The timer keeps running. The listener asks nothing more. If the timer rings mid-sentence, finish the sentence and skip any unasked prompt. Unasked prompts are not a debt. If you finish early, eat until the bell, or both say "card" and go on.
8. Read the Kitchen card and speak one open sentence. The card says where that sentence takes you, always through the hall. Leaving early still spends the kitchen, and you still use the card.

### The dark walk

Teal is night air, a dim corridor, or a streetlight. Coats on for the block. Indoors, a coat is optional.

1. Before the outer door opens, say "Block" or "Hallway." Weather, a safety no, or a body that does not want the outside closes the block. The hallway is a complete dark walk.
2. The block ends at the first corner or where the sidewalk ends, whichever comes first, and then you walk back the way you came. If that point would cross a road you will not cross, stop at the corner before it. The hallway is the longest indoor corridor you can both use, down and back. No corridor means a loop from the mark to the farthest corner and back. That loop is the whole walk, parade past the bookcase included.
3. Start the timer as you move: 12 minutes in the short version and in the seated variation, 20 otherwise. If it rings away from home, turn at once. Back early, with a round still unspoken, you may repeat that same route once. No new destination. You may also stop.
4. Speak the rounds only while a foot, a wheel, or, in the seated variation, a hand is moving. If you stop to think, the round waits. "Curb," "slower," "I'm cold," and "hall" are always allowed, and they are not a round.
5. Notices, each of you: one sound, one light or color, one texture. "The exit sign is a tired green" qualifies. A mood, or a theory of the relationship, does not qualify.
6. Then one sentence you will say only while moving. Small is allowed. The listener says "heard" and asks nothing. "I have no extra sentence" counts. Once you stop for the night, only the speaker may reopen it, on another day.
7. If time remains, one question, still moving: "What have we been walking past on ordinary days that you want noticed once, with you?" The person who has not asked yet is the one who asks. If neither has, the person who did not choose the walk asks. Finish on the threshold if the door arrives, then stop. No second question. If the timer is close, drop this question first, then the sentence you say only while moving. One notice each still completes the walk.
8. Read the Dark walk card and speak one open sentence. "Hall" said mid-route also completes the walk. Shortest way back, then the card.

### The quiet chair

Gold is one small lamp. Other lights in that room go off if you can still sit safely. If the bulb is harsh, move it farther from your faces. Keep cloth off a shade that heats. Phone screens go face down.

1. Sit where a hand could be offered and where declining it is ordinary. If you already do not want the hand, say "chair only" now. Pain, numbness, disinterest, and a simple no all use that sentence. You will not be asked again.
2. The person who chose the room reads the only question. If you chose together, the later name reads. Then silence: 2 minutes, or 1 minute in the short version. "What pace do you want once the day is over, and where does my pace help it or crowd it?"
3. The person who did not choose the room answers first, about 5 minutes, or about 3 in the short version. Stopping sooner is a full answer. Then the other answers, same limit, with no biography dropped into the middle.
4. Each listener may say, "I heard that your pace wants ___." The speaker says "yes" or gives one edit.
5. Unless someone said "chair only," the person who answered second may turn one hand palm up on their own thigh. The other may lay a hand on that palm for three breaths, then lift it. That is the whole touch. Either person may decline in the moment by not offering, by not taking the palm, or by saying "not the hand" or "chair only." If both offer at once, leave both palms empty. One offer only. The first answerer may say "I will offer instead," and you swap once.
6. A declined touch does not cancel the chair and is not a failed evening. A yes to sitting is not a yes to touch. Do not add a massage, a kiss, a pull, or a second round. A wish named later in the hall is not an agreement.
7. If there was no hand, stay three breaths. Then read the Quiet chair card and speak one open sentence. Leaving early spends the chair. An unfinished question is finished.

### The cards

Cut these into four, or read one line at its place. Version lock: count the room you are leaving. After one room in the short version, after two in the standard, and after three in the extended, onward room names close. Only "Hall," then "End the night," remains. You may end from the hall earlier. The lock beats an adventurous mood.

```cards

Both start the quiet chair at step 1. "End the night." The evening is finished. Go to Afterward. Spent rooms stay closed. If you disagree, use the disagreement rule. Both enter, or say "turn back" and read this card again. On a return, say the count first. If the version lock has been reached, only "End the night" is open. Ending here finishes the evening.
Kitchen | When the 10-minute timer ends, or if you left early, say one sentence, then pass through the hall. "Dark walk." At the mark, choose block or hallway, then dark walk step 1. "Quiet chair." Quiet chair step 1. "Hall." Say the count and read the Hall card. A room you have already entered is closed. Name one you have not entered, or say "Hall." "Hall instead," said before the next threshold, replaces the named room. If the version lock has been reached, only "Hall" is open.
```

## Three ways to play

**Short, about 30 minutes.** One room. About 5 minutes in the hall, about 20 in the room, about 5 to return, say "One room," and end. The kitchen still uses its 10-minute timer, the walk its 12-minute cap, the chair its shorter silence. Other sentences on that room's card are closed. One threshold turn back lets you pick a different room. A second turn back ends the short evening, and that ending is finished.

**Standard, about 100 minutes.** The hall plus two rooms: about 10 minutes to open, 35 in the first room, 5 to cross and say "One room," 35 in the second, 15 to close on "Two rooms" and "End the night." The third room stays closed. Ending after one room is still a finished evening.

**Extended, about two and a half hours.** All three rooms may open. About 10 minutes to open, 40 in each room, 5 for each passage through the hall, and 10 to close: about 150 minutes. After the third room, end. Ending before it also finishes the evening. The unused room can wait.

## Going deeper

The sentence from the walk stays closed tonight. The speaker can bring it back on another day. After you end, you may each name the choice you almost made, one sentence, with no campaign attached. If the pace question opened a real knot, book another night. You may also decide whether "chair only" is the default next time. That decision waits for the next sitting.

On the extended version, after all three rooms, one napkin line is available if you both still want it: "What should stay in this kitchen without a discussion?" One item each, or a pass. Then stop.

## Variations and replay

If the block and the hallway are both too far, take the seated dark walk at the mark. For 12 minutes, speak the walk rounds only while a hand moves along a sleeve, a wheel rim, or the seam of a chair. When the hand stops, the sentence waits. This uses the Dark walk card and spends the dark walk. It is a complete room.

In a hotel, the corridor is the hallway, the kettle is the kitchen, the desk chair is the quiet chair, and the inside of the door is the mark. A quiet-mouthed night uses only the appetite prompt, the walk's notices, and the chair question with "chair only" said at the start. You still end out loud. Thin talk creates no make-up conversation.

Replay with the same cards and a new walk sentence. Start in a room you skipped, or one you still think about. An old closed sentence, if you open it on purpose, belongs in the revisit lines. Retire a prompt that worked only because it startled. You may agree on one replacement chair question before you sit: one sentence, easy to decline, no follow-up. Daylight is allowed if you say so. The chair lamp still goes on.

## Examples

Rowan and Vale are fictional. They are not you. They play the extended version. This is the whole trail.

At the shoe, Rowan says "Dark walk." Vale says "Quiet chair." Vale flips, Rowan calls heads, the coin lands tails, and Vale picks. Both cross. Rowan, who did not choose the room, answers first: the first half hour at home should be quiet, and questions at the door crowd it. Vale wants about 10 minutes to put the day down, and hears silence at the keys as a shut door. Rowan reflects, "I heard that your pace wants a short report, then room." Vale says "yes." Vale reflects, "I heard that your pace wants an empty doorway." Rowan edits it: "Empty of questions. You can still say hello." Vale turns a palm up. Rowan says "chair only." Three breaths. The chair stands. Rowan says "Kitchen."

They pass the shoe. Warm light, toast, a spoon. Vale pours, so Rowan reads and Vale answers first: toast, and 10 minutes with no personality to solve. Rowan answers second: salt, and the missed walk. On care, Vale wants the glass set in reach and the drinking lecture stopped. Rowan wants the hovering stopped, and a hand at the neck only when asked. They repeat those sentences. Then the folded preferences: the heel of the bread, and the overhead off. The overhead goes off. The chair lamp comes in on loan. Rowan says "Dark walk."

Rain, and a street that looks wrong. Vale says "Hallway." Coats stay on the hook. The corridor is the whole walk. In motion they trade a radiator tick, a tired exit sign, a rough cuff, a neighbor's television, a dim stair light, and a cool handrail. Vale's closed sentence: "I was disappointed about the hand for a minute, and the minute is over." Rowan says "heard." Rowan says, "I still want the block on a dry night, and I don't want to settle it in this corridor." Vale says "heard." Vale asks what they have been walking past. Rowan names the bakery window that vanishes inside errand talk. At their door Vale says "Hall." At the shoe they say "Three rooms," then "End the night." The spoon gets washed. The minute and the bakery both stay where they were said.

## Afterward

Leave the lamp a minute if you like the room. The cups can wait. The count you already spoke is the summary. One practical check is enough, and skipping it is allowed: the door, the cold, whether "chair only" is the default next time, whether the walk's sentence is still closed.

A preference that is easy to keep may be kept this week. The heel of the bread. The overhead off. A glass set down without a lecture. Then the shoe goes back to being a shoe.
