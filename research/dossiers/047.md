## Source premise

> Use a three-mode game of revealing truths, funny dares, playful affection, and discovery.

## Must preserve

Three modes, freely chosen: a revealing truth, a funny dare, or a discovery. Playful affection can appear inside a dare only from a menu both people already accepted. Discover exists so a player can stay in the game without performing bravery or disclosure. The mode choice is the mechanism. Pressure to pick the “spicy” option destroys it.

Dares are optional, nonsexual unless separately agreed, and never humiliating.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. Do not attach A1997 or any disclosure study to a dare game. Those sources did not test truth-or-dare.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Truth or dare is a folk party game. This chapter adds Discover as a third mode and tightens the dare rules. It is not a trust intervention, not a clinical assessment, and not a reproduction of a commercial deck.

## Design notes

Each turn, the player picks Truth, Dare, or Discover. Truth uses a short original question list, with pass. Dare is funny, reversible, and nonsexual in the printed deck: speak in a whisper for two minutes, invent a ridiculous toast, swap seats, narrate the next minute as a nature documentary. Discover is a joint micro-exploration: recall a shared meal, teach a ten-second skill, describe a texture in the room. It is not a trail of clues or a list of objects to collect.

Playful affection, if used, comes from a pre-agreed menu such as a hand squeeze, a specific compliment, or a ten-second hug, and it can be declined with no replacement penalty. Sexual dares are outside the default rules. If a couple wants a sexual version, that requires a separate explicit agreement and should not be printed as the sample deck.

Failure points: social punishment for choosing Discover; escalating dares; public embarrassment; alcohol; “prove you trust me.”

Accessibility: every dare needs a seated, low-pain, no-balance alternative beside it. No dark-room searching. Long-distance favors truths and discovers; dares stay self-contained (a silly face, a sung line), not errands. Sensory overload: Discover can be “name a texture you like.” Vision and hearing: play by speech or text.

## Consent and harm

Pass returns the turn with no penalty. A declined dare is complete. A menu item for affection is still optional in the moment. A game choice never implies consent to sex or to a harder dare.

Distinguish 013, a single chosen disclosure received without a dare attached, and 081, a box of rarely asked questions with a free pass. Entry 047 is a three-mode party structure. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not copy a published truth-or-dare list. Do not sexualize the default deck. Do not imply the game measures trust or courage. Never print a humiliating dare, including body, ex-partner, pain, or public shame prompts. Do not turn Discover into a scavenger structure.
