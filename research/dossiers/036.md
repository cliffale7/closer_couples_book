# Dossier 036 — The Sensory Date

## Source premise

Create an experience based on music, scent, texture, taste, lighting, and welcome touch.

## Must preserve

The date is built from the senses in the premise: music, scent, texture, taste, lighting, and welcome touch. A restaurant dinner with a candle is not yet this chapter. Dumping every sense at full intensity also fails, because an experience has sequence and choice. Welcome touch can be skipped. The other senses still need consent about intensity.

## Evidence

J2017 is a review and model of wanted affectionate touch, not a trial of a multi-sensory date. Use it only for the touch element: unwanted touch is outside the claim, and the review does not test music, scent, texture, taste, or lighting. No empirical citation is claimed for the date as a whole. Do not attach a hormone or 20-second-hug claim to the touch station.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Sensory dinners, ambient playlists, and sensual evenings are a popular genre. Clinical sensate focus is a different, therapy-specific procedure and should not be copied from memory into this chapter. Classification stays reimagined. Six channels arranged as one consensual evening is a design.

## Design notes

Materials, chosen together before the room is set: a short playlist they have rights to play, an unscented option as well as any scent, one or two textures (a blanket, a sleeve, a warm cup), tastes that respect allergies and diet, lighting that can be raised as well as lowered, and a touch plan that can be "none." Alcohol is not a material. Budget ceiling agreed, including a version that uses only what is already at home.

Sequence, about 45 to 75 minutes. Agree boundaries first, including whether the evening stays nonsexual. Then three stations, not six simultaneous ones. Each person picks one channel to host. The remaining channels are either quiet background or explicitly off. Example arc: lighting set to a level both can stand, one piece of music, one taste, and a texture within reach. Touch, if welcome, is a separate station with the same overlap rule as a simpler menu: only what both marked welcome, receiver sets pressure, stop ends it.

Short: one channel each, twenty minutes. Extended: a second round in which they repeat the channel that was pleasant and retire one that was too much.

What makes the instructions fail: surprise essential oils; strobing or near-darkness as a default; a seduction ladder that assumes sex at the last station; every sense at once; playlists that require printed lyrics; one partner building the room alone and calling it mutual.

Accessibility. Migraine, asthma, allergy: unscented is the default, and any scent is opt-in, low, and ventilated. Sound: volume agreed, captions or no-lyric music if hearing or tinnitus is an issue, silence as a valid "music" choice only if they still use another channel on purpose. Vision and lighting: do not require dimness; high contrast and ordinary lamps are allowed. Taste: dietary rules, texture aversions, and no-alcohol. Touch: pain and sensory aversion can retire that station without retiring the date. Mobility: the date comes to the seated person. Long-distance: a shared playlist and the same tea at the same time is a reduced remote version; label it as reduced. It is not touch. Sensory overload: the short version, one or two channels, an easy exit.

Journal cue: which channels they used, which they would repeat, and the touch decision (welcome forms, or none). No body score.

## Consent and harm

Welcome touch follows J2017's scope only in the weak sense that unwanted touch is excluded. It is still not a studied station. Sex is a separate decision, made outside the sensory sequence, and the date does not lead there by default. Stop ends a station. Do not use darkness, intoxication, or surprise sensation to bypass a limit.

Entry 022 is the vocabulary of nonsexual touch. Entry 023 is one timed embrace. This chapter is a wider sensory experience in which touch is one optional channel.

## Red flags

Do not claim a six-sense evening is evidence-based. Do not cite aroma, music, or lighting studies. None was opened. Do not copy sensate-focus instructions. No hunt for items around the house; the materials are gathered in advance.
