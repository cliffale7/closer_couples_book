# Dossier 040 — The Intimacy Blueprint

- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Map conditions that support romance and desire, what impedes them, and how to design for both partners.

## Must preserve

A map with two authors. Each person records conditions that support romance or desire for them, and conditions that impede those states, then the pair designs one occasion that respects both maps. The mechanism disappears if the chapter crowns a single best setting, treats an impediment as a character flaw, or equates “we designed it” with “desire will appear.”

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this blueprint. I2024, a review of love-languages claims, finds little support for the ideas that each person has one primary love language, that there are exactly five, and that matching languages is what predicts satisfaction. People do seem to benefit from a range of affectionate behavior. Use I2024 only as a guardrail if a reader reaches for that quiz. It does not validate this mapping exercise. J2017’s review of wanted touch is relevant only if touch appears as one condition among others; unwanted touch is outside that claim.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Couples often talk about mood, timing, privacy, and what “kills the mood.” That folk practicality is the neighbor here. The blueprint is not a sex-therapy protocol, not a medical assessment of desire, and not Chapman’s five-language system (CHAPMAN is a popular book, not established science).

## Design notes

Separately, each person fills two columns: conditions that help, and conditions that shut romance or desire down. Prompt concrete items: time of day, privacy, humor, who initiates, rest, pain, alcohol, unfinished logistics, audience, lighting, noise. Then build a joint map with three marks: shared, mine only, yours only. Design one next occasion that includes at least one supporting condition for each person and removes one impediment. Call the design a hypothesis.

Failure points: averaging two different bodies into one recipe; listing only the partner’s faults in the impediment column; scheduling sex as the proof the map worked.

Accessibility: shift work, caregiving, medication, pain, disability, and long-distance delay are map entries, not excuses. Lighting and sound are design variables for sensory sensitivity. The map can be spoken, written in large type, or built from index cards. A long-distance blueprint names latency, cameras, and what “privacy” means in two rooms.

## Consent and harm

Writing a condition is not agreement to a sexual outcome on the designed night. Either person may leave a condition unnamed. Either may cancel the occasion without owing a replacement performance.

Distinguish 037, a specific unasked wish that need never be acted on, and 036, a date built from sensory materials. Entry 040 is the prior map of conditions and impediments for both people. Keep them separate. Distinguish 025, which compares behaviors that feel like love, not the conditions for romance and desire.

## Red flags

Do not diagnose low desire or promise to treat it. Do not assign initiation by gender. Do not turn the map into a love-languages quiz or a score. Do not describe alcohol or exhaustion as character problems.
