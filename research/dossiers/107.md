## Source premise

> Tell a true memory with one invented detail that symbolizes a wish, fear, or unrealized possibility; have the listener detect it.

## Must preserve

One true memory, one invented detail, and that detail has to mean something. The signature twist, from the project’s expanded brief: “The fabricated element symbolizes a wished-for turn, a feared outcome, or an unrealized possibility.” A random swapped color, a trick for its own sake, or a whole invented story fails the brief. After the reveal, tell the true version and why the fiction appealed. Formulated for this project, without a worldwide-novelty claim.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

“Two truths and a lie” is a folk detection game. Chapter 041 tells two true personal stories and one convincing fiction, and the fiction is an entire story. Here the story is true except for one load-bearing detail, and the detail is a symbol rather than a random lie. Keep both. Detection games are not a validated protocol for intimacy.

## Design notes

About 20–40 minutes. Each person prepares one memory they are willing to have examined. Fair-play rules: exactly one invented detail; everything else is true as the teller believes it; the invention is about the teller’s inner world, not a false motive pasted onto the partner; the listener gets three guesses, then the teller reveals even if nobody has caught it. The teller must be able to point to the detail without rewriting the story a second time. After the reveal, three beats: the true version; which kind of symbol it was (wish, fear, or unrealized possibility); why that fiction appealed. The listener does not grade the symbolism.

Example, original: a true rainy train platform where you did not say “stay.” The smuggled detail is that you did say it and they missed the train. Kind: unrealized possibility. True version: you said goodbye and watched the doors close. The fiction appeals because it gives the courage a rehearsal.

Another grain: a true calm argument in the kitchen. The smuggled detail is a bag already packed by the door. Kind: feared outcome. Use it only if both can hold that image. If not, choose a smaller fear.

Failure points: more than one invention; a detail so trivial nobody could care; a detail so alarming the guess becomes a crisis; the teller refusing to confirm after three guesses.

Accessibility: the story can be read. Guesses can be written. Long-distance works. No reenactment. Skip stories that require the listener to picture a sensory event they cannot access; the symbol should live in the plot, not in a color only one person sees.

## Consent and harm

Forbidden inventions, even as symbols: infidelity, pregnancy, illness, hidden money, violence, or any harm. Those are too costly to drop as a twist. The listener may decline to guess and still hear the reveal. Either person may discard a prepared story. The true memory should already be safe to tell. This is not 083, where the whole disclosure is a surprising truth and nothing is planted.

## Red flags

Do not claim the exercise improves memory or honesty. Do not use it to test whether a partner is gullible. Do not leave the true version untold. The fiction is meaningful only after the truth is back on the table.
