# Dossier 016 — No Armor Tonight

- Catalog ID: 016
- Part: 2 — Vulnerability and the Courage to Be Seen
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

Set aside habitual jokes, defensiveness, and impressive answers in favor of straightforward emotional honesty.

## Must preserve

The mechanism is a style constraint: jokes used as cover, defensiveness, and impressive answers stay off the table for a set period, and plain emotional honesty replaces them. A sincere conversation that still performs brilliance, or a ban on laughter itself, both miss the premise. They need a modest topic, or the constraint has nothing to bite on.

## Evidence

L1998: intimacy tracked self-disclosure and partner disclosure, with perceived partner responsiveness as a partial mediator. In Study 2, emotional disclosure was a stronger predictor than factual disclosure. The study did not test a ban on humor, and it did not measure defensiveness. Do not use it to justify "a decent response." Corrected after `review/evidence/batch-001-036.md`.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

"Drop the act" is folk advice. Do not attribute defensiveness, or any percentage about it, to Gottman. The whitelist's Gottman frames are only love maps, bids, repair, and the emotional bank account, and this chapter uses none of them. Classification stays reimagined.

## Design notes

Materials: a timer. An optional three-word card: joke-as-cover, defense, résumé.

Agree the window, about 15 minutes standard, 6 minutes short, 30 minutes extended with a break. Pick one modest topic in advance: something they felt in the last day, a hope, or a disappointment small enough to finish. During the window, if someone notices their own armor, they may say "let me try that plainly" and restate one sentence. The partner does not score hits. A laugh that is actually delight is allowed. A joke that changes the subject or makes the feeling cute is the habit to set down.

Each person speaks for half the window. The listener responds with one honest feeling of their own about what they heard, not a critique of the speaking style.

What makes the instructions fail: policing a nervous laugh; banning all humor for the night; impressive vulnerability ("watch how brave I am"); a topic so charged that armor was the only thing keeping them decent; no topic at all, so they stare and call it honesty.

Accessibility. Speech, writing, or AAC. A written plain sentence counts. Hearing: notes. Vision: no gaze requirement. Mobility and pain: any posture. Long-distance: timed call, cameras optional. Sensory overload: the short window and a prewritten sentence. People who use humor as a cultural style get to define which jokes are cover and which are just how they talk; the partner does not get to define that for them.

Journal cue: one plain sentence they managed to say, and which habit they noticed. No armor score.

## Consent and harm

Straight emotional honesty is not a license to be cruel. Insults are not "unarmored truth." Either person may end the window. If the honest content concerns harm or safety, the style game stops and they deal with the content directly. Do not require a history of trauma as the proof that armor came off.

## Red flags

Do not cite Gottman. Do not quote a percentage about defensiveness or divorce. Do not claim that humor is immature. Do not turn the signal "that sounded like armor" into a weapon the partner uses in later arguments.
