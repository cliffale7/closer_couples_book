## Source premise

> Allocate 100 imaginary points among forms of closeness and collaborate on a mutually appealing evening without demands.

## Must preserve

A private allocation of exactly 100 imaginary points, then a comparison that cannot be spent as a claim on the other person. Points are not entitlements. The signature twist, from the project’s expanded brief: “Design an evening that respects both people's genuine wishes and limits.” A chart with no evening, or an evening that overrides a limit because the number was higher, abandons the chapter. Formulated for this project. Not a worldwide-novelty claim and not a test of desire.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. I2024 reviews love-languages claims and finds little support for one primary language, for exactly five languages, or for matching languages as what predicts satisfaction. A range of affectionate behavior appears beneficial. This worksheet must not be drawn as five love languages. J2017 is a review and model of wanted affectionate touch, not a trial of point allocation. Unwanted touch sits outside that review’s claim. A high score in a touch category does not create a study, a duty, or a permission.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Pie charts of needs and “bid” metaphors are practitioner habits. Gottman’s emotional-bank metaphor is not a scoring system for this page and has no percentage here. The original mechanics are the fixed 100 points, the explicit rule that points are not entitlements, and the evening built only from overlap and volunteered offers.

## Design notes

About 30–60 minutes. Default categories, taken from the project brief, totaling 100: affection, romance, playfulness, meaningful conversation, sensuality, adventure, closeness, quiet, spontaneity, privacy. The couple may rename up to three. Sensuality may be split so sexual closeness is named separately, if both want that distinction; privacy may be renamed solitude when they mean kindly separate time. Integers. Zeros allowed. A zero describes this season or this evening. It is not a permanent identity.

Each person fills the sheet alone. They read their own chart aloud. The listener asks one curious question per category they do not understand. They do not say what the other should have scored.

Evening rule: each person circles up to three categories they would enjoy tonight and crosses out any that are off the table. The evening uses only categories nobody crossed out. Offers are volunteered. If the overlap is empty, the result is parallel quiet or a rain check, and that completes the chapter.

Examples, not norms. A chart heavy on quiet, meaningful conversation, and playfulness might become cooking side by side, one real conversation, and a game, with an early night. A chart that includes sensuality and privacy might become a flirtatious hour and a kindly separate hour, only if neither person crossed those out. If the couple split sensuality so sexual closeness has its own points, a 40 there is information. It is not a claim on a partner’s body. A 30 on meaningful conversation is not a debt of talk.

Failure points: averaging the charts into a compromise that erases a zero; treating the larger number as the winner; renaming categories until one person’s “no” disappears.

Accessibility: the evening must have a low-pain, low-cost, home version. Sex and adventure are easy to cross out. Long-distance: the evening may be a call plus a solitary pleasure each person actually wants. Large-print worksheet. No one has to disclose why a category is zero.

## Consent and harm

Either person may refuse the whole exercise. Crossing out is unilateral and sufficient. Points never outvote a limit, a headache, or a change of mind after the chart is shared. Privacy, including kindly separate time, can sit on the menu and can also be crossed out. This is not 034’s yes / no / maybe list, though a no there still governs here. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not imply that a healthy chart looks balanced or matched. Do not diagnose desire from a number. Do not cite I2024 as praise for love-language matching; the review cuts against that claim. The imaginary points expire at the end of the conversation.
