# Dossier 072 — The Shared Courage Experiment

- Catalog ID: 072
- Part: 9 — Shared Adventures and New Experiences
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Face a manageable, mutually chosen challenge with encouragement and teamwork.

## Must preserve

The challenge is manageable, chosen by both people, and faced with explicit roles for encouragement and teamwork. The person who feels the difficulty sets the ceiling. A smaller completed version counts. Encouragement stops when the person says stop. The chapter is not exposure therapy, not a surprise confrontation with a fear, and not a dare. Classification stays reimagined: courage-together is a designed custom here, without a study of this procedure.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. A2000’s novel, arousing laboratory task was tied to a short-term change in experienced relationship quality. It did not measure courage, teamwork, or fear, and it must not be cited as evidence that shared challenges build bravery or repair trust. No clinical exposure study was opened, and none should be imitated.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Doing a hard ordinary thing with a companion is a folk custom. Clinical exposure hierarchies for phobias, panic, or post-traumatic stress are a different practice. This chapter must not borrow their steps or claim to treat those conditions. If a fear is bound up with trauma or panic, the right use of this chapter is a milder everyday challenge or a pass, not a treatment plan.

## Design notes

Each person proposes two challenges that are actually manageable: a delayed phone call, a beginner class with an exit, a short flat walk, asking a clerk a question, cooking for a guest, a few sentences spoken aloud. Both agree. The plan names a stop word, a smaller success, and the partner’s role: presence, a reminder of the plan, or a celebration. The partner’s role is not pushing.

Debrief: what the partner did that helped, what added pressure, and whether to repeat. Failure points: a thrill from one person’s bucket list imposed on the other; surprise exposure; filming someone who is afraid; medical or legal risk; humiliation.

Accessibility: social, sensory, cognitive, and mobility courage all count. For chronic pain, a manageable challenge might be asking for a break. Long-distance: the partner can stay on the phone during a local challenge, with a plan if the call drops. Do not require heights, water, crowds, or driving. Seated and home-based challenges belong in the main list.

## Consent and harm

Either person may veto. The stop word ends the attempt. Encouragement is not coercion. No sexual courage task and no humiliating audience. Completing less than planned is a valid result.

Distinguish 065, a first time for both people with a reflection on novelty and the A2000 short-term limit. Distinguish 021, The Trust Walk, which is guided movement with covered eyes. Entry 072 is a chosen challenge plus encouragement roles. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

No “face your fear” bravado and no therapeutic protocol. Do not claim courage activities repair trust. Do not film, grade, or surprise. Do not describe clinical treatment steps.
