## Source premise

> Secretly collect short notes over days about moments your partner helped, impressed, or delighted you, then exchange them.

## Must preserve

A multi-day secret collection, then one exchange. The signature twist, from the project’s expanded brief: “The most poignant notes can concern moments so tiny the recipient can't remember them.” Forgetting is a feature of the form. Formulated for this project, without a claim of worldwide novelty. G2012 does not validate the ritual.

## Evidence

G2012 tied feeling appreciative and feeling appreciated to responsiveness, commitment, and relationship maintenance, and observed that appreciation can show in behavior. That is a relationship-maintenance finding. It is not proof that secret postcards change a couple, and it is not a test of this game.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Gratitude journals are usually private or immediately shared. The original mechanism is delayed, secret, and small enough to be forgettable to the recipient. Do not use the PostSecret name, submission rules, or card designs. Those belong to someone else’s project.

## Design notes

Choose a span of three to seven days. Each person hides a few blank cards or notes. Whenever the other helps, impresses, delights, or attracts them, they write one to four lines the same day: what happened, where, and the effect. Tiny counts: passed the salt and looked up; closed a door softly; remembered a name. Do not perform extra goodness in order to be postcarded, and do not tell the other that a note was just earned. At the end, exchange the stacks and read in the other’s presence or side by side. The reader may say “I don’t remember that.” The writer does not supply a guilt lesson. A keepsake method: a dated envelope in the journal, or a photo of the cards if both agree. Either person may withhold a card that, on reread, feels like pressure.

Printable template, original: front, “You didn’t know I kept this”; inside, lines for when, what you did, what it did to me; back, “Remembering this is optional.”

Failure points: notes that are requests; surveillance of a partner’s ordinary freedom; a stack used later as evidence in a fight; one partner writing ten and the other writing none and being scored for it.

Accessibility: voice memos in a locked folder, transcribed at the end, replace handwriting. Large print. Long-distance: date-stamped messages scheduled to send on exchange day. The observation period must respect people who see each other rarely; notes can be about calls and small digital kindnesses. No stalking of location or messages.

## Consent and harm

Tell each other, before day one, that secret kind notes are being kept, without revealing their contents. Secret means the wording is unseen, not that the practice is a surprise sprung on an unconsenting partner. Sexual notes only if both opted in. Skip anything that exposes a third party.

Distinguish 074. That chapter is one sitting in which you reveal past gestures whose impact the other underestimated. This chapter is a forward-looking secret log, and its sharpest notes may be moments the recipient cannot recall. Keep both.

## Red flags

Do not claim G2012 tested postcards. Do not require the recipient to feel the same size of feeling. Do not turn invisible care into a quota. A short stack of true tiny notes completes the exchange.
