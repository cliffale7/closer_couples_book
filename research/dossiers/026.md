# Dossier 026 — The Appreciation Avalanche

- Catalog ID: 026
- Part: 4 — Love, Affection, and Feeling Chosen
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: mixed
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

Exchange increasingly specific appreciations until you move beyond the obvious.

## Must preserve

They exchange, the appreciations get more specific across rounds, and they stop when they have moved past the obvious. A single heartfelt speech, or a pile of generic praise, misses the slope. Inventing compliments to keep the avalanche going also misses it. Past the obvious, they stop.

## Evidence

G2012: across methods, feeling appreciated and feeling appreciative of a partner were tied to responsiveness, commitment, and relationship maintenance. Appreciation can be visible in behavior. The paper does not test a scripted avalanche or a compliment contest.

G2004 is about capitalization: sharing a positive event, and the partner's active-constructive versus passive or destructive response. That is a different behavior from aiming appreciation at the partner. Do not cite G2004 as compliment research. It belongs here only as a limit, so the writer does not conflate "thanks for existing" with "tell me about your good news and meet it well."

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Gratitude lists and appreciation circles are common in workshops and households. Classification stays mixed. The research supports taking appreciation seriously as a relationship process. The three-round slope is a design, not a treatment that was trialed.

## Design notes

Materials: a timer, three blank lines each.

Round 1: one obvious, true appreciation. Round 2: the same quality, or a different one, pinned to a scene in the last month. Round 3: something easy to miss, tied to a small behavior. Equal numbers. About 25 minutes standard. Short: rounds 1 and 2 only. Extended: stop after round 3 unless a fourth appreciation arrives unforced; if they are stretching, end.

After each item, the receiver says only "I heard that" or corrects a factual miss. Receiving without deflection is the work of 030. Here, the engine is specificity. They can still decline to pile on.

What makes the instructions fail: "you're amazing" three times; backhanded praise ("you're so organized, unlike before"); appreciating a stereotype; a score for who was more specific; using the exercise to cover a harm that still needs accountability. If round 3 is empty, end while round 2 is still true.

Write fictional examples at the right grain: "You rinsed my mug before the sink got grim" rather than "you're thoughtful."

Accessibility. Speech or notes. Hearing: exchange paper. Vision: speak. Mobility and pain: seated. Long-distance: voice or text, still three rounds, still equal. Sensory overload: two rounds. No circle of friends, no public avalanche.

Journal cue: the round-3 line, if it was real. Skip the obvious round on the shared page if space is tight. No ranking.

## Consent and harm

Appreciation is voluntary. A person who cannot find a true line should say so rather than perform one. Do not aim the exercise at a partner in order to extract appreciation in return. Compliments about bodies belong only if the receiver has welcomed that kind of attention.

## Red flags

Do not claim the avalanche was shown to maintain relationships. G2012 did not test it. Do not instruct them to keep going for a set count once the specifics run out.
