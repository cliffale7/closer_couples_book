## Source premise

> Reflect on discoveries and design ongoing practices and future experiences together.

## Must preserve

A landing: a reflection on discoveries they actually made, then a very small design of ongoing practices and future experiences. A new curriculum covering every chapter, or a plan made without looking back, loses the premise.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Retreats and workbooks often end with goal setting. That custom is not a validated protocol. This chapter is the book’s own closing design conversation. It can install 094’s annual reintroduction if the couple wants a repeating container. It does not replace 094, and it does not preview Part 13’s original games as required homework.

## Design notes

About 30–50 minutes. Limit the look-back to experiences they truly did. Each person names one discovery about themselves, one about the other, one practice worth keeping, and one practice worth dropping. Then jointly choose at most two recurring practices and one dated future experience. Write them on a single journal page: what, how often, the smallest version on a hard week, and when you will notice how it is going. Stop. Further ideas go in a margin labeled “not now.”

Failure points: forty action items; guilt about skipped chapters; a future experience that spends money or stamina they do not have; turning discoveries into a verdict on the relationship’s health.

Accessibility: the smallest version of each practice is required, not optional. Include a version doable in bed, on a call, or in ten minutes. Long-distance couples name practices that survive travel and screens. No one must reread the book first. If memory of earlier chapters is fuzzy, they use whatever they still carry.

## Consent and harm

Discoveries that were vulnerable in earlier chapters are reused only with the speaker’s yes. The dated experience is a plan both want, not a surprise that overrides a limit. Either person may choose “no new practice; we are keeping one old kindness.” That is a complete next chapter. Do not use this landing to renegotiate safety, monogamy, or living arrangements by stealth; flag those for a dedicated conversation.

## Red flags

Do not claim the book’s arc is an evidence-based program. Do not imply that closeness requires finishing all 108 experiences. Do not sell a sequel inside the ritual. The next chapter is theirs, and it may be quiet.
