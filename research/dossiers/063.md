## Source premise

> Translate a recurring minor irritation into an underlying wish and a constructive request.

## Must preserve

Three lines, in order, about one recurring minor irritation: the complaint, the wish underneath it stated as a positive condition, and a constructive request that is specific and small. The partner repeats the wish before agreeing, modifying, or declining. Translation is the mechanism. It is not permission to stockpile grievances, and it is not a duty for the partner to comply. The three-line translation is this book’s design. It is not a controlled test, and it is not attributed to any clinician. Corrected after `review/evidence/batch-037-072.md`: complaint-to-wish is not one of the four Gottman metaphors on the whitelist.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this exercise. G2012 tied feeling appreciated and feeling appreciative to responsiveness, commitment, and relationship maintenance. It did not study complaints, and it does not show that reframing an irritation raises satisfaction. Do not cite GOTTMAN here. Complaint-to-wish is not love maps, bids, repair, or the emotional bank account.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Everyday life is full of repeated small irritations. This chapter’s preference for a wish and a small request is a writing choice, not a validated protocol and not a Gottman method. “Constructive” means specific and doable, not pleasant at the cost of honesty.

## Design notes

Each person brings one irritation already screened as minor and recurring: lateness, clutter, interruptions, noise. Exclude contempt, a value rupture, and anything involving safety. Line one names the complaint without “you always.” Line two names the wish (a positive condition: a heads-up, a clear counter, a quieter hour). Line three makes a request bounded in time (“this week, when you…”). The partner restates the wish, then accepts, edits, or declines.

Include one fictional before-and-after so the rewrite is visible. Failure points: contempt dressed as a minor complaint; a wish that the partner become a different person; five irritations instead of one; a request that ignores pain, schedule, or sensory limits.

Accessibility: irritations may be sensory (noise, smell, light) or about pace and pain. The request has to be physically possible. Long-distance requests concern calls, texts, and shared logistics. Large type or speech both work.

## Consent and harm

The partner may decline. A translated complaint creates no debt. The speaker may not ambush with a saved list. Serious harm does not qualify as minor and should not be laundered through this form.

Distinguish 026, The Appreciation Avalanche, which exchanges specific appreciations and does not start from a complaint. Distinguish 059, a live manageable disagreement aimed at a short experiment. Entry 063 starts from irritation and ends in a wish plus a request. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not claim this cures resentment. Do not hide a serious injury in a “minor” frame. Do not cite Gottman, a Gottman percentage, or a divorce statistic. Do not require the partner to feel grateful for the complaint.
