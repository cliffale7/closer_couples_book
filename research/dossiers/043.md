## Must preserve

The exact title, including the comma: “The Newlywed Game, Without the Wedding.” Privately written answers about the partner, then a comparison that includes knowledge, assumptions, and surprises. The joke in the title is that the pair need not be newly wed, or wed at all. ## Tradition or practitioner frame

A long-running television game show asks spouses to predict each other’s answers for laughs and prizes. This chapter is a private reimagining of that folk format for any adult pair. It is not affiliated with the show, not limited to husbands and wives, and not a clinical protocol. The writer must invent original questions and must not reproduce the show’s questions, set, host, or branding.

## Design notes

Separately, each person answers eight to twelve original items about the other: a current habit, a present stress, a taste, a comfort, a petty annoyance, a wish for the coming month. Reveal in pairs. An optional token for a match can be a joke. The debrief question is where the guess described an older version of the partner, or an assumption borrowed from someone else.

Failure points: sexual-embarrassment questions as the default deck; traps about weight, fidelity, or ex-partners; a winner’s podium; questions the answerer cannot know because the partner has never said them, used as proof of neglect.

Accessibility: private writing is the method, so speech is optional at the reveal. Large type and screen-reader-friendly digital notes both work. Long-distance uses sealed notes opened on a call. No physical dare and no studio set. The questions should make sense for unmarried pairs, same-gender pairs, and long-established couples.

## Consent and harm

Skip any item. Do not require answers about sex, family secrets, money, or a third person’s story. A wrong guess is information about an assumption, not an offense.

Near-duplicate 101, The Borrowed Brain Experiment, asks someone to answer a scenario as they think the partner would, then compares the reasoning, not only the final decision. Entry 043 compares trivia about habits, tastes, and knowledge, and lingers on surprises. One studies how a decision gets made; the other studies what you think you already know. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Preserve the chapter title, and do not imply that the television show endorses, sponsors, or is the source of the questions. Do not copy broadcast questions. Do not claim that couples who score well stay together, and do not cite a divorce statistic. Do not gender the roles as host and contestant.
