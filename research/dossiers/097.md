## Source premise

> Investigate a powerful emotional reaction layer by layer without prematurely interpreting it; discover the need below the surface.

## Must preserve

One strong reaction, investigated layer by layer, with the partner withholding explanations. The signature twist, from the project’s expanded brief: “As the explorer reflects, the original assumed cause of the emotion may change.” The archaeologist does not announce that revision. The explorer does. This formulation was made for this project. That is not a claim of worldwide novelty, and it is not a validated method.

## Evidence

L1998 found that daily intimacy tracked disclosure and perceived responsiveness, and that emotional disclosure outweighed factual disclosure in Study 2. R1988 is the model behind that finding: disclosure, response, and feeling understood. Both support a listener stance. Neither tested layer-by-layer inquiry, and neither shows that a buried need can be reliably excavated in one sitting.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Depth psychology and some coaching models talk about layers under a feeling. No such manual was opened, and none is the protocol. The original piece is the archaeologist’s ban on premature interpretation plus the explicit chance that the first cause was wrong.

## Design notes

About 25–50 minutes. The explorer picks one reaction: joy, jealousy, embarrassment, pride, sadness, or a cousin of those. They state the first assumed cause in a sentence. The archaeologist may ask only along these lines, one at a time: what happened just before; what the body did; what meaning arrived first; what else might be true; what the feeling might be protecting or wanting. After each answer they reflect words, not a theory, and ask “Is that close?” Banned moves: diagnostic labels, “so you’re really just…,” and a summary cleverer than the explorer’s own. A stop word ends the dig immediately. Close by thanking the explorer. The explorer gets the last sentence, which may keep, revise, or abandon the original cause. If the cause does not change, that is also a finished dig.

Failure points: treating the first theory as the find; digging into a feeling the explorer only mentioned as an example; turning the need into a work order for the partner.

Accessibility: questions in print; long pauses; a written dig for overload or distance. No lying down, dimmed lights, or touch is required. Fifteen minutes and one layer is a complete short path.

## Consent and harm

The explorer chooses the reaction and the depth. Jealousy and anger are allowed only as self-observation, not as a case against the partner. Stop for anything that is abuse, panic, or a crisis, and respond as a partner rather than an archaeologist. Distinguish 010, which stays with successive layers under a fear. 097 can start from any strong reaction, and the assumed cause is allowed to move. Do not merge them. 087’s ladder starts from an ordinary answer, not from a chosen emotion.

## Red flags

Do not call this analysis, diagnosis, or trauma processing. Do not claim L1998 validated the dig. Non-diagnostic language is part of the design, not a footnote. The need below the surface is a hypothesis the explorer may decline.
