# Dossier 082 — The Moral Gray Area

- Catalog ID: 082
- Part: 11 — Radical Curiosity and the Surprising Stuff
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Discuss dilemmas with competing values and no obvious right answers.

## Must preserve

A dilemma with at least two real values in conflict, and no answer key. If the scene has an obvious saint and an obvious villain, or if the couple must reach a verdict, the gray area is gone.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Moral dilemmas are a long philosophical and dinner-table tradition. Famous thought experiments are not a couple protocol, and this chapter does not need to reproduce them. The activity is a structured conversation, not a validated ethics intervention.

## Design notes

About 35–50 minutes. Use three original scenes, one at a time. After each, both people name the values that collide and the cost of each path. They may land in different places. A closing sentence is “what I want you to know about how I decide,” not “the correct choice.”

Scene seeds, written for this project: a friend asks you to keep a non-safety secret the person at the table would want to know; a favor would solve someone’s money problem by breaking a promise you made in public; a piece of work would fund a dream and take you away three nights a week for a year. Add one scene the couple writes, then either of them may veto it before discussion.

Failure points: debating to win; using a hypothetical as a thin cover for a real accusation; importing trolley-style puzzles that treat disabled or fat bodies as props; demanding a single shared verdict.

Accessibility: all talk, seated, pause-friendly. Long-distance works by phone. Offer the scenes in print ahead of time so a partner who processes slowly is not put on the spot. Either person may replace an abstract scene with a smaller everyday one.

## Consent and harm

If a scene is too close to a current wound, bereavement, infertility, immigration, or a real betrayal, retire it. No one has to disclose a past moral injury to play. Distinguish 085, which asks what you would do in an unexpected practical fork; 086, which shares an opinion you actually hold; and 101, which asks you to answer as your partner and compare reasoning. Do not merge these into one dilemma night.

## Red flags

Do not present the chapter as moral philosophy instruction or as a test of character. Do not copy proprietary game cards or textbook vignettes wholesale. Do not assume both partners share a religion, a politics, or a duty to family. Disagreement is a finished outcome.
