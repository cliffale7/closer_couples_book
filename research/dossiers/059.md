## Source premise

> Work through a manageable disagreement with warmth, curiosity, and respect.

## Must preserve

The disagreement is real, current, and manageable. Warmth, curiosity, and respect are concrete behaviors, not a demand that anyone feel calm. The aim is a next-step experiment, not a permanent verdict and not a performance of harmony. If the topic stops being manageable, stopping is the correct ending. ## Tradition or practitioner frame

Couples already have small disagreements about plans, chores, and preferences. This chapter structures one of those. It is not mediation, not counseling, and not a “gentle startup” script quoted from a book that was not opened here.

## Design notes

Before choosing the topic, write a veto list: breakups, money crises, jealousy investigations, and in-law emergencies are out of bounds for this sitting. Pick a preference, a chore, or a plan. Each person states their preference and the care underneath it, asks one curious question, and looks for an experiment that lasts about a week.

Warmth, specified: use names, sit side by side or at an angle if that helps, take a two-minute pause if voices rise, and skip sarcasm. Failure points: a topic that is actually a wound; “gentle” used to forbid upset; fake agreement; a curious question that is a cross-examination.

Accessibility: a fifteen-minute cap when pain or fatigue is present. The same structure works on a long-distance call, cameras optional. Written turns serve hearing access and slowing down. No eye contact is required, which also matters for sensory overload. If the topic touches disability or identity, the person who lives it describes it first.

## Consent and harm

Nobody has to concede. Either person may stop when the disagreement grows past “manageable.” Stopping needs no penalty and no make-up debate the same night.

Distinguish 045, which argues absurd propositions with fake gravity, and 086, which shares underexpressed opinions without needing a decision. Distinguish 063, which translates one irritation into a wish and a request. Entry 059 works a live, bounded disagreement toward a short experiment. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not cite a divorce prediction or a “gentle startup” success rate. Do not cast one partner as the calm adult and the other as the problem. The book’s readers are not assumed to be in a broken relationship; this is practice on a small difference, not crisis care.
