## Source premise

> Each partner plans an experience around a deeply meaningful interest and explains its significance.

## Must preserve

Both partners host. The interest must matter to the host, not merely fill an hour. The experience is accompanied by an explanation of significance: when it started, what it holds, and what the host is not asking the guest to become. The guest’s job is interested attention, not a review and not a conversion. Classification stays reimagined. No research finding supplies the guest’s manners.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this hosting design. G2004 found that telling someone about a good event was associated with greater positive affect and well-being beyond the event itself, and that perceived active-constructive responses were associated with higher relationship quality, while passive or destructive responses were not the helpful pattern. Those studies did not test a hosted tour of a lifelong interest, and they do not define a guest script. Do not assign “follow, ask, notice,” or any other behavior list, to G2004. ## Tradition or practitioner frame

Sharing a private enthusiasm — a team, a craft, a liturgy, a shelf of books — is an ordinary act of intimacy. The paired hosting structure, and any guest manners, are designed here.

## Design notes

Each host plans a scaled slice: half an hour of records and why they matter, a walk to one mural, a few pages read aloud, a backyard drill, a poem or prayer explained by the person who holds it. Explain significance before or during. The guest asks questions and does not score the interest.

Then switch hosts. Failure points: recruiting the guest into the fandom; shame about a “small” interest; expertise without meaning; a guest who critiques taste.

Accessibility: bend the slice to both bodies and both sensory profiles. A loud stadium interest needs a quieter version if noise harms. Long-distance: a camera tour, a shared file, a mailed object. Vision and hearing: tell the interest in the channel the guest has. Pain: seated versions of active interests. Cultural and religious material is explained by the holder.

## Consent and harm

The guest does not have to adopt the interest or pretend to. The host does not have to share anything that feels too private or sacred. Either person may stop a slice that becomes a sermon or a test.

Distinguish 067, a beginner introduction at the edge of the guest’s familiarity. Entry 070 centers meaning, and the guest may already like the interest or not. Distinguish 006, an imaginary museum of a life. Entry 070 is a real, hosted slice of a living interest. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not mock the interest. Do not claim G2004 measured hobbies or identity. Do not require conversion, fandom, or shared expertise. Label sample explanations fictional.
