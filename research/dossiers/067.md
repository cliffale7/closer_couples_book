# Dossier 067 — The Comfort Zone Exchange

- Catalog ID: 067
- Part: 9 — Shared Adventures and New Experiences
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Introduce each other to a mutually acceptable unfamiliar activity.

## Must preserve

One person hosts an activity they can already guide. The other finds it unfamiliar. Both accept it before it starts, after a plain description of physical, social, and sensory demands. Then they switch roles, the same day or later. The edge of familiarity is the point. Reluctance is information, not a failure to grow. Classification stays reimagined: this is a hosted introduction, and no study on the whitelist tests it.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. A2000’s laboratory result concerned a novel and arousing joint task and a short-term change in experienced relationship quality. It did not test one partner teaching the other a familiar hobby. Do not paste that citation onto this chapter as a promised benefit.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Showing a partner “my thing” is an ordinary courtship and friendship custom. This chapter adds mutual acceptability, a beginner scale, and a role reversal. It is not exposure therapy and not a hobbies-as-compatibility test.

## Design notes

Each person offers two activities they know well enough to host at beginner scale: the chorus of a song, a card game, a stretch, a dish, a flat route, a few phrases, a single craft stitch. The receiver picks one. The host teaches briefly, they do it together, then they swap. Describe demands in advance: standing, noise, mess, audience, cost, duration.

Failure points: an expert showing off; choosing something unfamiliar because it is frightening rather than interesting; treating a stop as resistance; skipping the second hosting so only one world is entered.

Accessibility: the host adapts pace, grip, noise, and posture. A comfort zone for one body may be pain or overload for the other, so acceptability is decided by the guest, not the host’s pride. Long-distance: teach on camera with materials both can get, or choose activities that need none. Vision and hearing: pick a channel the guest can actually use. Fine-motor and balance alternatives belong in the offer, not as afterthoughts.

## Consent and harm

Unfamiliar is not a surprise. The receiver may stop at the threshold. No sexual initiation disguised as stretching a comfort zone. No pressure to adopt the hobby afterward.

Distinguish 065, where the activity is new to both and the debrief asks how novelty changed the interaction, with A2000’s short-term limit. Entry 067 is hosted by someone who already knows the activity. Distinguish 070, where a partner plans an experience around a deeply meaningful interest and explains why it matters; the guest need not be at the edge of fear or awkwardness. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not promise a relationship-quality boost. Do not frame hesitation as a growth failure. Do not require public performance. Do not use clinical exposure steps.
