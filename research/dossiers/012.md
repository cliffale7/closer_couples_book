# Dossier 012 — The Permission Slip

- Catalog ID: 012
- Part: 2 — Vulnerability and the Courage to Be Seen
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: high

## Source premise

Write yourself permission to be imperfect, playful, uncertain, ambitious, needy, or emotionally open.

## Must preserve

The writer signs their own slip. The partner may witness it. The partner does not grant the permission. The premise's list (imperfect, playful, uncertain, ambitious, needy, emotionally open) is a menu of examples, not a set of six assignments. One true permission is enough.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Permission slips are a school object and, more recently, a self-help writing prompt. Self-compassion exercises in clinical writing sit nearby, but no such protocol was opened or tested against this slip. Classification stays reimagined: a familiar prop, used so one adult authorizes a specific way of being and another adult hears it without taking over.

## Design notes

Materials: a small card each, a pen, a flat surface. Optional: they keep the card in a wallet or a book.

Standard, about 20 minutes. Silent drafting. The sentence names the permission, the situation where it is hard, and one thing the person will stop requiring of themselves this week. Example shape, fictional and disposable: "I give myself permission to be uncertain in front of you when we talk about money, and I will stop producing a perfect answer before I speak." They read aloud only if they want. The witness says what they heard, then stops. No improvements to the wording unless the author asks. Swap. Short: one sentence, no weekly clause. Extended: a second slip for a different item on the menu, plus a note about how the witness can avoid trampling it.

What makes the instructions fail: the partner rewriting the slip into advice; six permissions cranked out so every adjective in the premise is covered; a slip that is actually a rule for the partner ("I give myself permission to need you to text back in five minutes"); jokes that dissolve the sentence before it is heard.

Accessibility. Dictate if writing is painful; the author still chooses the final words. Large print or a phone note. Vision: witness reads it back. Hearing: silent exchange of cards. Mobility: card can rest on a lap. Long-distance: read it on a call or send it after both have drafted. Sensory overload: one sentence, then a break. No ceremony, candles, or audience required.

Journal cue: the permission sentence, dated, and a later line for whether they used it. The witness's reaction stays off the card unless invited.

## Consent and harm

Witnessing is optional. A person may write the slip and put it away unread. Permission to be needy or emotionally open is not permission to ignore the partner's limits. If a slip describes a want that would land on the partner's body, time, or money, it remains a self-permission until they make a separate, decline-able request.

## Red flags

Do not cite a self-compassion trial. Do not present the six adjectives as a complete model of healthy personality.
