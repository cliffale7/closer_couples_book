## Source premise

> Choose between ludicrous, revealing, morally complicated, or romantic hypotheticals.

## Must preserve

A forced choice between two invented options, followed by the reason, across four tones: ludicrous, revealing, morally complicated, and romantic. “Unhinged” means surprising and playful. It does not mean cruel, sexually coercive, or aimed at a real wound. The answer is hypothetical. It must not be collected later as consent or as a confession.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. Choosing between absurd alternatives has no supporting study on the project whitelist.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

“Would you rather” is a widely played parlor game. Commercial card decks exist. This chapter needs an original prompt bank written for the book. It is not a moral-reasoning assessment and not a clinical values protocol.

## Design notes

Build four small suits the writer invents: absurd logistics, a value collision with no tragic real victim, a romantic choice that costs comfort or time, and a self-knowledge dilemma. Each turn, both people choose privately — a coin under a hand, a face-down card — then reveal, then give one sentence of reason. Print a “third door”: either person may reject the frame of the question.

Sample prompts in the chapter must be original and marked as examples. Include an absurd pair, a revealing pair that is still safe (“a year of only instrumental music, or a year of only live conversation at dinner”), and a romantic pair that does not assume a script. Failure points: options that humiliate a body or an identity; a whole suit of sexual dares; a “moral” pair that corners someone into discussing a real planned harm; stacking questions until someone is too tired to pass.

Accessibility: oral play or large-type cards. Long-distance is a natural fit. No mobility requirement. For cognitive fatigue or sensory overload, use fewer items and stay in the ludicrous suit. Hearing: pass the options in text.

## Consent and harm

A hypothetical yes is not consent to do the thing. Romantic options stay imaginary unless the couple later makes a separate plan. Either person may discard a card. Passing does not forfeit a turn to the other person’s choice.

Distinguish 082, which discusses dilemmas with competing values and no obvious right answer, and 085, which considers unexpected life dilemmas. Entry 042 is a game of paired hypotheticals, including silly ones, not an ethics seminar. Keep them separate.

## Red flags

Do not imply affiliation with a branded card game, and do not reproduce its prompts. The phrase “would you rather” is common speech; the chapter should still look like original writing. Do not use real abuse, slurs, or body shame as punchlines. Do not claim the answers reveal character.
