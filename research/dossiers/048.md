# Dossier 048 — The Secret Mission Exchange

- Catalog ID: 048
- Part: 6 — Playfulness, Laughter, and a Little Mischief
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Assign each other private thoughtful, funny, or affectionate missions over several days.

## Must preserve

Each person assigns missions; the other carries them out, adapts them, or declines them, over several days; then they compare notes. The missions are thoughtful, funny, or affectionate actions, not clues. Privacy means the assignment is between the two of them, not that one person is watched in secret. A declined mission is a successful use of the rules.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. G2012 linked feeling appreciative and feeling appreciated with responsiveness, commitment, and relationship maintenance, across its methods, and noted that appreciation can show up in behavior. It did not test assigned secret missions, and a scripted task is outside its claim. Do not cite it as proof that missions improve a relationship.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Friend groups sometimes draw names for small acts of care. This chapter is that custom narrowed to a pair, with three tones and an explicit decline. It is not a clinical ritual and not a surveillance game.

## Design notes

Each person writes three missions: one thoughtful, one funny, one affectionate. Each mission must fit the receiver’s week, budget, body, and privacy. Exchange them sealed. Over several days the receiver completes, shrinks, or passes. A pass is reported as a pass, not as a failed grade. At the reunion, talk about what it was like to be given care, not about performance quality.

Sample missions should be original and fictional in tone: leave a note on the tea tin, retell an ordinary errand as a radio bulletin, send a sentence about a quality you noticed. Failure points: spending the partner’s money, involving an unbriefed third person, pranks, tracking whether the mission happened through location or message logs, and any list of stops to visit in order.

Accessibility: sample missions include no driving, no late nights, and no lifting. A mission can be “receive this” rather than “perform this,” for pain or fatigue. Long-distance missions are messages, parallel actions, or a mailed note. Sensory limits belong in the assignment instructions (“no strong scent,” “daytime only”).

## Consent and harm

No sexual mission in the default set. No mission that outs the partner or drafts an audience. Either person may rewrite a mission smaller or return it.

Near-duplicate 099, The Invisible Postcards, secretly collects short notes about moments the partner helped, impressed, or delighted, then exchanges the notes. Entry 048 assigns an action to be done. One is observation gathered into notes; the other is a task. Distinguish also 032, in which each person chooses their own affectionate acts rather than receiving an assignment. Do not merge these.

## Red flags

Do not structure the days as a clue trail, a race, or a checklist of places and objects. Do not punish an incomplete mission. Do not instruct hidden monitoring. Do not claim research support from gratitude studies.
