# Dossier 020 — The Listening Experiment

- Catalog ID: 020
- Part: 3 — The Science and Experience of Closeness
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: mixed
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

Take turns speaking uninterrupted about something meaningful while the other listens and reflects back.

## Must preserve

Three mechanics, all required: turns, uninterrupted speech about something meaningful, and a reflection the speaker can correct. Equal time matters. If the listener gives advice, tells their own story, or reflects only the last three words, the experiment did not happen.

## Evidence

L1998: intimacy in diary interactions tracked self-disclosure and partner disclosure, with perceived partner responsiveness as a partial mediator. Study 2 found emotional disclosure a stronger predictor than factual disclosure. The study did not test a timed reflection drill. A good reflection is one the speaker recognizes, not one that sounds skilled.

G2004: telling someone about a good event was associated with greater positive affect and well-being beyond the event itself. Perceived active-constructive responses were associated with higher relationship quality; passive or destructive responses were not the helpful pattern. That work is about responses to positive events. It is not a test of this turn-taking exercise. If the speaker's meaningful topic is grief, worry, or anger, cheerfulness is the wrong import from G2004.

R1988 is the framework behind L1998: disclosure, response, and the feeling of being understood. It does not supply a script.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Reflective listening is a person-centered clinical teaching practice. Motivational interviewing and couples-education classes use related skills. Classification stays mixed. The chapter borrows a widely taught shape and sets it beside research on responsiveness and on responses to good news. Neither source tested these rounds.

## Design notes

Materials: a timer. Optional note card for the listener with three stems: "I heard...," "It sounded like you felt...," "What did I miss?"

Pick the topic before the timer, something meaningful and finishable: a decision, a hope, a hard day, a good thing that happened. Say whether it is good news. If it is good news, the listener may use this book's habit of staying with what was said. That habit is not a definition of G2004's active-constructive responding. If the topic is grief, worry, or anger, quiet accuracy matters more than enthusiasm. Corrected after `review/evidence/batch-001-036.md`.

Standard: speaker for 6 minutes, uninterrupted. Listener reflects for 2 minutes. Speaker corrects for 1 minute. Swap. Short: 3 minutes of speech and a four-sentence reflection, both sides. Extended: a second round on the same topic after the correction, so the speaker can go where the first reflection opened a door.

Listener bans during the speech: advice, diagnosis, a matching story, questions. Questions belong only in the correction minute, and only to check meaning.

What makes the instructions fail: paraphrasing tone without content; arguing with the feeling; reflecting as a setup for a point; unequal turns; a topic so vague ("life") that the speaker performs instead of talks. Write a fictional sample reflection in the chapter so "reflect back" is visible.

Accessibility. Speech, writing, or AAC. If the speaker writes, the listener reads the whole note before reflecting. Hearing: written reflections. Vision: spoken turns, no need to watch a face. Mobility and pain: any posture. Long-distance: a call with a visible timer. Sensory overload: the short version, one topic, no background audio. Pause the timer if someone needs a break; pausing is not interrupting.

Journal cue: the topic, one sentence the speaker was glad to have reflected accurately, and one miss. Do not archive advice that arrived after the exercise.

## Consent and harm

The speaker chooses the meaning and may stop. The listener may decline a topic that asks them to absorb an accusation or a traumatic scene they cannot hold tonight. Reflection is not agreement and not therapy. Harmful behavior still requires a real response later; being heard is not immunity (the same boundary as 013).

## Red flags

Do not claim the chapter is clinically validated. Do not tell listeners to be active-constructive about every feeling. Do not quote a Gottman statistic about bids or stonewalling. None is established for this drill.
