# Dossier 091 — What We Want More Of

- Catalog ID: 091
- Part: 12 — Building a Closer Future Together
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Identify experiences and actions you want to increase, not just problems to remove.

## Must preserve

An increase: experiences and actions to grow. A list that is only the removal of a partner’s flaws has left the premise, even if it is phrased with the words “more of.”

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. G2004 is about sharing positive events and responsive listening, not about scheduling more of a behavior. It can remind the writer that a wish for more closeness lands better when it is specific and received with curiosity. It is not proof this list works.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Solution-focused conversations and appreciation practices often ask what to grow rather than what to eliminate. That is a practitioner habit, not a validated protocol for this chapter. Neighbor 063 translates a complaint into a wish. 091 starts from appetite, before the complaint.

## Design notes

About 30–40 minutes. Each person lists five “more of” items that name a presence: shared walks, a kind of joke, a sexual or affectionate pattern if wanted, quiet in the same room, time with friends, making something, a weekday ritual. Read any item that is secretly “less of your flaw,” and rewrite it until the positive action is visible. If it cannot be rewritten without blaming, park it for a direct request outside this chapter.

Share the lists. Each person asks one curious question. Together, choose one shared increase and one individual increase to try for a short, stated period. The individual increase does not require the partner’s participation. Review by describing what happened, not by auditing compliance.

Failure points: quotas; a list that is entirely the other person’s labor; “more sex” or “more talk” with no texture; treating the list as a debt.

Accessibility: include items possible at home, at low cost, and on low-energy days. Do not assume travel, restaurants, or late nights. Long-distance: more of letters, overlapping calls, or a shared watch-list counts. Sensory and pain limits shape what “more” can mean. Journal the two chosen increases and a date to notice them.

## Consent and harm

Wishes about sex, time, money, and family are information, not entitlements. A partner may decline an increase that costs their body, sleep, or other relationships. “More of” never overrides a limit. Either person may keep one list item private. This chapter does not replace a repair conversation about an ongoing harm.

## Red flags

Do not imply that a good relationship is an ever-rising line. Maintenance and rest count as “more of.” Do not use the list to pathologize a partner’s need for solitude. Do not convert the five items into a score against 106’s point allocation; that chapter has its own rules.
