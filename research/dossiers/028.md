## Source premise

Write a playful personal owner’s manual, including emotional needs, warning signs, and useful maintenance.

## Must preserve

Each person writes their own manual. It stays playful, and it includes emotional needs, warning signs, and useful maintenance. A serious diagnostic file, or a manual one person writes for the other's behavior, drops the premise. Playful means specific and lightly worded. It does not mean the needs are a joke.

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

"Owner's manual" pages are a popular relationship-writing exercise and a metaphor from product instructions. Classification stays reimagined. The three required sections are design. They are not a validated self-assessment. Entry 025 compares exact loving behaviors. Entry 029 sorts help by state. This chapter is a portrait of operating conditions, early warning, and maintenance of the self.

## Design notes

Materials: one sheet each, three headings: Operating notes, Warning signs, Maintenance. A pen. About 25 minutes to draft, 20 to read.

Prompt the contents without turning them into a clinical form. Operating notes: two emotional needs, stated as situations ("I need a few quiet minutes after work before I can talk about plans"). Warning signs: two observable signs that the writer is overloaded, withdrawn, or prickly, described without diagnosing a disorder. Maintenance: two small things that help on ordinary weeks, at least one of which the writer can do for themselves. The partner is not the only tool in the kit.

Read aloud or exchange pages. The reader asks one curious question and does not sign a compliance line. They may say which maintenance item they can support and which they cannot. Swap. Short: one item per heading. Extended: a "please do not" line that names a common misfire, still playful, still not a house rule.

What makes the instructions fail: warning signs that are actually surveillance of the partner; needs written as quotas ("text within five minutes or I am unsafe"); diagnostic labels as flavor; a tone so cute that the need disappears; one partner editing the other's manual.

Accessibility. Dictation counts if the author approves the wording. Large type. Hearing: silent reading. Vision: read-aloud. Mobility and pain: extra time, lap desk, or phone note. Long-distance: exchange after both drafts are done. Sensory overload: the short sheet. Playful examples in the chapter should include more than one culture's way of asking for space and food and time, not a single household script.

Journal cue: the maintenance item they hope is still true in six months, dated, with room to revise. Manuals expire.

## Consent and harm

A need that lands on the partner's body, money, or time becomes a request, which they may decline. Warning signs are information, not permission to police mood. If a "sign" is actually a harmful behavior, name the behavior and the limit; do not hide it inside a charming manual. Either person may keep a line unread.

## Red flags

Do not invent a psychological instrument. Do not imply that following the manual guarantees love. Do not use trademarked personality-system names as the section headings.
