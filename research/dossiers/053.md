## Source premise

> Imagine life without external judgment and what you would pursue, express, or abandon.

## Must preserve

Three verbs under an imagined freedom from audience: pursue, express, and abandon. The audiences are named first so the fantasy is specific. Afterward, the speaker says which items, if any, they want help with, and which must stay theirs alone. The chapter fails if “abandon” is heard only as a threat, or if the exercise becomes a confession contest.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. Imagining the absence of judgment has no outcome study on the project whitelist.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Daydreaming about a life without spectators is a common private habit. This chapter gives it three prompts and a boundary about what affects the partner. It is not a clinical values protocol and not an authenticity test.

## Design notes

Name the usual audiences: family, coworkers, friends, the partner, a former self. Set them outside the room imaginatively. Each person answers with one pursuit, one expression, and one abandonment. At least one answer should be ordinary, so the hour is not required to be a revelation. Then sort: help wanted, or mine alone.

Build a fork into the rules for “abandon.” If the item is a private habit or a costume of politeness, it can stay a wish. If it would drop a duty that affects the partner — care, money, fidelity, safety — label it “fantasy of relief” or “an actual request,” and do not leave the ambiguity standing.

Failure points: the partner arguing the speaker out of a harmless wish; the speaker performing bravery they do not mean; treating every social expectation as illegitimate.

Accessibility: write first if shame makes speech hard. Long-distance works. Nothing must be acted out. Fatigue: one verb is enough. No mobility demand.

## Consent and harm

Imagined privacy is not permission to harm anyone or to drop care for a dependent. The partner may ask which fork an “abandon” sits on, and may say what they need if it is a real request. Either person may pass.

Distinguish 049, which starts from a past fork and the dream still attached, and 083, which volunteers a surprising fact. Entry 053 is a present-tense counterfactual about judgment. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not coach abandonment of safety, caregiving, or consent. Do not treat the partner’s needs as mere “external judgment.” Do not promise that a judgment-free life is available or would be kinder.
