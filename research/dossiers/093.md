# Dossier 093 — The Relationship Bucket List

- Catalog ID: 093
- Part: 12 — Building a Closer Future Together
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Build a shared collection of adventures, traditions, achievements, and delightfully ridiculous goals.

## Must preserve

A shared collection with range: adventures, traditions, achievements, and ridiculous goals. A solemn list of status milestones, or one partner’s private bucket list copied onto a joint page, loses the premise.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. A2000 linked shared novel and arousing activities with short-term experienced relationship quality in brief laboratory tasks and in correlational work. A bucket list is not that experiment. Novelty can be one design value. It is not a requirement that every item be arousing, and the paper does not show that completing a list changes a relationship’s long-term course.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Bucket lists are a popular culture form, often individual and often commercial. The reimagining is a joint collection that includes ridiculous and ordinary items beside achievements. It is not a validated planning protocol.

## Design notes

About 40–60 minutes. Aim for twelve items or fewer. Constraints: at least two that are delightfully ridiculous, at least two that are ordinary and repeatable, and a mix across the four kinds in the premise. Mark a cost band in words (free, modest, save-for) without requiring anyone to disclose numbers they want private. No item gets a deadline that functions as a marital exam. Optional: star one item for the next season and leave the rest as a menu.

Failure points: a travel brochure; items that spend only one person’s energy; a list so long it becomes homework; ridiculous items that humiliate.

Accessibility: include home, daytime, seated, low-pain, and low-sensory items. Adventure includes a new recipe, a local bench, and a game, not only flights. Long-distance: include at least one item doable apart and one that requires being in the same place, so the list does not punish geography. Journal the list as a living page with room to retire items.

## Consent and harm

Items involving sex, other people, or someone’s body need a later real consent; the list does not pre-consent. Either partner may veto an item off the shared page. Cultural and religious traditions are included only when both want them; a tradition one person experiences as harm is not a bonding challenge. Distinguish 092’s individual dream and 055’s personal before-I-die list. Shared does not mean every hope must be joint.

## Red flags

Do not imply that a relationship without grand adventures is thin. Do not partner with a branded bucket-list product or copy a published list. Do not treat unfinished items as failure. Retiring a goal is part of keeping the list honest.
