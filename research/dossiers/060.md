## Source premise

> Revisit a minor misstep and practice acknowledgment, accountability, and repair instead of blame.

## Must preserve

One minor, already cooled misstep, moved through three stations in order: acknowledgment of what happened, accountability for one’s own part, and a repair that fits the person who was affected. Blame, counter-accusation, and a gift that skips the acknowledgment all miss the premise. Forgiveness is not a required product. The shop metaphor is this book’s device; the repair language is a practitioner frame. That combination is why the seed classification mixed stays.

## Evidence

GOTTMAN’s clinical writing popularized repair attempts. Treat the idea as a practitioner metaphor. No journal article is cited for it, and no statistic about how often repairs succeed or how often couples divorce may be written. No empirical citation is claimed for this three-station exercise. It has not been tested as a treatment.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Apology and making amends are ordinary moral practices. Gottman-style “repair attempt” language is the clinical neighbor, not a script to quote as science. The workshop structure — three stations, one minor misstep — is designed for this book.

## Design notes

Both people agree which misstep, and that it is minor: a snapped reply, a forgotten plan, a joke that cut. Exclude affairs, abuse, legal matters, and any pattern of ongoing harm. Station one: what happened, without a countercharge. Station two: the person who acted names their part. Station three: a specific amend — a redo, an apology with content, or a small routine change. The affected person says whether it fits, asks for a different small repair, or postpones.

Failure points: mutual blame that never reaches accountability; a present offered instead of acknowledgment; reopening a large injury because “we are already talking.”

Accessibility: letters read aloud count. Long-distance works. Speech difficulty: printed blanks for the three stations. Shame is a reason to keep the misstep minor, not a reason to skip accountability. No eye contact requirement. Fatigue: one misstep only.

## Consent and harm

The affected person may refuse to revisit. The actor does not choose the repair alone. Nobody is owed forgiveness on a schedule. If the event is part of ongoing coercion or harm, this chapter is the wrong tool; say so briefly and do not design steps for that situation.

Distinguish 064, The Trust Bank, which notices small deposits and withdrawals over time, and 058, which compares intended and perceived meaning. Entry 060 is one misstep, three stations. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not claim repairs erase harm. Do not invent a Gottman percentage. Do not push a minor frame onto a serious injury. Do not require the affected person to comfort the person who acted.
