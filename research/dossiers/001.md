# Dossier 001 — The Stranger Across From Me

- Catalog ID: 001
- Part: 1 — The Art of Really Knowing Someone
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

Pretend you have just met. Interview one another as fascinating strangers and discover how familiarity can hide curiosity.

## Must preserve

These two people already know each other. The work is a timed stranger-interview that lets a courteous, specific question reach something familiarity had filed as settled. If they slip into their usual shorthand, or if one person hosts while the other performs, the premise is gone.

## Evidence

A1997, Study 1: a roughly 45-minute set of escalating self-disclosure and relationship-building tasks produced greater post-interaction closeness than a small-talk comparison. The participants were not a study of long-term couples falling in love. Studies 2 and 3 did not find closeness differences for attitude matching, expected liking, or making closeness an explicit goal. This chapter shares an interest in good questions. It is a different procedure, with a different population, and it does not inherit Study 1's result.

L1998: in diary studies of daily interactions, intimacy tracked self-disclosure and partner disclosure, with perceived partner responsiveness as a partial mediator. In Study 2, emotional disclosure was a stronger predictor than factual disclosure. Those diaries did not test a stranger role-play.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Gottman's clinical writing (GOTTMAN) popularized "love maps" for knowing a partner's inner world. That is a practitioner metaphor. It is not a test of this interview, and it supplies no percentage the chapter may quote. Classification stays reimagined: a familiar getting-to-know-you impulse, rebuilt as play for people who are no longer strangers.

## Design notes

Materials: two seats, a visible timer, paper or a notes app. No imported question list.

Short (about 10 minutes): one question each, plus a follow-up, equal time. Standard (about 30 minutes): each person drafts three questions a polite stranger could ask, then interviews the other for one full timed turn. The interviewer may ask two follow-ups and does not answer inside that turn. Extended: a second round aimed only at a detail the first round left unfinished.

End with two minutes. Each person names one fact that had gone dull because it felt already known.

Question shapes to write fresh: a year they felt most like themselves; a habit a new friend would misread; a belief they rarely say in a group. Mix memory, preference, and belief so the game cannot collapse into favorite colors.

What makes the instructions fail: finishing each other's sentences; asking only last week's topics; using the role to cross-examine or to flirt-test; the interviewer filling silence with their own story. Announce "our goal is closeness" only as a mood, never as a claimed booster. A1997's later studies did not find an effect for making closeness an explicit goal.

Accessibility. Hearing: pass written questions, or sit where speech can be read; typing the answers still counts. Vision: spoken questions are enough. Mobility and pain: stay seated. Long-distance: a timed call works; cameras stay optional. Sensory overload: one question at a time, and either person may pause the timer.

Journal cue: what the stranger frame made audible, not a transcript of every answer.

## Consent and harm

Either person may decline a question without offering a substitute confession. The role-play is not permission to reopen a subject they have already asked to leave alone. Stories about third parties, bodies, and childhood stay optional.

## Red flags

Do not copy the question list from the A1997 procedure. Do not claim this makes partners fall in love. Do not attach a divorce-prediction statistic or any other invented Gottman number.
