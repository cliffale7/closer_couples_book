## Source premise

Individually classify romantic, affectionate, sensual, and sexual ideas as yes, no, or maybe; compare without pressure.

## Must preserve

They classify alone first. The ideas cover four lanes: romantic, affectionate, sensual, and sexual. The compare step happens without pressure. Maybe is not yes. No is not a negotiation. A shared to-do list, or a debate aimed at moving a no, drops the premise.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Yes / no / maybe lists are a common consent practice in sexuality education and in kink negotiation. Many printed lists are copyrighted. Classification stays reimagined. This version adds romantic and affectionate lanes beside sensual and sexual ones, and it structures the compare so that privacy and refusal stay intact. It is not a validated compatibility test, and the chapter must not copy a published checklist.

## Design notes

Materials: two private sheets, four lanes, three columns (yes, no, maybe). A pen. No shared screen until the compare rules are done.

The chapter supplies a short original sample in each lane, marked as examples they may ignore. Grain that keeps the game real: a weeknight walk with phones left behind; sitting in comfortable silence; a kiss in the kitchen; a massage that stays where they have already agreed; a planned sexual evening; sending a suggestive message. Do not reproduce a circulating checklist item-for-item. Include room for their own additions. Include conditions beside maybe ("only when I am not exhausted," "not in public") so maybe has edges.

Procedure. (1) Private sort, including the right to leave items blank. (2) Each person stars up to five yeses they are willing to discuss and up to three nos they want known so they are not stumbled into. Unstarred nos can stay unseen. (3) Compare only starred items, affectionate and romantic lanes before sexual if they want a gentler on-ramp. (4) Maybe is curiosity, not a booking. (5) End by naming at most one shared yes that is easy and soon, and say out loud that nothing else is scheduled.

Short: affectionate and romantic lanes only. Standard: all four lanes, about 45 minutes including private time. Extended: add conditions and a review date. Sexual items are skippable even in the standard game.

What makes the instructions fail: reading every no aloud; sulking or persuading; treating the sheet as entitlement; alcohol as a way past a no; a "maybe" recorded as a yes because the partner smiled; anatomy assumptions; a heterosexual script as the default examples.

Accessibility. Plain language, large print, or a verbal sort the person records privately. Pain, disability, and fatigue: body limits are nos, not failures, and the sheet should say that. Vision and hearing: private recording in whatever form they control. Long-distance: the sort works apart; the compare can be a call, with the same starring rule. Sensory overload: one lane. No demonstration during the sort.

Journal cue: the one shared yes they chose to remember, and the review date. Do not archive the partner's nos in the shared book. Private sheets stay private.

## Consent and harm

No is complete. Maybe cannot be spent later as if it were consent. A yes on paper is not a yes in the moment; they still ask. Anything involving people who are not these two adults is out. Intoxication is not an item to push past a limit. A no about safer-sex practices is a no, not a maybe to workshop. The game is not a hunt through sex acts and not a compatibility score. If the lists reveal a painful gap, the evening ends without scheduling, and neither person owes comfort-sex to close the gap.

Entry 033 talks about how attraction has changed. This chapter sorts specific ideas and protects the nos.

## Red flags

Do not copy a published yes/no/maybe list. Do not claim the sort predicts satisfaction or sexual harmony. Do not include prompts about non-consent, coercion, or anyone who cannot consent. Adult partners only.
