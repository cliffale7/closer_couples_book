# Dossier 088 — The Thing I’m Still Figuring Out

- Catalog ID: 088
- Part: 11 — Radical Curiosity and the Surprising Stuff
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Share unresolved questions about self, life, intimacy, identity, or relationships.

## Must preserve

An unresolved question that stays unresolved. The listener’s success is company, not closure. Advice that ties the bow ends the chapter too early.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. L1998’s finding that perceived responsiveness helps intimacy become felt is a relevant stance for the listener. It is not a test of sitting with unanswered questions.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Spiritual direction, friendship, and late-night talk all include unfinished questions. None of those traditions was opened as a manual here. The activity is not a validated protocol for identity development or relationship satisfaction.

## Design notes

About 25–40 minutes. Each person completes one sentence: “I am still figuring out ___.” The blank should be a question they cannot honestly answer tonight, about self, life, intimacy, identity, or relationships. They may say what makes it alive now, what they have already tried, and what kind of company helps (silence, curiosity, a story from the listener’s own unknown, or simply being believed). The listener’s first response is not advice. One fair question is “What makes this alive right now?” Reassurance that cancels the question (“you’ll definitely be fine”) is a miss. After both turns, write the two questions in the journal as open items. Review is optional weeks later; solving them is not the assignment.

Failure points: a partner who researches the answer before the speaker has finished; turning a personal unknown into a referendum on the relationship; competing for whose question is heavier.

Accessibility: writing or speaking. Long-distance works well because the pace is slow. Offer a plain prompt beside any metaphorical one. People who dislike open loops may set a date to revisit without requiring a conclusion. No ritual objects are needed.

## Consent and harm

Questions about sexuality, gender, faith, leaving, staying, or having children can be offered and can be too charged for a casual night. The speaker chooses a question they can hold in this company. The listener does not recruit the question into a plan. If the unknown includes safety or a crisis, respond as a partner, not as a game participant, and seek outside help when needed.

Neighbors: 051 is about qualities still being developed; 056 is about questions that may have no human answer; 088 is a personal unknown that still belongs to the speaker. Keep the three apart.

## Red flags

Do not treat uncertainty as immaturity. Do not fill the silence with a framework that renames the question in clinical or spiritual jargon the speaker did not choose. Do not require both people to have equally dramatic unknowns.
