## Source premise

> Consider alternate lives produced by different choices and the dreams still attached to them.

## Must preserve

A real fork, an imagined life on the other side of it, and the dream that is still attached. The listener’s job is to hear the lingering desire, not to prosecute the choice or recruit the speaker back onto the old path. The chapter is lost if it becomes a regret trial of the partner, or a fantasy of being a different person entirely rather than a life this person might have entered.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. The project whitelist contains no study of counterfactual life review as a couple exercise, and none should be invented.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Wondering about a road not taken is a common literary and kitchen-table habit. This chapter uses that habit with a structure. It is not a grief protocol, not a career-counseling instrument, and not a clinical life-review therapy. Do not quote a poem or treat a famous title as the chapter’s source.

## Design notes

Each person chooses one fork that was imaginable from their own life: a city, a vocation, a timing, a training they did not start. Skip “if I had been born someone else.” Three beats: a single ordinary day in that life; the desire that still lives in this life; one thing from the present life they would not trade away. The listener reflects the desire in a sentence and does not argue that the speaker chose correctly or incorrectly.

Failure points: announcing a secret plan to leave under the cover of imagination; naming a partner or child as the reason a life was lost, without room for care; stacking several forks until the conversation is only catalogue.

Accessibility: conversation or letters. Long-distance fits. If the fork is a death, an illness, or another loss, the person may pass or choose a milder fork. No forced mourning. Speech or text both work. Fatigue: one fork each.

## Consent and harm

The partner is not on trial for the historical choice. Either person may decline a fork that is mostly trauma. Imagining a path is not agreeing to pursue it.

Distinguish 053, which imagines a present life without external judgment and asks what one would pursue, express, or abandon. Entry 049 starts from a past choice and the dream still stuck to it. Distinguish 003, which names experiences that did happen. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not claim that imagining alternatives increases commitment or resolves regret. Do not romanticize a path that would have required harming someone. Do not diagnose the speaker from the fork they chose to describe.
