## Source premise

> Lightheartedly impersonate each other, then discuss what is accurate or wildly wrong.

## Must preserve

Two halves of equal weight: a light impersonation, then a sorting conversation. The portrayed person says what was accurate, what was flattering fiction, and what stung. Without the sorting, the chapter is only a roast. “Ridiculous” licenses exaggeration of an already shared joke. It does not license mockery of identity, body, disability, or distress.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. Being imitated has no supporting study on the project whitelist, and this chapter should not borrow findings about empathy training.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Affectionate impressions are an ordinary comic custom among people who know each other. Therapeutic role-play in which partners switch positions inside a real conflict is a different practice and is not what this premise asks for. Keep the scenes mundane and the tone playful.

## Design notes

Agree the scene first: ordering dinner, narrating a weekday morning, or telling a harmless story at a party. Each person has about two minutes to “be” the other, exaggerating a gesture or phrase that is already an inside joke. Then the portrayed person sorts lines into accurate, flattering fiction, and “that stung.” The sting sort is required. If something stung, it is named before anyone defends it as a joke.

Before the timer, each person may veto one trait: a family accent, a speech pattern, a body habit, a sore mood. Failure points: performing the partner’s anger or sadness; sexual caricature; a cultural or disability impression; ending on the laugh and skipping the sort.

Accessibility: if a voice impression is difficult, write a short monologue in the partner’s cadence or play a single gesture. Seated performance is the default. Long-distance works on video or as exchanged scripts. Hearing: trade written dialogue. Do not ask anyone to mimic a stutter, a tic, an accent tied to ethnicity, or a mobility pattern.

## Consent and harm

Either person can stop the portrayal mid-scene. The discussion is not a debate the impersonator gets to win. What stung is data for an apology or a redo, not material for a second joke.

Distinguish 101, which compares reasoning on a scenario, and 018, which is silent communication. Entry 044 is style and manner, deliberately exaggerated, then corrected by the person who was portrayed. Do not merge those mechanisms.

## Red flags

Do not frame the sting as oversensitivity. Do not claim impressions reveal the unconscious. Do not use the partner’s mental-health history, body, or family as the joke. Label any sample dialogue as fictional.
