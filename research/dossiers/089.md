# Dossier 089 — The Relationship We Would Invent

- Catalog ID: 089
- Part: 12 — Building a Closer Future Together
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Design relationship expectations and rituals from scratch rather than borrowing assumptions.

## Must preserve

A deliberate draft of expectations and rituals made by these two people, after borrowed assumptions are named and set where they can be declined. If the chapter never surfaces the borrowed script, “from scratch” is only a slogan.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Couples inherit rituals from families, religions, media, and previous relationships. Designing agreements on purpose is a common therapeutic and secular practice. It is not a validated protocol. This chapter stays with the real relationship. It does not become 104’s fictional civilization, event deck, or constitution worksheet.

## Design notes

About 45–70 minutes. Three rounds.

Round 1: each person names two assumptions they imported from family, religion, culture, an app, or an ex-relationship. Examples: who apologizes first, how holidays work, whether solitude needs a defense, what counts as a date. Put them on a “borrowed” card. The card is a default you may decline, not a villain.

Round 2: draft four living agreements in plain language: how we greet after time apart, how we repair a small miss, how we celebrate, how we protect solitude. Each agreement must be something both can picture this month. Abstract values get translated into a behavior.

Round 3: choose one ritual to try for two weeks. Keep it tiny. Set a review, not a forever stamp.

Failure points: a utopia document; one partner’s family script renamed “from scratch”; agreements that police the other’s body, friends, or phone; so many rituals that the draft is a second job.

Accessibility: speech or writing. Long-distance couples design for the channel they actually have. Include at least one ritual that works during illness, low money, and low energy. No product purchases. Journal the four agreements and the borrowed cards on one page so the defaults remain visible.

## Consent and harm

Either person may refuse an agreement that affects sex, money, children, family contact, or public identity. Those topics can be named as “not tonight.” Agreements are revisable. They are not 095’s promises and not legal or religious vows. A partner who needs a familiar ritual for cultural or disability reasons can keep it on purpose; from scratch includes the freedom to retain, not only to discard.

## Red flags

Do not imply that traditional or cultural forms are immature. Do not assume marriage, monogamy, shared finances, or a child-free life. Do not present the draft as science. Two people inventing their relationship is a craft conversation.
