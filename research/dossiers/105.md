## Source premise

> Guess the lived story behind your partner’s deeper compliments before they disclose the actual source.

## Must preserve

Compliments specific enough to have a source, a guess about the lived episode, then the reveal. The signature twist, from the project’s expanded brief: “Reveal the real memory or behavior behind the compliment, which may be entirely different from what the recipient imagined.” A correct guess is pleasant. A surprised reveal is the mechanism working. Formulated for this project, without a worldwide-novelty claim.

## Evidence

G2012 tied feeling appreciative and feeling appreciated to responsiveness, commitment, and relationship maintenance. Appreciation can be visible in behavior. That is a relationship-maintenance finding. It is not proof that guessing the story behind a compliment works, and it is not a trial of a scripted compliment exercise.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Toasts and “what I love about you” lists are common. They usually stop at the adjective. The original piece is the hidden episode and the recipient’s guess before disclosure. Do not turn the sheet into a love-languages quiz. I2024 found little support for one primary language, for exactly five languages, or for matching as the key to satisfaction. People do seem to benefit from a range of affectionate behavior. This decoder is not that theory.

## Design notes

About 15–25 minutes. Each person writes five observations beyond looks and stock adjectives such as kind, beautiful, or amazing. Each line should point at a pattern or a behavior. The writer privately notes the real episode and does not show it yet. Decoder sheet columns: compliment; recipient’s guessed episode; actual episode; “same / different.” The recipient guesses the lived story. Then the writer reveals the memory, which may be another day entirely. Sample, original: compliment, “You make room for a story without parking in it.” Guess: “The night I talked about my sister.” Reveal: “Last Tuesday you put the dish down and did not offer a fix.” Both are respectful. The reveal simply tells the truth about the source.

One round of five is enough. A short path is three. The receiver may say the guessed episode also mattered; it does not replace the writer’s source.

Failure points: compliments that are advice (“you’re so good when you finally relax”); a guess grilled for accuracy; using the reveal to show the partner they do not pay attention.

Accessibility: spoken or written. Long-distance: exchange sealed lines, guess on the call, then reveal. No appearance ranking. A compliment that depends on hearing a tone or seeing a face should be phrased so the meaning is in the action. Journal the actual episodes the recipient wants to keep, not the misses.

## Consent and harm

Skip sexual or bodily compliments unless both opt in. Do not compliment someone for tolerating harm. The recipient may pass on guessing a line that feels loaded and still hear the reveal, or may pass on the reveal too. Distinguish 030, which practices receiving praise without deflection, and 078, which completes one unpaid thank-you. Here the work is decoding a specific memory the giver has in mind.

## Red flags

Do not claim G2012 tested the decoder. Do not score how well someone knows their partner. A completely different imagined source is a successful twist, not a relationship problem. Do not copy compliment lists from published games.
