# Dossier 037 — The Desire Nobody Asked About

## Source premise

> Discuss previously unexpressed wishes, fantasies, or forms of affection without obligations to act.

## Must preserve

The wish is allowed to remain a wish. One person names something previously unasked — a fantasy, a form of affection, or a private want — and the other receives it without turning the telling into a plan, a test, or a debt. Curiosity, and an explicit statement that hearing is not agreement, are the activity. If the chapter ends by scheduling the wish, the mechanism is gone. Any later enactment belongs to a separate conversation.

## Evidence

L1998 is a neighboring finding, not a test of this chapter. In diary studies of daily interactions, intimacy tracked self-disclosure and partner disclosure, with perceived partner responsiveness as a partial mediator. In Study 2, emotional disclosure predicted intimacy more strongly than factual disclosure. Those diaries did not study sexual or romantic fantasy, did not assign this conversation, and do not show that naming a desire changes a relationship. No clinical trial of this activity is claimed.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Wish lists and “yes / no / maybe” sorts are common in couples conversation and in sexuality education. They are practices, not a validated protocol for this chapter. Do not reproduce any published questionnaire.

## Design notes

Write before speaking. Offer three lanes: an affectionate gesture nobody has requested, a fantasy that may stay imaginary, and a form of closeness the speaker has edited out of ordinary talk. Share one lane at a time. The listener only restates what they heard, names something they appreciate about being told, and repeats that hearing it is not a yes. The speaker then marks the item “tell only,” “maybe another day,” or “please do not ask me to do this.”

A short sitting is one lane each. A longer sitting reopens a “maybe” only if the speaker brings it back. Failure points the writer should solve in the instructions: the listener problem-solves, bargains, or looks wounded; the speaker apologizes the wish down to nothing; either person treats silence as assent.

Accessibility: speaking, writing, or a voice note all complete the turn. Sexual vocabulary is optional. Pain, fatigue, and limited mobility are legitimate content — pace, posture, warmth without a required position — rather than lesser forms of desire. Long-distance works by voice or letter. For sensory overload, use one wish and a planned pause. Eye contact is unnecessary.

## Consent and harm

Passing a lane is a complete turn. Naming a fantasy is not consent to act it out, to hear a critique, or to repeat it later. A wish that would involve someone who has not agreed, or that depends on humiliation, stays in the “tell only” lane. The chapter should not provide a procedure for those acts.

Distinguish 034, which sorts ideas into yes, no, and maybe and then compares lists. Entry 037 is about what had not been asked, including items that will never be enacted. Distinguish 040, which maps conditions for romance rather than disclosing one unasked wish. These are neighboring chapters, not duplicates to merge.

## Red flags

Do not promise that disclosure will align desire or repair a dry spell. Do not assume who wants more, who wants less, or which gender script applies. Do not import scenes from pornography or from a commercial card deck. A passed wish is not a score.
