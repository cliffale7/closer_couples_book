# Dossier 087 — The Question Behind the Question

- Catalog ID: 087
- Part: 11 — Radical Curiosity and the Surprising Stuff
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Follow an ordinary answer with successive thoughtful questions revealing deeper meaning.

## Must preserve

A ladder that starts at an ordinary answer and descends by thoughtful questions, with the speaker remaining the authority on meaning. A single deep question, or an interviewer who supplies the “real” meaning, loses the mechanism.

## Evidence

L1998, in diary studies, found that intimacy tracked self-disclosure and partner disclosure, with perceived partner responsiveness as a partial mediator. In Study 2, emotional disclosure predicted intimacy more strongly than factual disclosure. R1988 is the process model that work tested: disclosure, response, and the feeling of being understood. Neither source tested a scripted ladder of follow-up questions. The ladder is a way of making room for a more emotional disclosure. It is not evidence that three questions produce intimacy.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Coaching, journalism, and some therapies use follow-up questions. Those professions are not the protocol here. The reimagining is a short, consent-based ladder from a mundane answer toward meaning, usable by adults who are not clinicians. Neighbor 010 explores layers under a fear. Neighbor 097, an original formulation, investigates one strong reaction while refusing to impose a cause. This chapter starts from an ordinary answer and does not require a powerful feeling.

## Design notes

About 20–30 minutes. Person A answers something ordinary: how the day was, what they had for lunch, what they are looking forward to. Person B asks up to four follow-ups, one at a time, aimed at meaning rather than trivia. Fair directions: what was good or costly about it; what it reminded them of; what they wanted and did not say; what they hope the other person understands. After each answer, a brief reflection of content and feeling, then a check: “Is that close?” Person A can stop the ladder, rewrite a question, or park the meaning. Then switch. The last word on what the answers mean belongs to the speaker.

Failure points: interrogation tone; questions that funnel toward a theory the listener already holds; fixing the problem that appears; stacking ladders until someone is exhausted.

Accessibility: share the question directions in print. Allow long pauses. A written ladder, passed back and forth, suits overload and long distance. No eye contact. Four questions is a maximum, not a quota.

## Consent and harm

Ordinary beginnings can open grief, work stress, or desire. The speaker sets depth. The listener does not convert a mood into a relationship verdict. Skip the exercise when either person is too depleted for questions. This is not 020’s longer uninterrupted listening turn, though the same respect for reflection applies.

## Red flags

Do not claim the ladder is the L1998 procedure. Do not promise that deeper questions are always kinder; poorly timed depth is pressure. Do not borrow copyrighted lists of “powerful questions.” The directions above are enough for the writer to phrase fresh wording in the chapter voice.
