## Source premise

> Explore consciousness, mortality, love, morality, fate, and mystery without forcing agreement.

## Must preserve

The questions stay open. Consciousness, mortality, love, morality, fate, and mystery are the field. Success is not agreement, a winner, or a shared creed. Each person may say “I don’t know” and describe what the question feels like rather than taking a position. The closing move names a question they want to keep unfinished.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. Nothing on the project whitelist measures philosophical conversation between partners, and no finding about consciousness, fate, or soulmates should be invented.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Unresolvable questions are the ordinary business of philosophy, religion, and late conversation. This chapter borrows that custom and adds an explicit ban on forcing agreement. It is not a debate round, not catechism, and not therapy.

## Design notes

Write one plain question for each theme, plus a blank, all original to the book. Sample questions should be answerable with uncertainty: what, if anything, you think persists; what love is doing when it is quiet; whether a hard choice was yours. Each person responds. The listener’s only required move is “what I heard,” not a correction.

A short version uses two themes. A longer version walks the set and ends with the unfinished question each person wants to carry. Failure points: correcting someone’s theology; proving a theory of mind; using mortality talk to pressure commitment or confession; scoring whose answer is braver.

Accessibility: keep the session short if grief, illness, or fatigue is present. Long-distance works. Questions should be plain language. A quiet room helps sensory sensitivity; walking is optional, never required. Faith and the absence of faith are both legitimate content and neither is the punchline.

## Consent and harm

Pass on any theme. If a recent death or illness makes mortality raw, switch themes without treating that as a problem to solve. Nobody has to land on the partner’s view.

Distinguish 082, which weighs dilemmas with competing values, and 050, which asks what makes a life worthwhile through concrete scenes. Entry 056 is inquiry that may not resolve. Distinguish 045, where the propositions are absurd on purpose. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not claim scientific results about consciousness, fate, or destiny. Do not mock a faith or an atheism. Do not steer the talk toward a conclusion the book prefers. No quotation from a sacred text or a philosopher should be presented as the couple’s answer.
