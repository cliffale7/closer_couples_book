# Dossier 062 — The Safety Map

- Catalog ID: 062
- Part: 8 — Trust, Communication, and Emotional Safety
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Map actions, situations, and words that create emotional safety or guardedness.

## Must preserve

A personal map of cues, in both directions: actions, situations, and words that help the speaker open, and those that bring guardedness up. Asymmetry between partners is a finding to respect, not a problem to average away. The map does not obligate openness once a “safe” condition is present. Guardedness may remain.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. L1998’s perceived responsiveness is a neighboring idea about feeling understood in daily interactions. That diary work did not map safety cues and is not cited as evidence that this worksheet works. No trauma, polyvagal, or “safe space” study was opened, and none should be implied.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Couples often learn, informally, which tones make honesty easier. This chapter turns that learning into an explicit two-region map. It is not a trauma protocol, not a clinical safety plan for violence, and not a guarantee that the relationship is safe.

## Design notes

A shared page with two regions, “I open” and “I guard.” Each person adds actions, situations, and words. Fictional examples the writer may include, clearly labeled: a phone put away, a joke at a sore spot, a rushed tone, being asked something personal in front of friends. Compare overlaps and differences. Choose one condition to try in a later conversation, and one guardedness cue the partner will respect rather than push through.

Failure points: a map that only lists the partner’s failures; equating safety with agreement; demanding disclosure because the map was completed.

Accessibility: safety includes sensory needs (light, noise, scent, pain), pace, and privacy. Long-distance entries may include response time and a word when a reply will be late. Directness and politeness differ by culture and family; the map is personal. Speech, large type, or cards all work. No eye contact is required to discuss the map.

## Consent and harm

Leaving a cue unnamed is allowed. The map is not a promise to be unguarded, and it is not a character verdict that someone is “an unsafe person.” If the real concern is violence or coercion, this worksheet is not the intervention; do not design steps for controlling or confronting a partner inside the chapter.

Distinguish 057, which specifies help and harm on a bad day, and 011, which describes parts of the self hidden from the wider world. Entry 062 maps situational and verbal cues that open or close the speaker with this partner. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not claim the map treats trauma or post-traumatic stress. Do not say that love and safety are the same thing. Do not require the guarded person to justify every closed door. No invented physiology.
