## Source premise

> Share personal bucket lists, including ordinary pleasures and grand adventures.

## Must preserve

A personal list, not a joint contract. Ordinary pleasures and grand adventures both belong on it; a list of only summits misses the premise. The partner witnesses, may offer help with one item, and must leave at least one item entirely to the speaker. Items labeled “someday, and maybe never” still count as worth saying.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. No mortality, bucket-list, or goal study was opened, and none on the project whitelist applies. Do not invent one.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

The bucket list is a popular-culture custom: a written set of experiences a person hopes to meet before death. This chapter uses that custom and insists on ordinary items beside grand ones. It is not a clinical mortality intervention and not a legacy-therapy protocol.

## Design notes

Each person drafts at least eight items, with at least half ordinary or cheap: a meal, a skill, a conversation, a nearby place, a repeated pleasure. Grand items are allowed and labeled as such. Share the lists. Each partner marks one item they would like to help with and one they will not take over.

Sort the finished list into three handwritten marks: “ordinary and soon,” “grand and maybe never,” and “help welcome.” The partner may initial only the help-welcome items they truly mean. A fictional sample list should mix a repeated Tuesday pleasure with one far-off adventure so the proportion is visible.

Failure points: a competition of ambition; treating the list as a shared obligation; skipping ordinary pleasures; funding promises made in the warmth of the conversation.

Accessibility: items need not assume walking, travel money, or a long future. Include prompts for “from this chair,” “in this city,” and “this year.” Chronic illness and pain can shape items around company, taste, and texture. Long-distance sharing is natural. If mortality talk floods the speaker, the list may be shortened or passed; the partner does not push for more items. Large type or speech both work.

## Consent and harm

Hearing a wish is not agreeing to fund it, to do something dangerous, or to include it in the couple’s plans. No pressure to add sexual, parental, or legacy items. Either person may keep an item private.

Distinguish 093, The Relationship Bucket List, which builds a shared collection of adventures, traditions, and goals. Entry 055 is personal. The partner’s role is witness plus optional help, not co-ownership. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not promise that listing experiences extends life, reduces fear, or organizes grief. Do not give medical advice. Do not shame a short list or a local one. Label sample lists fictional.
