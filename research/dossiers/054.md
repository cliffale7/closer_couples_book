## Source premise

> Tell your life as a legend or novel with themes, characters, antagonists, twists, and unfinished chapters.

## Must preserve

Emplotment, not a timeline. The telling needs a theme, characters, an antagonist, a twist, and an unfinished chapter. The antagonist may be a circumstance, an illness, a year, or a habit, not necessarily a villain. The partner retells the theme in one sentence and asks which part is metaphor. The chapter fails if the myth is treated as a diagnosis or if the unfinished chapter is closed for the speaker.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

People tell their lives as stories in families, fiction, and memoir. That is the frame. The chapter is not narrative therapy, not trauma treatment, and not a required “hero’s journey” with imported stages. A plain novel outline satisfies the premise as well as a legend does.

## Design notes

Offer a one-page structure: title, theme, three characters (ages of the self are allowed), one antagonist, one twist, one unfinished chapter. Tell it in under six minutes. The listener reflects the theme and checks the metaphor. Include a short fictional example so writers and readers can hear the tone.

Failure points: casting a living person as a caricatured villain; locking the teller into a tragic role they cannot revise; requiring mythic language; analyzing the story as a symptom.

Accessibility: oral, written, or a few drawn symbols with captions. Long-distance works. Cultural variety: do not require Western quest stages. Grief: an antagonist can be a season of life. Fatigue: title, twist, and unfinished chapter are a valid short form. Speech or text both work.

## Consent and harm

Living people appear only in terms the teller would say to their face, or as abstracted roles (“a gatekeeper”). The partner does not interpret the myth clinically. The teller controls which chapter stays unfinished.

Distinguish 006, The Museum of Me, which tours objects, people, songs, and events as exhibits, and 077, which maps a relationship timeline. Entry 054 is a plotted story of a single life. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not claim that rewriting a story heals trauma. Do not turn the exercise into exposure. Do not quote a copyrighted myth or novel as the template. Label the sample mythology fictional.
