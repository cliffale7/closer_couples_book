# Dossier 095 — A Promise I Actually Want to Keep

- Catalog ID: 095
- Part: 12 — Building a Closer Future Together
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Make a few realistic, meaningful relationship commitments with actionable follow-through.

## Must preserve

Few promises, each one wanted by the person making it, realistic, and attached to a follow-through they control. A stack of vows, or a promise extracted from the partner, loses the premise.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Promises, vows, and New Year resolutions are ancient folk forms. Gollwitzer (1999), a review opened for this dossier, distinguishes goal intentions (“I intend to reach x”) from implementation intentions (“When situation x arises, I will do y”). The review describes a research program in which if-then plans supported goal attainment in the domains studied. It is not a study of promises between partners, and its findings are not a guarantee inside a relationship. The chapter may offer an if-then line as optional scaffolding. It must not present that line as a tested couple treatment. Bare sincerity is a weak design; a cue and a small action are the actionable part. This remains a reimagined promise ritual, not an established protocol.

## Design notes

About 25–40 minutes. Each person offers one to three promises they would still want to keep if nobody applauded. Gates: it is meaningful to the relationship or to how they show up in it; they control it; it can be done within a month; it is specific. Optional second sentence: “When [cue], I will [act].” Example shape, not a script to impose: “When we sit down on Thursday, I will put my phone in the other room until the tea is finished.” The partner does not enlarge the promise. Set a review date. At the review, the outcomes are kept, rewritten, or retired. A broken promise is named and rewritten. Shame is not the follow-through.

Failure points: promises of feelings (“I will always desire you”); promises to police a partner’s other relationships; grand vows with no cue; a list longer than three.

Accessibility: the act must be possible on a low-energy week. Offer non-physical promises beside any that involve errands or outings. Long-distance: promises about calls, letters, and logistics. Write them where both can see the wording, in the speaker’s own verbs. Journal the promise, the cue, and the review date.

## Consent and harm

No one may demand a promise in exchange for affection or safety. Promises about sex are welcome only as commitments the speaker wants, and they never bind the other person’s body. Either person may refuse to witness a promise that pressures them. This is not 012’s permission slip and not 060’s repair of a past misstep. A promise is forward-looking and voluntary.

## Red flags

Do not quote Gollwitzer’s review as proof these vows will be kept. Do not borrow wedding liturgy. Do not treat a missed promise as evidence about character. Realistic includes small. Meaningful includes ordinary care.
