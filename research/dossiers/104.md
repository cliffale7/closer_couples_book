## Tradition or practitioner frame

Constitution games, salon utopias, and values card sorts are widespread. They are not a validated protocol. During play the couple stays inside the fiction. Rhymes with their actual life can be noticed afterward. A real crisis is discussed by setting the cards down, if both agree, and is not smuggled in as a draw.

## Design notes

About 45–90 minutes. Worksheet columns: number; principle in one sentence a stranger could follow; whose words started it; dissent margin; event that touched it; final wording. Aim for ten principles. Illustrate the sheet with a simple seal the couple draws. No art skill is required.

Phase 1, about fifteen minutes, silent: each person drafts six candidate laws, holidays, freedoms, or consequences. Examples of grain: “Rest days are real; no one schedules over them without a yes from the person resting.” “Guests may refuse a ritual.” “Praise is spoken to the person and is not posted as a ranking.”

Phase 2: merge to ten. A line stays only if both can live with it. Up to two protected dissents may be recorded: one person wants the sentence on the page, the other signs the margin “I do not share this.” A dissent is not a veto of the other’s safety, and it is not erased to force harmony.

Phase 3: shuffle the twelve event cards. Draw three, or four if time is easy. For each card, about eight minutes. Name the principle under strain. Answer the card’s three questions. Then choose one move. Amend: rewrite the principle; both must agree to the new sentence. Defend: keep the sentence and say the human cost aloud. Exception: one sentence that narrows the principle; both agree; mark strain +1. If they cannot choose, the card stays open and strain rises by 2. An open card is an unfinished argument inside the fiction.

Strain tallies the constitution, not the couple’s worth. At 4 or more, they either retire one principle or add one freedom that begins “Citizens may always…” There is no winner and no relationship score.

Close: read the ten lines. Each person adds one holiday or one ordinary ritual. Sign only if both want to. The signature is play, not a legal or relational contract. A yearly glance can live beside 094; it is not the same ritual.

### Event deck

Each card ends with: “Which principle strains? What does it cost to keep it? What does it cost to change it?”

1. The stores run low. People outside the civilization ask for a share of what your law called private.
2. A holiday you both love requires a ritual a guest did not agree to.
3. A citizen wants to leave for a year and still help decide the laws.
4. A running joke has started leaving one person out of the laugh.
5. Someone needs a silent day. Your law says hard news is spoken before sleep.
6. A guest repeats a story that was told in private.
7. The person who plans best asks for a permanent extra vote during crises.
8. A storm blocks the only road. Your law forbids ordering anyone to work.
9. Two citizens want a private calendar that skips the shared rest day.
10. An old promise and a new freedom now contradict each other.
11. Public praise has turned into a ranking on the wall.
12. A newcomer brings a skill you need and a custom your constitution never mentioned.

Reset: shuffle used cards back in. A second session draws from the remaining deck or rewrites principles before a new draw. Do not reuse a card in the same sitting. The deck may be copied for play. It is not a commercial expansion.

Failure points: principles so abstract the events cannot touch them; a dissent treated as sulking; using card 7 or card 10 to relitigate a real, sore vote; scoring strain as who compromised more.

Accessibility: seated; cards read aloud; a short path is six principles and two events. Long-distance: shared worksheet, one person draws and reads. Large print. No map-building on the floor. Principles about religion, children, money, sex, and mobility stay optional. Do not assume marriage, monogamy, or a shared purse. A citizen with limited stamina is already a full citizen; card 8 must not become a moral about who is useful.

## Consent and harm

Either person may discard a card that lands on a live grief or a real harm, and draw the next. The fiction is the safety. If a card opens an actual fear, pause the game. Distinguish 089, which designs this relationship’s expectations and rituals from scratch and has no civilization and no event deck. Keep both. Do not convert 104 into a talk about house rules unless both explicitly end the game first.

## Red flags

Do not present the constitution as enforceable between the partners. Do not copy another game’s cards or constitution. Do not claim values clarification has outcome data for this deck. Strain is a design counter. It is not a diagnosis of the bond.
