# Dossier 085 — What Would You Do If…?

- Catalog ID: 085
- Part: 11 — Radical Curiosity and the Surprising Stuff
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Consider unexpected dilemmas about money, morality, relationships, and life-changing opportunities.

## Must preserve

Unexpected forks in money, morality, relationships, and opportunity, answered in one’s own voice. The product is how each person approaches the fork, including the regret they would accept. It is not a score and not a secret test of whether the partners match.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Hypothetical “what would you do” talk is ordinary courtship and friendship. Structured rounds are a light reimagining, not a validated decision-making protocol.

## Design notes

About 35–50 minutes. Three rounds, drawn in an order neither person controls: one money fork, one moral or loyalty fork, one life-changing opportunity. Example seeds, original to this dossier: a sudden debt that could be cleared by selling something the other person loves; a sibling who asks you to take sides in a fight you did not witness; a one-year offer in another city that would advance a craft and interrupt your current life. Each person answers for themselves: the first pull, the value underneath, the regret they could live with, and what they would want to talk about before acting. The listener reflects the tradeoff. They do not announce the right answer.

A fourth round, “what would my partner do,” belongs to 101 and should not be slipped in here.

Failure points: hypotheticals that are actually announcements (“what if I spent our savings”); traps designed so only one answer is loving; rapid-fire dilemmas that never reach values.

Accessibility: show the fork in writing. Allow thinking time. Long-distance works. Offer a low-stakes silly fork as a warm-up if the first real one is too charged, then return to the premise. No physical challenge is attached.

## Consent and harm

Either person may discard a fork that mirrors a current crisis and replace it with a more distant one. Do not require disclosure of real debts, affairs, or job searches under the costume of a hypothetical. Relationship forks must respect the couple’s actual agreements; the chapter does not assume monogamy, marriage, or shared bank accounts. Distinguish 082, where the point is competing values and no verdict. Distinguish 042, where the hypotheticals are deliberately unhinged. This chapter stays in recognizable adult stakes.

## Red flags

Do not treat a hypothetical answer as a binding promise. Do not moralize poverty or ambition. Do not copy scenarios from board games or viral quizzes. Different answers are a successful round.
