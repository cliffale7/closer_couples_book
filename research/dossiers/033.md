# Dossier 033 — The Attraction Interview

- Catalog ID: 033

- Confidence: high

## Source premise

Explore first attraction, current attraction, and how your understanding of chemistry has evolved.

## Must preserve

Three movements, in order: first attraction, current attraction, and the change in how they understand chemistry. Skip the third and the chapter is only nostalgia. Treat current attraction as a score and the chapter becomes an evaluation. Chemistry here can include humor, mind, voice, competence, kindness, and want, not only a face or a body.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

"What first attracted you?" is folk conversation. Models of love and desire exist in relationship science and were not opened for this dossier. Classification stays reimagined. The three-part interview, with both people in both roles, is a design for adults who already share a relationship. It is not a validated assessment of chemistry.

## Design notes

Materials: a timer, optional cards labeled First, Now, and How I understand it now. No rating scales.

Standard, about 35 minutes. Person A interviews Person B for about eight minutes, covering all three movements, then they swap. The interviewer gets two follow-ups and does not grade the answers. A useful follow-up: "what do you notice now that you did not have language for then?" Short: one movement each, plus a single sentence on the other two. Extended: a second pass only on how chemistry has changed, including the possibility that it is quieter, narrower, or more specific than at the start.

Sample questions the chapter can offer as fiction-ready prompts: what drew you in the first hour; what draws you on an ordinary Tuesday; which early idea of chemistry you have retired. Include at least one prompt that is not visual.

What makes the instructions fail: a scorecard; comparison to other lovers as a required topic; pressure to perform desire they do not feel tonight; treating a change in attraction as a verdict on the relationship; one person always interviewing and never answering. If current attraction is complicated, "complicated, and here is one true part" is a complete answer.

Accessibility. Conversation works by voice, text, or letter. Vision: do not require appearance talk; attraction can be told through sound, humor, and action. Hearing: written interview. Mobility, pain, and illness: current attraction may include how they want closeness that fits their body now; the interview must allow that without making it the whole theme. Long-distance: a call is enough. Sensory overload: the short version, no simultaneous flirting game.

Journal cue: one line each for first, now, and the shift in understanding. Sexual detail stays off the shared page unless the writer of that page wants it there.

## Consent and harm

Either person may skip sexual or bodily detail and stay with the rest of attraction. Comments about the partner's body are offered, not demanded, and the partner may redirect. Do not require a history of past partners. Adult participants only; the interview is about these two adults. A flat or uncertain current answer is information, not an emergency the interviewer must fix in the room.

## Red flags

Do not import K1989. Two minutes of stranger gaze is not evidence about this interview. Do not import A1997's question list. Do not claim that talking about chemistry increases it. Do not use the chapter to negotiate a sexual act; 034 is the consent sort.
