# Dossier 079 — The Soundtrack of Us

- Catalog ID: 079
- Part: 10 — The Past, the Present, and the Story of Us
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Choose music representing chapters or feelings in your shared history and tell its stories.

## Must preserve

The story is the point; the track is the cue. Each piece of music is tied to a chapter or a feeling in the shared history, and the teller says what it holds. Pressing play without the story, or swapping lyrics as if they were the couple’s own words, loses the premise.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Mixtapes, “our song,” and wedding processions are widespread customs. They are not a validated protocol for relationship quality. No music-and-memory paper was opened for this dossier, so the chapter must not claim that songs reliably retrieve autobiographical memory or repair a bond.

## Design notes

Each person brings three pieces: one from early on, one from a difficult or ordinary middle stretch if they want it, and one that fits now. Time: 40–60 minutes. For each track, speak first: where you were, whose choice it was, what it carries. Then play a short excerpt at a volume both can stand, or skip playback and let the title stand. After all six, choose one piece to keep as a private encore and one story that should be told even when the song is unavailable.

Failure points: a quiz about whether the partner remembers the song; embarrassment as the joke; playlists so long the stories shrink; treating a private association (a former relationship, a grief) as public without warning.

Accessibility and sensory load: the story must work with the sound off. Offer a written title, artist, and two-sentence liner note. Headphones, a single speaker, or no playback at all are equal options. Tinnitus, misophonia, hearing aids, and noise sensitivity set the volume, including silence. Lyrics are optional; instrumental music counts. Long-distance: send titles in advance and listen on a call, or trade liner notes asynchronously. No dancing is required. 038 remains the chapter for moving together in silence.

Copyright: name the song. Do not print lyrics. A short paraphrase of why a line mattered is enough.

## Consent and harm

Warn before a song tied to grief, illness, or an ex-partner. Either person may skip a track without explaining the association. Music that mocks a community, or that one partner finds degrading, does not belong on a shared soundtrack by surprise.

## Red flags

Do not imply a couple needs a commercially romantic song. Do not diagnose taste. Do not build the chapter from a copyrighted playlist product or a branded “couple’s soundtrack” service. Shared history includes silence, news radio, and songs one person loved and the other tolerated. Those stories count.
