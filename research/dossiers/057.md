## Source premise

> Describe how you act when distressed and what kinds of support help or worsen things.

## Must preserve

Both halves: the visible pattern when the speaker is distressed, and the specific supports that help or worsen it. A menu of preferred comforts without “how I act” loses the premise, because partners usually meet the behavior first. The manual is a current description the author can revise. It is not a contract the other person may enforce, and it is not a diagnosis.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

“Here is how to help me when I am upset” is a common caring conversation. A playful owner’s-manual format is a teaching device, not a validated tool. Use it for ordinary bad days. It is not a crisis plan for danger, self-harm, or violence, and it does not replace urgent help when someone is at risk.

## Design notes

Each person writes one page: early signs; what I do that can mislead you; what helps in the first ten minutes; what makes it worse; what is not about you; when to give space; when to stay nearby. The partner reads it back and asks one clarifying question. They do not edit the manual into what is convenient for them. An optional practice uses a mild hypothetical, not a live crisis.

Failure points: filing the manual for later use as a weapon (“your sheet says you shut down”); writing only what the partner hopes to hear; a help list no one could actually do.

Accessibility: distress may look like shutdown, irritability, a pain flare, sensory overload, or silence. Valid instructions include “do not touch,” “do not talk,” “text me,” and “sit in the next room.” Long-distance manuals include a call script and a no-call option. The page is a written object, so it can be read with a screen reader or in large type. Keep bullets short. Rest is a support, not a failure to engage.

## Consent and harm

The manual is revocable. Following it is care, not obedience. Either person may say an instruction is beyond what they can do. Do not use the chapter for acute danger; seek urgent local help if someone is at risk. Do not describe methods of self-harm.

Distinguish 029, The Reassurance Menu, which lists helps for lonely, stressed, insecure, sad, or disconnected states. Entry 057 centers how the speaker behaves on a bad day and what worsens it. Distinguish 028, a broader playful manual of needs and maintenance. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not assign attachment styles. Do not cite a conflict percentage or a divorce prediction. Do not claim the manual prevents fights. Do not treat a bad-day pattern as the person’s character.
