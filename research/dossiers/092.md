# Dossier 092 — The Dream Exchange

- Catalog ID: 092
- Part: 12 — Building a Closer Future Together
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Share individual ambitions and imagine support that respects each person’s ownership.

## Must preserve

The ambition stays the dreamer’s. Support is imagined and then accepted, declined, or modified by the person whose dream it is. A partner who improves, funds, or schedules the dream until it is no longer owned has left the chapter.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Mentorship and couples counseling often discuss individual goals beside shared ones. Those practices are not a protocol for this exchange. No goal-setting paper was opened for this dossier; do not import effect sizes from other chapters’ sources.

## Design notes

About 30–45 minutes. Each person names one ambition that may or may not star the relationship: a craft, a trip, a change of work, a friendship, a way of living in a body, a creative project. Say what “support” has felt like in the past, including support that was too much. The partner offers from a spoken menu: witness, practical help, an introduction, money only if it is truly available, cheer, protected time, or “I need to think.” The dreamer accepts, declines, or rewrites the offer. A second ambition can wait for another day. One exchange done cleanly is enough.

Failure points: the partner’s résumé of how they would do the dream; a dream that is actually a demand on the other person’s life; turning encouragement into a project-management system; sulking at a declined offer.

Accessibility: ambitions must be allowed to be small, local, and compatible with pain, caregiving, and limited money. Do not treat a modest dream as a warmup for a grand one. Long-distance: the menu still applies; “presence” may be calls and logistics. Journal: the dream in the dreamer’s words, the offer, and the response, so ownership stays visible.

## Consent and harm

The listener does not have to fund, relocate, or give up their own ambition. Either person may say the dream is not ready to be helped. Dreams that involve a third person’s body, money, or consent stay incomplete until that person is actually asked, outside this exercise. Distinguish 093, a shared bucket list, and 055, a personal list of what one wants to experience before dying. 092 is one owned ambition plus negotiated support.

## Red flags

Do not assume both people want public achievement. Do not romanticize sacrifice as the only real support. Do not treat a dream of leaving a harmful situation as a playful exchange; respond to danger directly. Ownership includes the right to dream something the partner does not share.
