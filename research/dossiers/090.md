# Dossier 090 — The Future Memory

- Catalog ID: 090
- Part: 12 — Building a Closer Future Together
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Describe a happy future memory as if it already happened, then consider how to create it.

## Must preserve

One happy scene, told in the past tense as if it has already happened, followed by a practical look at how it might be made. The tense is the mechanism. A wish list, a five-year plan, or three centenarian memories belong elsewhere.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Visualization and “act as if” exercises circulate in sports and self-help. They are not a validated couple protocol. Schacter, Addis, and Buckner (2007), a review opened for this dossier, argue that imagining future events depends on much of the same neural machinery as remembering, and that future simulation recombines details from the past. That is a cognitive-neuroscience claim. It does not show that a couple exercise changes a relationship, does not show that imagining makes an event happen, and does not show that people cannot tell memory from imagination. The chapter must not borrow clinical cases from that review as relationship advice.

## Design notes

About 25–40 minutes. Each person writes one page: place, a line of dialogue, a sensory detail, and a feeling, all in the past tense, about a happy time that has not happened. Read it aloud as a memory. Then a second, shorter page: one condition already true, one condition that would have to be built, one step available this month, and one element that is weather and cannot be forced. If the two scenes conflict, they stay side by side. Nobody’s scene is edited into the other’s.

Failure points: a scene that spends the partner’s money, body, or family as props; skipping the practical page and calling the fantasy a plan; using the scene to complain about the present (“in the memory you finally…”).

Accessibility: dictate instead of writing. Choose scenes that do not require unstated physical ability; if a scene includes dancing, travel, or sex, mark it as one possible future, not a demand. Long-distance: exchange pages before the call so listening is the shared act. Journal both the memory and the practical page, labeled fiction-as-memory so later readers do not mistake it for a diary of record.

## Consent and harm

The partner is not obligated to want the scene. Desire, children, relocation, and marriage proposals disguised as memories need an explicit check before they are treated as a shared project. Either person may keep a scene private and offer only the practical step they control. Distinguish 098: that original chapter is a mutual centenarian interview in which contradictory life stories are part of the fun, and each person later names three invented memories to make real. 090 is one scene and one bridge. Distinguish 095: a future memory is not yet a promise.

## Red flags

Do not claim neuroscience validates the exercise. Do not promise outcomes. Do not quote or closely paraphrase the patient vignettes in the 2007 review. Happiness in the scene can be quiet. Do not require cinematic romance.
