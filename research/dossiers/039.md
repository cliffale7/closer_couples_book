# Dossier 039 — The Romantic Time Machine

- Access date: 2026-10-14

## Source premise

> Imagine meeting in a different era or circumstance and what would still attract you.

## Must preserve

The surviving attractor. The couple imagines a different meeting — another era, city, job, or accident of timing — and then names what would still draw them, once costume and circumstance are stripped off. The chapter fails if it becomes a history quiz, a costume party, or a complaint that the present partner is less romantic than the fictional one. Attraction here is recognition across a changed scene, not a new plot about invented selves.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. Counterfactual conversation has not been shown, in any source on the project whitelist, to increase desire or to reveal a stable trait called chemistry.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Wondering “what if we had met another way” is a familiar romantic conversation and a staple of fiction. It is not a clinical assessment and not a past-life exercise. The writer should treat it as imaginative play with a precise question at the end.

## Design notes

Each person chooses one meeting condition. Offer non-historical options beside eras: a delayed train, a different trade, a festival, a city neither has lived in. Historical expertise is unnecessary. Three beats keep the mechanism: how you notice each other in that scene; which quality is still this person and not the costume; one thing that other life would have hidden, which you are glad you know now.

Give the listener a job: reflect the quality they heard, then add one quality they would still notice. Failure points: scoring historical accuracy; using the fantasy to say the real relationship disappoints; staying in the costume so long that the “still attract you” question never arrives.

Accessibility: talk or write. Long-distance fits this chapter well. No mobility demand. Offer circumstance prompts so nobody has to role-play a period that carries family, political, or bodily pain. Vision and hearing needs are met by speech or text. Fatigue: one era each.

## Consent and harm

The imagined meeting is not a request to act a sex scene or to adopt period gender roles. Either person may discard an era. The present relationship is not on trial for failing to match the fantasy.

Distinguish 046, which invents alternate versions of both people and follows their meeting, attraction, and misadventures as a story. Entry 039 keeps the real persons and asks what attraction would survive a changed meeting. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not romanticize eras of legal inequality as simpler or more passionate. Do not imply that a “true” self is the one in the costume. Do not claim research on fate, soulmates, or timing. Avoid stock scenes that only work for a woman-and-man pair in formal clothes.
