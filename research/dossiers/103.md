## Source premise

> Solve cooperative puzzles involving personal history, partner knowledge, and complementary skills.

## Must preserve

A cooperative tabletop mystery in which each person holds different information. The signature twist, from the project’s expanded brief: “Neither person has the complete information; the clues and puzzles require joining each other's knowledge.” The catalog title stays. The play is not a purchased room, a business, or a kit. The win is a restored ledger page, written together. Formulated for this project, without a claim of worldwide novelty. The starter below was solved on paper for this dossier and has not been live-playtested with a couple.

## Design notes

Time: 60–90 minutes, seated. Materials: two envelopes, North and South; the packets below; a pencil; the grid; scrap paper. Phones face down if both agree. Choose North and South by preference, not by gender or puzzle pride.

Before clues: each player privately writes a place-slip (one shared place, a short phrase) and keeps it in their own packet. Each also writes a reason-slip: on the front, a food, song, or object they associate with the partner; on the back, why. The reason-slip goes into the partner’s envelope, back folded shut. Blank slips are allowed. Players who use blanks will finish by the bypass sentence in the answer key.

Shared prologue, read aloud: “A night ledger holds three evenings, and one row is misfiled. No single witness saw the whole night. Restore the grid from your own packet. Then the ledger asks for three locks only the two of you can open. You may read your clues aloud. You may not hand your packet across during play.”

Accessibility: large print; one numbered clue read at a time by a screen reader or by a third person, not by dumping the whole packet to the other player. If the partner is the only reader, they read a single requested clue and stop. Color is not a clue. No darkness, locks, crawling, audio shocks, or moving through the house. Long-distance: two private documents and one call for the joint grid. Either person may stop. A partial page is a finished night.

Every placement is spoken with the clue number that supports it, so one partner cannot solve silently and announce.

### Packet North

You know the toast’s hour and Cedar’s relation to the soup. You do not know which evening is the walk, or where the bread sits.

N1. The toast happens at 8.
N2. Cedar begins two hours before the soup.
N3. Harbor is not the walk.
N4. Bread is not served at the earliest hour.

### Packet South

You know which evening is the walk, where the bread sits, and that the question comes two hours before Harbor. You do not know the toast’s hour.

S1. Lantern is the walk.
S2. Bread is served at 10.
S3. The question is asked two hours before Harbor.
S4. Soup is not served at 10.

Hours are only 6, 8, and 10. Evenings are Cedar, Harbor, and Lantern. Foods are oranges, soup, and bread. First moves are a question, a toast, and a walk. Each item is used once.

### Locks, after the grid matches

Cedar lock: the player holding the partner’s reason-slip reads the front and guesses why. Then they may read the back. A wrong guess still opens the lock. Mocking the reason closes the night.

Harbor lock: each player tries to name the other’s place-slip, up to three guesses. Answers are only “yes” or “another try.” After three misses the holder may read the slip or pass. A pass opens the bypass: name any shared place both can picture.

Lantern lock: write one sentence that uses one true detail from either personal lock and the Cedar row. If both personal locks were passed, the bypass sentence in the answer key is a complete restoration.

### Answer key

Do not print this on the same spread as the packets.

1. S3 allows question-at-6 with Harbor-at-8, or question-at-8 with Harbor-at-10.
2. N1 puts the toast at 8, so the question cannot be at 8. The question is at 6. Harbor is at 8.
3. Harbor’s move is the toast.
4. The remaining hour, 10, is the walk. S1 says Lantern is the walk, so Lantern is at 10.
5. Cedar is the remaining evening, at 6, and holds the question.
6. S2 puts bread at 10, so Lantern serves bread.
7. N2: Cedar at 6 means the soup is at 8. Harbor serves soup.
8. Oranges remain with Cedar.
9. N3, N4, and S4 confirm the grid. If one of them conflicts, a clue was misread. There is no second solution.

Practice pair for an editorial solo check, not people the couple must play: North is Rowan, place-slip “ferry,” reason-slip front “ginger,” back “Ellis bought ginger chews for my sore throat and pretended they were ordinary.” South is Ellis, place-slip “library,” reason-slip front “pretzel,” back “Rowan broke a pretzel into the soup the night the stove failed.” The logic key does not change.

Bypass sentence, a complete solution when private locks stay closed: “We restored Cedar, at 6, oranges, and a question, and we left the private locks closed.”

### Reset

Erase the grid. Refold reason-slip backs. Return place-slips to their writers. Once this key has been read, the logic case is retired for that pair. A later night reuses the lock procedure with new slips. Do not rename evenings, hours, foods, or moves unless someone writes and test-solves a new key first. Reason-slips are journaled or destroyed at the writer’s choice.

Failure points: racing ahead; treating a missed personal guess as losing; a memory disagreement becoming a cross-examination.

## Consent and harm

Personal slips are optional. No sexual disclosure is required. Either person may stop without finishing the grid. The restored page is play, not a verdict on who knows the relationship better. Reader-facing instructions keep both people at the table with the two packets. Do not send them through the house or the neighborhood, and do not refer to any other project.

## Red flags
