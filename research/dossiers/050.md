## Source premise

> Discuss meaning, freedom, beauty, pleasure, contribution, and what makes life worthwhile.

## Must preserve

All six notes in the premise, not a single moral of contribution. Meaning, freedom, beauty, pleasure, and contribution each get a concrete scene from the speaker’s life, and then the pair talks about what makes life worthwhile without forcing a shared doctrine. The mechanism fails if answers must be inspiring, ranked, or converted into a productivity plan.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity. No meaning-in-life scale, mortality theory, or happiness study on the project whitelist applies, and none should be improvised.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Philosophical and ordinary conversation about a worthwhile life is the frame. It is not a religious test, not a clinical values protocol, and not a workbook from a named therapy school. The writer can be literate and plain without citing an unread book.

## Design notes

Use six cards: meaning, freedom, beauty, pleasure, contribution, and a blank the speaker names. For each card, one scene, not a definition. Then each person names one card they want more of in the next month, small enough to schedule, and one card they are content to leave as appreciation only.

The writer should model one fictional scene per card so readers see scale: freedom as leaving a party, pleasure as a repeated breakfast, contribution as a skill used for a neighbor. The month-ahead choice is one scene to repeat, not a new identity. Leave a blank line for a scene that did not fit any card.

Failure points: abstract essays; ranking whose answer is deeper; joking pleasure away while treating contribution as the only adult answer; rushing a partner who says they do not know.

Accessibility: scenes can be tiny — a window, a meal, a skill, a favor. Long-distance works. “I don’t know” is a complete answer; the partner does not repair it. Beauty is not only visual: sound, taste, motion, and moral beauty count. Fatigue and low mood: two cards are enough. Hearing and vision: speech or print.

## Consent and harm

Nobody owes an inspiring philosophy. Mortality can be set aside; entries 055 and 056 are the neighboring places for lists and unanswerable questions. Do not push a partner through numbness.

Distinguish 055, which lists experiences still wanted, including ordinary pleasures and grand adventures, and 056, which explores questions that may stay open. Entry 050 is about sources of worth already recognizable in a life. Do not merge them.

## Red flags

Do not imply that a person who cannot name meaning is unwell. Do not impose a religion or a refusal of religion. Do not turn the month-ahead note into a performance target. No “research shows” sentence belongs in the chapter.
