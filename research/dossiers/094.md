# Dossier 094 — The Annual Reintroduction

- Catalog ID: 094
- Part: 12 — Building a Closer Future Together
- Access date: 2026-10-14
- Classification: reimagined
- Confidence: medium

## Source premise

> Return yearly to rediscover changing needs, interests, values, and wishes.

## Must preserve

A repeating return, yearly if they can, whose purpose is rediscovery because people change. Needs, interests, values, and wishes are all in scope. A one-time recap of the book, or a date that assumes nobody has changed, loses the ritual.

## Evidence

No empirical citation is claimed for this activity.

## Tradition or practitioner frame

Anniversaries, birthdays, and retreat check-ins already give couples a calendar excuse to talk. The reimagining is an explicit reintroduction rather than a celebration script. It is not a validated protocol. It is not 001’s stranger interview, though curiosity is welcome. It is not 096’s closing design of practices after moving through this book, though 096 may be where a couple first decides to keep this ritual.

## Design notes

About 45–60 minutes, once a year, tied to a week they can actually protect. If they miss it, the next calm week counts. There is no penalty narrative. Five prompts, each person answers all five: what is new in your body, work, or daily life; what you want more of; what you want retired; what you still want known; which value has shifted, even slightly. Then one shared revision: a ritual to keep, a ritual to drop, or a wish to carry forward. Write a single journal page dated for next year’s comparison.

Failure points: turning the meeting into a performance review; saving every grievance for this day; identical answers copied from last year out of politeness; a lavish setting that makes the ritual easy to postpone.

Accessibility: kitchen table or video call. Offer the prompts in advance. A partner who is ill, grieving, or linguistically tired may answer three of the five. Long-distance is a natural fit. No gift is required. The ritual should be doable in a borrowed room, a hospital dayroom, or a walk only if walking is welcome. A seated default is the accessible form.

## Consent and harm

Changes in desire, faith, family role, or the wish to stay must be welcome as topics and must also be declinable if the speaker is not ready. The listener does not cross-examine a change. New agreements that touch sex, money, or living arrangements are noted and then given a separate sober conversation, not rushed to closure because the annual date is ending. Either person may bring a support person or a pause.

## Red flags

Do not claim that annual talks prevent breakup. Do not assume a New Year, a wedding anniversary, or a shared culture’s holiday. Do not require the relationship to display growth. “I want the same things, and I checked” is a valid reintroduction.
