# Prompt bank

Four prompts for every activity from 001 through 108: Partner A, Partner B, a shared narrative, and a revisit. Premises are the catalog premises. Prompts are invitations. They are not memories, and they are not filled in.

Partner A and Partner B are seats for one sitting. The prompts are different, and the handwriting allotment is the same. Either person may pass, pause, or change a prompt. A private page is optional to share.

When a writer embeds a prompt in a chapter, or a builder prints it in Our Story, the prompt is followed by at least eight handwriting rules. Printed baselines are at least 0.32 inch apart. The fenced block under each ID is the expansion marker. Add sheets rather than tightening the lines.

The chapter contract also asks for a second bullet under each partner. Use the second slot on `journal/templates/partner-page.md`, or a narrower follow-up inside the same premise. Do not replace the bank prompt with a generic question about feelings, and do not require a secret to be transcribed.

Activities 033–040 are discreet and optional. Skipping the page is a complete entry. A boundary, a change of mind, or a blank is enough. Do not archive a scene you would not want to reread.

Where a prompt says a title or a shape is enough, the confidential content can stay spoken.

This bank does not include sample answers.

Special sheets, when named, are in `journal/templates/`.

## Part 1 — The Art of Really Knowing Someone

### 001 — The Stranger Across From Me

Premise: Pretend you have just met. Interview one another as fascinating strangers and discover how familiarity can hide curiosity.

**Partner A:** Write the question you asked only because you were pretending you had just met. What did the answer show you that familiarity had been skipping?

**Partner B:** Write an answer you gave in the stranger’s seat that you rarely bother to say in full, and name the shorthand it usually replaces.

**Shared:** Narrate the interview as a first meeting: who began, one wrong assumption, and the detail that made the other person newly visible.

**Revisit:** Set this meeting-as-strangers beside the last one. Which fact you thought you knew turned out unfinished, and what curiosity came back?

### 002 — The Life You’ve Never Told Me

Premise: Share a significant episode of life the other has never heard in detail, including turning points and formative relationships.

**Shared:** Write the one sentence you both agree can live in the shared book, and how that older chapter still touches the present.

**Revisit:** If a later sitting opened another closed chapter, how was the telling different from the first time?
