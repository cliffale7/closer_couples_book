The Art of Really Knowing Someone

The Museum of Me

You curate four pieces of a life. Your partner is a visitor, not a critic.

  1. Each curator prepares four exhibits. Every label has three lines: what it is, which stretch of life it holds, and why it is in the collection. A mug, a person, a song, or a missed train can be an exhibit.
  2. If all four are achievements, replace one with something ordinary, odd, or unfinished.
  3. The curator speaks the labels in their own order. After each exhibit the visitor may ask one question that asks to see the piece more clearly. A question that replaces the piece with the visitor's theory does not count. Ask another, or ask none.
  4. Then the visitor becomes the curator.

The visitor names the exhibit they will remember, in the curator's words, and the curator may correct the label.

Any exhibit may stay in storage. Say "not on view" and continue.
